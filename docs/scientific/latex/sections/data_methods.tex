% P:methods-01
Loading is $\alpha=n_{\COtwo,\mathrm{added}}/n_{\mathrm{amine,total}}$, and unloaded-solvent mass fraction is $w_{\MEA}=m_{\MEA}/(m_{\MEA}+m_{\HtwoO})$.
Source molalities, measured temperatures and aggregate-species definitions determine the calculated counterpart of each measurement.
The objective contains eight disjoint groups and 307 quantities (\cref{tab:objective-groups}); 149 of these, comprising 47 pressures, 94 species fractions and eight integral heats, are shared with the reference fit defined in \cref{sec:fit-definitions}.
Seven additional species observations (four B\"ottinger and three Matin) and six Jakobsen ratios to total amine form separate objective groups.
The Jakobsen ratios are \MEAH, \MEACOO and \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}} relative to \ce{MEA + MEAH+ + MEACOO-} at each of two states \cite{Jakobsen2005}.
Cai et al.\ contribute a bubble temperature and vapor-water fraction at each of 25 binary states, giving 50 quantities \cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996}.

B\"ottinger et al.\ observe \ce{MEA + MEAH+} as an NMR sum and tabulate bicarbonate, noting that carbonate is practically absent \cite[p.~134, Table~3]{Bottinger2008}.
Their bicarbonate-labelled values are compared here with calculated \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}}.
Matin et al.\ infer bicarbonate from titration and carbon balance after neglecting carbonate in their working balances \cite[pp.~6615--6616, Eqs.~18--19b]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
Comparing these values with calculated \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}} is an observation approximation, consistent with their discussion of carbonate entering the inferred bicarbonate amount.
All 19 such Matin observations remain outside the objective.
Their figures label \SI{21}{\degreeCelsius}, approximated here by \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius}.
Quantities from the same prepared solution need not be statistically independent.

The six-source pressure comparison includes 159 observations from Aronu, Hilliard, Idris, Jou, Ma'mun and Xu \cite{Aronu2011,Hilliard2008,idrisSpeciationMEACO2Adducts2014,Jou1995,Mamun2005,Xu2011}.

\subsection{Caloric and density calculations}\label{sec:heat-method}
% P:methods-heat-01
Eight Vinjarapu integral heats are fitted at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal}, from initially carbon-free 30 wt\% solvent \cite[Sects.~1.1.1 and 2.2, Table~4]{HemaBhavya2025}.
The signed heat per mole of added \COtwo is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta h=\frac{H_{\mathrm{final}}-H_{\mathrm{initial}}-\Delta n_{\COtwo}h_{\COtwo}^{\mathrm{feed}}}{\Delta n_{\COtwo}},
 \label{eq:addition-heat}
\end{equation}
where both equilibrium liquid enthalpies and the pure-\COtwo feed enthalpy are evaluated at the stated temperature and pressure; negative values denote exothermic absorption.
Fourteen Arcis heats at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin} and \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} or \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal} are checks used in parameter selection \cite[Sect.~2.4, Table~5]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.

% P:methods-heat-02
Total enthalpy follows the same Helmholtz energy and reaction standard states as the equilibrium calculation:
\begin{equation}
 H=PV-RT^2\left(\frac{\partial(A/RT)}{\partial T}\right)_{V,\boldsymbol n},\qquad
 A=A^{\mathrm{ig}}+A^{\mathrm{res}}.
 \label{eq:total-enthalpy}
\end{equation}
Neutral ideal-gas functions supply the caloric reference; the ionic functions are completed from the five reaction laws, including $\Delta_rh^\circ=RT^2d\ln K_r/dT$ and the derivatives of the infinite-dilution reference corrections.
Reaction enthalpies are therefore included once through the standard-state functions.
Section~S8 gives the ideal-gas correlations, coefficients and reference convention, including the extrapolation of the water Shomate function below \SI{500}{\kelvin}.
Equilibrium isobaric heat capacity is $C_P=(dH_{\mathrm{eq}}/dT)_{P,\boldsymbol n_{\mathrm{feed}}}$, including the change in speciation.
The objective uses $C_P/n_{\mathrm{feed}}$ per mole of complete analytical feed, with source mass-specific values converted on that same basis.
The mass-specific value is $c_P=C_P/m_{\mathrm{feed}}$, where $m_{\mathrm{feed}}=n_{\MEA}M_{\MEA}+n_wM_w+n_{\COtwo}M_{\COtwo}$ is the complete analytical-feed mass.
The four fitted Hilliard heat capacities use 7 mol \MEA/kg water, calculation pressure \SI{101325}{\pascal} and a $10^{-6}$ loading approximation for the nominally unloaded states \cite[Sects.~4.5--4.6, App.~G.2]{Hilliard2008}.

% P:methods-heat-03
Densities are calculated directly from the equation of state, without volume translation.
The fitted loaded observations comprise 15 Amundsen and 32 Hartono densities; five carbon-free densities use exactly zero analytical carbon, and all 67 Han densities are comparisons \cite{Amundsen2009,Hartono2014,hanDensityWater12012}.
Five Amundsen densities at loading 0.4 are excluded after comparison with Hartono values interpolated to the same loading, temperature and solvent composition; all five remain in the complete comparison.
The calculations use \SI{100000}{\pascal} for Amundsen where source pressure is unreported; other pressures follow the individual observations.
The loaded-density scale is
\begin{equation}
 s_{\rho,j}=\left[u_{\rho,j}^2+(g_j u_{\alpha,j})^2\right]^{1/2},
 \label{eq:density-scale}
\end{equation}
where $g_j$ is the density--loading secant between neighboring observations at the same source temperature and solvent composition, using a one-sided secant at the ends.
Section~S3 gives the source-specific scales and qualifications.
The scales combine heterogeneous uncertainty information; $2s$ is not uniformly a 95\,\% interval, and Amundsen's precise high-temperature/loading uncertainty boundary remains unresolved \cite[pp.~3099--3100]{Amundsen2009}.

\section{Parameter estimation and numerical checks}\label{sec:parameters}
\subsection{Fit definitions, bounds and objective}\label{sec:fit-definitions}
% P:methods-04
The original-bound fit and restricted-bound fit use the same 16 adjustable parameters and 307 quantities.
The restricted-bound fit is the parameter set reported here.
The restriction changes the \MEAH segment-diameter bounds from 2--\SI{5.8}{\angstrom} to 5.35--\SI{5.8}{\angstrom}, and the \MEAH--water interaction bounds from $[-0.5,0.5]$ to $[-0.5,-0.161]$; all other physical bounds, observations, scales and weights are unchanged.
The reference fit is an earlier pressure--speciation--heat calibration to the shared 149 quantities, without density; it supplies comparison limits and the untranslated density baseline (Section~S1).
It has different fixed inputs and is not a control isolating one model term.
\Cref{tab:fitted-coordinates} gives the selected values, bounds and conditional standard errors.
Fixed inputs include earlier pure-water, binary and permittivity estimates, documented with their sources in Section~S9.

% P:methods-05
The bounded least-squares problem minimizes
\begin{equation}
 C(\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac12\sum_jw_jr_j^2,\qquad
 r_j=\begin{cases}(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}-y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})/s_j,&\text{difference},\\
 \ln(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})/s_j,&\text{log ratio}.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:fit-residuals}
\end{equation}
Loaded densities have weight $1/47$, so their group contributes a mean scaled squared error rather than a sum growing with its observation count.
They contribute 0.566 of the total cost 148.9, or 0.38\,\%; the separate $2s$ check prevents their small cost share from hiding individual failures.
Carbon-free densities have scale \SI{0.5}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} and weight 10; Cai temperature and vapor-fraction scales are \SI{1}{\kelvin} and 0.01 with unit weight, and Kiepe \COtwo--water pressures have weight $1/39$ \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002}.
These weights set fitting priorities; the heat scales are not measured standard uncertainties.
\Cref{tab:objective-groups} specifies every group.

% P:methods-06
The fit uses Ceres bounded Levenberg--Marquardt least squares with dense QR, affine parameter scaling and exact equation-of-state and implicit equilibrium derivatives.
The deposited starting parameter vectors and affine transformations specify the numerical scaling.
Function, parameter and gradient tolerances are $10^{-6}$, $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-8}$, respectively, with a maximum of 180 iterations.
Two sequential restricted fits were made, each from one start.
The first used a \MEAH size lower bound of \SI{5.49}{\angstrom}; the second started from its solution and lowered that bound to \SI{5.35}{\angstrom}, stopping on function tolerance after 12 iterations.
The restrictions were chosen from fixed-parameter probes: smaller diameters raise density but can overshoot the colder Hartono observations (Section~S5).
Phase searches were applied to the resulting parameter sets, rather than imposed as constraints at every optimizer iteration.

% P:methods-07
\Cref{tab:acceptance-limits} gives this study's comparison limits.
Allowances relative to the reference fit preserve pressure, species and heat agreement while other properties enter the fit: 10\,\% in shared-subset cost, two percentage points in pressure/species deviation and \SI{1}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} in each heat RMSE.
The Cai allowance is 5\,\% above a binary-only fit with a constant \MEA--water interaction, whose cost is 91.33.
The density limits are twice the assigned scale, or \SI{1}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} for carbon-free states; the phase requirement rejects any resolved lower-Gibbs-energy arrangement in the specified search.
These are study-specific tolerances for retaining property agreement, not published accuracy standards; the three density failures remain failures.

\subsection{Finite phase search}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:methods-phase-01
Both fits are searched at the same 73 states at 313.15--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin} and $\alpha=10^{-5}$--0.5 for nominal 30 wt\% solvent.
The state list was fixed before the restricted fits.
At each temperature and loading, pressures are 0.50, 0.80 and 0.95 times a fixed reference pressure $P_*$ listed in Section~S4; one grid state (\SI{318.15}{\kelvin}, loading 0.256) was also evaluated at a pressure rounded to 5918.5286~Pa, and both evaluations are counted, so the 73 states contain 72 distinct conditions (Section~S4).
Each parameter set is then solved at those same fixed pressures, so its single-liquid or liquid--vapor classification depends on its own bubble pressure.
The tangent-plane criterion follows Michelsen \cite[Sect.~2]{Michelsen1982}, extended here to reacting, charge-neutral trial phases.
For trial composition $\boldsymbol w$, the reduced distance is
\begin{equation}
 D(\boldsymbol w)=g(T,P,\boldsymbol w)/(RT)-\boldsymbol w^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda,
 \quad \sum_iw_i=1,\quad\sum_i z_iw_i=0,\quad w_i>0,
 \label{eq:tpd}
\end{equation}
where $B$ represents conserved component combinations and $\boldsymbol\lambda$ their equilibrium chemical-potential multipliers.
Liquid trials support all nine species; vapor trials support only \COtwo, \MEA and water.
The nine initial trials, Gibbs-energy error estimate and finite-transfer test are specified in Section~S4.
The reacting equilibria and trials use the ePC-SAFT solver's Newton and Ipopt procedures with exact derivatives, MA57 linear algebra and a tolerance of $10^{-10}$ on the recomputed residuals used to decide convergence, after the solver's row scaling.
An equilibrium is classified as unstable only when a negative tangent-plane distance is accompanied by a material-conserving finite transfer that decreases Gibbs energy by more than its numerical-error estimate.
Separately seeded two-liquid solves confirm selected instabilities of the original-bound fit; a two-phase classification by itself does not indicate a second liquid, because it includes liquid--vapor equilibrium.

\subsection{Conditional uncertainty and numerical verification}
% P:methods-08
For the ten parameters free of active bounds, the weighted residual Jacobian $J_I$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol\theta_I)=s_C^2(J_I^{\mathsf T}J_I)^{-1},\qquad s_C^2=\frac{2C}{307-10}.
 \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian has rank ten, 297 degrees of freedom and variance factor 1.003.
The six parameters at bounds have no symmetric standard error.
These local errors condition on the weights, fixed inputs, bounds and linearization; dependence between measurements from the same experiment is not propagated.
Section~S6 gives the parameter order and correlations.

% P:methods-09
The 375 equilibrium states for the fitted and compared pressure and species observations have maximum reported conservation, charge and stationarity residuals no greater than $2.2\times10^{-12}$ on their stated bases (Section~S8).
Agreement with observations is reported as average absolute relative deviation (AARD),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{AARD}=\frac{100\,\%}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}-1\right|,
 \label{eq:aard}
\end{equation}
root-mean-square error (RMSE) in the observable's units, and signed logarithmic bias $\langle\ln(y^{\mathrm{calc}}/y^{\mathrm{obs}})\rangle$ where applicable.
Mixed-property costs are not overall physical-accuracy measures.
