\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% P:conclusion-01
Electrolyte PC-SAFT with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions and explicit carbamate chemistry describes \COtwo partial pressure and liquid speciation in 30 wt\% aqueous \MEA with one set of liquid-phase parameters.
Fitted simultaneously to 48 \COtwo pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and 94 species measurements at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, the nine-species model reaches AARDs of 13.53 and 8.28\,\%.
Outside the fit, it gives 20.27\,\% on the 21 canonical pressure rows at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, and 24.51 and 25.45\,\% on 11 and 12 Wagner rows near 353 and \SI{392}{\kelvin} that were used at no earlier stage.
This extends the ePC-SAFT treatment of reactive MDEA to a carbamate-forming primary amine.

% P:conclusion-02
The Born term is essential in this system.
Refitting without it raises the cost from 32.99 to 98.79 and the fitted-pressure AARD to 53.90\,\%, and the five interaction coordinates cannot recover the loss within their bounds.
Two interactions of protonated \MEA carry the fit: fixing the \MEAH--\MEACOO or the \MEAH--water coefficient at zero raises the cost by 134.32 or 75.87, whereas the \MEAH--water temperature slope adds only 0.38 on 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} data.
Including a Born contribution matters far more than which form is used.
Refitted at three sets of inherited Born inputs, the SSM+DS and original Born formulations calibrate comparably, with costs of 31.72--34.58, fitted-pressure AARDs of 13.53--17.31\,\% and fitted-species AARDs of 7.98--8.28\,\%, and their differences at matched inputs, 0.26--1.59, are small against the rise to 98.79 without the term.
Which form has the lower cost depends both on the fitted observations and on the inherited Born inputs.
SSM+DS is lower without the titration bicarbonate data, 32.99 against 34.58, and the original Born term with them, 40.20 against 47.63; without those data, the original Born term is also lower with the three ion Born diameters multiplied by 0.8 or with \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}=24\).

% P:conclusion-03
These results give three practical rules for electrolyte equation-of-state models of reactive amines.
First, a comparison of electrolyte terms must state which speciation data are fitted: without the Born term, the model moves toward the titration bicarbonate values, with an AARD of 20.12\,\% on those observations against 59.81\,\% for the selected record, while losing pressure accuracy.
Because pressure and speciation data alone do not distinguish the two Born formulations, the choice between them should rest on independent evidence, such as solvation or activity data.
Second, the ion--ion interaction between protonated amine and carbamate, which exists only in loaded solution, must be fitted to ternary data; it carries most of the fit.
Third, data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} do not set the high-temperature response: refits to these data with source or dilute-solution temperature laws for the amine reactions give 28.58--29.33\,\% at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, against 20.27\,\% for the selected record; the temperature response must come from independent temperature data, such as heat of absorption or equilibrium measurements above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.

% P:conclusion-04
The record applies to \COtwo partial pressure and speciation in 30 wt\% \MEA at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}.
Transfer to 15 and 45 wt\% \MEA, liquid density and heat capacity remain outside its supported scope (\cref{sec:results-limits}), and the \HCOthree--water coefficient lies at its bound.
Qualifying density and heat capacity, and adding heat-of-absorption data to the fit, are the next steps toward absorber and stripper calculations.
