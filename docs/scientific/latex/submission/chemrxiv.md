## Title
Electrolyte PC-SAFT with carbamate chemistry for CO2 in aqueous monoethanolamine: the role of the Born term and of speciation data

## Author and affiliation
- Tanner W. Polley (corresponding author), ORCID 0009-0008-5957-4152, tpolley3@byu.edu
- Department of Chemical Engineering, Brigham Young University, Provo, UT 84602, United States

## Abstract
The effect of the Born term on reactive vapor–liquid equilibrium is examined for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA), whose carbamate chemistry changes the ion population with CO₂ loading. A nine-species electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model combines explicit ionic speciation with Debye–Hückel and Born contributions. Five binary interaction parameters are fitted to 47 CO₂ pressures at 40 and 60 °C, 94 liquid species measurements at 20–60 °C and eight integral heats at 40 °C in 30 wt% MEA. Fitted-pressure and species average absolute relative deviations (AARD) are 13.81 and 7.89 %. Wagner pressures near 80 and 119 °C, which entered no fitting or selection step, give 23.11 and 16.23 %; other high-temperature data informed fixed inputs. Within the tested model, bounds, objective and two starts, removing the Born term and refitting raises fitted-pressure AARD to 80.25 %, without establishing physical necessity or a mechanism. The solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation form retains the lower cost both with and without the titration bicarbonate pool. Against a matched no-heat fit, heat RMSE falls from 4.57 to 2.13 kJ/mol for fitted Vinjarapu data and from 11.63 to 8.36 for predicted Arcis data, while Hilliard/Jou packet pressure AARD rises from 18.12 to 29.66 % at 100 °C and from 24.11 to 41.55 % at 120 °C. This trade-off and the conditional parameter uncertainty limit temperature extrapolation and concentration transfer.
