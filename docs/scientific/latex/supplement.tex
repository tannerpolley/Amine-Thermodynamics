\section{Model equations, inputs and parameter values}\label{sec:s-parameters}
\subsection{Hard-chain, dispersion, association and Debye--H\"uckel contributions}\label{sec:s-contributions}
% P:supplement-contributions-01
These four contributions to the residual Helmholtz energy of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:epcsaft-residual-split}) are used in their published forms.
Below, $x_i$ is the mole fraction and $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}$ the segment count of species $i$, $\rho_N=N_{\mathrm A}\rho$ is the number density, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions and $z_i$ is a charge number.
The hard-sphere diameter of a neutral species or chain cation depends on temperature \cite{Gross2001},
\begin{equation}
    d_i=\sigma_i\left[1-0.12\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u_i}{k_BT}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-diameter}
\end{equation}
with the water diameter $\sigma_w(T)$ of the main text in place of a constant $\sigma_i$; a single-sphere ion has the temperature-independent diameter $d_i=0.88\sigma_i$.

% P:supplement-contributions-02
The hard-chain term is \cite{Gross2001}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}=\bar m\,a^{\mathrm{hs}}-\sum_i x_i\left(m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}-1\right)\ln g_{ii}^{\mathrm{hs}},
    \qquad
    \bar m=\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}},
    \label{eq:hard-chain}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_0}\left[
    \frac{3\zeta_1\zeta_2}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3^2}-\zeta_0\right)\ln\left(1-\zeta_3\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\frac{3\zeta_2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\right)^2\frac{2\zeta_2^2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^3},
    \qquad
    \zeta_n=\frac{\pi}{6}\rho_N\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}}d_i^n,
    \quad n=0,\dots,3,
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-contact}
\end{equation}
where $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}$ is the hard-sphere pair distribution function at contact and $\eta=\zeta_3$ is the packing fraction.

% P:supplement-contributions-03
The dispersion term is the second-order perturbation expansion of Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    =-2\pi\rho_N I_1\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\sigma_{ij}^3
    -\pi\rho_N\bar m\,C_1I_2\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\left(\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\right)^2\sigma_{ij}^3 ,
    \label{eq:dispersion}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    C_1=\left[1+\bar m\frac{8\eta-2\eta^2}{\left(1-\eta\right)^4}
    +\left(1-\bar m\right)\frac{20\eta-27\eta^2+12\eta^3-2\eta^4}{\left[\left(1-\eta\right)\left(2-\eta\right)\right]^2}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:dispersion-compressibility}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I_1=\sum_{k=0}^{6}a_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    I_2=\sum_{k=0}^{6}b_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    a_k(\bar m)=a_{0k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}a_{1k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}\frac{\bar m-2}{\bar m}a_{2k},
    \label{eq:dispersion-integrals}
\end{equation}
where $b_k(\bar m)$ has the form of $a_k(\bar m)$, and $a_{0k}$--$a_{2k}$ and $b_{0k}$--$b_{2k}$ are their universal constants.
The pair diameter $\sigma_{ij}$ and the pair energy $u_{ij}$ are those of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:dispersion-combining-rule}).

% P:supplement-contributions-04
Hydrogen bonding follows Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite{Kleiner2007}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{assoc}}=\sum_i x_i\sum_{A\in i}\left(\ln X_A-\frac{X_A}{2}+\frac12\right),
    \qquad
    X_A=\left[1+\rho_N\sum_j x_j\sum_{B\in j}X_B\Delta^{AB}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:association}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta^{AB}=g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}\,\sigma_{ij}^3\,\kappa^{AB}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\epsilon^{AB}}{k_BT}\right)-1\right],
    \label{eq:association-strength}
\end{equation}
where $X_A$ is the fraction of molecules $i$ not bonded at site $A$ and $\Delta^{AB}$ is the strength of a bond between site $A$ on molecule $i$ and site $B$ on molecule $j$.
Cross-association between an amine species and water uses the Wolbach--Sandler rule \cite{Kleiner2007},
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon^{AB}_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(\epsilon^{AB}_i+\epsilon^{AB}_j\right),
    \qquad
    \kappa^{AB}_{ij}=\sqrt{\kappa^{AB}_i\kappa^{AB}_j}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}}{\sigma_{ij}}\right)^3 .
    \label{eq:wolbach-sandler}
\end{equation}

% P:supplement-contributions-05
The Debye--H\"uckel term of Cameretti et al.\ describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ \cite{Cameretti2005}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{DH}}=-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm D}e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2\chi_i,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm D}^2=\frac{e^2\rho_N}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2,
    \label{eq:debye-huckel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \chi_i=\frac{3}{\left(\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^3}
    \left[\frac32+\ln\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    -2\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    +\frac12\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^2\right],
    \label{eq:debye-huckel-chi}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the elementary charge, $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity, $\kappa_{\mathrm D}$ is the inverse Debye screening length and $\chi_i$ accounts for the finite ion size.

\subsection{MEA and the shared species}
% P:supplement-18
Neutral MEA parameters are the 2B values of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, except the association volume described below \cite{Baygi2015}.
\COtwo segment count, diameter and dispersion energy are from Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}.
The segment diameters and dispersion energies of bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, and the water interactions of hydronium and hydroxide, are those of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}, which Uyan et al.\ also use \cite{Uyan2015}; the water interactions of bicarbonate and carbonate are fitted to salt data (Section~\ref{sec:s-salt}).
Held et al.\ determined these water interactions with their own water parameters; they are used here unchanged with the water parameters of this work.
Ion segment count one and the diameter ties are modeling assumptions.
The Born diameter of hydronium is that of the hydrogen ion in Figiel et al.\ \cite{Figiel2025}.
The Born diameter of hydroxide, \SI{\res{mea-fixed-hydroxide-born-diameter}}{\angstrom}, was calculated here from the Born equation, $d^{\mathrm{Born}}=z^2e^2N_{\mathrm A}(1-1/\varepsilon_r)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0|\Delta G^{\mathrm{hyd}}|)$, where $N_{\mathrm A}$ is the Avogadro constant, with the hydration free energy $\Delta G^{\mathrm{hyd}}$ of $-106.4$ kcal/mol of Palascak and Shields \cite{palascakAccurateExperimentalValues2004} and the relative permittivity of water at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, $\varepsilon_r=78.37$.
The Born diameters of bicarbonate and carbonate are set here at \SI{3.0}{\angstrom}; in an MEA fit with the water--bicarbonate interaction fitted to the MEA data (Section~\ref{sec:s-salt}) and with all other parameters fixed, setting the bicarbonate diameter to 2.5, 3.5 or \SI{4.0}{\angstrom} or the carbonate diameter to 2.5 or \SI{4.0}{\angstrom} changes the objective value of the MEA fit by at most \res{mea-electrostatics-diameter-maximum-fixed-objective-change}\,\% and individual fitted \COtwo partial pressures by up to about \res{mea-bicarbonate-born-diameter-maximum-pressure-change-supplement}\,\% for bicarbonate and by less than \res{mea-carbonate-born-diameter-pressure-change-upper-reporting-bound-supplement}\,\% for carbonate.
None of these inputs is fitted in the calibrations of this work.
All other interactions are zero by assumption except the fixed water interactions listed below.
\Cref{tab:supplement-species-parameters,tab:supplement-interaction-matrix} list the exact values.
Water's solvation factor and ion-region permittivity follow Figiel et al.; unit solvation factors are set here for the other components \cite{Figiel2025}.
The ion packing and Debye--H\"uckel diameters follow the ties stated in the main text.

\res{mea-equivalent-dielectric-branches} coefficient pairs, (\res{mea-dielectric-larger-volume-coefficient} L/mol, \SI{\res{mea-dielectric-larger-volume-temperature}}{\kelvin}) and (\res{mea-dielectric-smaller-volume-coefficient} L/mol, \SI{\res{mea-smaller-volume-dielectric-temperature}}{\kelvin}), give the same permittivity at the measurement temperature, \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, at any density.

\subsection{Water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions from sodium-salt data}\label{sec:s-salt}
% P:supplement-salt-data
The water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fitted to osmotic coefficients of aqueous sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate, with no amine in the calculation.
The data are the values that Peiper and Pitzer tabulate from their ion-interaction model at 278.15, 288.15, 298.15, 308.15 and \SI{318.15}{\kelvin} and nominal molalities of 0.01--\SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram}, 45 for each salt \cite{peiperThermodynamicsAqueousCarbonate1982}; they are model-derived reference values, not direct measurements.
Each row is calculated at the equilibrium ionic composition printed with it, the dissolved \COtwo is removed as an ideal solute, and the residual scale is 0.01.
The sodium and chloride ion parameters and the interactions between sodium and the anions are those of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}; like the amine ions, these ions have Born and Debye--H\"uckel diameters equal to their segment diameter and a unit solvation factor.

% P:supplement-salt-fit
Each fitted interaction is linear in temperature, $k(T)=a_0+a_1(T-\SI{298.15}{\kelvin})$, with bounds of $\pm1$ on $a_0$ and $\pm\SI{0.02}{\per\kelvin}$ on $a_1$, and the three fits are made in sequence.
The water--sodium intercept is fitted first, to \res{salt-sodium-chloride-fitted-osmotic-row-count} osmotic coefficients of sodium chloride at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} and 0.1--\SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram} \cite{HamerWu1972}, with the slope of Held et al.\ retained; it ends on its lower bound, $\res{salt-sodium-intercept-at-lower-bound-supplement}$, and the sodium chloride coefficients deviate by \res{salt-sodium-chloride-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\%.
The water--bicarbonate interaction is fitted next and is not on a bound: $a_0=\res{salt-water-bicarbonate-original-intercept}$ and $a_1=\SI{\res{salt-water-bicarbonate-linear-slope}}{\per\kelvin}$, which is \res{salt-water-bicarbonate-value-at-reference-temperature} at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, with an AARD of \res{salt-sodium-bicarbonate-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\% on the \res{salt-sodium-bicarbonate-fitted-osmotic-row-count} sodium bicarbonate coefficients.
The water--carbonate interaction is fitted last: its intercept ends on the lower bound, $a_0=\res{salt-water-carbonate-intercept-at-lower-bound-supplement}$, with $a_1=\SI{\res{salt-water-carbonate-linear-slope}}{\per\kelvin}$, which is $\res{salt-water-carbonate-value-at-reference-temperature}$ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, and the \res{salt-sodium-carbonate-fitted-osmotic-row-count} sodium carbonate coefficients deviate by \res{salt-sodium-carbonate-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\%, the calculated values being low.
For \res{salt-mixed-assessment-available-osmotic-row-count} coefficients of mixed bicarbonate--carbonate solutions that were not fitted, the deviation is \res{salt-mixed-salts-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\%.

% P:supplement-salt-target
The printed water activities of Peiper and Pitzer satisfy $-\ln a_w/M_w=\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}\sum_im_i=\phi_{\mathrm{st}}\sum_s\nu_sm_s$ within printed precision, where the first sum runs over the equilibrium solute species and the second over the stoichiometric salt.
The fitted target is the equilibrium coefficient of the ions: the molality of dissolved \COtwo, treated as ideal, is subtracted from the equilibrium numerator, and the remainder is divided by the charge-closed sum of the ionic molalities.

Every result of the four amine models is conditional on the \res{salt-lower-bound-intercept-count-supplement} intercepts that lie on their bounds.
The sodium carbonate coefficients are already low at \SI{0.01}{\mole\per\kilogram}, where the water--carbonate interaction has almost no effect, which suggests that this interaction does not carry most of the remaining error; no decomposition by term was made.
The salt data end at \SI{318.15}{\kelvin}; extrapolated, the water--bicarbonate law reaches \res{salt-water-bicarbonate-absorber-extrapolation} at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and \res{salt-water-bicarbonate-stripper-extrapolation} at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.

\subsection{DEA, MDEA and AMP}
% P:supplement-transfer-parameters
Table~\ref{tab:supplement-transfer-parameters} lists the value and unit of each amine-specific parameter of the DEA, MDEA and AMP models, including the permittivity coefficients and the coefficients of the carbamate hydrolysis (R4) and amine dissociation (R5) correlations.
A molality-basis constant follows from the mole-fraction constant of its source by
\begin{equation}
    \ln K_{m,r}=\ln K_{x,r}-\Big(\sum_{i\ne\HtwoO}\nu_{ri}\Big)\ln\!\left(M_{\HtwoO}m^{\circ}\right),
    \label{eq:molality-conversion}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_{ri}$ is the stoichiometric number of species $i$ in reaction $r$, $M_{\HtwoO}$ is the molar mass of water and $m^{\circ}=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram}$.
These correlations are written in the form $\ln K(T)=a+b/T+c\ln(T/T_{\mathrm{ref}})+dT$ on the common-molality reference basis, with $T$ in K and $T_{\mathrm{ref}}=\SI{313.15}{\kelvin}$, which is equivalent to main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:reaction-equilibrium-constant-temperature}).
For DEA and AMP, the fitted-parameter table of the main text gives the enthalpy increment $\delta h$ and the change of $\ln K_4$ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} relative to the starting R4 correlation, that of Aroua et al.\ for DEA \cite{arouaTemperatureDependencyEquilibrium1997} and that of Silkenb\"aumer et al.\ for AMP \cite{silkenbaumerSolubilityCarbonDioxide1998}; the final coefficients listed here already include both.
At 298.15, 313.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} the fitted DEA correlation differs from its starting correlation in $\ln K_4$ by $\res{dea-fitted-r4-log-constant-change-low-temperature}$, $\res{dea-fitted-r4-log-constant-change-reference-temperature}$ and $\res{dea-fitted-r4-log-constant-change-high-temperature}$, and the fitted AMP correlation by $\res{amp-fitted-r4-log-constant-change-low-temperature}$, $\res{amp-fitted-r4-log-constant-change-reference-temperature}$ and $\res{amp-fitted-r4-log-constant-change-high-temperature}$.
At \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} the conditional standard errors are \res{dea-r4-log-constant-conditional-standard-error} for DEA and \res{amp-r4-log-constant-conditional-standard-error} for AMP.
The measurements behind the DEA correlation span 303--\SI{331}{\kelvin} and the model estimates behind the AMP correlation 313.15--\SI{373.15}{\kelvin}, so the first and last of these temperatures are extrapolations.
The dispersion energy of \ce{DEACOO-}, \SI{\res{dea-carbamate-dispersion-energy-supplement}}{\kelvin}, is that of \ce{MEACOO-} in the MEA model, and its segment diameter is the \ce{MEACOO-} diameter of that model, \SI{\res{dea-transferred-mea-carbamate-diameter}}{\angstrom}, multiplied by \res{dea-carbamate-segment-volume-scaling}, the cube root of the ratio of $m^{\mathrm{seg}}\sigma^3$ of DEA to that of MEA.
The \ce{DEAH+}--bicarbonate interaction is not fitted; it is the \ce{MEAH+}--bicarbonate interaction of the MEA model.
The dispersion energy of \ce{AMPH+} is that of AMP.
The dispersion energy of \ce{AMPCOO-}, \SI{\res{amp-carbamate-dispersion-energy-supplement}}{\kelvin}, and its interaction with water, $\res{amp-transferred-mea-carbamate-water-interaction}$, are those of \ce{MEACOO-} in the MEA model.
The \ce{AMPCOO-} diameter is fitted, with the \ce{MEACOO-} diameter of the MEA model, \SI{\res{amp-transferred-mea-carbamate-diameter-lower-bound}}{\angstrom}, as its lower bound, on which it ends.
Fixed ionic parameters are model assumptions, not independent measurements of ionic size.
The packing diameter is the hard-sphere diameter used in the repulsive contribution.

\section{Source conventions, objective scales and weights}\label{sec:s-corrections}\label{sec:s-objective}
\subsection{MEA data}
% P:supplement-mea-species
B\"ottinger et al.\ report \ce{MEA + MEAH+} as an NMR sum and tabulate bicarbonate while noting practically absent carbonate \cite{Bottinger2008}.
Their bicarbonate-labeled observations are compared with calculated \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}}.
Matin et al.\ neglect carbonate when inferring bicarbonate from titration and carbon balance \cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}; the same comparison with the calculated sum is therefore an approximation, and these bicarbonate values are not fitted.
The Matin series labeled \SI{294.15}{\kelvin} is calculated at \SI{293.15}{\kelvin}.
Jakobsen species-to-total-amine ratios enter the objective, whereas their carbonate shares of dissolved carbon do not.
For the Jakobsen NMR calculations, dilution changes the solvent composition, and the reported NMR loading is used instead of the maximum loading \cite{Jakobsen2005}.

Because $P_{\mathrm{src}}$ is fixed, the reference chemical potentials depend on temperature only, the phases compared in a stability search or a two-liquid calculation share one Gibbs energy, and its pressure derivative at fixed amounts is $V_{\mathrm{EOS}}$ (main-text \cref{main-eq:pressure-tracking}).

\section{Phase stability}\label{sec:s-phase}
\subsection{Search conditions and results}
% P:supplement-phase-method
Trial phases obey reaction equilibrium and electroneutrality and exclude species that cannot form from the feed at the searched state.
Charge-neutral initial liquid compositions include a uniform composition and near-pure neutral-species compositions; initial vapor compositions contain only the three neutral species.
Only a negative tangent-plane distance confirmed by a finite, mass-conserving transfer that lowers the Gibbs energy by more than its numerical error estimate is counted as a detected instability.
Trial compositions at which a Gibbs-energy decrease was found with other parameter sets during this work are also tested; a negative distance at one of these compositions can show that a state is unstable, but not toward which kind of phase, and it is reported separately.

% P:supplement-phase-grid
For MEA, the liquid grid contains \res{mea-liquid-grid-dense-requests} states at 15, 25, 35 and 45 wt\% MEA, 313.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin} in \SI{10}{\kelvin} steps, and loading $10^{-5}$ plus 0.025--0.6 mol/mol in 0.025 steps, at 1.05 and 3.0 times the bubble pressure of the parameter set being searched.
Another \res{mea-liquid-grid-census-requests} states use the composition of the liquid--vapor search at those two ratios.
A second liquid grid, offset from the first, contains \res{mea-liquid-grid-offset-requests} states at 20, 30 and 40 wt\%, 318.15--\SI{388.15}{\kelvin} and loadings 0.0125--0.5875, at 1.5 times the bubble pressure.
These are the \res{mea-liquid-grid-requests-supplement} liquid states of the main text, and none has a detected instability.

% P:supplement-phase-census
The MEA liquid--vapor search contains six temperatures, 313.15, 318.15, 333.15, 353.15, 373.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, and nine loadings, $10^{-5}$, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.256, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 mol/mol.
Each temperature--loading pair uses three pressures, 0.50, 0.80 and 0.95 times a fixed reference bubble pressure, which is not the bubble pressure of the MEA model.
The MEA model is searched at these \res{mea-liquid-vapor-census-requests-supplement} states.
The feed is 9.7292821 mol MEA, 76.9751925 mol water and $9.7292821\alpha$ mol \COtwo.
The equilibrium at these states is liquid plus vapor at \res{mea-census-two-phase-parent-count-supplement} and one liquid at \res{mea-single-liquid-census-parents-supplement}, and no search detects an instability.
A separate search at the \res{mea-measured-state-search-requests-supplement} measured MEA states detects none.

% P:supplement-phase-transfer-grid
For DEA, MDEA and AMP, the search covers the measured states and a grid.
The grid spans 313.15--393.15 K in 10 K steps and loadings of $10^{-5}$ and then 0.1 upward in steps of 0.1 to the largest loading in the data of the amine, which is added, at 1.05 and 3.0 times the calculated bubble pressure; its amine concentrations are those of the fitted data, with concentrations within 1 wt\% of each other searched once.
For MDEA each fitted concentration is searched on its own, at the grid loadings that lie within the range of loadings measured at that concentration, so that no state is searched at a loading that was not measured at its concentration.

% P:supplement-phase-dea
For DEA, the search covers \res{dea-phase-search-measurement-requests} measured states and \res{dea-phase-search-grid-requests} grid states, the latter at the \res{dea-reported-grid-stocks-supplement} fitted DEA concentrations (10, 20, 25 and 30 wt\%), the \res{dea-reported-grid-temperatures-supplement} grid temperatures and \res{dea-reported-grid-loadings-supplement} loadings up to \res{dea-reported-grid-maximum-loading-supplement} mol/mol at both pressures.
No search detects an instability.
The measured states include states that are not fitted: the densities of Weiland et al.\ \cite{Weiland1998} at 298.15 K and 10--40 wt\% DEA, searched at an assigned pressure of 101.325 kPa, and the pressures of Lawson and Garst at 50--54.5 wt\% DEA \cite{lawsonGasSweeteningData1976}.

% P:supplement-phase-mdea
For MDEA, \res{mdea-measurement-derived-searches-attempted-supplement-design} searches are made at measured states and one at each of the \res{mdea-restricted-grid-states} grid states.
No search finds a liquid--liquid split, and no grid state has a detected instability.
At \res{mdea-distinct-descent-states-supplement-total} measured states the searches detect an instability toward a vapor, because the measured or assigned pressure lies below the bubble pressure of the model: \res{mdea-distinct-descents-sobrino-supplement} states at 393.15 K and 0.1 MPa \cite{sobrinoViscosityDensityMeasurements2016}, \res{mdea-distinct-descents-bottinger-supplement} at 293.15--333.15 K and 0.5 or 2.5 MPa \cite{bottingerOnlineNMRSpectroscopic2008}, \res{mdea-distinct-descents-zheng-supplement} at 323.15 K and 0.53--4.19 MPa \cite{zhengCO2HeatAbsorption2020} and \res{mdea-distinct-descents-merkley-supplement} at 333.15 K and 1.121 MPa \cite{merkleyEnthalpiesAbsorptionCarbon1987}.
A further \res{mdea-measured-searches-unclassified-supplement} searches, at heat-measurement states of Merkley et al., are assigned to neither class.
Of these, \res{mdea-merkley-searches-outside-caloric-interval-supplement}, at 288.71 and 422.04 K, lie outside the temperature interval of the caloric functions (Section~\ref{sec:s-files}), so the Gibbs energy is not evaluated there.
At the other \res{mdea-merkley-searches-within-resolution-supplement}, at 333.15 and 388.71 K and 1.121 MPa, the smallest tangent-plane distance found is zero within its numerical resolution, and each of the \res{mdea-supplied-trial-count-supplement} trial compositions supplied to a search gives a positive distance.

% P:supplement-phase-amp
For AMP, the search covers \res{amp-reported-screen-measured-states-supplement} measured states and \res{amp-reported-screen-grid-states-supplement} grid states, all at 1.05 and 3.0 times the bubble pressure of the AMP model, the latter at \res{amp-reported-grid-minimum-stock-supplement} and \res{amp-reported-grid-maximum-stock-supplement} wt\% AMP, the \res{amp-reported-grid-temperatures-supplement} grid temperatures and \res{amp-reported-grid-loadings-supplement} loadings up to \res{amp-reported-grid-maximum-loading-supplement} mol/mol.
No search detects an instability.
The grid ends at 393.15 K; the measured states, which extend to the Tong et al.\ pressures at 393.20 K, are searched at their own temperatures, and the AMP model was not searched above \res{amp-reported-screen-maximum-temperature} K.
At the \res{amp-first-data-measured-splits-supplement} measured states where the first AMP fit predicts a second liquid (Section~\ref{sec:s-amp-slope}), \res{amp-hartono-373-pressure-control-count-supplement} Hartono et al.\ states at 373 K and \res{amp-tong-measured-pressure-control-count-supplement} Tong et al.\ states, the AMP model was also calculated at the measured total pressures: it gives liquid plus vapor at \res{amp-reported-measured-pressure-control-vapor-directed-supplement}, the \res{amp-hartono-vapor-directed-controls-supplement} Hartono states and \res{amp-tong-vapor-directed-controls-supplement} Tong state at 373.2 K, where the measured pressure lies below its bubble pressure, and one liquid at \res{amp-reported-measured-pressure-control-no-descent-supplement}.

\subsection{The MDEA--water bound}\label{sec:s-mdea-bound}
% P:supplement-bound-mdea
The MDEA--water interaction $k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}(T)=k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}(423\ \mathrm{K})+(\mathrm{d}k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}/\mathrm{d}T)(T-423\ \mathrm{K})$ is the only fitted parameter that acts on the \COtwo-free solution.
With all other parameters fixed, the largest value without a gap in the lower convex envelope of the liquid Gibbs energy over \res{mdea-binary-hull-compositions} compositions at \res{mdea-binary-hull-pressure} MPa rises from $\res{mdea-binary-hull-cold-no-gap-limit}$ at 290 K to $\res{mdea-binary-hull-hot-no-gap-limit}$ at 423 K, and $k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}(423\ \mathrm{K})$ is bounded above at $\res{mdea-bound-binary-limit-supplement}$, $10^{-4}$ below it.
At the reported solution only this bound is active.
Without the bound, an MDEA fit has an instability at \res{mdea-historical-unbounded-instabilities} of the \res{mdea-unbounded-binary-states-searched} \COtwo-free MDEA--water liquids searched at 1.466 MPa, which are 13 MDEA mole fractions from 0.01 to 0.24 at each of 393, 403, 413, 413.15, 422.04 and 423 K.
For the MDEA model the fitted interaction equals the bound at 423 K and lies below the onset value at each of the \res{mdea-binary-gap-tested-temperatures-supplement} tested temperatures from 290 to 423 K (main-text \cref{main-fig:mdea-gap-onset}).
The bound was fixed before fitting and independently of the deviations.
At 423 K it lies below the largest tested value without a gap at 1.121, 1.466 and 1.811 MPa alike, and with the fitted temperature law extrapolated the gap appears just above 423 K at all three pressures, so the bound suppresses the calculated gap through the tested temperatures and no further.
No bound acts on the loaded MDEA states.

\subsection{First AMP fit}\label{sec:s-amp-slope}
% P:supplement-amp-first
The AMP model fits all loaded total pressures to \SI{393}{\kelvin} because a first fit, with fewer of them, predicted a second liquid.
That fit used the \res{amp-first-data-fitted-pressure-count-supplement} loaded total pressures of Hartono et al.\ at 313--\SI{353}{\kelvin}, the binary data and the carbamate fractions, \res{amp-first-data-objective-row-count-supplement} observations, with the water--bicarbonate interaction fitted to the MEA data (Section~\ref{sec:s-salt}).
Its temperature coefficient of the \ce{AMPH+}--water interaction, $b_{\mathrm{AMPH^+,w}}$, is \SI{\res{amp-first-data-fitted-slope-free-starts}}{\kelvin} with a conditional standard error of \SI{\res{amp-first-data-cation-water-slope-conditional-se-supplement}}{\kelvin}.
Searched at the \res{amp-first-data-screen-states-supplement-first-fit} states of Section~\ref{sec:s-phase}, it has an instability toward a second liquid at \res{amp-first-data-detected-instabilities-supplement}, all at \res{amp-first-data-minimum-instability-temperature-supplement}--\res{amp-first-data-maximum-instability-temperature-supplement} K, above its fitted pressures and among them measured states of Hartono et al.\ and of Tong et al.\ (\cref{fig:amp-splits}).
At 373--\SI{393}{\kelvin} it also underpredicts every measured total pressure of the two sources, by \res{amp-first-data-free-hartono-aard-supplement} and \res{amp-first-data-free-tong-aard-supplement}\,\% on average.
Above the fitted pressures its coefficient raises $k_{\mathrm{AMPH^+,w}}$ from $\res{amp-first-data-cation-water-interaction-fitted-range-end}$ at \SI{353}{\kelvin} to $\res{amp-first-data-cation-water-interaction-stripper-range-end}$ at \SI{393}{\kelvin}, and with the weaker cation--water attraction a second liquid rich in \ce{AMPH+}, bicarbonate and AMP lowers the Gibbs energy.
The AMP model adds the \res{amp-reported-hot-hartono-fitted-pressure-count-supplement-additions} pressures of Hartono et al.\ at 373 and \SI{393}{\kelvin} and the \res{amp-reported-tong-fitted-pressure-count-supplement-additions} loaded pressures of Tong et al.\ at 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin} to the objective; its coefficient is \SI{\res{amp-reported-fitted-slope-slope-comparison}}{\kelvin}, and it has no detected instability at the same states.
The first fit differs from the AMP model in its data, in the water--anion interactions and in its carbamate inputs, so the comparison does not isolate the cause of the second liquid, and no measurement used here tests it.

\section{Published comparisons that are not on identical observations}\label{sec:s-published}
% P:supplement-published-dea
For the DEA data no source gives a numerical deviation of a model on the observations used here: Lawson and Garst report scatter against smoothed curves \cite{lawsonGasSweeteningData1976}, Lal et al.\ compare the Kent--Eisenberg treatment graphically \cite{lalSolubilityH2SCO21985}, and Horstmann et al.\ fit binary vapor--liquid equilibrium and excess enthalpy together and give no deviation for either \cite{horstmannPhaseEquilibriumExcess2002}.
The electrolyte cubic-two-state equation of state of T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros reports 11.1\,\% for 99 pressures of loaded DEA at 298.15--394.26 K and 10.2--50.0 wt\% DEA, fitted with one ionic and seven binary parameters \cite{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013}; its observations are not those used here.

% P:supplement-published-amp
For AMP, Kundu et al.\ report 14.3\,\% for their activity-coefficient model on 56 of their observations, some of which entered its fit \cite{kunduVaporLiquidEquilibriumCO22003}; their Table~10 labels the statistic AAD, but its footnote prints a signed relative-deviation formula. The statistic and the 56 observations therefore do not support a direct AARD comparison with the \res{amp-unfitted-kundu-partial-pressure-count} observations used here.
Hartono et al.\ (2021) compare an existing electrolyte NRTL model with their AMP pressures graphically, without a scalar deviation on these observations \cite{hartonoNewSolubilityHeat2021}.
The eNRTL models of Yi et al.\ \cite{yiOpenAccessThermodynamically2024} and of Hartono et al.\ (2020) \cite{hartonoSolubilityCO201M2020}, whose deviations the main text quotes, were both fitted to these observations, and the 45 pressures of Tong et al.\ include five of the \COtwo-free solution.

% P:supplement-published-kuranov
For MDEA, three ePC-SAFT studies report deviations from the total pressures of Kuranov et al.\ \cite{kuranovSolubilitySingleGases1996} that were calculated without fitting a parameter to the \COtwo--MDEA--water system.
Uyan et al.\ report 19.22\,\% at 19 wt\% MDEA and 25.91\,\% at 32 wt\%, and 22.9\,\% for 82 observations \cite{Uyan2015}.
Wangler et al.\ report 19.44\,\% at 32 wt\% and 19.7\,\% for 82 observations, and they redetermine the 82-observation value of Uyan et al.\ as 34.4\,\% \cite{Wangler2018}.
Cleeton et al.\ report 20.26\,\% at 18.9 wt\% and do not state the formula of their deviation \cite{Cleeton2020}.
The value of the MDEA model is calculated on the \res{mdea-source-matched-kuranov-count-supplement} total pressures of Table~2 of Kuranov et al.\ matched to the source rows one by one; the published 82-observation values are identified with them by their count only, and main-text Table~\ref{main-tab:published-models} lists them with the \res{mdea-kuranov-all-pressure-aard-supplement}\,\% of the MDEA model on those observations, of which it fitted \res{mdea-fitted-kuranov-pressure-count-published-fit-coverage} loaded and \res{mdea-source-matched-kuranov-free-fitted-count} \COtwo-free pressures, without ranking them.
The unfitted row 72, at 393.16 K, is calculated at \res{mdea-kuranov-row-72-reported-reference-pressure} MPa against \res{mdea-kuranov-row-72-measured-pressure} MPa measured.
The subset values are for different observation sets from the \res{mdea-fitted-kuranov-pressure-count-literature-subsets} pressures fitted here, from which the MDEA model deviates by \res{mdea-fitted-pressure-aard-literature-subsets}\,\%, and they are listed without a comparison.
