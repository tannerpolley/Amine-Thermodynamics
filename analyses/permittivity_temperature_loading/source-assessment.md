# Temperature and loading limits of aqueous-amine permittivity

Research for Forgejo issue #265, read against base `30db72afad45232c36f8bfce82ea80278f3267e2` on 6 November 2026. This is a source assessment before design readiness review. It contains no new Engine calculation or parameter recommendation accepted for adoption.

## Finding and intended use

Measurements above 328 K and in loaded MEA exist. Their existence does not establish accurate static permittivity for the thermodynamic electrostatic terms. Hajj, Pré and Curet report microwave spectra and Cole–Cole-inferred static values for loaded 30 wt% MEA through 353.15 K. Their section 3.3.1 explicitly warns that the frequency interval is insufficient to accurately determine the static limit. No uncertainty interval for that extrapolation is supplied. The less-than-1% repeatability of the measured spectra cannot supply that interval. Consequently the conditional instruction to refit when discrepancy exceeds measurement uncertainty cannot yet be applied to these static estimates.

A separate Hajj paper reports unloaded 30 wt% MEA at 333.15 K. Neither paper establishes transfer to 393 K. The bounded source search found no loaded-MDEA measurements or aqueous-MDEA measurements above 328.15 K. This is a search limit, not proof of nonexistence.

The owner selected comparison-only use of Hajj on 6 November 2026: retain the current law, quantify ±5% sensitivity, and add a named sensitivity case representing Hajj's implied loading difference and temperature trend. Do not fit a law to Hajj. The owner approved 300 gross added executable lines against base `30db72af`. Independent design readiness review remains required before Build.

## Sources actually inspected

1. **Agieienko and Buchner (2024)**, *What is behind a gas stream scrubbing liquid? Monoethanolamine/water mixtures as seen by dielectric relaxation spectroscopy*, DOI `10.1039/D3CP05027K`. Main article sections 2.1–2.3 and 3.1; supporting information Tables S1/S2, p. 2. Measurements cover unloaded mixtures at 298.15 K across composition. Spectra span 0.05–89 GHz; the inferred static permittivity is the sum of relaxation amplitudes and the high-frequency limit. S2 reports relative uncertainty generally better than 1% for static permittivity. Its 14 values are the source of the current MEA fit, not additional assessment observations. Main article section 2.2 reports spectral uncertainty better than 2%. Table S2's uncertainty concerns the inferred static quantity and must be distinguished from spectral uncertainty.
2. **Hajj, Pré and Curet (2024)**, *Dielectric dispersion of 30% aqueous monoethanolamine solution loaded with CO2 at microwave frequencies*, DOI `10.1016/j.molliq.2024.124819`. Sections 2.1–2.4, 3.2, 3.3.1–3.3.4, conclusion, and Table 1, p. 9. The measured frequency interval is 915–2450 MHz; temperatures are 293.15–353.15 K in 10 K steps; loading is 0.22, 0.33, 0.44 and 0.55 mol CO2/mol MEA. The stock solution is 30 wt% MEA before loading. Static permittivity, relaxation time, distribution exponent and conductivity are fitted simultaneously to the complex spectra with optical permittivity fixed from refractive index. The separate Kent–Eisenberg speciation calculation interprets those results; its predicted species are not experimental species data. Table 1's static estimates at 353.15 K are respectively **60.1, 63.4, 68.8 and 72.4**. Section 2.2 gives maximum relative standard deviation below 1% for repeated complex spectra, and water drift deviations of 0.83% and 3.24% for the real and loss parts. Section 2.1 reports a CO2-content check within 5% on a bicarbonate standard. These statements do not quantify the uncertainty in inferred static permittivity. Section 3.3.1 and the conclusion warn about insufficient low-frequency data, especially at lower temperature and higher loading. Preserve this qualification when comparing with the thermodynamic law.
3. **Hajj et al. (2024)**, *Dielectric dispersion of 30% aqueous Ethanolamine solution at microwave frequencies*, DOI `10.18573/ejme.9`, pp. 20–24, Materials and methods, Dielectric Dispersion model, Table 1 and Results. Unloaded 30 wt% MEA is measured at 293.15 and 333.15 K over 915–2450 MHz. Table 1 gives Cole–Cole static estimates **69.10 and 58.27**, respectively, with optical permittivity fixed at 1.88. Repeated measurements and water-drift checks concern spectra; Table 1 does not supply a static-limit uncertainty interval. The limited bandwidth and fixed optical value must accompany comparison. Added to the registered MEA Zotero collection through `cse-zotero acquire`; parent `FZV2VWIC`, PDF `PUY6BTRS`.
4. **Yang et al. (2024)**, *Composition-dependence of relative static permittivity in ePPC-SAFT for mixed-solvent alkali halides*, DOI `10.1016/j.fluid.2024.114103`. Section 2.1 and Table 6, p. 6: temperature/density/composition-dependent solvent formulation; fixed water values are dielectric volume 0.3777 dm3/mol and temperature 1403 K. The studied mixed solvents are water–methanol and water–ethanol with alkali halides. This paper is the law's methodological source, not MEA temperature or loaded-amine validation.
5. **Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh (2015)**, local reading copy sections 3.1–3.3 and conclusion: MEA PC-SAFT pure parameters are fitted to saturation pressure/density, binary interaction to binary vapor–liquid data, then ternary pressure and mixing heat assessed. This establishes a nearby staged estimation example; it supplies no new static-permittivity measurements.
6. **Uyan et al. (2015)**, local reading copy electrostatic formulation, Eq. 5, high-concentration discussion and conclusion: MDEA solvent permittivity is assumed independent of CO2 and ions, using a mass-weighted water/MDEA solvent value. Ternary pressure comparison is not a direct dielectric validation. Its conclusion identifies unavailable loaded-solution dielectric evidence at publication. This historical statement cannot override the later Hajj measurements.

The Hsieh–Chen–Li source (`10.1021/je600515j`) was checked through its publisher and NIST ThermoML records for coverage: aqueous MDEA and other amines, 303.15–328.15 K. Its full methods were not read in this pass and no uncertainty or frequency claim is inferred from those records.
