Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript "Electrolyte PC-SAFT with carbamate chemistry for CO2 in aqueous
monoethanolamine: the role of the Born term and of speciation data" for publication in
*Fluid Phase Equilibria*.

The manuscript is a reactive phase-equilibrium study. It couples five liquid reactions,
explicit ion activities from electrolyte PC-SAFT, and the equality of CO2, water and amine
fugacities, so that one model describes both the CO2 partial pressure and the liquid
speciation of loaded aqueous monoethanolamine.

Five interaction coordinates are fitted to ternary MEA–CO2–H2O data: 48 CO2 partial pressures
at 40 and 60 °C and 94 NMR and titration species measurements. Because parameters are fitted
to the ternary mixture, the manuscript does not call its calculations predictions; results at
other conditions are reported as tests outside the fit.

The work makes three contributions:

1. It compares the solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation Born term of Figiel et al. with
   the original Born term in a reactive, carbamate-forming amine, using identical fitted
   coordinates and data. The ranking of the two forms reverses with the fitting role of
   Matin et al.'s titration bicarbonate observations, which those authors report as higher
   than NMR values.
2. It measures how the fitted response depends on fixed Born inputs (solvation factors,
   ion-region permittivity, Born diameters, MEA permittivity) and on each interaction
   coordinate, including a refit without the Born term, and shows where Born inputs and
   interaction coefficients compensate for one another.
3. It tests the record outside the fit. At 80 °C, the 11 Jou 1995 pressure targets give an
   average absolute relative deviation of 9.24 %, and all 21 canonical rows give 20.27 %;
   inherited reaction shifts and the fixed CO2 dispersion energy were chosen against sets that
   included 80 °C data, as the manuscript states.

The manuscript reports the tested limits as results: pressure transfer to 15 and 45 wt% MEA,
extrapolation to 100–120 °C, an interaction coefficient at its bound, the carbonate
partition, earlier-record density and heat-capacity deficits, and two unavailable probe
calculations. It claims no physical validation at new conditions. The parameter records,
retained calculations and figure data will be made public before submission in the repository
cited in the Data and Code Availability statement, under the tag `manuscript-v1`.
