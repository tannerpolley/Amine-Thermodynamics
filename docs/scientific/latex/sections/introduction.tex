\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% P:introduction-00
Aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) absorbs \COtwo from flue gas at about 313--\SI{333}{\kelvin} and is regenerated by steam stripping at about 373--\SI{393}{\kelvin} \cite[Fig.~1]{Freguia2003}.
The solvent's thermodynamics governs both equipment design and energy use: equilibrium pressure sets the gas--liquid driving force, speciation supplies concentrations for reaction-rate expressions, and absorption heat and heat capacity enter the energy balance \cite{Zhang2011}\cite[Eq.~13]{Freguia2003}.
Absorption heat also affects the absorber temperature profile \cite[Figs.~10--11]{Kvamsdal2008}, while density enters column sizing and mass-transfer correlations \cite{Amundsen2009}.
These properties are needed at the same states; calculating them from one thermodynamic description keeps their equilibrium, volumetric and caloric contributions consistent.

% P:introduction-02
MEA forms carbamate through an overall reaction consuming two amine molecules per \COtwo molecule; carbamate reversion to bicarbonate becomes important at higher \COtwo content \cite[Eqs.~1--4]{MacDowell2010}.
This differs from tertiary N-methyldiethanolamine (MDEA), which promotes bicarbonate formation without forming carbamate \cite{Vaidya2007}.
Pressure, speciation and absorption heat therefore depend on the same reaction equilibria, which a model must combine with nonideal activities of the molecular and ionic species.

\subsection{Thermodynamic descriptions of reactive MEA}
% P:introduction-03
Activity-coefficient models combine nonideal liquid activities with reaction equilibria and a separate vapor description.
Liu et al.\ review the progression from Kent--Eisenberg to Debye--H\"uckel, Pitzer and electrolyte non-random two-liquid (eNRTL) treatments, warning that simultaneous estimation of reaction constants and interactions can overfit scattered solubility data \cite{Liu1999}.
B\"ottinger et al.\ instead refitted a Pitzer model's carbamate constant to nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) observations \cite[Tables~3--6]{Bottinger2008}.
Refined eNRTL and extended universal quasi-chemical (UNIQUAC) models combine binary or ternary fitting with speciation comparisons \cite{Hessen2010,Aronu2011}.

% P:introduction-04
Multi-property fitting is established for activity models: Hilliard jointly fitted pressure, absorption heat, heat capacity and NMR, but 18 of 35 parameter estimates were smaller than their standard errors \cite[Tables~13.4-2--13.4-3]{Hilliard2008}.
Zhang et al.\ report fitted pressure deviations of 13.5--35.5\,\% by source \cite[Table~9]{Zhang2011}; Akula et al.\ fit pressure and differential heat, leaving species and heat capacity outside the objective \cite{Akula2023a}.
Their models need separate descriptions of vapor properties and liquid density.

% P:introduction-05
Statistical associating fluid theory (SAFT) describes both phases with one equation of state and supplies liquid density \cite{MacDowell2010}.
Earlier aqueous-MEA applications range from association without reactions or ions \cite{Button1999} to molecular PC-SAFT or Huang--Radosz SAFT combined with literature reaction constants and ideal liquid activities \cite{Nasrifar2010,Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}.

% P:introduction-06
Reactive-association models represent reaction products through molecular bonding: SAFT for potentials of variable range and group-contribution SAFT-\(\gamma\) Mie infer species from bonded fractions \cite{MacDowell2010,Rodriguez2012,perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}.
Lloret et al.'s soft-SAFT absorption heat, estimated from the pressure--temperature slope, falls below measurement at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, which they attribute to the uncertainty amplified by differentiation \cite[Eq.~26, Fig.~10b]{lloretConsistentTransferableThermodynamic2017}.
Wang et al.'s cubic-plus-association model also underestimates the heat magnitude at higher loading and suggests ionic contributions as an improvement \cite[Fig.~18]{wangImprovementPRCPAEquation2018}.

% P:introduction-hong
Hong et al.\ represent carbamate and bicarbonate implicitly through two-site and single-site MEA--\COtwo association in PC-SAFT, without ions or chemical-equilibrium equations; they fit a pathway weight at each temperature and loading to eNRTL output, compare species with that reference model and calculate absorption heat from the pressure--temperature derivative \cite[Sects.~2.2--2.3, 3.2, Eq.~17, SI Table~S1]{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.

% P:introduction-07
Explicit-ion models instead carry the ions as separate species with their own nonideal activities.
MEA ions, including carbamate, appear this way in T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros's electrolyte cubic two-state association model \cite{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013} and Neumann et al.'s combined Helmholtz-energy/eNRTL model \cite[Table~3]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Najafloo et al.\ included carbamate ions of 2-((2-aminoethyl)amino)ethanol (AEEA) in electrolyte Huang--Radosz SAFT with mean spherical approximation and Born terms \cite{najaflooMeasuringSolubilityCarbon2015}.
These studies use equations of state other than PC-SAFT.

% P:introduction-08
Cameretti et al.\ introduced ePC-SAFT by adding a Debye--H\"uckel contribution and fitting ion parameters to aqueous-salt vapor pressures and densities \cite[Table~2]{Cameretti2005}.
Held et al.\ added mean ionic activity coefficients to parameter estimation because they were more sensitive to ion parameters than VLE \cite{Held2008}; revised ePC-SAFT subsequently included cation--anion dispersion \cite{Held2014}.
For amines, Uyan et al.\ predicted MDEA ternary pressures and speciation after binary estimation \cite{Uyan2015}, and Wangler et al.\ fitted additional interactions to osmotic coefficients \cite{Wangler2018}.
Cleeton et al.\ treated competitive acid-gas absorption in MDEA \cite{Cleeton2020}, while B\"ulow et al.\ extended the treatment to mixed solvents \cite{Bulow2021a}.
These four studies treat MDEA, which forms no carbamate.

% P:introduction-prior-mea
Polley and Hedengren have already coupled explicit-ion ePC-SAFT for the same nine MEA liquid species, including carbamate, to an absorber model \cite[Sect.~2.2, App.~B]{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}.
Their parameters were calibrated in separate steps to ion and species data, binary VLE, pressure and calorimetry; the column used empirical caloric properties rather than equation-of-state enthalpy derivatives \cite[App.~B]{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}.
The present contribution is joint estimation from pressure, species, caloric, density and binary data, absorption heat and density from the same Helmholtz energy, and a tangent-plane check showing that a fit of similar quality can predict an additional liquid.


% P:introduction-09
Ionic activities, density and residual enthalpy can share one Helmholtz energy, while reaction constants supply chemical standard states.
Cleeton et al.\ add reaction heats to an equation-of-state derivative \cite[Eq.~15]{Cleeton2020}; Wangler et al.\ combine them with a Henry-coefficient dissolution term \cite[Eqs.~22--28]{Wangler2018}.
Born terms with concentration-dependent permittivity add temperature derivatives to enthalpy \cite[Eq.~6]{Bulow2020}.
Born diameter and permittivity can compensate each other \cite[Figs.~3--4]{Novak2023}; electrolyte permittivity results for inorganic salts \cite{Rueben2024} therefore do not determine reacting-amine parameters.

\subsection{Property constraints and phase stability}
% P:introduction-10
Different properties constrain different model terms.
Reported \COtwo pressures over 30 wt\% MEA can differ by an order of magnitude \cite{wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}, and similar pressures can accompany different calculated speciation \cite{smithGibbsEnergyMinimization}.
Species observations need analytical definitions: B\"ottinger reports molecular plus protonated MEA as a sum \cite[Table~3]{Bottinger2008}, and Raman carbamate calibration can depend on an amine balance \cite[Eq.~13, Table~4]{Wong2015}.
Shared NMR peaks also limit quantification of minor species \cite{Jakobsen2005}.

% P:introduction-11
Direct calorimetry differs from heat inferred through pressure slopes \cite{Kim2007}, while loaded-liquid densities constrain volumetric behavior \cite{Hartono2014}.
Neumann et al.'s VLE-fitted model predicts density deviations of 0.92\,\% against Hartono and 3.5\,\% against the complete Han dataset, motivating multi-property fitting \cite[Fig.~6]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Combining these measurements adds constraints, but property agreement alone does not test phase stability.

% P:introduction-12
Michelsen applies Gibbs's tangent-plane criterion before flash calculations because phase number is initially unknown \cite{Michelsen1982}.
Reactive amine stability or splitting has been studied with eNRTL and SAFT-\(\gamma\) Mie \cite{Tsanas2022,perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}; electrolyte ePC-SAFT stability searches and formal electroneutral tangent-plane criteria have also been reported for nonreacting salts or strong electrolytes \cite{Ascani2022,Perdomo2025}.
The cited ePC-SAFT MDEA studies do not report tangent-plane assessments \cite{Uyan2015,Wangler2018,Cleeton2020,Bulow2021a}.
A single-phase fitting objective contains no stability condition, so matching properties can coexist with a predicted additional liquid.

\subsection{Aim of this work}
% P:introduction-13
This work asks whether one ePC-SAFT formulation with carbamate chemistry can represent the equilibrium, caloric and density properties of loaded aqueous MEA.
Following separate water, \COtwo--water and dielectric fits, sixteen parameters are estimated jointly, including three in the \COtwo-hydrolysis and protonated-MEA dissociation constants.
Calculated properties are assessed at nominal 30 wt\% MEA, pressures also at 15 and 45 wt\%, and two fits are compared in a search for additional phases.
A brief MDEA demonstration examines the same estimation and comparison procedure with two representations of its protonated amine.
