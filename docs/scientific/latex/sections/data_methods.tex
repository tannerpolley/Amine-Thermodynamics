\section{Data and data use}\label{sec:data-methods}
% P:methods-01
Loading is $\alpha=n_{\COtwo,\mathrm{added}}/n_{\mathrm{amine,total}}$, and the unloaded-solvent mass fraction is $w_{\MEA}=m_{\MEA}/(m_{\MEA}+m_{\HtwoO})$.
Neither is a true-species mole fraction of the reacting liquid.
Source molalities, measured temperatures and aggregate-species definitions are preserved in each observation operator.
The objective has eight disjoint groups and 307 rows (\cref{tab:objective-groups}); the common group contains 47 pressures, 94 species and eight integral heats, which are not counted again as additional groups.
The seven restored species and six Jakobsen bicarbonate-plus-carbonate/total-amine amount ratios are additional fitted quantities.
The latter ratios differ from the carbonate share of dissolved carbon used in the comparison outside the objective.
Cai et al.\ \cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996} contribute a bubble temperature and a vapor-water fraction at each of 25 binary states, giving 50 quantities rather than 50 independent experiments.

Matin et al.\ derive \MEA, \MEAH and \MEACOO from titrations and carbon balance; their bicarbonate operator includes carbonate \cite[pp.~6614--6616, Eqs.~18--19b]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.

All 19 Matin bicarbonate/carbonate pool rows remain outside the A7 objective.
The NMR and titration quantities sharing one prepared solution need not be statistically independent.
Actual fitted membership follows observation identity and operator, including the restored species; it is not assigned from nominal temperature alone.

\subsection{Caloric and density observation operators}\label{sec:heat-method}
% P:methods-heat-01
The eight Vinjarapu integral heats are fitted at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal}, from initially carbon-free 30 wt\% solvent \cite[Sects.~1.1.1 and 2.2, Table~4]{HemaBhavya2025}.
The signed heat per mole of added \COtwo is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta h=\frac{H_{\mathrm{final}}-H_{\mathrm{initial}}
 -\Delta n_{\COtwo}h_{\COtwo}^{\mathrm{feed}}}{\Delta n_{\COtwo}},
 \label{eq:addition-heat}
\end{equation}
where the extensive equilibrium liquid enthalpies and the pure-\COtwo feed enthalpy use the same temperature and pressure.
Negative heat denotes exothermic absorption.
Fourteen Arcis values at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}, at \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} or \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}, are acceptance checks rather than independent validation \cite[Sect.~2.4, Table~5]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
No matched A7 calculation omitting heat from the objective was made, so no isolated heat-fitting effect is inferred.

% P:methods-heat-02
All densities are native equation-of-state values, without empirical volume translation.
The 47 fitted loaded rows comprise 15 Amundsen and 32 Hartono observations; five \COtwo-free densities use exactly zero analytical carbon.
The density calculations use \SI{100000}{\pascal} where declared, including all Amundsen rows; this is a calculation convention where the source pressure is unreported.
The declared loaded-density scale $s_j$ retains source-local loading sensitivity and source uncertainty qualifications.
It is not uniformly a measured standard uncertainty, and $2s$ is not a 95\,\% interval.
Amundsen's exact high-temperature/loading uncertainty boundary remains unresolved \cite[pp.~3099--3100]{Amundsen2009}.
The four Hilliard heat capacities use 7 mol \MEA/kg water and \SI{101325}{\pascal}, with nominal unloaded calculations using the declared $10^{-6}$ loading proxy.
Their comparison is on the complete analytical-feed mass basis; enthalpy and proxy checks are reported in S9.

\subsection{Weighted objective and restricted fits}
% P:methods-05
The bounded least-squares problem minimizes
\begin{equation}
 C(\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac12\sum_j w_j r_j(\boldsymbol\theta)^2,
 \qquad
 r_j=\begin{cases}
 (y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}-y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})/s_j,&\text{difference},\\
 \ln(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})/s_j,&\text{log ratio}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:fit-residuals}
\end{equation}
\Cref{tab:objective-groups} states each observation operator, units, scale and weight.
Loaded densities have weight $1/47$; the \COtwo-free rows have scale \SI{0.5}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} and weight 10.
Cai temperature and vapor-fraction scales are \SI{1}{\kelvin} and 0.01 with unit weight, and Kiepe pressures retain weight $1/39$.
The pressure and species scales express fitting priorities, and the printed heat intervals are not established independent one-standard-deviation errors.
Thus the share of cost assigned to one property reflects the objective as well as its deviations.

\subsection{Finite phase search}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:methods-phase-01
The paired A6/A7 search uses 73 specified temperature/pressure/loading states at 313.15--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin} and $\alpha=10^{-5}$--0.5 on the same software build.
Each state uses its declared pressure; the lowest, middle and highest pressure levels vary with temperature and loading.
Supplement S4 gives every exact state, preserving the additional index-0 witness separately from index 41.
At a converged reacting state, nine declared trials examine tangent-plane, donor and finite-split descent, comparing numerical descent with its estimated numerical error.
A negative value inside that error estimate is not a resolved descent.
A resolved descent rejects the candidate even if a subsequent split solve fails; successful split-seeded solves provide separate phase evidence.
The A6 split-confirmation states retain their original software identity in S4.
A two-phase classification by itself does not identify a liquid--liquid split: the allowed supports include neutral vapor.

\subsection{Conditional uncertainty and numerical verification}
% P:methods-07
The weighted residual Jacobian is mapped to the named scientific coordinates using the signed optimizer transformations of \cref{tab:fitted-coordinates}.
For the ten coordinates free of active bounds, the local covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol\theta_I)=s_C^2(J_I^{\mathsf T}J_I)^{-1},
 \qquad s_C^2=\frac{2C}{307-10},
 \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
with rank ten, 297 degrees of freedom and variance factor 1.002729.
The six bound-active coordinates have no symmetric standard error.
These errors describe local sensitivity conditional on weights, fixed inputs, active bounds and local linearization.
They are neither measurement uncertainty nor prediction intervals, and a variance factor near one proves nothing about the scales.
Residual dependence among quantities from a shared experiment is not propagated.
Supplement S6 supplies the coordinate order and correlations.

% P:methods-08
The 375 assessed equilibrium states have maximum reported conservation, charge and stationarity residuals no greater than $2.2\times10^{-12}$ on their declared bases.
A separate 153-identity replay of the selected record reproduces the required common-subset and heat-capacity quantities within $8.39\times10^{-16}$ relative difference.
These are numerical verification results; neither validates phase behavior or absorber/stripper performance.
Supplement S9 distinguishes the replay, solution residuals and caloric consistency checks.
Agreement with observations is summarized by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{AARD}=\frac{100\,\%}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n
 \left|y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}-1\right|,
 \label{eq:aard}
\end{equation}
with root-mean-square error (RMSE) in the observable's units and signed logarithmic bias $\langle\ln(y^{\mathrm{calc}}/y^{\mathrm{obs}})\rangle$ where applicable.
Mixed-property cost or relative-error averages are not interpreted as overall physical accuracy.
