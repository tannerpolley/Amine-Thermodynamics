\section{Results and Discussion}\label{sec:mea-system-modeling-results}
% P:results-01
The results separate four observation roles: the 142 fitted targets, comparisons outside the fit that were inspected in earlier model development, report-only species observations, and extrapolated or transferred conditions.
Each number below names its group and count, and \cref{tab:residual-summary} collects them for both Born forms.
All values are numerical evaluations of fixed parameter records against existing measurements; none is a physical validation at new conditions.

\subsection{Calibrated model}\label{sec:results-calibration}
% P:results-02
The selected SSM+DS record fits 48 \COtwo partial-pressure targets at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} with an AARD of 13.53\,\%.
It fits 94 liquid species targets at 20, 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} with an AARD of 8.28\,\%.
The species targets are 40 targets from the NMR study of B\"ottinger et al.\ and 54 \MEA, \MEAH and \MEACOO targets from the titration study of Matin et al.~\cite{Bottinger2008,matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
The mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of the fitted pressure targets is 0.011, so the calibration carries no material pooled pressure bias.

\subsection{The Born-form ranking depends on the observations fitted}\label{sec:results-born}
% P:results-04
SSM+DS and Original Born were fitted with the same five interaction coordinates, bounds, weights, reaction constants and ion inputs.
On the historical 160-target objective, which also contained Matin's 18 bicarbonate-pool targets, Original Born has the lower cost: 40.20 against 47.63 for SSM+DS (\cref{fig:born-cost-groups}).
The 7.42 cost difference is concentrated in the Matin species, which cost 11.34 more for SSM+DS.
The pressure and B\"ottinger targets favor SSM+DS by 1.78 and 2.14, and the Matin bicarbonate pool alone contributes 6.02 of the net difference.

% P:results-05
With the 18 Matin bicarbonate-pool targets report-only, the converged 142-target fits favor SSM+DS: 32.991897 against 34.584032 for Original Born (\cref{fig:born-ranking}).
This is a 4.6\,\% advantage relative to Original Born.
A sensitivity fit without any Matin targets, with 88 targets, gives 10.47 against 16.85.
When the two 142-target fits are scored on all 160 historical targets, Original Born is again lower, 43.62 against 53.77.
The ranking therefore follows the role assigned to the Matin bicarbonate observations, and \cref{sec:results-sensitivity} shows that it also follows inherited Born inputs; it is not an intrinsic property of either Born form.
The advantage is a narrow difference in a local weighted least-squares cost, and no statistical test supports it.

% P:results-06
The two forms differ less on unweighted comparisons (\cref{tab:residual-summary}).
SSM+DS gives the lower fitted-pressure AARD, 13.53 against 17.31\,\%, whereas Original Born gives a slightly lower fitted-species AARD, 8.23 against 8.28\,\%.
On the 104 canonical pressure rows at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, the two forms give 20.58 and 20.78\,\%.

\subsection{Titration bicarbonate reads high against NMR}\label{sec:results-matin}
% P:results-07
Matin et al.\ determined speciation in 30 wt\% \MEA from acid titration for total alkalinity, strong-base titration for bicarbonate and a total inorganic carbon measurement, at an ambient temperature labeled \SI{21}{\degreeCelsius} in their figures~\cite[Experimental Section and Method Description, pp.~6614--6616]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
Their bicarbonate follows from the base titration through Eqs.~(18)--(19b), with the carbonate amount incorporated into the bicarbonate estimate.
The authors report bicarbonate above the NMR values of Jakobsen et al., particularly at loadings of 0.3--0.45 (Fig.~2, p.~6616), and an order-of-magnitude difference from the NMR bicarbonate in their comparisons, whereas their free amine, protonated amine and carbamate agree more closely (Fig.~3 and discussion, p.~6617)~\cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012,Jakobsen2005}.
B\"ottinger et al.\ measured bicarbonate by \ce{^{13}C} NMR and could observe only the sum of \MEA and \MEAH because of fast proton exchange~\cite{Bottinger2008}.
The fit therefore compares Matin and B\"ottinger bicarbonate with the modeled \ce{HCO3- + CO3^2-} pool, and the Matin measurements enter at \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius}.

% P:results-08
Because the source authors identify the disagreement with NMR as specific to bicarbonate, the 18 Matin pool targets were made report-only while the 54 Matin amine and carbamate targets remained fitted.
With the pool outside the objective, the selected record lies below those 18 observations with a signed relative mean of \(-58.84\,\%\) and an AARD of 59.81\,\%; Original Born gives \(-34.30\,\%\) and 35.12\,\%.
The form favored after this source-based role change is therefore the one that departs further from the titration bicarbonate.
The residual signs do not establish that titration is biased in general, that the NMR values are exact, or that either Born form represents bicarbonate correctly.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the fitted response to Born inputs and interaction coordinates}\label{sec:results-sensitivity}
% P:results-19
The Born-form comparison above contrasts two fitted optima; the calculations below ask how the cost responds when a Born input or an interaction coordinate changes.
Fixed-interaction scans of one Born input at a time, refits with one interaction coordinate fixed at zero and refits without the Born term use the 142-target objective, the selected SSM+DS record and the Original Born record at its own 142-target optimum; the local Jacobian diagnostics belong to the historical 160-target optimum.
A fixed-interaction scan measures the response of the cost to one input with every other parameter held at the record; it does not optimize that input.
All costs in this subsection are local and conditional on the chemistry, bounds, weights and fixed inputs of the records, and none is a statistical significance test.

% P:results-20
\Cref{fig:born-input-sensitivity} shows the scans of both forms.
Lowering the water solvation factor from 1.5 to 1, which removes the solvation shell, raises the SSM+DS cost from 32.99 to 424.16, of which the 48 pressure targets contribute 392.92; factors of 1.25, 1.75 and 2 give 69.07, 42.57 and 52.42.
Raising the \MEA solvation factor from 1 to 1.25, 1.5 and 1.75 gives 41.11, 65.92 and 107.16.
The ion-region permittivity \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=4\), 16 and 32, in place of 8, gives 911.67, 171.91 and 335.97; the calculation at \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=2\) is unavailable (see below).
Original Born contains none of these three inputs, so its cost stays at 34.58 on these scans.
Scaling the Born diameters of \MEAH, \MEACOO and \HCOthree together by 0.8, 0.9, 1.1 and 1.2, with the other Born diameters and all packing and Debye--H\"uckel diameters fixed, gives 63.69, 38.50, 38.03 and 48.70 for SSM+DS and 32.18, 32.96, 36.56 and 38.66 for Original Born.
Setting \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}\) to 24 and 40, in place of 32, gives 33.12 and 33.35 for SSM+DS and 32.18 and 38.71 for Original Born.

% P:results-27
At 0.8 times the three ion diameters, or at \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}=24\), Original Born without any refit reaches a cost of 32.18, below the 32.99 of the selected SSM+DS record.
The three ion Born diameters are inherited project values, not measured properties of these ions (\cref{tab:full-ionic-ssm-ds-parameters}), and \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}\) is held at one value for all temperatures.
Within the scanned ranges, the ranking of the two Born forms on the 142-target objective therefore depends on the inherited Born inputs as well as on the role of the Matin bicarbonate observations.
These are fixed-interaction evaluations, not optimized inputs, and they identify neither a better record nor a physical diameter or permittivity.

% P:results-21
The same scans at the historical 160-target optima set the expected scale; they belong to the earlier objective, not to the selected record.
At the historical SSM+DS optimum, \(f_{\HtwoO}=1\) raised the cost from 47.63 to 454.51 and the pressure-group cost from 10.065 to 386.263, while the B\"ottinger species cost fell from 5.480 to 4.865.
Among the scanned values, the water factor of 1.5 of Figiel et al.\ and the \MEA factor of 1 gave the lowest historical costs.
The historical costs at \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=4\) and 32 were 1647.30 and 359.64.
Uniform Born-diameter multipliers of 0.8 and 1.2 gave 84.28 and 59.98 for SSM+DS but 39.11 and 43.51 for Original Born.
\(\varepsilon_{\MEA}=24\) and 40 moved the two forms in opposite directions: 48.80 and 47.03 for SSM+DS, 39.08 and 43.42 for Original Born.
A historical refit of the five interactions after changing three ion Born diameters lowered the SSM+DS cost from 47.63 to 43.95.
This reduction of 3.67 falls short of 3.71, half the cost gap to Original Born, which was set in advance as the test of whether the diameters explain that gap (Supplementary Section~S1).

% P:results-17
Two Born probe calculations remain unavailable, both at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}: the shell-only form, \((c_{\mathrm{shell}},c_{\mathrm{dielectric}})=(1,0)\), at \texttt{vle\_obs\_0119}, and an ion-region permittivity \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=2\) at \texttt{vle\_obs\_0193}.
Continuation, bounded Newton probes and alternate starts found no solution that meets the convergence criteria for either state.
Their cause is undetermined: neither case demonstrates that no physical solution exists or that the solver fails at an existing solution.
The fixed-interaction scan of the selected record at \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=2\) stops at the same state, with an equilibrium residual of \(2.1\times10^{-4}\) after 61 iterations, so that scan point has no full cost.
Neither case enters the Born-form ranking.

% P:results-22
Refits with one interaction coordinate fixed at zero measure what that coordinate contributes once the other four can adjust (\cref{tab:parameter-significance}).
Each refit started from two points, the selected SSM+DS coordinates and the coordinates of the Original Born record, and used the convergence test of \cref{sec:data-methods}; all refits converged, and the two starts agreed in cost within a relative \(7\times10^{-13}\).
Fixing the \MEACOO--water, \MEAH--water, \HCOthree--water and \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficients at zero raises the cost by 9.85, 75.87, 11.93 and 134.32, and removing the slope \(b\) of \cref{eq:kij-temperature} raises it by 0.38.
The corresponding fitted-pressure AARDs are 14.89, 26.45, 16.64, 85.05 and 14.29\,\% and the fitted-species AARDs 9.43, 15.86, 9.52, 10.61 and 8.25\,\%, against 13.53 and 8.28\,\% for the selected record.
The \MEAH--\MEACOO and \MEAH--water coefficients therefore carry most of the fit.
Without the \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficient the Matin species cost falls by 7.54 while the pressure cost rises by 119.68, so an improvement in one target group does not stand in for the total.
The slope contributes little on these data, which constrain temperature only through pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and species at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.
The \HCOthree--water coefficient stays at its upper bound of \(+0.5\) in the \MEAH--\MEACOO and slope refits; its own refit tests whether that coefficient is needed at all, a separate question from whether its bound is active.

% P:results-23
Refits without the Born term, which leave the Debye--H\"uckel term as the only electrostatic contribution, measure how much of the fitted response the Born term carries.
With all five coordinates refitted from the same two starts, the cost is 98.79, against 32.99 for SSM+DS and 34.58 for Original Born, with fitted pressure and species AARDs of 53.90 and 10.92\,\%.
The \HCOthree--water coefficient reaches its upper bound of \(+0.5\) and the \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficient its lower bound of \(-1\).
The pressure targets carry most of the increase, 54.87 of 65.80, whereas the Matin species cost falls by 4.20.
Within their bounds, the five interaction coordinates therefore do not compensate for removal of the Born term.

% P:results-24
Two properties of \cref{eq:born-ssm-ds,eq:born-shell-diameter} limit how separately the Born inputs can be interpreted.
At \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}=0\), the shell diameter \(D_i\) equals \(d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\), so the dielectric-saturation term vanishes and \((c_{\mathrm{shell}},c_{\mathrm{dielectric}})=(0,1)\) is identical to Original Born, \((0,0)\).
The retained evaluation at the historical 160-target Original Born optimum confirms this equality: both selector pairs give a cost of 40.201537, with a largest weighted-residual difference of zero.
Because every component except water has \(f_k=1\), \(D_i\) depends on \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}\) and \(f_{\HtwoO}\) only through the product \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}(f_{\HtwoO}-1)\), so the two cannot be estimated separately from this dependence.

% P:results-25
A local Jacobian analysis at the historical 160-target SSM+DS optimum shows that, for small changes, the interaction coordinates can reproduce most of the residual change caused by a Born input.
The fraction of each Born-input column of the weighted Jacobian that lies in the span of the five interaction columns is 0.9928 for the \MEAH Born diameter, 0.9997 for \MEACOO, 0.9969 for \HCOthree, and 0.9939 for each of \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}\) and \(c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}\).
This compensation is local to that historical optimum and to small input changes.
A joint attribution that includes the \HthreeO Born diameter is unavailable, because its finite-difference column agreed on only 157 of 160 entries.
Large fixed-interaction cost changes therefore do not by themselves show that the data identify the Born inputs.
The compensation does not extend to removal of the Born term, which the refits above leave at a cost of 98.79.

% P:results-15
At the selected record, the \HCOthree--water interaction coefficient lies at its upper bound of \(+0.5\) with an outward cost gradient of \(-12.47\), so the cost would decrease further if the bound were relaxed.
The column-normalized singular values of the five-column weighted Jacobian are 1.550, 1.134, 0.899, 0.698 and 0.125.
All five columns are numerically nonzero, but the smallest singular value is one-twelfth of the largest, and a local Jacobian does not establish practical or global identification.
No unconstrained covariance or confidence band is reported at the bound.

\subsection{Temperature and concentration outside the present fit}\label{sec:results-80c}
% P:results-09
The \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} isotherm is excluded from the current five-coordinate, 142-target fit, but its data informed two fixed inputs (below), so these comparisons test the selected record outside its fit and are not predictions.
For the 11 Jou 1995 targets at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} (packet targets \texttt{vle\_obs\_0203}--\texttt{vle\_obs\_0213})~\cite{Jou1995}, the selected record gives a pressure AARD of 9.24\,\%, a mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-0.017\) and a root-mean-square \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of 0.117 (\cref{fig:pressure}).

For the 11 packet species targets at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, the AARD is 11.15\,\% for SSM+DS and 7.36\,\% for Original Born (\cref{fig:speciation}).

The selected record was also scored on 23 \COtwo partial pressures of Wagner et al.\ in 30 wt\% \MEA that no earlier fit, selection or assessment of this work had used~\cite{wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}.
It gives an AARD of 24.51\,\% with a mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-0.283\) on 11 rows near \SI{353}{\kelvin}, and 25.45\,\% with \(-0.308\) on 12 rows near \SI{392}{\kelvin}.
Because the inputs above carry \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} information, these scores also test the record outside its fit; they are not predictions.

% P:results-29
A separate fit tested whether the data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} determine the temperature response of the model.
R2 and R5 were returned to their source correlations, R4 already uses its source correlation, and the \COtwo dispersion energy was returned to the published \SI{169.21}{\kelvin}~\cite{Gross2001}.
The five interaction coordinates were then refitted to the same 142 targets, and the resulting record was frozen before any \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} calculation.
The binary \COtwo--water and \MEA--water interactions keep their fits to binary data at 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin}~\cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002} and 363--\SI{432}{\kelvin}~\cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996}, so this fit remains conditional on binary-subsystem information at higher temperatures and on the fixed SSM+DS, permittivity and association inputs.
It preserves the fit at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, with a cost of 33.01 against 32.99, but it represents higher temperatures worse than the selected record.
On the 21 canonical rows at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} it gives 29.33\,\% against 20.27\,\%, on the 11 Wagner rows near \SI{353}{\kelvin} 52.30\,\% against 24.51\,\%, and on the 57 canonical rows at 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} 52.05\,\% against 26.44\,\%.
Its mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is \(-0.375\) on the canonical \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} rows and \(-0.748\) on the Wagner rows, so its calculated pressures are too low.
Data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} therefore do not determine the temperature response of this model, and independent temperature information is required to fix it.

\subsection{Pooled pressure assessment}\label{sec:results-pooled}
% P:results-11
On the 104 canonical pressure rows at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, the selected record gives an AARD of 20.58\,\%, below the 35\,\% limit adopted for the working domain.
This group combines rows that coincide with fitted targets and rows inspected in earlier work, so it measures pooled agreement, not accuracy outside the fit.
The source-specific AARDs are 32.99\,\% for Aronu et al.\ (36 rows), 10.87\,\% for Hilliard (30 rows), 15.06\,\% for Jou et al.\ (28 rows) and 20.47\,\% for Idris et al.\ (10 rows)~\cite{Aronu2011,Hilliard2008,Jou1995,idrisSpeciationMEACO2Adducts2014}.
The mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is \(-0.398\) for the Aronu rows and \(-0.150\) for all 104 rows.
The pooled value therefore contains a source-dependent misfit, dominated by underestimation of the Aronu measurements.

\subsection{Limits of the selected record}\label{sec:results-limits}
% P:results-14
The carbonate share of dissolved carbon was compared with the NMR values of Jakobsen et al.\ at 20 and \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius}~\cite{Jakobsen2005}.
Over nine points, the ratio of calculated to observed share ranges from 0.42 to 1.33 for SSM+DS and from 1.21 to 3.19 for Original Born.
The nine-point summary excludes the \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius}, loading 0.21 point, whose observed share of 0.119 compares with 0.010--0.019 at the other four \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} loadings; all ten comparisons are retained in the supporting data.

Density and heat capacity were not assessed for the selected record.
For the earlier record \texttt{868a5018} on \texttt{epcsaft} wheel \texttt{48a639e7\ldots}, loaded-solution densities were 12.5--16.8\,\% above the measurements of Amundsen et al.\ at 50 and \SI{70}{\degreeCelsius} and loadings 0.3 and 0.4~\cite{Amundsen2009}.
Heat-capacity deviations against Hilliard were \(-9.8\,\%\) for unloaded solution at \SI{45}{\degreeCelsius}, \(-10.2\,\%\) at loading 0.358 and \SI{45}{\degreeCelsius}, and \(-14.6\,\%\) at loading 0.358 and \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}~\cite{Hilliard2008}.
These are deficits of the earlier record, not measured errors of the selected record.
Density and heat-capacity qualification of the selected record therefore remains unestablished, and no absorber or stripper use is supported.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-18
Unlike the electrolyte NRTL model of Akula et al., which left NMR speciation outside its fit~\cite{Akula2023a}, the present model fits speciation together with pressure, so the residuals of its 94 fitted species targets are calibration results (\cref{tab:literature-model-comparison}).
Published pressure deviations for 30 wt\% \MEA cover different row sets and temperature ranges, so no accuracy ranking against those models is claimed.
