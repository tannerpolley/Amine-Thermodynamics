\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model of aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo) is calibrated with its reaction equilibrium constants fixed at literature correlations, to test whether its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters can be held fixed for diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP), and whether finite phase-stability searches detect a second liquid.
Eleven MEA parameters are estimated jointly from pressure, speciation, absorption-heat, heat-capacity, density and binary equilibrium data; DEA, MDEA and AMP need four, eight and nine more.
For MEA, the fitted \COtwo pressures at nominal 30 wt\% have an average absolute relative deviation of 11.6\,\% and the 112 not fitted 24.9\,\%; no instability is detected, although the fit did not impose stability.
Pressures at 15 and 45 wt\% MEA, carbonate shares and high-temperature absorption heats remain inaccurate.
Fitted loaded-solution pressures of the other amines deviate by 10.4\,\% (AMP) to 28.5\,\% (DEA); no instability is detected for DEA, but its water--DEA excess enthalpies have the wrong sign.
For MDEA, no liquid--liquid gap of the \COtwo-free solution is detected at the reported parameters, interpolated toward a stability-penalized fit; one loaded-solution split remains, at \SI{262.9}{\mega\pascal}, far above process pressures.
The AMP model has at least 73 calculated splits, all above the measured temperature range of its dissociation constant; an alternative set with none detected deviates by 20.2\,\% from the same pressures.
The models are calibrations against data examined during development, with conditional standard errors; they provide neither validation nor a basis for absorber design.

\end{abstract}
