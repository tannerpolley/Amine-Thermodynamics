Dear Editors of Fluid Phase Equilibria,

Please consider “Modeling CO₂ solubility in aqueous alkanolamine solutions with ePC-SAFT” as a research article.

The paper describes CO₂ solubility in four aqueous amines with one ePC-SAFT model with explicit ions. The four calibrations share one base set that no amine data determine: water, CO₂, the inorganic ions and reactions, and water–anion interactions from osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts. Monoethanolamine (MEA) is fitted jointly to pressures, species fractions, absorption heats, heat capacities and densities. Diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are then fitted one by one, each through its own species, and every fitted model is searched for a second liquid.

What is new:

- **Four amines on one base without amine data.** The fitted CO₂ partial pressures of MEA at 30 wt% and of DEA deviate by 11.3 and 14.7%, and the fitted total pressures of MDEA and AMP by 4.2 and 10.8%. DEA and AMP each hold two parameters at their MEA values, so the base and these parameters give the pressures of a primary, a secondary, a tertiary and a hindered amine from one Helmholtz energy.
- **Water–anion interactions from salt data.** The water–bicarbonate and water–carbonate interactions are fitted to osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts and adopted because data without an amine determine them. The supplement compares the deviations with those of an interaction fitted to the MEA data.
- **Phase stability of the fitted models.** Reactive, electroneutral tangent-plane searches at measured and gridded states find no confirmed second liquid in the four models, and the fitted data decide that outcome. A DEA fit without the Lawson–Garst pressures predicts a second liquid. Weighting the CO₂-free MDEA pressures like the loaded ones removes a calculated liquid–liquid gap from that solution. An AMP calibration with other water–anion and carbamate inputs and no loaded pressures above 353 K predicts a second liquid, and adding the remaining loaded pressures alone removes it. A phase-stability search therefore tests a fitted electrolyte model in a way that its deviations from the fitted data do not.

The work fits the journal's scope in equations of state for electrolyte and reacting systems, chemical and phase equilibrium, and phase-stability analysis. It builds on ePC-SAFT studies of aqueous amines and electrolytes published in Fluid Phase Equilibria. The MEA model is compared with the author's earlier reactive ePC-SAFT model of MEA (Polley and Hedengren, Next Chemical Engineering 3 (2026) 100132), whose parameters were estimated in separate steps.

The four models supply thermodynamic properties for absorber design within the ranges and deviations that the limitations section states property by property. That section also gives the conditions where the deviations are larger, among them MEA pressures at 15 and 45 wt% and absorption heats at 393 K.

A supplement gives the model contributions, parameters, data conventions, deviations by data set and phase-stability details. The electronic data comprise processed observations, parameter values, numerical results and the analysis and figure code; the deposit also contains the epcsaft build used for the reported values, whose source is public.
