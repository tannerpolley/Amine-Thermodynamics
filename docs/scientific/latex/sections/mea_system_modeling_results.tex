\subsection{Calibrated model}\label{sec:results-calibration}
% P:results-02
The fitted SSM+DS parameter set fits 48 \COtwo partial-pressure targets at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} with an AARD of 13.53\,\%.
It fits 94 liquid species targets at 20, 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} with an AARD of 8.28\,\%.
The species targets are 40 targets from the NMR study of B\"ottinger et al.\ and 54 \MEA, \MEAH and \MEACOO targets from the titration study of Matin et al.~\cite{Bottinger2008,matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
The mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of the fitted pressure targets is 0.011, so the calibration carries no material pooled pressure bias.
\Cref{fig:pressure,fig:speciation} compare the calculated pressures and species with all measurements, including those outside the fit.

\subsection{What the fitted response depends on}\label{sec:results-sensitivity}
% P:results-22
Refits with one binary interaction parameter fixed at zero measure what that parameter contributes once the other four can adjust (\cref{tab:parameter-significance}).
Each refit started from two points, the fitted SSM+DS parameters and the fitted original Born parameters, and used the convergence test of \cref{sec:data-methods}; all refits converged, and the two starts agreed in cost within a relative \(7\times10^{-13}\).
Fixing the \MEACOO--water, \MEAH--water, \HCOthree--water and \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficients at zero raises the fitted-pressure AARD from 13.53\,\% to 14.89, 26.45, 16.64 and 85.05\,\%, and the fitted-species AARD from 8.28\,\% to 9.43, 15.86, 9.52 and 10.61\,\%; the corresponding cost increases are 9.85, 75.87, 11.93 and 134.32.
Removing the slope \(b\) of \cref{eq:kij-temperature} gives AARDs of 14.29 and 8.25\,\% and raises the cost by 0.38.
The \MEAH--\MEACOO and \MEAH--water coefficients therefore dominate the tested ablations.
Without the \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficient the Matin species cost falls by 7.54 while the pressure cost rises by 119.68, so an improvement in one target group does not stand in for the total.
The slope contributes little on these data, which constrain temperature only through pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and species at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.
The \HCOthree--water coefficient stays at its upper bound of \(+0.5\) in the \MEAH--\MEACOO and slope refits; its own refit tests whether that coefficient is needed at all, a separate question from whether its bound is active.

% P:results-23
Refits without the Born term, which leave the Debye--H\"uckel term as the only electrostatic contribution, measure how much of the fitted response the Born term carries.
With all five parameters refitted from the same two starts, the fitted-pressure AARD rises from 13.53 to 53.90\,\% and the fitted-species AARD from 8.28 to 10.92\,\%; the cost is 98.79, against 32.99 for SSM+DS and 34.58 for original Born.
The \HCOthree--water coefficient reaches its upper bound of \(+0.5\) and the \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficient its lower bound of \(-1\).
The pressure targets carry most of the cost increase, 54.87 of 65.80, whereas the Matin species cost falls by 4.20.
On the 18 Matin \ce{HCO3- + CO3^2-} pool observations at \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius}, which are compared but not fitted, this refit gives an AARD of 20.12\,\%, against 59.81\,\% for the fitted parameter set, and its cost with those observations included is 102.14.
Removing the Born term therefore moves the calculated pool toward the titration bicarbonate while losing pressure accuracy, so the choice of electrostatic terms and the role of the titration bicarbonate data are coupled.
Within their bounds, the five binary interaction parameters do not compensate for removal of the Born term.
A comparison of electrolyte terms in this system is therefore interpretable only together with a statement of which speciation data are fitted.

% P:results-15
At the fitted parameter set, the \HCOthree--water interaction coefficient lies at its upper bound of \(+0.5\) with an outward cost gradient of \(-12.47\), so the cost would decrease further if the bound were relaxed.
The column-normalized singular values of the five-column weighted Jacobian are 1.550, 1.134, 0.899, 0.698 and 0.125.
All five columns are numerically nonzero, but the smallest singular value is one-twelfth of the largest, and a local Jacobian does not establish practical or global identification.
No unconstrained covariance or confidence band is reported at the bound.

\subsection{Born form}\label{sec:results-born}
% P:results-04
The SSM+DS and original Born forms were fitted with the same five binary interaction parameters, bounds, weights, reaction constants and ion inputs, and they calibrate the data comparably (\cref{tab:born-formulation-refits}).
At the baseline inputs, SSM+DS gives the lower fitted-pressure AARD, 13.53 against 17.31\,\%, whereas original Born gives a slightly lower fitted-species AARD, 8.23 against 8.28\,\%; on the 104 measured pressures at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, the two forms give 20.58 and 20.78\,\%.
Across three sets of fixed Born inputs, the baseline and the three ion Born diameters multiplied by 0.8 or \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}=24\), the refitted forms give fitted-pressure AARDs of 13.53--17.31\,\%, fitted-species AARDs of 7.98--8.28\,\% and costs of 31.72--34.58.
Each altered-input fit was started from the fitted parameters of both forms; all converged, and the two starts agreed in cost within a relative \(8\times10^{-13}\).
Including a Born contribution matters far more than which form is used: the refit without it gives a fitted-pressure AARD of 53.90\,\% and a cost of 98.79, whereas the form-to-form cost differences at matched inputs are 0.26--1.59.
The comparison applies the SSM+DS construction of Figiel et al.~\cite{Figiel2025,Figiel2026Correction} to a reactive, carbamate-forming amine at 20--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}.
Figiel et al.\ fitted Born diameters to infinite-dilution solvation Gibbs energies in water, solvent factors to NaBr activity coefficients, and aqueous ion interaction parameters to salt activity or osmotic data~\cite[Model Parameters, pp.~9411--9412]{Figiel2025}.

% P:results-05
Which form has the lower cost changes with the role of the titration bicarbonate data and with the fixed Born inputs.
With Matin's 18 bicarbonate-pool targets compared but not fitted, SSM+DS has a 4.6\,\% lower cost, 32.99 against 34.58; this is a local cost comparison, not a statistical test.
In the fit including the titration bicarbonate data, original Born is lower, 40.20 against 47.63, and the Matin bicarbonate pool alone contributes 6.02 of that 7.42 difference; in the fit without the titration data, SSM+DS is lower, 10.47 against 16.85.
At the altered inputs, original Born is lower with the three ion Born diameters multiplied by 0.8, 31.72 against 31.99, and with \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}=24\), 32.08 against 32.90; it gains its advantage in the Matin species, whereas SSM+DS keeps the lower pressure and B\"ottinger costs at every input set.
For the datasets and input sets examined here, the pressure and speciation fits give no consistent preference between the two Born formulations; independent solvation or activity data could help choose between them.
Supplementary Section~S1 decomposes these costs by target group and gives fixed-interaction scans of each Born input, the local compensation between Born inputs and binary interaction parameters, and a refit of the Born diameters.

% P:results-08
Matin et al.\ obtained bicarbonate from a strong-base titration that assigns carbonate to bicarbonate, and they report bicarbonate above the NMR values of Jakobsen et al., whereas their free amine, protonated amine and carbamate agree more closely (Fig.~2, p.~6616; Fig.~3 and discussion, p.~6617)~\cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012,Jakobsen2005}.
On that basis the 18 Matin pool targets are compared but not fitted; the fitted parameter set lies below them, with a signed relative mean of \(-58.84\,\%\) and an AARD of 59.81\,\%, against \(-34.30\,\%\) and 35.12\,\% for original Born.
The residual signs do not establish which measurement is correct.

\subsection{Temperature and concentration outside the present fit}\label{sec:results-80c}
% P:results-09
The \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} isotherm is outside the five-parameter fit to 142 targets.
For the 11 Jou et al.\ measurements at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, at loadings 0.017--0.689~\cite{Jou1995}, the fitted parameter set gives a pressure AARD of 9.24\,\%, a mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-0.017\) and a root-mean-square \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of 0.117 (\cref{fig:pressure}).
The calculation of all 161 measured pressures gives the same 9.24\,\% on these 11 measurements.
All 21 measured pressures at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, which add 10 measurements of Aronu et al.~\cite{Aronu2011}, give 20.27\,\% with a mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-0.199\), so the model underestimates the added rows.
Without the two Jou et al.\ measurements at loadings 0.118 and 0.578, which were used to estimate the fixed reaction shifts, the remaining 19 rows give 21.83\,\%.
For the 11 B\"ottinger et al.\ species observations at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, the AARD is 11.15\,\% for SSM+DS and 7.36\,\% for original Born (\cref{fig:speciation}).

% P:results-10
Two fixed inputs were estimated with \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} data (\cref{sec:data-methods}): the R2 and R5 reaction shifts were estimated on a set that included the Jou 1995 measurements at loadings 0.118 and 0.578, two of the 11 at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, and the fixed \COtwo dispersion energy of \SI{173.440}{\kelvin} was chosen against a set that included 21 pressure measurements and 11 species observations at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}.

% P:results-28
The 23 \COtwo partial pressures of Wagner et al.\ near 30 wt\% \MEA were used at no earlier stage of this work, by any fit, selection or assessment~\cite{wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}.
The fitted parameter set gives an AARD of 24.51\,\% with a mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-0.283\) on 11 rows near \SI{353}{\kelvin}, and 25.45\,\% with \(-0.308\) on 12 rows near \SI{392}{\kelvin}.

% P:results-29
A separate fit tested whether the data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} determine the temperature response of the model.
R2 and R5 were returned to their source correlations, R4 already uses its source correlation, and the \COtwo dispersion energy was returned to the published \SI{169.21}{\kelvin}~\cite{Gross2001}.
The five binary interaction parameters were then refitted to the same 142 targets, and the resulting parameter set was fixed before any \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} calculation.
The binary \COtwo--water and \MEA--water interactions keep their fits to binary data at 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin}~\cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002} and 363--\SI{432}{\kelvin}~\cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996}, so this fit remains conditional on binary-subsystem information at higher temperatures and on the fixed SSM+DS, permittivity and association inputs.
It preserves the fit at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, with a cost of 33.01 against 32.99, but it represents higher temperatures worse than the fitted parameter set.
On the 21 measured pressures at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} it gives 29.33\,\% against 20.27\,\%, on the 11 Wagner rows near \SI{353}{\kelvin} 52.30\,\% against 24.51\,\%, and on the 57 measured pressures at 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} 52.05\,\% against 26.44\,\%.
Its mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is \(-0.375\) on the \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} pressures and \(-0.748\) on the Wagner rows, so its calculated pressures are too low.
Data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} therefore do not determine the temperature response of this model, and independent temperature information is required to fix it.
In a diagnostic refit otherwise identical to this one, with R2 at its source correlation and R4 and R5 given independent temperature dependences fitted to the dilute-solution \MEA carbamate constants of Aroua et al.~\cite[Table~3]{arouaEquilibriumConstantCarbamate1999} and \MEA dissociation constants of Kim et al.~\cite[Table~7]{kimThermodynamicsProtonationAmines2011} at or below \SI{333}{\kelvin}, the refit to the same targets reaches a cost of 32.84 but gives 28.58\,\% on the 21 measured pressures at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} and 51.61\,\% on the same 11 Wagner rows.
The tested dilute-solution temperature laws for R4 and R5 did not recover the fitted parameter set's higher-temperature pressure agreement; because source measurement uncertainties were unavailable, agreement with the literature constants was not assessed.
For modelers, the temperature response of this model must come from data that carry it directly, such as heat of absorption or equilibrium measurements above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.

% P:results-31
Two further refits tested R2, the reaction of dissolved \COtwo with water to bicarbonate, keeping the dilute-solution R4 and R5 laws and the published \COtwo dispersion energy of the preceding diagnostic refit; both were fitted to the same 142 targets and fixed before any calculation at or above \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}.
In the first, R2 follows the pure-water first ionization constant of carbonic acid of Plummer and Busenberg~\cite{plummerSolubilitiesCalciteAragonite1982a}, \(\log_{10}K_1=-6.338\) at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, consistent with \(-6.33\pm0.03\) of Stef\'ansson et al.~\cite[Table~2]{stefanssonCarbonicAcidIonization2013}.
This constant refers to the combined \ce{CO2(aq) + H2CO3} pool, and the Austgen correlation, taken from Edwards et al.~\cite{edwardsVaporliquidEquilibriaMulticomponent1978}, is assumed to use the same convention; the error limits for the four-term representation of the Plummer--Busenberg law were widened to 0.002 in \(\ln K\) and \SI{0.15}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} before any loaded-solution fit.
This parameter set preserves the fit at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, with a cost of 32.68 against 32.99, but gives 27.61\,\% on the 21 measured pressures at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} and 50.05\,\% on the 11 Wagner rows near \SI{353}{\kelvin}.
In the second, \(\ln K_2\) at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and the R2 reaction enthalpy are fitted with the five binary interaction parameters, starting from the Austgen correlation.
The fitted enthalpy shift, \SI{-5.90}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, has the same sign as the fixed shift of \SI{-8.20}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, which was estimated with \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} data (\cref{sec:data-methods}); with a cost of 31.67, this parameter set gives 23.02\,\% and 42.78\,\% on the same rows, against 20.27\,\% and 24.51\,\% for the fitted parameter set.
Because two binary interaction parameters lie at their bounds, the shift is conditional on them and is a calibration value, not an identified hydration enthalpy or a validated temperature law.
The data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} thus move the R2 enthalpy in the direction the \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} data required and recover part of the \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} agreement, whereas the pure-water constant changes it little; equilibrium or heat-of-absorption data above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} are needed to set the temperature response.

% P:results-13
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, the 57 measured pressures give an AARD of 26.44\,\%: 20.39\,\% for Jou et al.\ (20 rows), 32.02\,\% for Ma'mun et al.\ (19 rows) and 27.26\,\% for Xu and Rochelle (18 rows)~\cite{Jou1995,Mamun2005,Xu2011}.
These temperatures lie above \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} and above the \SI{323.15}{\kelvin} upper limit of the R4 and R5 source correlations, so these values are extrapolations.

\subsection{Pooled pressure comparison}\label{sec:results-pooled}
% P:results-11
On the 104 measured pressures at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, the fitted parameter set gives an AARD of 20.58\,\%.
This group combines rows that coincide with fitted targets and rows examined when the objective was chosen, so it measures pooled agreement, not accuracy outside the fit.
The source-specific AARDs are 32.99\,\% for Aronu et al.\ (36 rows), 10.87\,\% for Hilliard (30 rows), 15.06\,\% for Jou et al.\ (28 rows) and 20.47\,\% for Idris et al.\ (10 rows)~\cite{Aronu2011,Hilliard2008,Jou1995,idrisSpeciationMEACO2Adducts2014}.
The mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is \(-0.398\) for the Aronu rows and \(-0.150\) for all 104 rows.
The pooled value therefore contains a source-dependent misfit, dominated by underestimation of the Aronu measurements.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-18
Like the electrolyte NRTL model of Zhang et al., which was fitted to \COtwo solubility, heat of absorption, heat capacity and NMR speciation~\cite{Zhang2011}, and unlike that of Akula et al., which left NMR speciation outside its fit~\cite{Akula2023a}, the present model fits speciation together with pressure, so the residuals of its 94 fitted species targets are calibration results (\cref{tab:literature-model-comparison}).
Unlike the ePC-SAFT models of MDEA~\cite{Uyan2015,Wangler2018,Bulow2021a}, it fits ion interactions to the loaded ternary mixture, including the \MEAH--\MEACOO interaction, which exists only in loaded solution and dominates the tested ablations.
Published pressure deviations for 30 wt\% \MEA cover different row sets and temperature ranges, so no accuracy ranking against those models is claimed.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:results-limits}
% P:results-14
The fitted parameter set describes \COtwo partial pressure and liquid speciation in 30 wt\% \MEA at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}.
Its parameters are calibrated for 30 wt\% \MEA: applied unchanged, with the same chemistry, it gives AARDs of 75.95\,\% on 33 measured pressures at 15 wt\% \MEA and 46.15\,\% on 37 at 45 wt\%, with mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(+0.453\) and \(-0.519\), so the error changes sign with amine concentration.
The \HCOthree--water coefficient lies at its bound (\cref{sec:results-sensitivity}), so its value is set by the bound rather than estimated from the data.
Over nine Jakobsen NMR points at 20 and \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius}, the calculated carbonate share of dissolved carbon is 0.42--1.33 times the observed share for SSM+DS and 1.21--3.19 times for original Born~\cite{Jakobsen2005}; the excluded \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius}, loading 0.21 point has an observed share of 0.119, against 0.010--0.019 at the other four \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} loadings, and all ten comparisons are retained in the supporting data.

% P:results-16
Liquid density and heat capacity are not qualified.
For the fitted parameter set, homogeneous-liquid densities at an assumed ambient pressure of \SI{101325}{\pascal} exceed Amundsen et al.'s measurements by 13.17 and 17.80\,\% at \SI{50}{\degreeCelsius} and by 12.99 and 17.39\,\% at \SI{70}{\degreeCelsius}, for loadings 0.3 and 0.4~mol \COtwo/mol \MEA on a \COtwo-free 30 wt\% \MEA solvent basis~\cite[Table~3, p.~3097]{Amundsen2009}, whose stated loaded-density uncertainty is \(\pm\SI{0.002}{\gram\per\cubic\centi\metre}\) (approximately \(\pm0.2\,\%\))~\cite[Discussion, pp.~3099--3100]{Amundsen2009}.
The near-unloaded control at \SI{50}{\degreeCelsius} and loading 0.000101 gives \SI{0.993873}{\gram\per\cubic\centi\metre}, 0.423\,\% below the unloaded value of \SI{0.9981}{\gram\per\cubic\centi\metre}~\cite[Table~1, p.~3097]{Amundsen2009}, so the deviation appears in loaded solution.
For an earlier parameter set, heat-capacity deviations against Hilliard were \(-9.8\,\%\) for a near-unloaded proxy at loading \(10^{-5}\) and \SI{45}{\degreeCelsius}, \(-10.2\,\%\) at loading 0.358 and \SI{45}{\degreeCelsius}, and \(-14.6\,\%\) at loading 0.358 and \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}~\cite{Hilliard2008}.
The fitted parameter set is therefore a phase- and speciation-equilibrium model; absorber and stripper calculations would require density and heat-capacity qualification first.
