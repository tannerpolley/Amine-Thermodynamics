\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% P:conclusion-01
Electrolyte PC-SAFT with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions and explicit carbamate chemistry describes \COtwo partial pressure and liquid speciation in 30 wt\% aqueous \MEA with one set of liquid-phase parameters.
Fitted through five binary interaction parameters to 47 \COtwo pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and 94 species measurements at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, the nine-species model reaches AARDs of 14.0 and 8.3\,\%.
On Wagner et al.\ pressures near 80 and \SI{119}{\degreeCelsius} (353 and \SI{392}{\kelvin}), which entered no fitting or selection step, it gives 22.5 and 22.3\,\%; the other data at 80--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} informed fixed reaction shifts and are not independent temperature tests.
This extends the ePC-SAFT treatment of reactive MDEA to a carbamate-forming primary amine.

% P:conclusion-02
Within the declared bounds, the tested refit without a Born term does not recover agreement: the fitted-pressure AARD rises from 14.0 to 53.6\,\%.
A decomposition of the calculated pressure shows why within this model: the ion-concentration part of the Born contribution lowers the activity sum that sets the \COtwo pressure increasingly with loading, the refitted interactions offset its removal at low loading but not at high loading, and at high loading a compensating speciation change cancels most of the activity-sum change, so the pressure error of the refit is largest at intermediate loading.
The SSM+DS and original Born formulations calibrate comparably, and which has the lower cost depends on the fitted observations: SSM+DS without the titration bicarbonate pool and original Born with it.

% P:conclusion-03
These results suggest three practical lessons for fitting this model.
First, a comparison of electrolyte terms must state which speciation data are fitted, because fitting the titration bicarbonate pool alone changes which Born form has the lower cost; independent solvation or activity data could guide the choice of form.
Second, some parameters are not determined by these data: the \MEAH--water slope, \SI{-112.6}{\kelvin}, has a conditional standard error of \SI{119.7}{\kelvin}, and the \HCOthree--water coefficient lies on its bound.
Third, data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} do not determine the temperature response: a refit with literature reaction temperature laws and the published \COtwo dispersion energy matches the fit at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} but gives 51.0\,\% at 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, so heat-of-absorption or equilibrium data above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} are needed to fix it.

% P:conclusion-04
The selected parameter set applies to \COtwo partial pressure and speciation in 30 wt\% \MEA.
Applied unchanged at 15 and 45 wt\% \MEA it gives AARDs of 76.7 and 46.3\,\% (\cref{sec:results-80c}), and it overestimates loaded-solution density by 13.0--17.8\,\%.
Qualifying density and caloric properties, and adding heat-of-absorption data to the fit, are the next steps toward absorber and stripper calculations.
