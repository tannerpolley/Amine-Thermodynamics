\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\COtwo}{CO2} solubility}\label{sec:results-pressure}
% P:results-pressure-01
The model pressure rises with analytical loading and temperature across the calculated isotherms (\cref{fig:pressure}).
At loading 0.69 it increases from \SI{159}{\kilo\pascal} at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} to \SI{2.848}{\mega\pascal} at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
The AARD is 11.86\,\% over the 47 fitted pressures and 20.55\,\% over the complete 159-pressure comparison, including 112 observations not fitted.
By source, the latter comparison gives 9.42\,\% for Hilliard, 19.37\,\% for Jou and 29.76\,\% for Aronu (Section~S2).
The deviation panel exposes discrepancies that are less apparent on the logarithmic pressure axis: Aronu's non-fitted \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} points at analytical loading 0.46--0.52 are 46--70\,\% below measurement.
The difference between source-specific errors prevents the fitted average alone from describing pressure accuracy.

% P:results-pressure-02
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, the 54 six-source pressures give AARD 24.35\,\%, with bias direction differing between sources.
The 23 additional Wagner pressures give AARDs of 22.20\,\% near \SI{353}{\kelvin} and 9.25\,\% near \SI{392}{\kelvin} (Section~S2).
Pressure agreement deteriorates at other solvent concentrations: the 33 Aronu observations at 15 wt\% and 37 at 45 wt\% give AARDs of 29.5 and 56.0\,\%, respectively.
Their mean logarithmic biases are $-0.204$ and $+0.149$, so the model underpredicts at 15 wt\% and overpredicts at 45 wt\% on logarithmic average.
The reported parameters therefore do not support concentration transfer across that range.

\subsection{Liquid speciation}\label{sec:results-species}
% P:results-species-01
The speciation curves show conversion of molecular MEA into protonated amine and carbamate as loading increases, followed by increasing bicarbonate (\cref{fig:speciation}).
The combined \ce{MEA + MEAH+} observable gives AARD 2.32\,\% over 25 compared observations, of which 20 were fitted; all 94 species fractions in the shared fitted subset give 7.10\,\%.
Agreement with this amine sum does not independently test the separate molecular and protonated fractions.
Calculated carbonate mole fraction remains at or below $1.8\times10^{-5}$ on the curves.
The ten Jakobsen carbonate-share comparisons are also low: calculated/observed share has mean 0.0731 and range 0.00633--0.267 (Section~S3).
Those calculations account for dilution of the NMR samples and use their NMR loadings \cite[Sample preparation, Tables~A1--A2]{Jakobsen2005}; Jakobsen's warning about quantifying minor components is discussed by Matin et al.\ \cite[p.~6616]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.

% P:results-species-02
The 19 non-fitted Matin bicarbonate + carbonate totals give AARD 67.48\,\% and mean logarithmic bias $-1.266$.
Their systematic underprediction remains despite agreement with other titration species.
Matin et al.\ found their inferred bicarbonate values higher than the NMR values \cite[pp.~6616--6617]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}, so the discrepancy may partly reflect disagreement between sources as well as limitations of the model.
The carbonate share and titration bicarbonate comparisons constrain the interpretation of a good pressure fit.

\subsection{Heat of absorption}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The fitted Vinjarapu integral heats give RMSE \SI{1.83}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} (\cref{fig:heat}).
For Arcis at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}, the mean calculated-minus-measured differences are \SI{+10.21}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} and \SI{+3.14}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}: the calculated absorption is less exothermic.
Their pressure-separated RMSEs are 10.33 and \SI{3.37}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, respectively.
The model's pressure effect is small; increasing pressure between those two series changes the calculated heat by about \SI{0.19}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at the same loading.

% P:results-heat-02
The Arcis measurements themselves differ between pressure series: heats at nearby loadings 0.181 and 0.183 are $-92.6$ and $-85.9$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, and at loadings 0.391 and 0.405 are $-94.4$ and $-85.5$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \cite[Table~5, pp.~3355--3356]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
These are not exact matched-loading comparisons, and both temperature and pressure differ from Vinjarapu's conditions.
The available data do not isolate a cause for the discrepancy.
Arcis was used in parameter selection, and absorption heat has been checked only at 313.15 and \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}; these comparisons do not establish its temperature dependence across an absorber or stripper.

\subsection{Density and heat capacity}\label{sec:results-density}
% P:results-density-01
Loaded densities are calculated directly without volume translation (\cref{fig:density-curves}).
The 47 fitted loaded densities give AARD 0.18\,\%, with 44 within $2s$.
Three fitted values at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} are 0.40--0.53\,\% low: Amundsen at loading 0.3 has error \SI{-5.52}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} ($-2.28s$), and Hartono at loadings 0.29 and 0.39 has errors \SI{-4.16}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} ($-2.05s$) and \SI{-4.41}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} ($-2.43s$).
All five fitted \COtwo-free densities lie within \SI{1}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} of measurement.
Section~S3 gives the source scales, the complete density comparison and the density-error figure.

% P:results-density-02
None of Han's densities was fitted.
For its five loaded 30 wt\% observations at each temperature, the mean signed error grows from $-0.43$\,\% at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} to $-0.72$\,\% at \SI{373.15}{\kelvin} and $-1.20$\,\% at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
The largest error is \SI{-20.02}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} at loading 0.56 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}: \SI{1037.58}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} calculated against \SI{1057.60}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} measured.
Non-fitted 30 wt\% Amundsen loaded observations at 25--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} lie up to about 1.5\,\% above the model and above the other sources in \cref{fig:density-curves}.
The five loading-0.4 Amundsen observations excluded from fitting remain in this comparison; the cross-source differences do not establish which source is more accurate (Section~S3).

% P:results-density-03
Heat capacity is fitted only at 318.15 and \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, at nominally zero loading and loading 0.358.
The four signed errors are $+5.53$ and $+2.57$\,\% at \SI{318.15}{\kelvin}, and $+1.15$ and $-2.13$\,\% at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, in unloaded/loaded order (Section~S2).
The largest error remains at the unloaded colder state despite inclusion in the objective.
These four observations do not determine heat capacity over the full temperature and loading domain of the pressure curves.

The model calculates the five target properties within one thermodynamic formulation, using ePC-SAFT, reaction standard states and ideal-gas caloric references.
Loaded densities are calculated directly without volume translation.
The comparisons support use as a representation of the selected nominal 30 wt\% equilibrium observations, with the property-specific errors above.
They do not establish concentration transfer, accurate minor-species fractions or caloric behavior beyond the measured temperatures, nor do they qualify absorber or stripper performance.

\subsection{Phase stability}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:results-phase-01
A comparison fit with wider bounds on the protonated-amine diameter and its water interaction gives similar property agreement but predicts additional liquid phases at some tested states.
Narrowing both bounds and refitting removes the detected instabilities in the finite search, while increasing the fitting cost by 0.45\%.
The \MEAH segment diameter changes from \SI{4.16}{\angstrom} in the comparison fit to \SI{5.35}{\angstrom} in the reported parameters, and its water interaction at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} changes from $+0.027$ to $-0.5$.
The number of fitted loaded densities within $2s$ changes from 47/47 to 44/47; the three misses occur at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, above the searched temperatures.
This comparison measures a difference between local fits, not a minimum density penalty or a global optimum.

% P:results-phase-02
The finite search covers 73 specified temperature, pressure and loading states for nominal 30 wt\% MEA at 313.15--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin} and $\alpha=10^{-5}$--0.5 (\cref{fig:phase-family}).
The tangent-plane criterion \cite[Sect.~2]{Michelsen1982} is applied to reacting, charge-neutral trials, with reduced distance
\begin{equation}
 D(\boldsymbol w)=g(T,P,\boldsymbol w)/(RT)-\boldsymbol w^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda,
 \qquad \sum_iw_i=1,\quad\sum_i z_iw_i=0,\quad w_i>0,
 \label{eq:tpd}
\end{equation}
where $B$ represents conserved component combinations and $\boldsymbol\lambda$ their chemical-potential multipliers in the existing equilibrium.
A resolved instability requires a negative distance and a material-conserving finite transfer that lowers Gibbs energy beyond its numerical-error estimate; Section~S4 gives the complete method and states.
The comparison fit admits descent at 12 states, whereas none is resolved for the reported parameters.
Separately seeded calculations confirm a water-rich liquid and an amine- and ion-rich liquid at \SI{333.15}{\kelvin}, loadings 0.4 and 0.5 (Section~S4).
A liquid--vapor classification alone is not evidence for two liquids.
The search does not establish stability over a continuous property domain or at the warmer density and pressure states.

% P:results-phase-03
The main articles cited here do not report a tangent-plane stability assessment of their parameter sets \cite[Sect.~4]{Uyan2015}\cite[Sect.~6]{Wangler2018}\cite[Results and Discussion]{Bulow2021a}\cite[Sects.~4--7]{Bottinger2008}\cite[Sects.~4--6]{Nasrifar2010}\cite[Sect.~3]{Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}\cite[Sects.~2--3]{Cleeton2020}\cite[Sects.~2--4]{Schick2023}.
Schick et al.\ report experimentally that no demixing was observed \cite[Sect.~3.2]{Schick2023}, which is a different kind of evidence.
The present calculation illustrates why property agreement should be accompanied by a search for additional phases; it is not physical validation of phase behavior.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-literature-01
Zhang et al.\ provide the closest multi-property comparison, with 12 ternary coefficients fitted to solubility, differential heat, heat capacity and NMR \cite[Sect.~3.3, Tables~9--11]{Zhang2011}.
Their fitted pressure deviations range from 13.5 to 35.5\,\% by source; the present values are 11.86\,\% over 47 fitted pressures and 20.55\,\% over the 159-pressure comparison.
Najafloo and Zarei and Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh report 34.71 and 36.42\,\%, respectively, over 691 ternary comparisons after pure/binary estimation \cite[Table~5]{Najafloo2018,Baygi2015}.
Their data sets, fitting roles and domains differ, and Zhang's differential heat differs from the integral heat assessed here.
\Cref{tab:literature-model-comparison} gives the quantities and data roles for these and other MEA models; the statistics locate the present model within the published range without establishing equal or better accuracy.
