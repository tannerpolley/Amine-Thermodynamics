Dear Editors of Fluid Phase Equilibria,

Please consider “Thermodynamics of CO₂-loaded aqueous amines with ePC-SAFT: monoethanolamine calibration, transfer to DEA, MDEA and AMP, and phase stability” as a research article.

The paper reports an ePC-SAFT model of CO₂-loaded aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) and tests how far its shared parameters carry to three further amines. The MEA model is calibrated first, with its reaction equilibrium constants fixed at literature correlations; its water, CO₂ and inorganic-ion parameters are then held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted, and every fitted model is searched for phase instability.

What is new:

- **Four models with one set of shared parameters.** The fitted loaded-solution pressures deviate by 11.3 % for MEA, 42.71 % for DEA, 4.15 % for MDEA and 10.84 % for AMP. Transfer here means that the further amines could be fitted with the shared parameters held fixed, not that MEA determines those parameters. For DEA and AMP the carbamate-hydrolysis constant is fitted, MDEA needed its loaded-solution pressures weighted against its many density and heat data, and DEA is reported with a negative result: no tested water–DEA interaction represents the binary excess enthalpies and bubble pressures together.
- **Shared water–anion interactions fixed without amine data.** The water–bicarbonate and water–carbonate interactions are fixed from osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts. They are adopted for that reason, not because the amine data are fitted better. With the pressure weights of the MDEA model they fit both the 66 MDEA total pressures and 101 unfitted MDEA CO₂ partial pressures better than an interaction fitted to the MEA data; with the first weights they traded one for the other.
- **Phase stability as a check on extrapolation and failure.** The searches detect no instability for MEA or at the 666 searched AMP states and confirm two calculated liquid–liquid splits for MDEA, at 204 and 263 MPa, far above the measured pressures. For DEA they confirm splits at four states whose densities were measured on samples treated as one liquid, where the DEA model fails. A first AMP fit, whose loaded-solution pressures ended at 353 K, gave calculated splits at 363–393 K; a refit that added 58 measured pressures at those temperatures confirms none, although at four measured states the phase identity remains unresolved.

The work fits the journal's scope in equations of state for electrolyte and reacting systems, chemical and phase equilibrium, and phase-stability analysis. It builds on ePC-SAFT studies of aqueous amines and electrolytes published in Fluid Phase Equilibria. The MEA model is compared with the author's earlier reactive ePC-SAFT model of MEA (Polley and Hedengren, Next Chemical Engineering 3 (2026) 100132), whose parameters were estimated in separate steps.

The limits are gathered in one section: pressures at 15 and 45 wt% MEA, carbonate shares and high-temperature absorption heats remain inaccurate, the calculated phase behaviors are not established by the measurements, and the models, calibrated against data examined during development, are not a basis for absorber design.
