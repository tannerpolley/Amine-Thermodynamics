\section{Thermodynamic model}\label{sec:theory}
% P:theory-01
Each liquid contains molecular \COtwo, water, the amine A and its cation \ce{AH+}, together with bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide; the MEA, DEA and AMP liquids also contain the carbamate \ce{ACOO-}, which MDEA does not form.
Only \COtwo, water and the molecular amine enter the vapor, and one ePC-SAFT equation of state describes both phases.

\subsection{ePC-SAFT contributions}\label{sec:contributions}
% P:theory-02
The reduced residual Helmholtz energy, $a^{\mathrm{res}}=A^{\mathrm{res}}/(nRT)$, is the sum of hard-chain, dispersion, association, Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions \cite{Gross2001,Gross2002,Cameretti2005,Held2014,Figiel2025}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{res}}
    =
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}
    +a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    +a^{\mathrm{assoc}}
    +a^{\mathrm{DH}}
    +a^{\mathrm{Born}} .
    \label{eq:epcsaft-residual-split}
\end{equation}
A neutral species is a chain of $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}$ spherical segments of diameter $\sigma_i$ and dispersion energy $u_i$; an ion is a single charged sphere, except the DEA and MDEA cations, which keep the chain of their amine.
Pressure and chemical potentials follow from the density and composition derivatives of $a^{\mathrm{res}}$ \cite{Gross2001} and caloric properties from its temperature derivatives; all include the variation of the relative permittivity (\cref{eq:solvent-permittivity}).
Below, $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions, and $\varepsilon$ denotes a relative permittivity, as distinct from the association energy $\epsilon^{AB}$.

% P:theory-02a
The hard-chain and dispersion terms are those of Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}, the association term follows Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite{Kleiner2007}, and the Debye--H\"uckel term is that of Cameretti et al.\ \cite{Cameretti2005}; Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-contributions} of the Supplementary Material gives the four terms.
The hard-sphere diameter $d_i$ of a neutral species or chain cation depends on temperature \cite{Gross2001}, with the water diameter $\sigma_w(T)=\sigma_0+a_1\exp(b_1T)+a_2\exp(b_2T)$ in place of a constant $\sigma_i$ (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-parameters}).
A single-sphere ion has the temperature-independent diameter $d_i=0.88\sigma_i$.

% P:theory-contributions-03
The unlike-pair diameter is $\sigma_{ij}=(\sigma_i+\sigma_j)/2$, and the pair energy is
\begin{equation}
    u_{ij}=\sqrt{u_iu_j}\,\left(1-k_{ij}\right).
    \label{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}
\end{equation}
Dispersion between ions of the same charge sign, including a single-sphere ion with itself, is excluded ($u_{ij}=0$), while ion--solvent and cation--anion pairs keep \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule} \cite{Figiel2025}; the two chain cations keep the dispersion between their own segments, a choice of this model.
A temperature-dependent interaction is linear in reciprocal temperature,
\begin{equation}
 k_{ij}(T)=k_{ij}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})+b_{ij}(T^{-1}-T_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}),
 \qquad T_{\mathrm{ref}}=\SI{313.15}{\kelvin},
 \label{eq:kij-temperature}
\end{equation}
except the MDEA--water interaction, which is linear in temperature, $k_{ij}(T)=k_{ij}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})+(\mathrm{d}k_{ij}/\mathrm{d}T)(T-T_{\mathrm{ref}})$.

% P:theory-contributions-04
Only donor--acceptor pairs bond, and cross-association between an amine species and water uses the Wolbach--Sandler rule \cite{Kleiner2007}.
The Debye--H\"uckel term describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$.

% P:theory-04
The Born term has the solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation form of Figiel et al.\ \cite{Figiel2025}, with its Eq.~7 as corrected in 2026 \cite{Figiel2026Correction}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{Born}}
    =
    -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0k_BT}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}x_iz_i^2
    \left[
        \frac{1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{-1}}{D_i}
        +c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}\left(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}^{-1}\right)
        \left(\frac{1}{d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}}-\frac{1}{D_i}\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:born-ssm-ds}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    D_i=d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\left[1+c_{\mathrm{shell}}\frac{f_{\mathrm{mix}}-1}{|z_i|}\right],
    \qquad
    f_{\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_kx_kf_k}{\sum_kx_k}.
    \label{eq:born-shell-diameter}
\end{equation}
Here \(d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\) is the Born diameter, \(D_i\) is the outer diameter of the first solvation shell, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=8\) is the permittivity of the region between them, and \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}=c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}=1\).
The solvation factor is \(f_k=1.5\) for water \cite{Figiel2025} and 1 for every other component.
Figiel et al.\ restrict the average in \cref{eq:born-shell-diameter} to ions and solvents; here it runs over all components, so \(f_{\mathrm{mix}}=1+0.5\,x_{\HtwoO}\) and the shell diameter changes with loading.

% P:theory-05
The bulk permittivity has the Schreckenberg form reported by Yang et al.\ \cite{Yang2024}:
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}=1+\frac{n_s}{V}d_{V,\mathrm{mix}}
 \left(\frac{d_{T,\mathrm{mix}}}{T}-1\right),\qquad
 d_{q,\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}n_i d_{q,i}}{n_s},
 \quad q\in\{V,T\},
 \label{eq:solvent-permittivity}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the phase volume, the neutral set $\mathcal S$ comprises water, the molecular amine and molecular \COtwo, and $n_s=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}n_i$.
Ions affect the permittivity only through the phase volume and the neutral amounts.

\subsection{Species and shared parameters}\label{sec:species}
% P:theory-shared
Six species are common to the four amines: \COtwo, water and the four inorganic ions.
Their parameters, their interactions with water and the equilibrium-constant correlations of their reactions (R1--R3, \cref{sec:reactions}) are determined once, with MEA, and are held fixed for DEA, MDEA and AMP; \cref{tab:species-parameters,tab:interaction-matrix} mark them.
Interactions of an amine species with a shared species are amine-specific.
Water has one donor and one acceptor association site, with parameters fitted to the IAPWS-95 reference equation \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}, and \COtwo has one induced-association site of each type that bonds only with water \cite{Kleiner2007,Schick2023,Pabsch2020}.

% P:theory-amine-species
MEA and AMP are two-site chains and DEA and MDEA four-site chains; the MEA, DEA and MDEA parameters are taken from the literature, and the five pure-component parameters of AMP were fitted here to vapor pressures and liquid densities (\cref{tab:species-parameters,tab:transfer-species}, Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-transfer-data}).
No amine species bonds with \COtwo.
The MEA and AMP cations and the three carbamates are single spheres; the DEA and MDEA cations keep the segment count and association sites of their amine, with zero self-association energy.
For every amine ion the Debye--H\"uckel and Born diameters equal the segment diameter, so no amine ion has a separately fitted Born diameter.
These diameters define the parameterization and are not measured ion sizes.

% P:theory-permittivity-inputs
The permittivity coefficients of each amine were fitted beforehand to measured permittivities without \COtwo: for MEA those of the aqueous solution at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} \cite{agieienkoWhatGasStream2024a}, and for DEA, MDEA and AMP those of Hsieh et al.\ \cite{hsiehDielectricConstantsAqueous2007}.
The water coefficients are those of Yang et al.\ \cite{Yang2024} and the \COtwo coefficients are set in this work; both are fixed.
Use of \cref{eq:solvent-permittivity} at other temperatures and in loaded solutions is a model assumption for all four amines.

\subsection{Reactions and phase equilibrium}\label{sec:reactions}
% P:theory-06
A calculation starts from an apparent feed of \SI{1}{\mole} of amine, the water amount given by the source composition and \(\alpha\)~\si{\mole} of \COtwo, where \(\alpha\) is the loading.
The liquid conserves the elements of this feed, which implies electroneutrality and leaves the independent reactions of \cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants}: R1--R3 for the shared species, carbamate hydrolysis (R4, absent for MDEA) and amine dissociation (R5).
Bubble states equate the chemical potentials of \COtwo, water and the molecular amine between the phases, and every reacting liquid satisfies, for each reaction \(r\),
\begin{equation}
    \sum_i\nu_{ri}\ln a_i=\ln K_r(T).
    \label{eq:activity-reaction-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
With the fugacity coefficient $\ln\phi_i=\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}/(RT)-\ln Z$ and compressibility factor $Z=P/(\rho RT)$, the activities are
\begin{equation}
 a_w=x_w\frac{\phi_w(T,P,\boldsymbol x)P}{\phi_w^{\mathrm{pure}}(T,P^\circ)P^\circ},\qquad
 a_i=\frac{x_i}{M_wm^\circ}\frac{\phi_i(T,P,\boldsymbol x)P}{\phi_i^{\infty,w}(T,P^\circ)P^\circ}\quad(i\ne w),
 \label{eq:activities}
\end{equation}
where $M_w$ is the molar mass of water and $m^\circ=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram}$.
The infinite-dilution reference is evaluated along a charge-neutral dilution path in pure water; only the charge-neutral combinations of species that appear in the reactions require it.
Every constant is applied on one aqueous-molality basis (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-parameters}).

% P:theory-07
The correlations of R1--R3 and of MEA have the form \cite{Austgen1991}
\begin{equation}
 \ln K_r=A_r+B_r/T+C_r\ln T+D_rT+\Delta_r,
 \label{eq:reaction-equilibrium-constant-temperature}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_r$ converts the source to the common basis; Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-transfer-parameters} gives those of the other amines in an equivalent form.
R1--R3 and the R4 and R5 correlations of MEA are fixed at their literature forms, and so is R5 of DEA, MDEA and AMP.
The R4 correlations of DEA and AMP start from a literature correlation and are then fitted: the fit adjusts the constant term and an enthalpy increment $\delta h$ that changes the temperature dependence through
\begin{equation}
 \delta\ln K(T)=-\frac{\delta h}{R}\left(\frac1T-\frac1{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right).
 \label{eq:reaction-shift}
\end{equation}
\Cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants} gives the source and the measured temperature range of each constant.

\subsection{Enthalpy, heat capacity, density and excess enthalpy}\label{sec:caloric-model}
% P:theory-caloric-01
Total enthalpy follows the same Helmholtz energy and reaction standard states as the equilibrium calculation:
\begin{equation}
 H=PV-RT^2\left(\frac{\partial(A/RT)}{\partial T}\right)_{V,\boldsymbol n},\qquad
 A=A^{\mathrm{ig}}+A^{\mathrm{res}}.
 \label{eq:total-enthalpy}
\end{equation}
Neutral ideal-gas functions supply the caloric reference, and the ionic functions follow from the equilibrium-constant correlations through $\Delta_rh^\circ=RT^2\,\mathrm{d}\ln K_r/\mathrm{d}T$; no separate reaction-enthalpy term is added (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-files}).
Equilibrium heat capacity is $C_P=(\mathrm{d}H_{\mathrm{eq}}/\mathrm{d}T)_{P,\boldsymbol n_{\mathrm{feed}}}$, including the change in speciation.
Density is the mass of the apparent components divided by the equilibrium liquid volume, without volume translation, and a binary excess enthalpy subtracts the mole-fraction-weighted pure-liquid enthalpies from the mixture molar enthalpy.
