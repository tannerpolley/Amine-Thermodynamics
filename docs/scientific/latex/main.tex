\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
Can two calibrations of an equation of state that reproduce the measured pressures of aqueous amines loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo) about equally well predict different liquid phases?
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model of monoethanolamine (MEA) is calibrated first, and its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters are held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.
Phase-stability searches separate calibrations that the pressure deviations do not.
For AMP, two fits deviate by 10.3 and 10.5\,\% from the same fitted total pressures, yet the first gives 73 calculated liquid--liquid splits above \SI{353}{\kelvin}, the highest temperature of the fitted loaded-solution pressures, and the second none.
For MDEA, changing one shared MEA interaction lowers the deviation of the fitted total pressures from 20.1 to 11.2\,\% but raises that of the \COtwo partial pressures outside the fit from 20.1 to 33.6\,\%, and its searches confirm two calculated splits at states where none was found with the original value.
The MEA and DEA models have no detected instability, and DEA is a negative result: with the adopted DEA pure-component and association parameters, no tested water--DEA interaction represents the binary excess enthalpies and bubble pressures together.
The four models and their phase searches are offered as a testbed for calibration practice, in which a fit is judged by the phases it predicts as well as by its deviations, rather than as a basis for absorber design.

\end{abstract}
