Dear Editors of Fluid Phase Equilibria,

Please consider “Modeling CO₂ solubility in aqueous alkanolamine solutions with ePC-SAFT” as a research article.

The paper reports an ePC-SAFT model of CO₂-loaded aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) and tests how far its shared parameters carry to three further amines. The MEA model is calibrated first, with its reaction equilibrium constants fixed at literature correlations; its water, CO₂ and inorganic-ion parameters are then held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted, and every fitted model is searched for phase instability.

What is new:

- **Four models with one set of shared parameters.** The fitted CO₂ partial pressures deviate by 11.34 % for MEA and 42.75 % for DEA, and the fitted total pressures by 4.39 % for MDEA and 10.82 % for AMP, with every reaction reference at 100 kPa so that equilibrium, density and enthalpy follow from one Gibbs energy; the MDEA fit is provisional. Transfer here means that the further amines could be fitted with the shared parameters held fixed, not that MEA determines those parameters. For DEA and AMP the carbamate-hydrolysis constant is fitted, MDEA needed its loaded-solution pressures weighted against its many density and heat data, and DEA is reported with a negative result: no tested water–DEA interaction represents the binary excess enthalpies and bubble pressures together.
- **Shared water–anion interactions fixed without amine data.** The water–bicarbonate and water–carbonate interactions are fixed from osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts. They are adopted for that reason, not because the amine data are fitted better; with the 100 kPa references, the MEA-fitted alternative fits none of three compared MDEA sets better, as an endpoint sensitivity.
- **Phase stability as a check on extrapolation and failure.** The searches detect no instability for MEA or at 666 AMP states scaled to the bubble pressure and confirm no split for MDEA. For DEA they confirm splits at four states whose densities the single-liquid calculation matches, so the model's own phase calculation contradicts the single-liquid treatment of those samples. A first AMP fit, whose loaded-solution pressures ended at 353 K, gave calculated splits at 363–393 K; a refit that added 18 Hartono pressures at 373–393 K and 40 Tong pressures at 313–393 K confirms none; at the six measured-pressure controls, where the first fit splits, none has a confirmed second liquid: four descents point toward a vapor below the refit's bubble pressure, and two controls have no descent.

The work fits the journal's scope in equations of state for electrolyte and reacting systems, chemical and phase equilibrium, and phase-stability analysis. It builds on ePC-SAFT studies of aqueous amines and electrolytes published in Fluid Phase Equilibria. The MEA model is compared with the author's earlier reactive ePC-SAFT model of MEA (Polley and Hedengren, Next Chemical Engineering 3 (2026) 100132), whose parameters were estimated in separate steps.

The limits are gathered in one section: pressures at 15 and 45 wt% MEA, carbonate shares and high-temperature absorption heats remain inaccurate, the calculated phase behaviors are not established by the measurements, and the models, calibrated against data examined during development, are not a basis for absorber design.
