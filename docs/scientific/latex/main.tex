\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
An equation of state can reproduce the measured pressures of a solvent and still predict a liquid--liquid split that no measurement supports.
Here a phase-stability search is used to find where such a calibration lacks data.
Electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) models of aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA), diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo) share their water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters, and each amine is fitted with its own species and reactions.
A first AMP fit used loaded-solution pressures only up to \SI{353}{\kelvin}.
A tangent-plane search with electroneutral, reaction-constrained trial phases found liquid--liquid splits in it at 363--\SI{393}{\kelvin}, at 73 of 666 searched states, where total pressures had been measured but not fitted.
With those pressures in the fit, no search detects an instability, and the reported AMP model deviates by 10.9\,\% from its 105 fitted total pressures.
The calculated splits thus marked the temperatures at which the fit lacked data; no measurement used here shows whether loaded AMP solutions form one liquid or two there.
The shared water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fixed from osmotic coefficients of sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate solutions, because the MEA data do not determine them.
Compared with a shared set fitted to MEA data, this lowers the deviation of the fitted MDEA total pressures from 20.1 to 10.1\,\% but raises that of 101 unfitted \COtwo partial pressures over MDEA from 17.9 to 38.4\,\%, and the fitted DEA pressures and the unfitted MEA pressures also deviate more.
The four models are calibrations for studying fitting practice, not a basis for absorber design.

\end{abstract}
