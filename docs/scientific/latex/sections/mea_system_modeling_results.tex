% P:results-shared-choice
The MEA data do not determine the shared water--bicarbonate interaction.
Fitted as an eleventh MEA parameter, it is 0.765 with a conditional standard error of 3.18 and conditional correlations of 0.960 and $-0.974$ with the carbamate--water and the \MEAH--water interactions.
Fixed at 0.1325 or at its upper bound, 1.0, with the other ten parameters refitted, it raises the objective by 1.023 and 0.024, both inside the reference change of 2.24 (\cref{sec:parameters}); only at $-0.5$, with a rise of 15.89, is a value excluded (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-uncertainty}).
A parameter that an MEA fit leaves free between about 0.13 and 1.0 cannot be passed to another amine as a property of water and bicarbonate, and \cref{sec:results-mdea} shows that MDEA is sensitive to it.
The reported shared set therefore takes this interaction and the water--carbonate interaction from the sodium-salt data (\cref{sec:species}), and the MEA fit adjusts the \MEAH--bicarbonate interaction in its place.

% P:results-parameter-interpretation
With the salt-fitted laws the MEA objective is 152.96, against 150.76 for the MEA-fitted shared set on the same 307 observations and weights, and the conditional standard errors of the carbamate--water and \MEAH--water interactions fall from 0.55 and 0.62 to 0.11 and 0.12.
Two of the 11 parameters are on bounds: the \MEAH--\MEACOO interaction on its lower bound, where it doubles the unlike-pair dispersion energy under \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}, and the \MEAH--bicarbonate interaction on its upper bound, where that energy vanishes.
Refits that hold either one up to a tenth of its range inside the bound raise the objective by at most 0.75 (Table~\ref{supp-tab:bound-profiles}), so neither bound is sharply required.
The MEA--water interaction has a small conditional standard error (\cref{tab:interaction-matrix}), and the calculated bubble temperatures follow both isobars of Cai et al.\ \cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996} with an RMSE of \SI{1.783}{\kelvin} (Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:mea-water}).
The diameters of the two MEA ions have a conditional correlation of $-0.997$ and are not separately identified, so the fitted ion parameters are effective values, not molecular properties.
Propagated to the 47 fitted \COtwo partial pressures, the parameter covariance gives relative standard errors of 5.9--17.0\,\%, of the same size as the deviations themselves.

% P:results-phase-opening
For loaded aqueous MEA, B\"ottinger et al.\ observe a homogeneous liquid in their measured subset \cite{Bottinger2008}.
The MEA model shows no detected instability at any of the 2484 liquid states or 162 liquid--vapor states searched, and every one of the 2646 searches finishes; the same holds for the MEA-fitted shared set.

% P:results-restrictions
The restrictions that define the MEA model cost accuracy outside the fit.
From the 16-parameter calibration to the 11-parameter form, the number of parameters on bounds falls from six to one and the fitted pressure and density deviations stay nearly unchanged, but the deviation of the 19 \COtwo partial pressures of Ma'mun et al.\ \cite{Mamun2005} near \SI{393}{\kelvin}, which no fit includes, rises from 19.4 to 33.5\,\%; with the salt-fitted interactions it is 38.4\,\% (\cref{tab:published-models}, Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-restrictions}).

\subsubsection{Pressure and speciation at 30 wt\%}\label{sec:results-pressure}
% P:results-pressure-01
At nominal 30 wt\% MEA the bubble curves follow the measured \COtwo partial pressures over the logarithmic pressure range, while differences between sources remain visible (\cref{fig:pressure}).
The 47 fitted pressures, at 313--\SI{333}{\kelvin}, deviate by 11.3\,\%; the 112 pressures of the same six sources that are not fitted deviate by 27.8\,\%, against 24.9\,\% with the MEA-fitted shared set (\cref{tab:mea-data}, Table~\ref{supp-tab:pressure-sources}).

% P:results-species-01
The species curves show molecular MEA being replaced by protonated MEA and carbamate as loading increases, followed by increasing bicarbonate (\cref{fig:speciation}).
The carbonate share of dissolved carbon remains far below the ten observations of Jakobsen et al.\ \cite{Jakobsen2005}, with an AARD of 89.9\,\% and a geometric mean of the calculated-to-observed ratios of 0.070 (\cref{fig:jakobsen-carbonate-share}); with the MEA-fitted shared set the ratio is 0.032.
Jakobsen et al.\ obtain the carbonate share from the chemical shift of the merged bicarbonate--carbonate \ce{^{13}C} signal and state that their carbonate is most likely overpredicted \cite{Jakobsen2005}, which limits the accuracy of these shares.
The salt-fitted interactions thus halve the deficit without removing it, and none of the electrostatic changes tested removes it either (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-carbonate}).
With the MEA-fitted shared set the calculated pH lay 0.6--0.8 below electrode measurements, and a pH shift of that size recovers only part of the missing carbonate, so the deficit is not explained by the proton activity alone (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-carbonate}).
Its cause is unresolved, and the calculated carbonate concentrations should not be used.

\subsubsection{Caloric properties, density and other concentrations}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The integral heats of Vinjarapu et al.\ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} are fitted and deviate by 2.0\,\% \cite{HemaBhavya2025}; those of Arcis et al.\ at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin} are development data, and the calculated absorption there is less exothermic than measured (\cref{fig:heat}) \cite{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
The semi-differential heats of Kim and Svendsen deviate most at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, with an RMSE of \SI{32.51}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} of \COtwo over all 20 intervals, including the four flagged for source problems (Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-mea-comparison}).
Extrapolated equilibrium constants, pressure histories that had to be approximated and chemistry missing from the model may all contribute, and caloric accuracy at high temperature is not demonstrated.
The heat capacities of Weiland et al.\ \cite{weilandHeatCapacityAqueous1997} and of Concepci\'on et al.\ \cite{concepcionHeatCapacitiesDifferent2023}, which are not fitted, deviate by 5.0 and 2.2\,\%, and without volume translation the fitted loaded densities deviate by 0.15\,\% (Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:density-curves}).

% P:results-transfer
At 15 and 45 wt\% MEA, where no pressure is fitted and no concentration-specific parameter is estimated, the pressures of Aronu et al.\ deviate by 44.2 and 47.0\,\%, against 31.6\,\% for their pressures at 30 wt\%, which are not fitted either (Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:concentration-pressure}, \cref{tab:mea-data}).
Aronu et al.\ fitted an extended UNIQUAC model to their own data at 15--60 wt\% and report 24.3\,\% for all their \COtwo partial pressures together \cite{Aronu2011}, so the concentration dependence is a weakness of the present calibration.
