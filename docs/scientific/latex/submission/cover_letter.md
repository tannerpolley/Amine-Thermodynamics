Dear Editors of Fluid Phase Equilibria,

Please consider “Phase-stability searches as a data diagnostic for ePC-SAFT models of CO₂-loaded aqueous amines” as a research article.

The paper uses a phase-stability search to find where a fitted electrolyte equation of state lacks data. ePC-SAFT models of CO₂-loaded aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA), diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) share their water, CO₂ and inorganic-ion parameters, and each is searched for calculated liquid–liquid splits with electroneutral, reaction-constrained trial phases.

What is new:

- **A calculated split that marked missing data.** A first AMP fit used loaded-solution pressures only up to 353 K. The search found liquid–liquid splits in it at 363–393 K, at 73 of 666 searched states, where total pressures had been measured but not fitted. With those pressures in the fit no search detects an instability, and the reported AMP model deviates by 10.9 % from its 105 fitted total pressures. No measurement used here shows whether loaded AMP solutions form one liquid or two.
- **Shared ion interactions fixed from salt data, with the cost stated.** The MEA data do not determine the shared water–bicarbonate interaction, so it and the water–carbonate interaction are fixed from osmotic coefficients of sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate solutions. Compared with fitting them to MEA data, this lowers the deviation of the 66 fitted MDEA total pressures from 20.1 to 10.1 % and raises that of 101 unfitted CO₂ partial pressures over MDEA from 17.9 to 38.4 %; the 30 fitted DEA pressures deviate by 36.6 % instead of 28.5 %.
- **Calculated MDEA splits that depend on the shared interaction and the weights.** With the MEA-fitted shared set two calculated splits appear or disappear with the value of the water–bicarbonate interaction and with the weights of the objective, so they are not a property of the solvent. DEA is reported as a negative result: with the adopted DEA pure-component and association parameters, no tested water–DEA interaction represents the binary excess enthalpies and bubble pressures together.

The work fits the journal's scope in equations of state for electrolyte and reacting systems, chemical and phase equilibrium, and phase-stability analysis. It builds on ePC-SAFT studies of aqueous amines and electrolytes published in Fluid Phase Equilibria and on stability-constrained parameter estimation for non-reacting mixtures. The MEA model is compared with the author's earlier reactive ePC-SAFT model of MEA (Polley and Hedengren, Next Chemical Engineering 3 (2026) 100132), whose parameters were estimated in separate steps.

The four models are calibrations for studying fitting practice. The limits are gathered in one section: pressures at 15 and 45 wt% MEA, carbonate shares and high-temperature absorption heats remain inaccurate, each fit was started from a few points without a global search, the calculated phase behaviors are not established by the measurements, and the models, calibrated against data examined during development, are not a basis for absorber design.
