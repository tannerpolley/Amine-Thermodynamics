% P:results-water-01
Five water parameters fitted to IAPWS-95 at 20 temperatures from 298.15 to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} give mean absolute relative deviations of 0.0363\,\% for saturated-liquid density, 0.0754\,\% for saturation pressure and 0.439\,\% for heat capacity at \SI{0.3}{\mega\pascal} \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}.
At 19 interleaved temperatures not fitted, the corresponding deviations are 0.0343, 0.0700 and 0.415\,\% (Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:water-iapws}).
These comparisons test reproduction of the reference equation rather than independent measurements; Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-parameters} gives the diameter law and fitting conditions.



% P:results-co2-water-01
The \COtwo--water interaction and its temperature slope were fitted to 39 Kiepe bubble pressures at 313.2--\SI{393.2}{\kelvin} and up to \SI{9.3}{\mega\pascal}, then held fixed \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002}.
The AARD is 2.67\,\% and mean signed deviation $+0.33$\,\% (\cref{fig:co2-water}); the largest deviation is $-11.3$\,\% at \SI{393.2}{\kelvin}.
These pressures remain as checks on the fixed inputs in the joint objective.

\subsection{Fitted parameters and their interpretation}\label{sec:results-joint}
% P:results-joint-01
Tables~\ref{tab:species-parameters} and \ref{tab:interaction-matrix} give the final parameter set, with fitted quantities and fixed inputs identified by source; reaction coefficients are in \cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants}.
\Cref{tab:residual-summary} gives the deviations of each fitted data group at the final parameter set.
Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-fitted-parameters} gives their bounds and conditional standard errors.

% P:results-parameter-interpretation
Six parameters reach bounds: the \MEAH--water, bicarbonate--water and \MEAH--\MEACOO interactions, both fitted Born diameters and the R5 enthalpy shift.
Both Born diameters are \SI{5}{\angstrom}; the \MEAH segment diameter is \SI{4.90455}{\angstrom}, inside the original wide interval.
The unlike-ion interaction $k_{ij}=-1$ doubles the unlike-ion dispersion energy in \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}.
Three free coordinates have conditional standard errors larger than their absolute values: the carbamate--water interaction, \MEAH--water temperature slope and R2 enthalpy shift.
The largest one-standard-error fractional sensitivities among the retained predictions are 2.094 for \MEAH dispersion energy and 1.868 for its segment diameter, chiefly affecting pressure and bicarbonate.
These are local derivatives multiplied by marginal errors, not nonlinear displaced predictions.
Including correlations gives pressure standard errors of 5.41--16.00\,\% at the 47 retained pressure states, 313--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin}.
The covariance is conditional on the model, source inputs, weights and six fixed bound coordinates; it excludes model discrepancy and future measurement noise.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-uncertainty} reports the inward profiles, sensitivity ranking and weak parameter directions.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\COtwo}{CO2} solubility}\label{sec:results-pressure}
% P:results-pressure-01
\Cref{fig:pressure} compares calculated \COtwo partial pressures with measurements from six sources at 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin}.
The AARD is 11.79\,\% for 47 fitted and 23.82\,\% for 112 non-fitted pressures (20.27\,\% overall).
By source, the 159-pressure comparison gives 9.06\,\% for Hilliard (31 observations), 19.43\,\% for Ma'mun (19), 18.84\,\% for Jou (48), 21.10\,\% for Idris (10), 25.91\,\% for Xu (15) and 29.68\,\% for Aronu (36).
The deviation panel exposes discrepancies that are less apparent on the logarithmic pressure axis.
At Aronu's non-fitted \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} observations with analytical loading 0.46--0.52, the calculated \COtwo partial pressures are 46--70\,\% below the measured values.
The sources disagree there: at loading 0.489, Aronu reports \SI{2.86}{\kilo\pascal}, whereas Hilliard reports \SI{1.10}{\kilo\pascal} at loading 0.491; the model gives \SI{1.17}{\kilo\pascal} at Aronu's state.
Hilliard and Jou pressures at \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} and loading 0.46--0.52 lie within 24\,\% of the model.

% P:results-pressure-02
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, the 54 six-source pressures give AARD 23.67\,\%, with bias direction differing between sources.
The 23 additional Wagner pressures give AARDs of 23.72\,\% near \SI{353}{\kelvin} and 9.22\,\% near \SI{392}{\kelvin} (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-outside}).
For 33 Aronu states at 15 wt\% and 37 at 45 wt\%, pressure AARDs are 33.89 and 44.53\,\%, respectively.
Their mean logarithmic biases are $-0.00215$ and $-0.0514$; the large absolute deviations therefore do not represent a uniform signed offset.
These are fixed-parameter concentration-transfer errors, reported without a new fit-quality threshold.

% P:results-species-conditions
The curves in \cref{fig:speciation} use \SI{101325}{\pascal}, nominal 30 wt\% MEA and 31 wt\% at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}; the calculated bubble pressure exceeds the imposed pressure above loading 0.51 at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and 0.59 at \SI{333.15}{\kelvin}.
The curves extend to loading 0.6, while observations end at 0.53.
Mole fractions use total true-species liquid amount; the amine sum and bicarbonate-labeled observations follow the aggregate definitions in Section~\ref{sec:data-methods}, with the \SI{21}{\degreeCelsius} titration series approximated at \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius}.
Matin's free MEA is compared in Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:species-parity}; at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, the largest retained mole fractions of carbonate and dissolved \COtwo, which are not plotted separately in panel (a), are $1.0\times10^{-5}$ and $3.7\times10^{-4}$, respectively.

% P:results-species-01
The speciation curves show conversion of molecular MEA into protonated amine and carbamate as loading increases, followed by increasing bicarbonate (\cref{fig:speciation}).
The combined \ce{MEA + MEAH+} observable gives AARD 2.31\,\% over 25 compared observations, of which 20 were fitted; the 94 species fractions in the pressure--species--heat objective group (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-objective}) give 7.11\,\%; \cref{tab:residual-summary} lists the other seven fitted species fractions separately.
Agreement with this amine sum does not independently test the separate molecular and protonated fractions.
The five non-fitted B\"ottinger states at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and 31 wt\% test the model above the fitted species temperatures: the amine sum agrees within 1.3\,\% up to loading 0.25 but is 6.3 and 14.3\,\% low at loadings 0.34 and 0.40, where calculated carbamate is 9 and 26\,\% high.
The ten non-fitted Jakobsen carbonate shares, which differ from the six fitted Jakobsen ratios, are also low: calculated/observed share has mean 0.0213 and range 0.00195--0.0749 (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-density}).
Those calculations account for dilution of the NMR samples and use their NMR loadings \cite[Sample preparation, Tables~A1--A2]{Jakobsen2005}; Jakobsen's warning about quantifying minor components is discussed by Matin et al.\ \cite[p.~6616]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.

% P:results-species-02
The 19 non-fitted Matin bicarbonate + carbonate totals give AARD 67.77\,\% and mean logarithmic bias $-1.285$.
Matin's other titration species agree more closely, with AARDs of 7.71\,\% for \MEAH and 8.13\,\% for \MEACOO, although free \MEA is 16--35\,\% low above loading 0.4.
Matin et al.\ found their inferred bicarbonate values higher than the NMR values \cite[pp.~6616--6617]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}, so the discrepancy may partly reflect disagreement between sources as well as limitations of the model.
A good pressure fit therefore does not ensure accurate bicarbonate and carbonate fractions.

\subsubsection{Absorption heat and heat capacity}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The fitted Vinjarapu integral heats give RMSE \SI{1.84}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} (\cref{fig:heat}).
For Arcis at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}, the mean calculated-minus-measured differences are \SI{+10.18}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} and \SI{+3.12}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}: the calculated absorption is less exothermic.
Their pressure-separated RMSEs are 10.30 and \SI{3.34}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, respectively.
The model's pressure effect is small; increasing pressure between those two series changes the calculated heat by about \SI{0.19}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at the same loading.

% P:results-heat-02
The Arcis measurements themselves differ between pressure series: heats at nearby loadings 0.181 and 0.183 are $-92.6$ and $-85.9$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, and at loadings 0.391 and 0.405 are $-94.4$ and $-85.5$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \cite[Table~5, pp.~3355--3356]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
These are not exact matched-loading comparisons, and both temperature and pressure differ from Vinjarapu's conditions.
The available data do not isolate a cause for the discrepancy.

% P:results-heat-kim-01
Kim and Svendsen's calorimetry extends the heat comparison to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}: their nominal 30 wt\% MEA measurements at 313.15, 353.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} are compared with fixed-parameter calculations on a \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} isobar for loadings $\alpha\leq0.60$ (Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:kim-heat-temperature}) \cite[Experimental and Results, pp.~5803--5807, Tables~1--3]{Kim2007}.
Their semi-differential heats average each finite \COtwo addition, so the calculation uses the equilibrium-liquid enthalpies at both ends of each interval and the pure-\COtwo feed enthalpy at the same temperature and pressure; these heats are per mole of absorbed \COtwo, not the cumulative heat per mole of MEA.
At 313.15 and \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, the finite-interval RMSEs are 4.810 and \SI{7.535}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} absorbed \COtwo, with AARDs 4.097 and 6.896\,\% over 27 and 26 observations.
At \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} the deviation is much larger: RMSE \SI{25.204}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} and AARD 18.392\,\% over 18 observations.
The calculated heat release there is below the measured value at every scored interval except the highest-loading run~1 interval.
This comparison omits the run~2 rows at loadings 0.078 and 0.445, whose printed heat columns disagree with each other.
Including them gives RMSE \SI{29.227}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} and AARD 55.680\,\% over 20 observations; also omitting the first interval of each run, whose heat lies far above the rest of that run, gives \SI{15.787}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} and 15.851\,\% over 16 observations.
Even with both exclusions, the \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} deviation remains larger than at the two lower temperatures, so source anomalies explain only part of it.

% P:results-heat-kim-02
Kim reports two runs at each temperature, so each \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} run can be compared with each \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} run at common loadings.
In three of these four run pairs, the measured heat at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} exceeds that at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} at every compared loading below 0.60.
In the fourth pair, \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} run~1 with \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} run~2, the \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} heat falls below the \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} heat and then rises above it again; the calculated difference for this pair does not change sign, so the model misses both reversals.
Taking the sum of the two runs' $\pm2.2$\,\% bounds as the uncertainty of their difference, the differences on both sides of the upward reversal exceed it, whereas the downward reversal starts from a difference within it.
Kim's measurements therefore show no crossover of the 313.15 and \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} heats common to all run pairs.
The model's limiting differential heat at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} falls below its \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} value near loading 0.560; because finite-interval heats average over unequal loading steps, this crossing is a different quantity from the crossings of the measured interval heats.

% P:results-heat-kim-03
None of these heat comparisons is blind validation: the Arcis data informed parameter selection, Kim intervals entered an earlier fit, and the \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} Kim data were previously inspected.
Kim's pressure histories and exact addition doses are unavailable, so the \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} isobar and rounded loading endpoints approximate the measured intervals; R4 and R5 are extrapolated above their \SI{323.15}{\kelvin} source limit, and the model omits 2-oxazolidone.
The Kim deviations grow with temperature; the comparisons extend the heat check to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} but do not qualify temperature-dependent heat predictions for absorber or stripper use.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-literature-01
Zhang et al.\ provide the closest multi-property comparison, with 12 ternary coefficients fitted to solubility, differential heat, heat capacity and NMR \cite[Sect.~3.3, Tables~9--11]{Zhang2011}.
Their fitted pressure deviations range from 13.5 to 35.5\,\% by source; the present values are 11.79\,\% for 47 fitted and 23.82\,\% for 112 non-fitted pressures (20.27\,\% overall).
Najafloo and Zarei report 34.71\,\% and Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh report 36.42\,\%, each over 691 ternary comparisons after pure/binary estimation \cite[Table~5]{Najafloo2018,Baygi2015}.
Their data sets, fitting roles and domains differ: Zhang's differential-heat data span 313--393 K and differ from the integral heat assessed here, and the heat-capacity fit includes several solvent concentrations \cite[Table~9]{Zhang2011}.
Here integral heat is fitted at one temperature and heat capacity at four states, while loaded density is included in the joint fit.
Neumann et al.'s ternary density comparisons instead assess predictions after VLE fitting \cite[Sect.~4.2]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
\Cref{tab:literature-model-comparison} gives the quantities and data roles for these and other MEA models; the different observation sets prevent a common-data accuracy ranking.
Polley and Hedengren already model explicit MEA ions; the present joint density and caloric fit and phase-search comparison distinguish this work from their staged calibration and empirical column enthalpies \cite[App.~B]{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}.

% P:results-literature-hong
Hong et al.\ report 1.53\,\% pressure AARD against 90 eNRTL-reference states after fitting a weight at each state, 5.51\,\% AARD for the carbamate-pathway fraction against eNRTL over loadings 0.1--0.7 mol \COtwo/mol MEA, and 9.48\,\% absorption-heat deviation against experiment \cite[Sects.~2.3, 3.2, Table~3, Figs.~5--6]{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.
Their state-specific calibration and model-reference pressures differ from the present experimental comparisons and fixed parameter set.

\subsection{Phase stability}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:results-phase-04
Instability is detected for the historical unstable fit at 54 states, whereas the final parameter set has no additional liquid detected at the 162 searched states (\cref{tab:two-fit-comparison,tab:phase-search-summary}).
Similar property agreement accompanies these different phase predictions (\cref{tab:two-fit-comparison}).
Separately seeded calculations confirm two liquids at 32 of the 54 detected-instability states, at every searched temperature; water mole fractions are 0.40--0.74 in the water-poor liquid and 0.68--0.97 in the water-rich one (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-phase}).

% P:results-phase-01
The selected fit instead enforces a native tangent-plane stability requirement during optimization within the original wide bounds.
Its observation cost is 148.5105, compared with 148.8833 for the outgoing restricted fit on the same inputs and Engine wheel, while fitted loaded densities within $2s$ increase from 44 to 46.
Fresh searches resolve no descent at 1903 enforced and 576 offset states, as well as the 162 census states.
All five previously excluded states are available without resolved descent.
Thus the earlier diameter restriction and its property trade-off are unnecessary for these retained states; finite searches do not establish global constrained optimality.
