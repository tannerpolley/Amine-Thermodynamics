\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% P:conclusion-01
Electrolyte PC-SAFT with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions and explicit carbamate chemistry describes \COtwo partial pressure and liquid speciation in 30 wt\% aqueous \MEA with one set of liquid-phase parameters.
Fitted through five binary interaction parameters to 47 \COtwo pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and 94 species measurements at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, the nine-species model reaches AARDs of 13.98 and 8.26\,\%.
Outside the fit, it gives 19.58\,\% on the 21 measured pressures at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, and 22.50 and 22.27\,\% on 11 and 12 Wagner et al.\ pressures near 353 and \SI{392}{\kelvin} that entered no fit; because \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} data informed fixed reaction shifts, these are not independent temperature predictions.
This extends the ePC-SAFT treatment of reactive MDEA to a carbamate-forming primary amine.

% P:conclusion-02
Within the declared bounds, the tested refits without a Born term do not recover agreement: the fitted-pressure AARD rises from 13.98 to 53.60\,\%.
A decomposition of the calculated pressure shows why within this model: the ion-concentration part of the Born contribution lowers the activity sum that sets the \COtwo pressure increasingly with loading, and the refitted interactions offset its removal at low loading but not at high loading.
The SSM+DS and original Born formulations calibrate comparably, and which has the lower cost depends on the fitted observations: SSM+DS without the titration bicarbonate pool, 33.03 against 34.14, and original Born with it, 39.70 against 47.63.

% P:conclusion-03
These results suggest three practical lessons for fitting this model.
First, a comparison of electrolyte terms must state which speciation data are fitted, because the titration bicarbonate pool alone reverses the Born-form ranking; independent solvation or activity data could guide the choice of form.
Second, some parameters are not determined by these data: the \MEAH--water slope, \SI{-112.6}{\kelvin}, has a conditional standard error of \SI{119.7}{\kelvin}, and the \HCOthree--water coefficient lies on its bound.
Third, data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} do not determine the temperature response: a refit with literature reaction temperature laws and the published \COtwo dispersion energy matches the fit at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} but gives 28.91\,\% at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} and 51.05\,\% at 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, so heat-of-absorption or equilibrium data above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} are needed to fix it.

% P:conclusion-04
The selected parameter set applies to \COtwo partial pressure and speciation in 30 wt\% \MEA.
Applied unchanged at 15 and 45 wt\% \MEA it gives AARDs of 76.71 and 46.29\,\% (\cref{sec:results-80c}), and it overpredicts loaded-solution density by 13.0--17.8\,\%.
Qualifying density and caloric properties, and adding heat-of-absorption data to the fit, are the next steps toward absorber and stripper calculations.
