# ChemRxiv submission draft

## Title

Thermodynamics of CO₂-loaded aqueous amines with ePC-SAFT: monoethanolamine calibration, transfer to DEA, MDEA and AMP, and phase stability

## Author and affiliation

Tanner W. Polley; ORCID 0009-0008-5957-4152; tpolley3@byu.edu. Department of Chemical Engineering, Brigham Young University, Provo, UT 84602, United States.

## Abstract

An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model is calibrated for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (CO₂), and its water, CO₂ and inorganic-ion parameters are then held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted. The shared water–bicarbonate and water–carbonate interactions are fixed beforehand from osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts, so that no amine data determine them. The MEA model is the benchmark and the source of the fixed carbamate energy of DEA and AMP; it has 11 fitted parameters and literature equilibrium constants, and its fitted CO₂ partial pressures at 30 wt% deviate by 11.3%, and its pressures at other concentrations and at stripper temperatures are less accurate. With the shared parameters fixed, the fitted loaded-solution pressures, CO₂ partial pressures for DEA and total pressures for MDEA and AMP, deviate by 42.71% for DEA, 4.15% for MDEA and 10.84% for AMP. Phase-stability searches of the fitted models detect no instability for MEA and AMP and one calculated liquid–liquid split for MDEA, far outside the measured conditions. For DEA they confirm liquid–liquid splits at four states where the density of one loaded solution was measured, so the DEA model fails there. A first AMP fit, whose loaded-solution pressures ended at 353 K, gave calculated splits at 363–393 K; fitting the measured pressures at those temperatures removed them. A phase-stability search is thus a useful check on where a fit has been extrapolated or fails. The models are calibrations against data examined during development, not a basis for absorber design.

## Keywords

ePC-SAFT; monoethanolamine; diethanolamine; methyldiethanolamine; 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol; parameter transfer; phase stability.

## Data availability

The electronic data comprise processed observations, parameter values, numerical results and the analysis and figure code under the MIT license; third-party measurements remain under the terms of their sources. They will be deposited in Zenodo, and the DOI will be added at submission. A software index in the deposit lists the versions, checksums and commands needed to repeat the calculations. The four reported models were calculated with version 1.3.1 of the epcsaft package, and the MEA fits with the MEA-fitted shared set with version 1.3.0. Referees will receive access to the compiled epcsaft package through the corresponding author.
