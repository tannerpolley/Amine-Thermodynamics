\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated a now-retired fixed SSM+DS modified-Born ePC-SAFT parameter set against 161 VLE pressure records and 74 NMR states for 30 wt\% aqueous \MEA.  The nine-species calculation converged at every experimental state.  For positive measured values, median absolute \(\log_{10}(\mathrm{model}/\mathrm{data})\) residuals were 0.074 for \MEA, 0.033 for \MEAH, 0.040 for \MEACOO, 0.461 for \HCOthree, 0.599 for \COthree, and 0.066 for the \ce{MEA + MEAH+} aggregate.  The historical set therefore describes the measured amine-family composition more closely than the carbonate family.

The controlled pressure comparison does not support transfer of the historical set as a predictive pressure parameterization.  On the same 31 Jou et al.\ measurements, the median absolute \(\log_{10}(P_{\mathrm{model}}/P_{\mathrm{data}})\) residual was 0.160 for the ideal-activity baseline and 0.495 for the activity calculation.  The activity treatment improved 4 records and worsened 27.  Across all 161 pressure records, its median absolute residual was 0.493.

The evidence locates three limitations.  First, the historical \MEAH and \MEACOO parameters lack a reproducible estimation history.  Second, the NMR studies directly measure \MEACOO at all 74 states but resolve \MEA and \MEAH separately at only 35 states each; the \ce{MEA + MEAH+} aggregate covers all states.  Third, carbonate-only sensitivity and the absence of independent \HthreeO and \Hydroxide observations leave the electrostatic terms weakly identified.  A predictive model requires a preregistered joint regression of pressure, measured speciation, and reaction-equilibrium constraints, followed by independent validation.  These results characterize the retired calculation but do not establish predictive accuracy or an active MEA parameter set.
