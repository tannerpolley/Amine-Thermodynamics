\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\subsection{MEA absorption and the required properties}
% P:introduction-01
Models of \COtwo absorption in aqueous monoethanolamine (\MEA) need consistent descriptions of equilibrium pressure, liquid species, heat of absorption, density and heat capacity.
Equilibrium pressure determines the gas--liquid driving force, while speciation supplies concentrations for reaction-rate expressions \cite[Sect.~1, p.~67]{Zhang2011}.
Freguia and Rochelle separate reboiler duty into desorption heat, water vaporization and sensible heating \cite[Eq.~13, Fig.~3, p.~1680]{Freguia2003}; absorption heating also affects the absorber temperature profile \cite[Sect.~5.3, Figs.~10--11]{Kvamsdal2008}.
Density and viscosity enter column sizing and mass-transfer correlations \cite[Introduction, p.~3096]{Amundsen2009}.

% P:introduction-02
MEA forms carbamate through an overall reaction consuming two amine molecules per \COtwo molecule; carbamate reversion to bicarbonate becomes important at higher \COtwo content \cite[Eqs.~1--4, pp.~1883--1884]{MacDowell2010}.
This differs from tertiary N-methyldiethanolamine (MDEA), which promotes bicarbonate formation without forming carbamate \cite[Sect.~1, p.~1467]{Vaidya2007}.
The resulting coupling of chemistry, phase equilibrium and caloric properties requires more than a pressure correlation.

\subsection{Activity-coefficient models}
% P:introduction-03
Liu et al.\ trace the progression from Kent--Eisenberg's ideal activity and fugacity coefficients with fitted reaction constants to Deshmukh--Mather's extended Debye--H\"uckel treatment, the interaction parameters tabulated by Weiland and co-workers, Li--Mather's Pitzer treatment, and Austgen's electrolyte non-random two-liquid (e-NRTL) model \cite[Introduction, pp.~2080--2081]{Liu1999}.
In their MEA refit, Liu et al.\ warn that simultaneous estimation of the carbamate constant and interaction parameters can overfit scattered solubility data \cite[p.~2083]{Liu1999}.
B\"ottinger et al.\ instead refitted a Pitzer model's carbamate constant to nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) observations after finding systematically low calculated carbamate \cite[Sect.~5]{Bottinger2008}.
Refined e-NRTL fits included binary vapor--liquid equilibrium (VLE), freezing-point depression and excess enthalpy, with speciation predicted \cite[Sect.~5, p.~3646]{Hessen2010}.
Aronu et al.\ extended the concentration range to 15--60 wt\% MEA using an extended universal quasi-chemical (UNIQUAC) activity model fitted to solubility data, with NMR speciation predicted \cite[Sects.~1, 4.3]{Aronu2011}.

% P:introduction-04
Joint fitting to several properties is established practice for activity models.
Hilliard fitted e-NRTL parameters to pressure, absorption heat, heat capacity and NMR data, but 18 of 35 parameter estimates were smaller than their standard errors \cite[Tables~13.4-2--13.4-3, pp.~440--441]{Hilliard2008}.
Zhang et al.\ also fitted these properties jointly; their \COtwo-pressure average absolute relative deviations (AARDs) of 13.5--35.5\,\% by source describe fitted data \cite[Sect.~3.3, Table~9, p.~72]{Zhang2011}.
Akula et al.\ fitted pressure and differential heat using an analytical excess-enthalpy expression, leaving speciation and heat capacity as comparisons outside the fit \cite[Sect.~1, p.~2; pp.~10--11]{Akula2023a}.
These studies establish multi-property precedents, while parameter correlation and the separate vapor-phase description remain motivations for examining equations of state \cite[Table~7]{Akula2023a}\cite[Introduction, p.~1884]{MacDowell2010}.

\subsection{SAFT and other equations of state}
% P:introduction-05
Statistical associating fluid theory (SAFT) permits one equation of state for both phases \cite[Introduction, p.~1884]{MacDowell2010}, but its treatments of reaction products differ.
Button and Gubbins's early aqueous-MEA application used association without reactions or ions \cite[Sect.~1, p.~176]{Button1999}.
Nasrifar and Tafazzol combined perturbed-chain SAFT (PC-SAFT) fugacities for molecular species with literature reaction constants and ideal ions confined to the liquid \cite[Sect.~4]{Nasrifar2010}.
Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh's PC-SAFT and Najafloo and Zarei's Huang--Radosz SAFT likewise used ideal activities in the chemical equilibria \cite[Sect.~3.3]{Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}.

% P:introduction-06
Reactive-association models represent chemistry through molecular bonding, treating carbamate and protonated amine as neutral bound pairs in SAFT for potentials of variable range (SAFT-VR) \cite[pp.~1885, 1887]{MacDowell2010}.
Rodriguez et al.\ obtained speciation from bonded fractions \cite[Fig.~9b, p.~1341]{Rodriguez2012}, as did Perdomo et al.\ with group-contribution SAFT-\(\gamma\) Mie \cite[Sect.~2.2, Eqs.~7--8; Fig.~14]{perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}.
Soft-SAFT describes absorption as aggregation and estimates absorption heat from the pressure--temperature slope \cite[Sect.~4.7, Eq.~26]{lloretConsistentTransferableThermodynamic2017}.
Caloric agreement remains a concern: Wang et al.\ report that their Peng--Robinson cubic-plus-association model underestimates MEA absorption enthalpy by 49\,\% when carbamate forms, and suggest adding ionic terms \cite[Sect.~4.2.4, p.~83]{wangImprovementPRCPAEquation2018}.

% P:introduction-07
Explicit MEA ions, including carbamate, already appear in T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros's electrolyte cubic two-state association model \cite[Sect.~3, p.~48]{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013} and Neumann et al.'s combined Helmholtz-energy/e-NRTL model \cite[Sect.~3, Table~3]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Najafloo et al.\ included carbamate ions of 2-((2-aminoethyl)amino)ethanol (AEEA) in electrolyte Huang--Radosz SAFT with mean spherical approximation and Born terms \cite[p.~148, App.~A]{najaflooMeasuringSolubilityCarbon2015}.
These precedents leave open the representation of MEA and its carbamate within electrolyte PC-SAFT.

\subsection{Electrolyte SAFT and ePC-SAFT}
% P:introduction-08
Cameretti et al.\ introduced electrolyte PC-SAFT (ePC-SAFT) by adding a Debye--H\"uckel contribution and fitting ion parameters to aqueous-salt vapor pressures and densities \cite[Sects.~2--3, Table~2]{Cameretti2005}.
Held et al.\ added mean ionic activity coefficients to parameter estimation because they were more sensitive to ion parameters than VLE \cite[Sect.~3]{Held2008}; revised ePC-SAFT subsequently included cation--anion dispersion \cite[Sect.~3.1.2]{Held2014}.
In another electrolyte SAFT family, Schreckenberg et al.\ combined SAFT-VR with mean spherical approximation and Born terms and predicted \COtwo salting-out in aqueous sodium chloride \cite[Sects.~2.1--2.2, 5.2.5, Fig.~14]{Schreckenberg2014}.
For amines, Uyan et al.\ predicted MDEA ternary pressures and speciation after binary estimation \cite[Sects.~4.1--4.3]{Uyan2015}, and Wangler et al.\ fitted additional interactions to osmotic coefficients \cite[Sects.~6.1--6.2]{Wangler2018}.
Cleeton et al.\ treated competitive acid-gas absorption in MDEA \cite[Sect.~2]{Cleeton2020}, while B\"ulow et al.\ extended the treatment to mixed solvents \cite[Theory, ePC-SAFT parameters]{Bulow2021a}.

% P:introduction-09
Ionic activities, density and residual enthalpy can share one Helmholtz energy, but reaction constants and their heats remain external correlations.
Cleeton et al.\ add van 't Hoff reaction terms to an equation-of-state derivative \cite[Sect.~2.4, Eq.~15]{Cleeton2020}; Wangler et al.\ combine reaction enthalpies with a Henry-coefficient dissolution term \cite[Sect.~5, Eqs.~23--24]{Wangler2018}.
Electrostatic choices also matter: ePC-SAFT advanced combines a Born term with concentration-dependent permittivity, whose temperature derivative enters enthalpy \cite[Sect.~2.1, Eq.~6]{Bulow2020}.
Rueben et al.'s dielectric-decrement model gives average activity-coefficient deviations below 5\,\% for the studied lithium, sodium and potassium salts and hydrogen halides at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} \cite[Sect.~3, Fig.~1]{Rueben2024}.
Born diameter and permittivity choices can compensate \cite[Sect.~3.2, Figs.~3--4]{Novak2023}; their application to reacting amine ions therefore needs assessment beyond inorganic salts.

\subsection{Data and multi-property fitting}
% P:introduction-10
Different measurements constrain different parts of the description.
Wagner et al.\ report literature \COtwo pressures over 30 wt\% MEA differing by up to an order of magnitude \cite[Introduction, p.~883]{wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}.
Smith et al.\ show that different constants and activity parameters can reproduce similar pressures while implying different speciation, making species measurements more discriminating \cite[Sect.~6, PDF p.~19]{smithGibbsEnergyMinimization}.
Those measurements also require care: B\"ottinger's NMR reports molecular and protonated MEA as a sum \cite[Sect.~3, Table~3]{Bottinger2008}, whereas Wong's Raman carbamate concentration is inferred from the amine balance \cite[Sect.~3.2]{Wong2015}.
Jakobsen et al.\ estimate NMR errors of about 5--10\,\% and warn that shared peaks make minor species difficult to quantify \cite[Results]{Jakobsen2005}.

% P:introduction-11
Kim and Svendsen distinguish directly measured absorption heats from values inferred through solubility slopes and caution against converting integral heats to differential values \cite[Results and Discussion, Eq.~1, Figs.~3, 6]{Kim2007}.
Loaded-liquid densities constrain volumetric behavior: Hartono et al.\ correlate them through a loading-dependent volume-expansion term \cite[Sect.~3.1, Eqs.~3--5]{Hartono2014}.
After VLE-only fitting, Neumann et al.\ predict density with 0.92\,\% AARD against Hartono but 3.5\,\% against a 60 wt\% dataset, and recommend multi-property fitting \cite[Sect.~4, p.~7; Sect.~4.2, p.~8]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Joint equilibrium, caloric and loaded-density fitting thus addresses complementary observations, but property agreement alone does not assess phase stability.

\subsection{Phase stability}
% P:introduction-12
Michelsen's tangent-plane criterion tests phase stability before a flash calculation because the number of phases is not known beforehand \cite[Introduction, pp.~1--2; Sect.~2]{Michelsen1982}.
Relevant electrolyte and amine precedents already exist.
Tsanas et al.\ applied reactive stability analysis to a carbamate-forming, demixing amine mixture using e-NRTL \cite[Sects.~2.5, 3.1]{Tsanas2022}.
Perdomo et al.\ obtained liquid splits in implicit-reaction SAFT-\(\gamma\) Mie amine mixtures \cite[Sect.~2.2]{perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}, while Ascani et al.\ searched stability in ePC-SAFT advanced for fully dissociated salts without reactions \cite[Sect.~3.3, Fig.~1]{Ascani2022}.
Perdomo et al.'s later formal tangent-plane criterion incorporates phase electroneutrality, with its derivation restricted to strong electrolytes \cite[Sects.~1, 6]{Perdomo2025}.
The explicit-ion ePC-SAFT amine studies reviewed here do not report a tangent-plane assessment of their parameter sets \cite[Sect.~4]{Uyan2015}\cite[Sect.~6]{Wangler2018}\cite[Sects.~2--4]{Cleeton2020}\cite[Results and Discussion]{Bulow2021a}.
This leaves their phase behavior as a separate question from agreement with fitted properties.

\subsection{Gap and this work}
% P:introduction-13
No explicit-ion ePC-SAFT treatment of MEA or its carbamate was found in the literature reviewed here.
We ask whether one such formulation with carbamate chemistry can represent loaded-MEA equilibrium, caloric and density properties.
Relative to the MDEA ePC-SAFT studies it adds carbamate chemistry; relative to T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros's ternary VLE fit it adds caloric and loaded-liquid density observations to joint estimation \cite[Sect.~4, p.~48]{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013}.
We use Figiel et al.'s solvation-shell Born term, extending its \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} inorganic-ion basis to protonated MEA and carbamate at other temperatures; its Born diameter was not tested at other temperatures in that study \cite[ePC-SAFT section, Table~1]{Figiel2025}.
Water, \COtwo--water and dielectric parameters are fitted separately, followed by 16 parameters fitted jointly to pressure, species, caloric, loaded-density and binary VLE observations.
Calculated curves are compared with measurements at nominal 30 wt\% MEA, and a finite phase search tests the reported parameters for additional phases.
That search assesses specified states and does not establish global stability or physical validation of phase behavior.
