\subsubsection{Parameters, their uncertainty and phase stability}\label{sec:results-parameters}
% P:results-design
The MEA model has 11 jointly fitted parameters: the MEA--water interaction and its temperature coefficient, the diameters and dispersion energies of \MEAH and \MEACOO, their interactions with water, one with a temperature coefficient, and with each other, and the shared water--bicarbonate interaction.
Its 307 fitted observations are those of \cref{tab:calibration-data}, its equilibrium constants are fixed at their literature correlations, and its final optimization used no stability penalty; both starts descended from the stability-penalized 16-parameter calibration (\cref{sec:parameters}).
The results of this section are therefore those of one local solution reached from two related starts.

% P:results-parameters
\Cref{tab:species-parameters,tab:interaction-matrix} list the parameters of the MEA model, and \cref{tab:mea-data} and Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-mea-comparison} give its deviations from every data set, beside those of the model of Polley and Hedengren on identical observations \cite{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}.

% P:results-parameter-interpretation
Only the \MEAH--\MEACOO interaction is on a bound; its lower-bound value doubles the unlike-pair dispersion energy under \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}.
The MEA--water interaction is well determined (\cref{tab:interaction-matrix}), and the calculated bubble temperatures follow both isobars of Cai et al.\ \cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996} (Fig.~\ref{supp-fig:mea-water}), with an RMSE of \SI{1.785}{\kelvin} (\cref{tab:mea-data}).
The water--bicarbonate interaction and the opposing size changes of the two MEA ions are weakly determined, and their large conditional standard errors preclude reading the fitted values as unique molecular properties.
Propagated to the 47 fitted \COtwo partial pressures, the parameter covariance gives relative standard errors of 6.0--16.9\,\%, of the same size as the deviations themselves.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-uncertainty} gives the standard errors of the ion parameters, the correlations and refits that hold the bound interaction slightly inside its bound.

For loaded aqueous MEA, B\"ottinger et al.\ observe a homogeneous liquid in their measured subset \cite{Bottinger2008}, which does not establish homogeneity over the whole searched domain.
The 2646 finite searches are an endpoint check, separate from the optimization: the MEA model shows no detected instability at any of the 2484 liquid states or 162 liquid--vapor states searched, and every search finished.

\subsubsection{Pressure and speciation at 30 wt\%}\label{sec:results-pressure}
% P:results-pressure-01
At nominal 30 wt\% MEA the bubble curves follow the measured \COtwo partial pressures over the broad logarithmic pressure range, while differences between sources remain visible (\cref{fig:pressure}).
The 47 fitted pressures, at 313--\SI{333}{\kelvin}, constrain the lower-temperature part of this comparison, and the higher-temperature pressures are calculated without further fitting (\cref{tab:mea-data}; Table~\ref{supp-tab:pressure-sources} gives the deviation for each source).

% P:results-species-01
The species curves show molecular MEA being replaced by protonated MEA and carbamate as loading increases, followed by increasing bicarbonate (\cref{fig:speciation}).
Agreement with the NMR amine sum does not independently establish the separate molecular and protonated fractions.
The carbonate share of dissolved carbon remains far below the ten observations of Jakobsen et al.\ (AARD of 95.0\,\%; \cref{fig:jakobsen-carbonate-share}), even though their species-to-total-amine ratios are fitted \cite{Jakobsen2005}: the geometric mean of the calculated-to-observed ratios is 0.032.
Fast proton exchange merges bicarbonate and carbonate into one \ce{^{13}C} NMR signal, and Jakobsen et al.\ obtain the carbonate share from its chemical shift, calibrated against standards of known bicarbonate-to-carbonate ratio; they state that fractions below about 10\,\% cannot be evaluated accurately and that their carbonate is most likely overpredicted \cite{Jakobsen2005}, which limits the accuracy of these shares.
The changes tested do not remove it: other Born diameters for bicarbonate and carbonate, a permittivity changed by $\pm5\,\%$, a solvation factor of 1.5 for \COtwo or for MEA, and a second pair of MEA permittivity coefficients all leave the geometric-mean ratio between 0.016 and 0.040 (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-carbonate}).
A refit with the solvation factor of MEA at 1.5 lowers the objective value by 1.096 but also lowers the ratio, to 0.020, so it gives no support for that change.
The cause of the deficit is unresolved, and the fitted pressures do not determine this minor-species partition.

\subsubsection{Absorption heat, heat capacity and density}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The integral heats of Vinjarapu et al.\ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} are fitted \cite{HemaBhavya2025}; those of Arcis et al.\ at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin} are not, although they were used to select the parameters \cite{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
The calculated absorption at the Arcis conditions is less exothermic than measured, with the larger discrepancy in the lower-pressure series (\cref{fig:heat}).
The measurements themselves differ between the two series at nearby loadings, so this comparison isolates neither a pressure effect nor a model contribution.

At \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}, open and filled squares are \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} and \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}.}

% P:results-heat-kim
The semi-differential heats of Kim and Svendsen deviate most at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, with an RMSE of \SI{31.77}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} of \COtwo over all 20 intervals, including the four flagged for source problems that Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-temperature-bands} reports separately (Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-mea-comparison}).
Extrapolated equilibrium constants, pressure histories that had to be approximated and chemistry missing from the model may all contribute; the comparison isolates no cause, and caloric accuracy at high temperature is not demonstrated.
Heat capacities outside the fitted states, from Weiland et al.\ \cite{weilandHeatCapacityAqueous1997} and from Concepci\'on et al.\ \cite{concepcionHeatCapacitiesDifferent2023}, deviate by less than 5\,\% (Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-mea-comparison}).

\subsubsection{Other MEA concentrations}\label{sec:results-concentration}
% P:results-transfer
At 15 and 45 wt\% MEA, where no pressure is fitted and no concentration-specific parameter is estimated, the pressures of Aronu et al.\ deviate by 45.1 and 45.9\,\%, against 31.2\,\% for their pressures at 30 wt\%, which are not fitted either (\cref{fig:concentration-pressure}, \cref{tab:mea-data}, Table~\ref{supp-tab:pressure-sources}).
Aronu et al.\ fitted an extended UNIQUAC model to their own data at 15--60 wt\% and report 24.3\,\% for all their \COtwo partial pressures together, without a value for each concentration \cite{Aronu2011}, so the two are not compared on identical observations.

\subsubsection{Effect of the parameter restrictions}\label{sec:results-restrictions}
% P:results-joint-01
From the 16-parameter calibration to the MEA model, five fewer adjustable parameters leave the objective value and the fitted pressure and density deviations nearly unchanged, and the number of parameters on bounds falls from six to one.
Deviations outside the fit rise.
On the 19 \COtwo partial pressures of Ma'mun et al.\ \cite{Mamun2005} near \SI{393}{\kelvin}, which neither fit includes, the deviation rises from 19.4\,\% to 33.5\,\% (\cref{tab:published-models}).
That change is the combined cost of fixing R2 and R5 and of tying the Born diameters, not the isolated effect of either.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-restrictions} gives the full sequence of restrictions; every more restricted set in that sequence has larger fitted and not-fitted pressure deviations than the MEA model.
