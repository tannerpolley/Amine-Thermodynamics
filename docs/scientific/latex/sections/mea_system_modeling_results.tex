\section{Results and discussion}\label{sec:mea-system-modeling-results}
\subsection{Pure-water parameters}\label{sec:results-water}
% P:results-water-01
Five water parameters were fitted to IAPWS-95 saturated-liquid densities, saturation pressures and heat capacities at 20 temperatures from 298.15 to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}: the segment count, the diameter-law constant $\sigma_0$, the dispersion energy, and the association energy and volume.
The heat capacities are at \SI{0.3}{\mega\pascal} and at three atmospheric states; the two exponential terms of $\sigma_w(T)$ were fixed (Section~S1).
At the 20 fitted temperatures, the mean absolute relative deviations are 0.0363\,\% for saturated-liquid density, 0.0754\,\% for saturation pressure and 0.440\,\% for heat capacity at \SI{0.3}{\mega\pascal}, and the largest are 0.0985, 0.171 and 1.49\,\% (\cref{fig:water-iapws}); the three atmospheric heat capacities give 0.784\,\%.
The calculated heat capacity is low at every temperature, most at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}.
At 19 interleaved temperatures that were not fitted, the mean deviations are 0.0343, 0.0700 and 0.416\,\%.
These are comparisons with a reference equation rather than with independent measurements.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\MEA}{MEA} permittivity}\label{sec:results-permittivity}
% P:results-permittivity-01
The \MEA dielectric coefficients of \cref{eq:solvent-permittivity} were fitted by weighted least squares to 14 permittivities of unloaded \MEA--water at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, from water to pure \MEA, using equation-of-state densities and relative scale 0.01 \cite[ESI Tables~S1--S2]{agieienkoWhatGasStream2024a}.
The water and \COtwo coefficients were fixed (Section~S1).
The coefficients were not refitted after the joint fit changed the \MEA--water interaction, and with it the calculated densities.
With the reported parameters, the 14 permittivities give RMSE 0.301, largest absolute deviation 0.464 and AARD 0.453\,\% (\cref{fig:mea-permittivity}).
The data are at one temperature and without \COtwo, so they do not test the temperature or loading dependence of the permittivity.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\COtwo}{CO2} solubility}\label{sec:results-pressure}
% P:results-pressure-01
\Cref{fig:pressure} compares calculated \COtwo partial pressures with measurements from six sources at 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin}.
The AARD is 11.86\,\% over the 47 fitted pressures and 20.55\,\% over the complete 159-pressure comparison, including 112 observations not fitted.
By source, the 159-pressure comparison gives 9.43\,\% for Hilliard (31 observations), 18.57\,\% for Ma'mun (19), 19.36\,\% for Jou (48), 21.05\,\% for Idris (10), 27.46\,\% for Xu (15) and 29.76\,\% for Aronu (36).
The deviation panel exposes discrepancies that are less apparent on the logarithmic pressure axis.
At Aronu's non-fitted \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} observations with analytical loading 0.46--0.52, the calculated \COtwo partial pressures are 46--70\,\% below the measured values.
The sources disagree there: at loading 0.489, Aronu reports \SI{2.86}{\kilo\pascal}, whereas Hilliard reports \SI{1.10}{\kilo\pascal} at loading 0.491; the model gives \SI{1.17}{\kilo\pascal} at Aronu's state.
Hilliard and Jou pressures at \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} and loading 0.46--0.52 lie within 24\,\% of the model.

% P:results-pressure-02
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, the 54 six-source pressures give AARD 24.35\,\%, with bias direction differing between sources.
The 23 additional Wagner pressures give AARDs of 22.19\,\% near \SI{353}{\kelvin} and 9.27\,\% near \SI{392}{\kelvin} (Section~S7).
Pressure agreement deteriorates at other solvent concentrations: the 33 Aronu observations at 15 wt\% and 37 at 45 wt\% give AARDs of 29.5 and 56.0\,\%, respectively.
Their mean logarithmic biases are $-0.205$ and $+0.149$, so the model underpredicts at 15 wt\% and overpredicts at 45 wt\% on logarithmic average.
The reported parameters therefore do not support concentration transfer across that range.

% P:results-species-01
The speciation curves show conversion of molecular MEA into protonated amine and carbamate as loading increases, followed by increasing bicarbonate (\cref{fig:speciation}).
The combined \ce{MEA + MEAH+} observable gives AARD 2.32\,\% over 25 compared observations, of which 20 were fitted; all 94 species fractions in the shared fitted subset give 7.10\,\%.
Agreement with this amine sum does not independently test the separate molecular and protonated fractions.
The five non-fitted B\"ottinger states at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and 31 wt\% test the model above the fitted species temperatures: the amine sum agrees within 1.3\,\% up to loading 0.25 but is 6.4 and 14.5\,\% low at loadings 0.34 and 0.40, where calculated carbamate is 9 and 26\,\% high.
The ten Jakobsen carbonate-share comparisons are also low: calculated/observed share has mean 0.0727 and range 0.00629--0.266 (Section~S5).
Those calculations account for dilution of the NMR samples and use their NMR loadings \cite[Sample preparation, Tables~A1--A2]{Jakobsen2005}; Jakobsen's warning about quantifying minor components is discussed by Matin et al.\ \cite[p.~6616]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.

% P:results-species-02
The 19 non-fitted Matin bicarbonate + carbonate totals give AARD 67.46\,\% and mean logarithmic bias $-1.265$.
Matin's other titration species agree more closely, with AARDs of 7.76\,\% for \MEAH and 8.09\,\% for \MEACOO, although free \MEA is 16--35\,\% low above loading 0.4.
Matin et al.\ found their inferred bicarbonate values higher than the NMR values \cite[pp.~6616--6617]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}, so the discrepancy may partly reflect disagreement between sources as well as limitations of the model.
A good pressure fit therefore does not ensure accurate bicarbonate and carbonate fractions.

\subsubsection{Heat of absorption}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The fitted Vinjarapu integral heats give RMSE \SI{1.84}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} (\cref{fig:heat}).
For Arcis at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}, the mean calculated-minus-measured differences are \SI{+10.21}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} and \SI{+3.15}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}: the calculated absorption is less exothermic.
Their pressure-separated RMSEs are 10.33 and \SI{3.37}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, respectively.
The model's pressure effect is small; increasing pressure between those two series changes the calculated heat by about \SI{0.19}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at the same loading.

% P:results-heat-02
The Arcis measurements themselves differ between pressure series: heats at nearby loadings 0.181 and 0.183 are $-92.6$ and $-85.9$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, and at loadings 0.391 and 0.405 are $-94.4$ and $-85.5$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \cite[Table~5, pp.~3355--3356]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
These are not exact matched-loading comparisons, and both temperature and pressure differ from Vinjarapu's conditions.
The available data do not isolate a cause for the discrepancy.
Because the Arcis data informed parameter selection, and absorption heat has been checked only at 313.15 and \SI{322.5}{\kelvin}, these comparisons do not establish its temperature dependence across an absorber or stripper.

\subsection{Phase stability}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:results-phase-02
The search of Section~\ref{sec:methods-phase} covers 162 temperature, pressure and loading states for nominal 30 wt\% MEA: six temperatures from 313.15 to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, nine loadings from $\alpha=10^{-5}$ to 0.6, and pressures of 0.50, 0.80 and 0.95 times a reference pressure, the bubble pressure of the reported fit at each temperature and loading (\cref{tab:phase-search-summary}).
The reported parameters and the wider-bound fit are searched at the same states.
\input{tables/phase_search_summary}

% P:results-phase-04
The wider-bound fit has a resolved lower-Gibbs-energy arrangement at 54 of the 162 states, between seven and ten at each temperature; the reported parameters have none, with all 1458 trial optimizations completing.
Separately seeded calculations for the wider-bound fit confirm two liquids at 32 of its 54 descent states, at every temperature (Section~S8).
In the confirmed splits, the water mole fraction is 0.40--0.74 in the water-poor liquid and 0.68--0.97 in the water-rich one.
This is a finite search at specified states, not a global-stability certificate or physical validation of phase behavior.


% P:results-phase-01
Apart from these splits, the wider-bound fit gives property agreement similar to that of the reported fit.
Narrowing both bounds and refitting removes the detected instabilities in the finite search, while increasing the fitting cost by 0.45\%.
Both narrowed limits appear in Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-fitted-parameters}: the reported \MEAH segment diameter lies on the new \SI{5.35}{\angstrom} lower bound, and the \MEAH--water interaction lies at $-0.5$, the unchanged lower end of its narrowed range $[-0.5,-0.161]$; Section~S9 describes how the limits were chosen and lists the wider-bound-fit parameters, and Section~S10 gives the acceptance limits.
The diameter changes from \SI{4.16}{\angstrom} in the wider-bound fit to \SI{5.35}{\angstrom}, and the water interaction at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} changes from $+0.026$ to $-0.5$.
The restriction is therefore active: within this formulation, removing the detected splits costs density agreement.
The number of fitted loaded densities within $2s$ falls from 47/47 to 44/47: a larger protonated-amine diameter lowers the calculated loaded density (Section~S9), and all three misses are low, at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}.
Both parameter sets were obtained by local bounded least-squares fitting; this comparison does not establish the minimum fitting-cost increase needed to avoid detected instabilities in the specified search.

\FloatBarrier

% P:results-phase-03
The homogeneous-liquid density comparisons discussed by Neumann et al.\ do not test phase splitting at the states searched here \cite[Sect.~4.2]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Schick et al.\ report experimentally that no liquid--liquid demixing was observed in their \COtwo--electrolyte-solution measurements \cite[Sect.~3.2]{Schick2023}; this observation is a different kind of evidence from a calculated stability search.
The present calculation illustrates why property agreement should be accompanied by a search for additional phases.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-literature-01
Zhang et al.\ provide the closest multi-property comparison, with 12 ternary coefficients fitted to solubility, differential heat, heat capacity and NMR \cite[Sect.~3.3, Tables~9--11]{Zhang2011}.
Their fitted pressure deviations range from 13.5 to 35.5\,\% by source; the present values are 11.86\,\% over 47 fitted pressures and 20.55\,\% over the 159-pressure comparison.
Najafloo and Zarei and Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh report 34.71 and 36.42\,\%, respectively, over 691 ternary comparisons after pure/binary estimation \cite[Table~5]{Najafloo2018,Baygi2015}.
Their data sets, fitting roles and domains differ: Zhang's differential-heat data span 313--393 K and differ from the integral heat assessed here, and the heat-capacity fit includes several solvent concentrations \cite[Table~9]{Zhang2011}.
Here integral heat is fitted at one temperature and heat capacity at four states, while loaded density is included in the joint fit.
Neumann et al.'s ternary density comparisons instead assess predictions after VLE fitting \cite[Sect.~4.2]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
\Cref{tab:literature-model-comparison} gives the quantities and data roles for these and other MEA models; the statistics locate the present model within the published range without establishing equal or better accuracy.

\subsection{Transfer of the workflow to MDEA}\label{sec:mdea-transfer}
% P:results-mdea-01
A separate MDEA calculation tests transfer of the workflow by declaring the species and reactions, assigning fitting and assessment data, fitting the MDEA parameters jointly, applying a phase gate and testing pressures outside the fit.
Water, \COtwo, \HthreeO, \Hydroxide, \HCOthree and \COthree parameters remain frozen at their MEA values; nine MDEA-specific coordinates are fitted.
For the pre-registered test of 88 \COtwo partial pressures from Ermatchkov et al.~\cite{ermatchkovSolubilityCarbonDioxide2006} at no more than 49 wt\% MDEA, Variant B, with \ce{MDEAH+} represented as a charged molecule, gives AARD 19.15\,\% and passes the 24.2\,\% limit; Variant A, with a single-sphere cation, gives 26.21\,\% and fails.
At the same frozen endpoints, the 20 outside-fit measurements of Xiao et al.~\cite[Table~4]{xiaoCO2AbsorptionAqueous2018a} give \COtwo partial-pressure AARDs of 20.40\,\% for Variant A and 28.28\,\% for Variant B, reversing the Ermatchkov ranking without changing the pre-registered transfer verdict.
The selected fits also differ in their active bounds: four for A3 (Variant A) and two for B1 (Variant B).
The two representations therefore give different outcomes under this test, but the comparison does not isolate representation as the cause; a new amine should test both conventions.

% P:results-mdea-02
This is conditional calibration plus outside-fit assessment, verified numerically, over 293.15--\SI{395}{\kelvin}, with no more than 49 wt\% MDEA for the pressure test and 50 wt\% for other properties.
The dielectric temperature law above \SI{328.15}{\kelvin} is assumed, the bicarbonate follow-up is unavailable, and there is no external benchmark or absorber qualification.
Section~S12 gives the endpoint comparisons, saturation cap and limits.
This cross-amine calculation refits MDEA-specific parameters; it does not resolve the concentration non-transfer of the fixed MEA parameters reported above.

\subsection{Domain of use}\label{sec:results-domain}
% P:results-scope-01
For nominal 30 wt\% MEA, the comparisons assess \COtwo partial pressure, the \ce{MEA + MEAH+} and carbamate fractions, integral heat near \SI{313}{\kelvin}, loaded density up to \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and heat capacity at the measured states, with the deviations reported above.
The property-specific fitting temperatures and loadings are listed in \cref{tab:current-data-inventory}.
Pressure and species comparisons above \SI{333.15}{\kelvin} are extrapolations relative to their fitted data; these comparisons do not establish a continuous domain of process use.
The reported parameters have no detected instability at the 162 searched states (Section~\ref{sec:phase-method}).

% P:results-scope-02
The agreement depends on calibrated chemistry as well as on ePC-SAFT activities: the fitted \MEAH dissociation constant lies 0.49 p$K$ from Bates--Pinching at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, whereas B\"ottinger et al.\ kept it fixed (\cref{tab:literature-model-comparison}), and six of the 16 parameters lie on bounds.
These values indicate compensation among solvation, dispersion and reaction terms (Section~S6), not physical ion sizes or reaction enthalpies.

% P:results-scope-03
Every property is least constrained above the fitted temperatures.
Fitted pressures end near \SI{333}{\kelvin} and fitted densities at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, and R4 and R5 are extrapolated above their \SI{323.15}{\kelvin} source limit, with the R5 enthalpy shift at its bound.
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} the pressure AARD is 24.35\,\%, the \COtwo--water pressures are up to 11.3\,\% low at \SI{393.2}{\kelvin}, loaded densities are on average 0.7--1.2\,\% low for Han's 30 wt\% observations at 373.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, and absorption heat is untested.
