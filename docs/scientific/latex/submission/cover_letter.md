Dear Editors of Fluid Phase Equilibria,

Please consider “Thermodynamics of CO₂-loaded aqueous amines with ePC-SAFT: monoethanolamine calibration, transfer to DEA, MDEA and AMP, and phase stability” as a research article.

The paper asks whether two calibrations of an electrolyte equation of state that reproduce the pressures of CO₂-loaded aqueous amines about equally well predict the same liquid phases. An ePC-SAFT model of monoethanolamine (MEA) is calibrated first, with its reaction equilibrium constants fixed at literature correlations; its water, CO₂ and inorganic-ion parameters are then held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.

What is new:

- **Near-equal fits with different calculated phases.** Two AMP fits deviate by 10.3 and 10.5 % from the same 47 fitted total pressures. The first gives 73 calculated liquid–liquid splits above 353 K, the highest temperature of the fitted loaded-solution pressures; the second gives none in 666 finite phase-stability searches. The two fits give similar objective values and fitted total-pressure deviations.
- **A controlled test of one shared parameter.** Changing the shared water–bicarbonate interaction lowers the deviation of the 66 fitted MDEA total pressures from 20.1 to 11.2 %, raises that of 121 outside-fit CO₂ partial pressures from 20.1 to 33.6 %, and its searches confirm two calculated splits at states where none was found with the original value. Transfer here means that the further amines could be fitted with the shared parameters held fixed, not that MEA determines those parameters.
- **Four models reported on their own pressure bases.** The MEA model, with eleven jointly estimated parameters, deviates by 11.6 % from 47 fitted CO₂ partial pressures and the DEA model by 28.5 % from 30; neither has a detected instability. For DEA and AMP the carbamate-hydrolysis constant is fitted, not fixed. With the reported MDEA model no second liquid is confirmed at the measured states. DEA is reported as a negative result: with the adopted DEA pure-component and association parameters, no tested water–DEA interaction represents the binary excess enthalpies and bubble pressures together.

The work fits the journal's scope in equations of state for electrolyte and reacting systems, chemical and phase equilibrium, and phase-stability analysis. It builds on ePC-SAFT studies of aqueous amines and electrolytes published in Fluid Phase Equilibria. The MEA model is compared with the author's earlier reactive ePC-SAFT model of MEA (Polley and Hedengren, Next Chemical Engineering 3 (2026) 100132), whose parameters were estimated in separate steps.

The intended use is a testbed for calibration practice, in which a fit is judged by the phases it predicts as well as by its deviations. The limits are gathered in one section: pressures at 15 and 45 wt% MEA, carbonate shares and high-temperature absorption heats remain inaccurate, the calculated phase behaviors are not established by the measurements, and the models, calibrated against data examined during development, are not a basis for absorber design.
