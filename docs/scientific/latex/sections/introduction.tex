\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% P:introduction-01
Aqueous amine solutions are widely used to remove carbon dioxide (\COtwo) from gas streams by absorption and stripping, and the design of these processes starts from the solubility of \COtwo in the solvent and the other thermodynamic properties of the loaded solution \cite{Zhang2011}.
The \COtwo partial pressure that expresses the solubility, species concentrations, heat release and liquid density enter different parts of an absorber calculation but describe the same reacting solvent \cite{Zhang2011,Freguia2003,Amundsen2009}.
An equation of state gives them all from one Helmholtz energy, but each depends on different derivatives of it and on different parameters, so each has to be tested against its own data.

% P:introduction-02
Amines of different classes absorb \COtwo through different reactions.
Primary and secondary amines, such as monoethanolamine (MEA) and diethanolamine (DEA), form carbamate \cite{xuRepresentationEquilibriumSolubility1992}.
The tertiary amine methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) forms none \cite{Nasrifar2010}, and in the hindered primary amine 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) steric hindrance inhibits carbamate formation, so that bicarbonate formation dominates \cite{xuRepresentationEquilibriumSolubility1992}.
Thermodynamic models of these solvents share water, \COtwo and the inorganic ions and differ in the amine species and their reactions.
If the parameters of the shared species could be determined once and held fixed, each further amine would need only its own species, interactions and reaction constants.

% P:introduction-03
Electrolyte models of these solvents have long reused subsystems.
Austgen et al.\ fitted the interaction parameters of the electrolyte non-random two-liquid (eNRTL) model to binary data and to the solubility of a single acid gas in aqueous MEA and DEA, and then represented mixed \ce{H2S}--\COtwo solubility without further adjustment \cite{austgenModelVaporliquidEquilibria1989}.
Vall\'ee et al.\ described \COtwo and \ce{H2S} in aqueous DEA with a cubic equation of state extended to ions, fitting its ionic interaction parameters to solubility data \cite{valleeRepresentationCO2H2S1999}.
Zhang et al.\ estimate eNRTL parameters for MEA jointly from pressure, heat, heat capacity and species data \cite{Zhang2011}.
Electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) adds ionic electrostatics to a molecular equation of state \cite{Cameretti2005,Held2014}.
For MDEA, Uyan et al.\ take neutral and inorganic-ion parameters from earlier work and derive the protonated-amine parameters from molecular MDEA \cite{Uyan2015}.
Wangler et al.\ add ion--water interactions fitted to osmotic coefficients of salt solutions and fit no parameter to the ternary system \cite{Wangler2018}.
Cleeton et al.\ find that agreement deteriorates as the MDEA content increases \cite{Cleeton2020}.
MDEA forms no carbamate, so these studies do not determine the parameters of carbamate-forming amines.
Polley and Hedengren describe the explicit MEA ions, including carbamate, with ePC-SAFT, but they estimate the parameters in separate steps and use empirical caloric properties in their column calculations \cite{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}.

% P:introduction-04
In equations of state of the statistical associating fluid theory (SAFT) without explicit ions, the reaction products are represented by association, and the association or group parameters are carried between compounds.
Mac Dowell et al.\ describe \COtwo in aqueous MEA with association parameters taken from ethanol and ethylamine \cite{MacDowell2010}.
Rodr\'iguez et al.\ extend that approach to MEA, DEA, MDEA and AMP, the four amines studied here \cite{Rodriguez2012}.
Reactive-association and group-contribution models share parameters between compounds \cite{lloretConsistentTransferableThermodynamic2017,perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}.
Perturbed-chain SAFT (PC-SAFT) and Huang--Radosz SAFT (SAFT-HR) have also been combined with ideal-activity reaction equilibria for MEA, DEA and MDEA \cite{Baygi2015,Nasrifar2010,Pahlavanzadeh2013,Najafloo2018}.
These studies judge a calibration by its deviations from measured pressures and compositions.

% P:introduction-05
Deviations do not show whether the calculated liquid is stable.
A single-liquid objective contains no requirement of stability, and ePC-SAFT ion parameters can predict liquid--liquid demixing that contradicts observation \cite{Held2014}.
For nonreactive binary mixtures, parameters fitted to vapor--liquid data alone are known to give false liquid--liquid splits, and stability has been imposed as a constraint on the estimation \cite{mitsosBilevelOptimizationFormulation2009,bollasBilevelOptimizationFormulation2009,glassParameterEstimationCubic2018,gauReliableNonlinearParameter2000}.
The tangent-plane criterion of Michelsen tests whether an additional phase lowers the Gibbs energy \cite{Michelsen1982}; in an electrolyte the trial phase must be electroneutral \cite{Perdomo2025}, and in a reacting liquid it must also satisfy the chemical constraints \cite{Tsanas2022,Ascani2022}.
Tsanas et al.\ applied such a reactive electrolyte stability analysis to a demixing amine blend described with eNRTL and did not parameterize the model \cite{Tsanas2022}.
Perdomo et al.\ describe the reactions implicitly by association in the SAFT-$\gamma$ Mie group-contribution approach and predict ternary phase diagrams of amine solvents that separate into two liquids \cite{perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}.
Neither study applies the test to conventional amines measured as one liquid and described with explicit ions in one equation of state.

% P:introduction-06
This work models the \COtwo solubility in aqueous MEA, DEA, MDEA and AMP with ePC-SAFT, calibrating the four amines on one fixed base and searching each fitted model for a second liquid.
The base is set without amine data.
It holds the parameters of water, \COtwo and the inorganic ions, the \COtwo--water interaction, the equilibrium constants of the inorganic reactions, and the water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions, fixed from osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts as Wangler et al.\ fixed the first of them \cite{Wangler2018}.
The MEA model is fitted jointly to pressures, species fractions, absorption heats, heat capacities and densities, with its amine equilibrium constants fixed at literature correlations.
DEA, MDEA and AMP are each fitted through their own species.
DEA and AMP each retain two MEA parameter values.
