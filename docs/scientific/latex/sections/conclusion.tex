\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% P:conclusion-01
Electrolyte PC-SAFT with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions and explicit carbamate chemistry describes \COtwo partial pressure and liquid speciation in 30 wt\% aqueous \MEA with one set of liquid-phase parameters.
Fitted simultaneously to 48 \COtwo pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and 94 species measurements at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, the nine-species model reaches AARDs of 13.53 and 8.28\,\%.
Outside the fit, it gives 20.27\,\% on the 21 assessment rows at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, and 24.51 and 25.45\,\% on 11 and 12 Wagner rows near 353 and \SI{392}{\kelvin} that were used at no earlier stage.
This extends the ePC-SAFT treatment of reactive MDEA to a carbamate-forming primary amine.

% P:conclusion-02
Born-term removal substantially worsens the bounded refits examined here.
Refitting without the term raises the cost from 32.99 to 98.79 and the fitted-pressure AARD to 53.90\,\%, and the five binary interaction parameters cannot recover the loss within their bounds.
Two interactions of protonated \MEA dominate the tested ablations: fixing the \MEAH--\MEACOO or the \MEAH--water coefficient at zero raises the cost by 134.32 or 75.87, whereas the \MEAH--water temperature slope adds only 0.38 on 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} data.
Including a Born contribution matters far more than which form is used.
Refitted at three sets of fixed Born inputs, the SSM+DS and original Born formulations calibrate comparably, with costs of 31.72--34.58, fitted-pressure AARDs of 13.53--17.31\,\% and fitted-species AARDs of 7.98--8.28\,\%, and their differences at matched inputs, 0.26--1.59, are small against the rise to 98.79 without the term.
Which form has the lower cost depends both on the fitted observations and on the fixed Born inputs.
SSM+DS is lower without the titration bicarbonate data, 32.99 against 34.58, and the original Born term with them, 40.20 against 47.63; without those data, the original Born term is also lower with the three ion Born diameters multiplied by 0.8 or with \(\varepsilon_{\MEA}=24\).

% P:conclusion-03
These results suggest three practical lessons for fitting this model.
First, a comparison of electrolyte terms must state which speciation data are fitted: without the Born term, the model moves toward the titration bicarbonate values, with an AARD of 20.12\,\% on those observations against 59.81\,\% for the fitted parameter set, while losing pressure accuracy.
For the datasets and input sets examined here, the pressure and speciation fits give no consistent preference between the two Born formulations; independent solvation or activity data could help choose between them.
Second, the \MEAH--\MEACOO interaction should be retained when fitting this model to loaded-\MEA data: fixing it at zero produces the largest cost increase among the tested ablations.
Third, data at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} do not set the high-temperature response: refits to these data with literature temperature laws for the amine and \COtwo-hydration reactions give 27.61--29.33\,\% at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, against 20.27\,\% for the fitted parameter set, and fitting the hydration enthalpy recovers only part of that difference, with 23.02\,\%; the temperature response must come from independent temperature data, such as heat of absorption or equilibrium measurements above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.

% P:conclusion-04
The fitted parameter set applies to \COtwo partial pressure and speciation in 30 wt\% \MEA at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}.
Transfer to 15 and 45 wt\% \MEA, liquid density and heat capacity remain outside its supported scope (\cref{sec:results-limits}), and the \HCOthree--water coefficient lies at its bound.
Qualifying density and heat capacity, and adding heat-of-absorption data to the fit, are the next steps toward absorber and stripper calculations.
