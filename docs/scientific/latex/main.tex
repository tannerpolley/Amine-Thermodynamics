\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
We examine whether one explicit-ion electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) formulation with carbamate chemistry can represent the equilibrium, caloric and density properties of loaded aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA).
After separate water, \COtwo--water and dielectric fits, sixteen parameters are fitted jointly to pressure, species, integral absorption heat, density, heat capacity and binary equilibrium data.
For nominal 30 wt\% MEA, one parameter set thus represents \COtwo pressure, the amine and carbamate fractions, integral heat at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and loaded density through \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}.
For nominal 30 wt\% MEA, pressure average absolute relative deviations are 11.86\,\% over 47 fitted observations at 313.00--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin} and 20.55\,\% over 159 observations at 313.00--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, including 112 not fitted but examined during model development.
The shared subset of 94 fitted species fractions gives 7.10\,\% at 293.15--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin}.
The fitted integral-heat root-mean-square error is \SI{1.84}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo; the average absolute relative density deviation is 0.18\,\% over 47 fitted loaded densities, calculated without volume translation, with 44 within twice the assigned scale and three accepted misses at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}.
The 159-pressure deviation lies within the range of pressure deviations reported for related MEA models, which use different data sets and fitting roles.
A fit with wider bounds on the protonated-amine diameter and its water interaction matches the properties similarly but predicts a second liquid phase at some states; narrowing these bounds removes the detected instabilities in a finite search at 162 states between 313.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, with a 0.45\,\% higher objective value.
Carbonate shares, titration bicarbonate, the \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} Arcis integral heats, loaded densities above \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and the unloaded heat capacity at \SI{318.15}{\kelvin} remain discrepant, and pressure deviations reach 29.5 and 56.0\,\% at 15 and 45 wt\% MEA.
The available comparisons do not yet establish suitability across absorber and stripper temperatures.
\end{abstract}
