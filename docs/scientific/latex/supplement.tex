\section{Model equations, inputs and parameter values}\label{sec:s-parameters}
\subsection{Hard-chain, dispersion, association and Debye--H\"uckel contributions}\label{sec:s-contributions}
% P:supplement-contributions-01
These four contributions to the residual Helmholtz energy of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:epcsaft-residual-split}) are used in their published forms.
Throughout, $x_i$ is the mole fraction and $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}$ the segment count of species $i$, $\rho_N=N_{\mathrm A}\rho$ is the number density, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions and $z_i$ is a charge number.
The hard-sphere diameter of a neutral species or chain cation depends on temperature \cite{Gross2001},
\begin{equation}
    d_i=\sigma_i\left[1-0.12\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u_i}{k_BT}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-diameter}
\end{equation}
with the water diameter $\sigma_w(T)$ of the main text in place of a constant $\sigma_i$; a single-sphere ion has the temperature-independent diameter $d_i=0.88\sigma_i$.

% P:supplement-contributions-02
The hard-chain term is \cite{Gross2001}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}=\bar m\,a^{\mathrm{hs}}-\sum_i x_i\left(m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}-1\right)\ln g_{ii}^{\mathrm{hs}},
    \qquad
    \bar m=\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}},
    \label{eq:hard-chain}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_0}\left[
    \frac{3\zeta_1\zeta_2}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3^2}-\zeta_0\right)\ln\left(1-\zeta_3\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\frac{3\zeta_2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\right)^2\frac{2\zeta_2^2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^3},
    \qquad
    \zeta_n=\frac{\pi}{6}\rho_N\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}}d_i^n,
    \quad n=0,\dots,3,
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-contact}
\end{equation}
where $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}$ is the hard-sphere pair distribution function at contact and $\eta=\zeta_3$ is the packing fraction.

% P:supplement-contributions-03
The dispersion term is the second-order perturbation expansion of Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    =-2\pi\rho_N I_1\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\sigma_{ij}^3
    -\pi\rho_N\bar m\,C_1I_2\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\left(\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\right)^2\sigma_{ij}^3 ,
    \label{eq:dispersion}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    C_1=\left[1+\bar m\frac{8\eta-2\eta^2}{\left(1-\eta\right)^4}
    +\left(1-\bar m\right)\frac{20\eta-27\eta^2+12\eta^3-2\eta^4}{\left[\left(1-\eta\right)\left(2-\eta\right)\right]^2}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:dispersion-compressibility}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I_1=\sum_{k=0}^{6}a_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    I_2=\sum_{k=0}^{6}b_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    a_k(\bar m)=a_{0k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}a_{1k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}\frac{\bar m-2}{\bar m}a_{2k},
    \label{eq:dispersion-integrals}
\end{equation}
where $b_k(\bar m)$ has the form of $a_k(\bar m)$, and $a_{0k}$--$a_{2k}$ and $b_{0k}$--$b_{2k}$ are their universal constants.
The pair diameter $\sigma_{ij}$ and the pair energy $u_{ij}$ are those of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:dispersion-combining-rule}).

% P:supplement-contributions-04
Hydrogen bonding follows Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite{Kleiner2007}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{assoc}}=\sum_i x_i\sum_{A\in i}\left(\ln X_A-\frac{X_A}{2}+\frac12\right),
    \qquad
    X_A=\left[1+\rho_N\sum_j x_j\sum_{B\in j}X_B\Delta^{AB}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:association}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta^{AB}=g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}\,\sigma_{ij}^3\,\kappa^{AB}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\epsilon^{AB}}{k_BT}\right)-1\right],
    \label{eq:association-strength}
\end{equation}
where $X_A$ is the fraction of molecules $i$ not bonded at site $A$ and $\Delta^{AB}$ is the strength of a bond between site $A$ on molecule $i$ and site $B$ on molecule $j$.
Cross-association between an amine species and water uses the Wolbach--Sandler rule \cite{Kleiner2007},
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon^{AB}_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(\epsilon^{AB}_i+\epsilon^{AB}_j\right),
    \qquad
    \kappa^{AB}_{ij}=\sqrt{\kappa^{AB}_i\kappa^{AB}_j}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}}{\sigma_{ij}}\right)^3 .
    \label{eq:wolbach-sandler}
\end{equation}

% P:supplement-contributions-05
The Debye--H\"uckel term of Cameretti et al.\ describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ \cite{Cameretti2005}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{DH}}=-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm D}e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2\chi_i,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm D}^2=\frac{e^2\rho_N}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2,
    \label{eq:debye-huckel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \chi_i=\frac{3}{\left(\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^3}
    \left[\frac32+\ln\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    -2\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    +\frac12\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^2\right],
    \label{eq:debye-huckel-chi}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the elementary charge, $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity, $\kappa_{\mathrm D}$ is the inverse Debye screening length and $\chi_i$ accounts for the finite ion size.

\subsection{MEA and the shared species}\label{sec:s-mea-species}
% P:supplement-18
Neutral MEA parameters are the 2B values of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, except the association volume described below \cite{Baygi2015}.
\COtwo segment count, diameter and dispersion energy are from Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}.
The segment diameters and dispersion energies of bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, and the water interactions of hydronium and hydroxide, are those of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}, which Uyan et al.\ also use \cite{Uyan2015}; the water interactions of bicarbonate and carbonate are fitted to salt data (Section~\ref{sec:s-salt}).
Held et al.\ determined these water interactions with their own water parameters; they are used here unchanged with the water parameters of this work.
Ion segment count one and the diameter ties are modeling assumptions.
The Born diameter of hydronium is that of the hydrogen ion in Figiel et al.\ \cite{Figiel2025}.
The Born diameter of hydroxide, \SI{\res{mea-fixed-hydroxide-born-diameter}}{\angstrom}, was calculated here from the Born equation, $d^{\mathrm{Born}}=z^2e^2N_{\mathrm A}(1-1/\varepsilon_r)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0|\Delta G^{\mathrm{hyd}}|)$, where $N_{\mathrm A}$ is the Avogadro constant, with the hydration free energy $\Delta G^{\mathrm{hyd}}$ of $-106.4$ kcal/mol of Palascak and Shields \cite{palascakAccurateExperimentalValues2004} and the relative permittivity of water at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, $\varepsilon_r=78.37$.
The Born diameters of bicarbonate and carbonate are set at \SI{3.0}{\angstrom}.
With all other parameters fixed, the tested bicarbonate diameters (2.5, 3.5 and \SI{4.0}{\angstrom}) and carbonate diameters (2.5 and \SI{4.0}{\angstrom}) change the objective by at most \res{mea-born-objective-change}\,\% and the fitted \COtwo partial pressures by at most \res{mea-born-bicarbonate-pressure-change}\,\% for bicarbonate and \res{mea-born-carbonate-pressure-change}\,\% for carbonate.
None of these inputs is fitted in the calibrations of this work.
All other interactions are zero by assumption except the fixed water interactions listed below.
\Cref{tab:supplement-species-parameters,tab:supplement-interaction-matrix} list the exact values.
Water's solvation factor and ion-region permittivity follow Figiel et al.; unit solvation factors are set here for the other components \cite{Figiel2025}.
The ion packing and Debye--H\"uckel diameters follow the ties stated in the main text.

\subsection{Water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions from sodium-salt data}\label{sec:s-salt}
% P:supplement-salt-data
The water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fitted to osmotic coefficients of aqueous sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate, with no amine in the calculation.
The data are the values that Peiper and Pitzer tabulate from their ion-interaction model at 278.15, 288.15, 298.15, 308.15 and \SI{318.15}{\kelvin} and nominal molalities of 0.01--\SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram}, 45 for each salt \cite{peiperThermodynamicsAqueousCarbonate1982}; they are model-derived reference values, not direct measurements.
Each row is calculated at the equilibrium ionic composition printed with it, the dissolved \COtwo is removed as an ideal solute, and the residual scale is 0.01.
The sodium and chloride ion parameters and the interactions between sodium and the anions are those of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}; like the amine ions, these ions have Born and Debye--H\"uckel diameters equal to their segment diameter and a unit solvation factor.

% P:supplement-salt-fit
Each fitted interaction is linear in temperature, $k(T)=a_0+a_1(T-\SI{298.15}{\kelvin})$, with bounds of $\pm1$ on $a_0$ and $\pm\SI{0.02}{\per\kelvin}$ on $a_1$, and the three fits are made in sequence.
The water--sodium intercept is fitted first, to \res{salt-sodium-chloride-fitted-osmotic-row-count} osmotic coefficients of sodium chloride at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} and 0.1--\SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram} \cite{HamerWu1972}, with the slope of Held et al.\ retained; it ends on its lower bound, $\res{salt-sodium-intercept-at-lower-bound-supplement}$, and the sodium chloride coefficients deviate by \res{salt-sodium-chloride-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\%.
The water--bicarbonate interaction is fitted next and is not on a bound: $a_0=\res{salt-water-bicarbonate-original-intercept}$ and $a_1=\SI{\res{salt-water-bicarbonate-linear-slope}}{\per\kelvin}$, which is \res{salt-water-bicarbonate-value-at-reference-temperature} at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, with an AARD of \res{salt-sodium-bicarbonate-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\% on the \res{salt-sodium-bicarbonate-fitted-osmotic-row-count} sodium bicarbonate coefficients.
The water--carbonate interaction is fitted last: its intercept ends on the lower bound, $a_0=\res{salt-water-carbonate-intercept-at-lower-bound-supplement}$, with $a_1=\SI{\res{salt-water-carbonate-linear-slope}}{\per\kelvin}$, which is $\res{salt-water-carbonate-value-at-reference-temperature}$ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, and the \res{salt-sodium-carbonate-fitted-osmotic-row-count} sodium carbonate coefficients deviate by \res{salt-sodium-carbonate-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\%, the calculated values being low.
For \res{salt-mixed-assessment-available-osmotic-row-count} coefficients of mixed bicarbonate--carbonate solutions that were not fitted, the deviation is \res{salt-mixed-salts-osmotic-aard-supplement}\,\%.

% P:supplement-salt-target
The printed water activities of Peiper and Pitzer satisfy $-\ln a_w/M_w=\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}\sum_im_i=\phi_{\mathrm{st}}\sum_s\nu_sm_s$ within printed precision, where the first sum runs over the equilibrium solute species and the second over the stoichiometric salt.
The fitted target is the equilibrium coefficient of the ions: the molality of dissolved \COtwo, treated as ideal, is subtracted from the equilibrium numerator, and the remainder is divided by the charge-closed sum of the ionic molalities.

\subsection{DEA, MDEA and AMP}
% P:supplement-transfer-parameters
Table~\ref{tab:supplement-transfer-parameters} lists the value and unit of each amine-specific parameter of the DEA, MDEA and AMP models, including the permittivity coefficients and the coefficients of the carbamate hydrolysis (R4) and amine dissociation (R5) correlations.
A molality-basis constant follows from the mole-fraction constant of its source by
\begin{equation}
    \ln K_{m,r}=\ln K_{x,r}-\Big(\sum_{i\ne\HtwoO}\nu_{ri}\Big)\ln\!\left(M_{\HtwoO}m^{\circ}\right),
    \label{eq:molality-conversion}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_{ri}$ is the stoichiometric number of species $i$ in reaction $r$, $M_{\HtwoO}$ is the molar mass of water and $m^{\circ}=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram}$.

At \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} the conditional standard errors are \res{dea-r4-log-constant-conditional-standard-error} for DEA and \res{amp-r4-log-constant-conditional-standard-error} for AMP.
The measurements behind the DEA correlation span 303--\SI{331}{\kelvin} and the model estimates behind the AMP correlation 313.15--\SI{373.15}{\kelvin}, so the first and last of these temperatures are extrapolations.
The dispersion energy of \ce{DEACOO-}, \SI{\res{dea-carbamate-dispersion-energy-supplement}}{\kelvin}, is that of \ce{MEACOO-} in the MEA model, and its segment diameter is the \ce{MEACOO-} diameter of that model, \SI{\res{dea-transferred-mea-carbamate-diameter}}{\angstrom}, multiplied by \res{dea-carbamate-segment-volume-scaling}, the cube root of the ratio of $m^{\mathrm{seg}}\sigma^3$ of DEA to that of MEA.

\begin{equation}
 \ln K_{r,P^\circ}^{\mathrm{ig}}=\ln K_{r,\mathrm{src}}+\Big(\sum_{i\ne w}\nu_{ri}\Big)\ln(M_wm^\circ)+\Gamma_r+\Delta\nu_r\ln(P_{\mathrm{src}}/P^\circ),
 \label{eq:pressure-standard-conversion}
\end{equation}
where $K_{r,\mathrm{src}}$ is the source correlation on the molality basis and $P_{\mathrm{src}}=\SI{100}{\kilo\pascal}$ for every reaction, so the last term vanishes; the values of $\ln K_{r,\mathrm{src}}$ and $\Gamma_r$ in Table~\ref{tab:mea-reference-state} are on the common molality basis, and their sum is the same on the mole-fraction basis of the R1 and R3 sources.
Its temperature derivative gives the reaction enthalpies of the last rows of Table~\ref{tab:mea-reference-state}, $\Delta_rh_{P^\circ}^{\mathrm{ig}}=RT^2\,\mathrm{d}\ln K_{r,\mathrm{src}}/\mathrm{d}T+RT^2(\partial\Gamma_r/\partial T)_{P_{\mathrm{src}}}$, as the sum of a source term and a fugacity term.
Because $P_{\mathrm{src}}$ is fixed, the reference chemical potentials depend on temperature only, the phases compared in a stability search or a two-liquid calculation share one Gibbs energy, and its pressure derivative at fixed amounts is $V_{\mathrm{EOS}}$ (main-text \cref{main-eq:pressure-tracking}).

\section{Phase stability}\label{sec:s-phase}
\subsection{Search conditions and results}
% P:supplement-phase-method
Trial phases obey reaction equilibrium and electroneutrality and exclude species that cannot form from the feed at the searched state.
Charge-neutral initial liquid compositions include a uniform composition and near-pure neutral-species compositions; initial vapor compositions contain only the three neutral species.
Only a negative tangent-plane distance confirmed by a finite, mass-conserving transfer that lowers the Gibbs energy by more than its numerical error estimate is counted as a detected instability.
A set of trial compositions at which other parameter sets lower the Gibbs energy is also tested; a negative distance at one of these compositions can show that a state is unstable, but not toward which kind of phase, and it is reported separately.

% P:supplement-phase-grid
For MEA, the liquid grid contains \res{mea-liquid-grid-dense-requests} states at 15, 25, 35 and 45 wt\% MEA, 313.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin} in \SI{10}{\kelvin} steps, and loading $10^{-5}$ plus 0.025--0.6 mol/mol in 0.025 steps, at 1.05 and 3.0 times the bubble pressure of the parameter set being searched.
Another \res{mea-liquid-grid-census-requests} states use the composition of the liquid--vapor search at those two ratios.
A second liquid grid, offset from the first, contains \res{mea-liquid-grid-offset-requests} states at 20, 30 and 40 wt\%, 318.15--\SI{388.15}{\kelvin} and loadings 0.0125--0.5875, at 1.5 times the bubble pressure.
None of these \res{mea-liquid-grid-requests-supplement} liquid states has a detected instability.

% P:supplement-phase-census
The MEA liquid--vapor search contains six temperatures, 313.15, 318.15, 333.15, 353.15, 373.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, and nine loadings, $10^{-5}$, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.256, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 mol/mol.
Each temperature--loading pair uses three pressures, 0.50, 0.80 and 0.95 times a fixed reference bubble pressure, which is not the bubble pressure of the MEA model.
The MEA model is searched at these \res{mea-liquid-vapor-census-requests-supplement} states.
The feed is 9.7292821 mol MEA, 76.9751925 mol water and $9.7292821\alpha$ mol \COtwo.
The equilibrium at these states is liquid plus vapor at \res{mea-census-two-phase-parent-count-supplement} and one liquid at \res{mea-single-liquid-census-parents-supplement}, and no search detects an instability.
A separate search at the \res{mea-measured-state-search-requests-supplement} measured MEA states detects none.

% P:supplement-phase-worked-control
The Hartono et al.\ state at \SI{373}{\kelvin}, \SI{\res{amp-worked-control-pressure}}{\kilo\pascal} and a loading of \res{amp-worked-control-loading} shows how a split is confirmed under the fit without the pressures of Tong et al.\ and of Hartono et al.\ above \SI{353}{\kelvin}.
The smallest tangent-plane distance over the trial phases is $\res{amp-worked-control-trial-distance}$, in units of $RT$ per mole.
The separate two-liquid calculation at the same feed, temperature and pressure gives liquids of \res{amp-worked-control-first-density} and \SI{\res{amp-worked-control-second-density}}{\kilo\mole\per\cubic\metre} with water mole fractions of \res{amp-worked-control-first-water} and \res{amp-worked-control-second-water} and amounts of \res{amp-worked-control-first-amount} and \SI{\res{amp-worked-control-second-amount}}{\mole} for a feed of one mole of AMP.
Its residuals and element balances meet the row tolerance of $\res{amp-worked-control-row-tolerance}$, and the Gibbs energy over $RT$ falls by \SI{\res{amp-worked-control-gibbs-decrease}}{\mole} against an estimated numerical error of $\res{amp-worked-control-gibbs-error}$\,mol.

% P:supplement-phase-decision-rule
A split is confirmed only when the two-liquid calculation meets the row tolerance with two positive phase amounts, its pressure, normalization and charge residuals and scaled element balances meet their tolerances, the two phases differ in composition or density beyond a fixed coincidence tolerance, each phase density matches a liquid root of the equation of state at its composition, and the decrease in Gibbs energy exceeds the summed numerical error estimates of the single liquid and the split.
A trial that returns to the present liquid has a tangent-plane distance of zero and counts as no descent.
A negative distance without a confirmed split does not identify the new phase.
A state is unclassified when its search ends in an exception, or when the search cannot assign the present phase or a trial at its smallest distance to the liquid or the vapor density root; a smallest distance of zero within its numerical resolution is then not known to belong to the present liquid.
