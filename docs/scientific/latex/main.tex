\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory model is calibrated for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo), and its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters are held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.
The shared water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fixed from sodium-salt osmotic coefficients.
With every reaction reference at \SI{100}{\kilo\pascal}, equilibrium, density and enthalpy follow from one Gibbs energy, as verified numerically at fitted states.
The MEA model has 11 fitted parameters and literature equilibrium constants; its fitted \COtwo partial pressures at 30 wt\% deviate by 11.34\,\%, and the model is less accurate at other concentrations and stripper temperatures.
Fitted loaded-solution pressures deviate by 42.75\,\% for DEA, 4.39\,\% for MDEA and 10.82\,\% for AMP; the MDEA fit is provisional, because only one of 64 drawn starts could be evaluated.
Phase-stability searches find no instability for MEA or at 666 bubble-pressure-scaled AMP states, and no confirmed MDEA split.
For DEA they confirm splits at four states of measured density: the single-liquid calculation matches those densities while the model's own phase calculation favors two liquids, contrary to treating the samples as one liquid.
A first AMP fit, with pressures to \SI{353}{\kelvin}, split at 363--\SI{393}{\kelvin}; a refit adding 18 pressures at 373--\SI{393}{\kelvin} and 40 at 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin} confirms none, with four of six measured-pressure controls unresolved.
Phase-stability searches show where a fit is extrapolated or fails.
The models are calibrations, not design tools.

\end{abstract}
