\section{Results and discussion}\label{sec:mea-system-modeling-results}
\subsection{Selected endpoint and the phase/density trade-off}\label{sec:results-tradeoff}
% P:results-01
A7 meets every acceptance limit in \cref{tab:acceptance-limits} except loaded density, and no descent is resolved at any family state.
On the paired finite search, A6 has resolved descent at 12 of 73 states and A7 at none (\cref{fig:phase-family}).
Most A6 descent points lie at \SI{333.15}{\kelvin} (\cref{fig:phase-family}), where separately seeded split solves confirmed lower-Gibbs-energy two-liquid states at loadings 0.4 and 0.5 (Section~S4).
No descent was numerically resolved in 657 trials at 73 specified states (313.15--\SI{333.15}{\kelvin}, $\alpha$ $10^{-5}$--0.5).
There were no failed A7 trial optimizers; its final classifications comprise 69 two-phase and four single-liquid states.
Those two-phase classifications are not evidence of two liquid phases.
The absence of resolved descent applies only at the specified temperature, pressure and loading combinations in Section~S4.
No stability claim accompanies pressure calculations at temperatures above \SI{333.15}{\kelvin} or loadings above $\alpha=0.5$, and none is made at the \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} density misses.

% P:results-02
A6 meets the loaded-density requirement at all 47 fitted states, whereas A7 meets it at 44 (\cref{fig:density-errors,tab:density-failures}).
The three failures are 0.40--0.53\,\% below the measured densities at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, beyond the temperature range of the phase family.
\Cref{tab:acceptance-limits} shows that the common-subset cost, pressure/species deviations, heat checks and binary constraints remain within their respective limits.
This is an observed trade-off between these local endpoints, not a minimum unavoidable density penalty for excluding phase descent.
Changing ion sizes, interactions and reaction coordinates together also prevents attribution to an isolated Born or heat effect.

% P:results-03
A fixed-parameter size probe around the first restricted endpoint explains why reducing one diameter alone did not remove all density failures.
At sampled \MEAH segment diameters from \SI{5.49}{\angstrom} down to \SI{5.35}{\angstrom}, smaller size raised the loaded densities, improving the warm failures while creating positive cold-density deviations.
None of those sampled vectors met every loaded-density limit, although none had resolved descent on its finite phase search.
The second joint fit at the lower size bound reduced the worst miss from $2.70s$ to $2.43s$ and retained the same three failing states.
These probes neither exclude an untested passing size nor certify the parameter box; Section~S5 gives the sampled values and loading-basis sensitivity.

The 47 fitted loaded densities give AARD 0.177022\,\%, but the three failures in \cref{tab:density-failures} remain visible despite that small average.
All five \COtwo-free densities lie within the declared \SI{1.0}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} absolute-error limit (\cref{tab:acceptance-limits}).
\Cref{fig:density-errors} also shows all 210 A7 densities, separating fitted rows from non-fitted and excluded comparisons; the fitted $2s$ requirement is not extended to every row.
For the 68 Amundsen observations matched in temperature, loading and unloaded-solvent mass fraction, the untranslated F1 densities have AARD 11.8433\,\% and A7 0.490695\,\% (Table~S3).

All 67 Han densities are comparisons outside the objective, with RMSE \SI{5.905}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre}.

\subsection{Comparisons outside the objective}\label{sec:results-comparisons}
% P:results-08
The canonical 159-pressure view has AARD 20.5537\,\%, mixing fitted observations with previously accessed comparisons.
Wagner pressures near 353 and \SI{392}{\kelvin} give 22.2026 and 9.2542\,\%; their mean logarithmic biases are $-0.252026$ and $-0.0718582$.
These rows were assessed after A7 was selected but had been examined during model development, so their deviations are not independent validation.
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, canonical and packet pressure AARDs are 24.3499 and 27.5380\,\% on 54 and 20 rows, respectively.
The cohorts overlap but differ in membership and source-state basis; Section~S2 lists each view separately.
Their mean logarithmic biases also differ in sign, so a single high-temperature bias direction would hide source dependence.

% P:results-09
The previously accessed concentration-transfer comparisons give AARDs of 29.5070\,\% at 15 wt\% and 56.0168\,\% at 45 wt\%.
Their mean logarithmic biases are $-0.204430$ and $+0.148690$: the average logarithmic pressure deviation changes sign between concentrations.
In particular, the 45 wt\% result limits concentration transfer despite the agreement on fitted 30 wt\% rows.
None of these higher-temperature or concentration comparisons extends the finite phase-search domain.

% P:results-10
Arcis heat RMSE is \SI{8.11440}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo, with calculated-minus-observed bias \SI{+7.18190}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}; A7 is less exothermic on average.
The pressure-separated RMSEs are \SI{10.3310}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} and \SI{3.36566}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} at \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}.
This discrepancy remains visible beside the smaller Vinjarapu calibration error in \cref{fig:heat}.
Arcis was used in acceptance and cannot supply independent physical validation or isolate a pressure effect from the different source conditions.

% P:results-11
The 19 report-only Matin pool rows give AARD 67.4808\,\% and mean log ratio $-1.26582$ (\cref{fig:speciation}).
The modeled bicarbonate/carbonate pool is therefore systematically low on this comparison, even though other titration species enter the fit.
The ten Jakobsen carbonate-share observations are all retained: calculated/observed share has mean 0.07305 and range 0.00633--0.26703.
The calculation uses unloaded \MEA mass fraction 0.27142748 and the NMR loading at each sample, rather than interpolation at the printed maximum loading.
An earlier version of this comparison used nine of the ten observations; the excluded one is retained here.
These shares differ from the six amount ratios to total amine for \MEAH, \MEACOO and \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}} at each of two Jakobsen states used for calibration.
The carbonate, pool, heat-capacity and 45 wt\% discrepancies together bound the use of the selected parameterization.

\subsection{Parameters and local identifiability}\label{sec:results-uncertainty}
% P:results-12
Six coordinates are at bounds: \MEAH--water and \MEAH--\MEACOO interactions, \MEAH segment diameter, both fitted Born diameters and total R5 enthalpy shift.
The free bicarbonate--water coefficient is $0.7253$ with conditional standard error $1.2671$; the R2 total shift is approximately $-1.4\pm5.3$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, and the \MEAH--water slope is $-41.93$~\si{\kelvin} with conditional standard error \SI{60.29}{\kelvin}.
These local uncertainties and the correlations in Section~S6 limit physical interpretation of individual coefficients.
They do not identify unique ion dimensions or reaction enthalpies, and they do not supply prediction intervals for pressures or process duties.

\subsection{Predecessor and published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-13
F1 is the predecessor used to establish the common-subset acceptance limits and the native-density baseline; Section~S1 preserves its separate six-fit study and the matched F1--A7 comparison.
Those calculations concern a different parameterization and supply no A7 comparison of Born forms, pool inclusion or source reaction laws.
Among the published models in \cref{tab:literature-model-comparison}, Zhang et al.\ provide the closest multi-property calibration, combining solubility, absorption heat, heat capacity and NMR information \cite[Sect.~3.3, Tables~6 and 9]{Zhang2011}.
Nasrifar and Tafazzol provide a PC-SAFT predecessor with ideal reaction speciation and ions lumped into an effective water component \cite[Sects.~3, 4 and 6, Table~3]{Nasrifar2010}.
Lloret et al.\ additionally estimated absorption enthalpy from the pressure--temperature derivative after their soft-SAFT solubility fit \cite[Sect.~4.7, Eq.~26, Fig.~10b]{lloretConsistentTransferableThermodynamic2017}.
The data sets, operators, fitted properties and access histories differ, so no accuracy ranking against published models is made.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:results-limits}
% P:results-14
The result is one adopted local parameterization with joint calibration, numerical verification and a finite phase test.
Its three warm-density failures remain accepted with disclosure, while the phase family covers only its stated temperature/pressure/loading combinations.
The chosen bounds, single starts, conditional covariance, assumed dielectric transfer and source reaction extrapolations prevent a global-optimum or broad predictive claim.
Comparisons examined during model development and the Arcis acceptance check are not independent validation.
Physical phase validation and absorber/stripper performance validation remain absent.
