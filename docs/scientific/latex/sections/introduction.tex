\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% P:introduction-01
Monoethanolamine (\MEA) in water is the reference solvent for \COtwo absorption, and its vapor--liquid equilibrium (VLE) has been measured extensively~\cite{Jou1995,Mamun2005,Hilliard2008,Aronu2011,Xu2011}.
Absorbed \COtwo reacts with water and amine to form protonated amine, carbamate, bicarbonate and carbonate.
The equilibrium \COtwo partial pressure at a given loading therefore depends on both the phase equilibrium and the liquid speciation.
A model can reproduce the pressure with an implausible distribution of \MEA, \MEAH, \MEACOO, \HCOthree and \COthree, so speciation measurements provide a separate test of the same thermodynamic state.

% P:introduction-02
Speciation measurements for loaded \MEA differ in what they observe.
Quantitative NMR resolves carbamate, but fast proton exchange merges \MEA with \MEAH and bicarbonate with carbonate into common peaks: B\"ottinger et al.\ report the amine sum~\cite[p.~134]{Bottinger2008}, whereas Jakobsen et al.\ separate each pair through calibrated chemical shifts~\cite[pp.~9895--9896]{Jakobsen2005}.
Matin et al.\ obtained all four major species from acid and base titrations combined with a total carbon measurement~\cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
They reported agreement with NMR for free amine, protonated amine and carbamate, and bicarbonate higher than the NMR values~\cite[pp.~6616--6617]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
When such observations enter a parameter fit, the choice of which measurements to fit can affect which model form is selected.

% P:introduction-03
Excess-Gibbs-energy models with chemical equilibria represent this system with fitted interaction parameters.
B\"ottinger et al.\ refitted the \MEA carbamate-formation constant to NMR speciation while retaining the Bates--Pinching protonation constant and published \MEA interaction parameters~\cite{Bottinger2008}.
Zhang et al.\ fitted an electrolyte non-random two-liquid (NRTL) liquid model, with vapor fugacities from the perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (PC-SAFT), to \COtwo solubility, heat of absorption, heat capacity and NMR speciation together~\cite[Sects.~2--3.3, Tables~6 and 9]{Zhang2011}, and Akula et al.\ fitted an electrolyte NRTL model jointly to \COtwo pressure and heat of absorption, leaving NMR speciation and heat capacity outside the fit~\cite{Akula2023a}.
Equations of state based on statistical associating fluid theory (SAFT) describe association and chain formation within one Helmholtz energy~\cite{Gross2001,Gross2002}.
Binary \MEA--water and \MEA--hydrocarbon mixtures have also been described with simplified PC-SAFT and cubic-plus-association equations of state~\cite[Introduction and Conclusion]{avlundApplicationAssociationModels2011}.
For loaded \MEA, SAFT models with variable-range potentials (SAFT-VR) represented the reactions implicitly as association between \MEA and \COtwo sites~\cite{MacDowell2010,Rodriguez2012}, and Wang et al.\ used pseudo-chemical association in a Peng--Robinson cubic-plus-association equation of state and predicted the liquid speciation~\cite{wangImprovementPRCPAEquation2018}.
Chremos et al.\ used the SAFT-$\gamma$ square-well group-contribution approach to fit \COtwo solubility and predict carbamate and bicarbonate from the fractions of bonded association sites~\cite[Sect.~3.6]{chremosModellingPhaseChemical2016}.
Perdomo et al.\ extended this association-based treatment with SAFT-$\gamma$ Mie group parameters, fitting phase-equilibrium data and predicting the species concentrations without fitting speciation data~\cite[Sects.~2.2--2.3 and 4.3.3]{perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}.
These species predictions represent associated, electrically neutral ion pairs; they do not require separate ionic activity coefficients.
Lloret et al.\ fitted reactive \COtwo association sites in soft-SAFT to ternary equilibrium data and calculated absorption enthalpy from the pressure--temperature derivative, and tested concentration transfer~\cite[Sect.~4.7, Eq.~26, Figs.~10--11]{lloretConsistentTransferableThermodynamic2017}.
PC-SAFT and Huang--Radosz SAFT (SAFT-HR) models instead combined neutral-species fugacities with ideal reaction equilibria~\cite{Nasrifar2010,Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}.
Najafloo and Zarei set reaction activities equal to mole fractions and compared ternary solubility after selecting pure-\MEA parameters and binary interactions~\cite[Sects.~2 and 3.1--3.3]{Najafloo2018}.
Nasrifar and Tafazzol solved the reaction equilibria of aqueous \MEA, diethanolamine and methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) as an ideal liquid, lumped the ions with water and predicted \COtwo and \ce{H2S} solubility from PC-SAFT pure-component parameters and amine--water interaction parameters from binary vapor--liquid equilibria, without parameters fitted to the loaded solutions~\cite[Sects.~3 and 6, Table~3]{Nasrifar2010}.
The neutral-species PC-SAFT and SAFT-HR treatments do not carry explicit ion activities.

% P:introduction-07
Other equations of state for loaded alkanolamines carry ions explicitly.
T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros added a Debye--H\"uckel term, with protonated-amine and carbamate ions, to a cubic two-state association equation of state for \MEA, diethanolamine and MDEA~\cite{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013}, and Neumann et al.\ combined a Helmholtz-energy equation of state for \MEA--water--\COtwo with an electrolyte NRTL model whose long-range part contains Pitzer--Debye--H\"uckel and Born terms~\cite{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Najafloo et al.\ applied an electrolyte SAFT-HR equation of state with explicit carbamate ions to MDEA blended with a carbamate-forming diamine~\cite{najaflooMeasuringSolubilityCarbon2015}, and Raeispour Shirazi and Lotfollahi added mean-spherical-approximation and Born terms to PC-SAFT for MDEA, with ion interaction parameters fitted to bubble pressures~\cite{raeispourshiraziModelingH2SSolubility2019,raeispourshiraziApplicationEPC_SAFTMBEoS2020}.
These approaches motivate testing a PC-SAFT treatment with separate ion activities for carbamate-forming \MEA.

% P:introduction-04
Electrolyte PC-SAFT (ePC-SAFT) adds a Debye--H\"uckel contribution for charged species~\cite{Cameretti2005}, and Held et al.\ estimated ion parameters from densities and osmotic coefficients of aqueous salt solutions, with dispersion between ions of the same sign excluded~\cite{Held2014}.
With explicit ions and activity-based reaction equilibria, ePC-SAFT described acid-gas solubility in aqueous MDEA from parameters obtained from pure components and binary subsystems~\cite{Uyan2015,Wangler2018}, and was extended to competitive \ce{H2S}--\COtwo absorption~\cite{Cleeton2020}.
B\"ulow et al.\ added the Born term for the solvation Gibbs energy of ions to MDEA blends with sulfolane or an ionic liquid, which reduced the number of binary interaction parameters~\cite{Bulow2021a}.
Related work added concentration-dependent permittivity to the Born and Debye--H\"uckel terms~\cite{Bulow2020,Rueben2024} and predicted \COtwo solubility in aqueous and organic salt solutions~\cite{Schick2023}.
Figiel et al.\ modified the Born term to account for the solvation-shell volume and dielectric saturation near an ion (SSM+DS), with parameters estimated from ion solvation and salt activity data in water and alcohols at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}~\cite{Figiel2025}; a later correction restored a missing bracket and the ion-region permittivity in its Eq.~(7)~\cite{Figiel2026Correction}.
MDEA is a tertiary amine and forms no carbamate.
The natural next step in this line is the primary amine \MEA, in which carbamate formation makes the ion population and the solvent composition change together with \COtwo loading.

% P:introduction-05
Density and phase descent test different consequences of the same fitted Helmholtz energy.
A pressure/speciation fit need not reproduce liquid volume, and a converged equilibrium state can still admit a lower-Gibbs-energy phase arrangement.
These checks must therefore accompany a joint fit when the intended use requires both volumetric agreement and a reacting liquid at prescribed conditions.
A finite search can reject a parameterization by finding descent; the absence of resolved descent at its specified states does not establish global phase stability.

% P:introduction-06
This work asks whether one nine-species ePC-SAFT parameterization with explicit carbamate chemistry can jointly represent pressure, speciation, integral heat, density, heat capacity and the \MEA--water binary while showing no resolved descent on a declared phase family.
The selected parameterization, A7, is compared with an earlier local endpoint, A6, to show the observed phase/density trade-off.
The contributions are a joint multi-property calibration with explicit observation operators and weights, a paired finite phase search, and disclosure of the density failures and other discrepancies that remain.
All fitted deviations describe calibration; comparisons outside the objective retain their data-access history.
The resulting scope is thermodynamic assessment of these local endpoints, without qualification for absorber or stripper calculations.
