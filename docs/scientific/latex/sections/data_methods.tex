\section{Data and parameter estimation}\label{sec:data-methods}
\subsection{Experimental data}\label{sec:data-roles}
% P:methods-01
Loading is $\alpha=n_{\COtwo,\mathrm{added}}/n_{\mathrm{amine,total}}$, and the amine content is the amine mass fraction of the unloaded solution, written in wt\%.
\emph{Fitted} means that an observation enters the objective of the reported fit, and \emph{not fitted} that it is outside that objective.
\emph{Comparison only} marks a different quantity or different conditions from those the model was built for, and \emph{development} data are observations outside the objective that informed a choice of parameters or of an initial parameter set.
Every observation was examined during model development, so no comparison in this paper is an independent validation or a blind test.
\Cref{tab:mea-data} lists the MEA data with their roles and deviations, and \cref{tab:transfer-deviations} those of DEA, MDEA and AMP.
The objectives contain \res{mea-objective-observations-methods} observations for MEA, \res{dea-objective-observations-methods} for DEA, \res{mdea-objective-observations-methods} for MDEA and \res{amp-reported-objective-row-count-methods} for AMP; Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-mea-comparison} gives the MEA sets that are compared and are not pressures.
The \res{mea-kiepe-pressure-count-methods} \COtwo--water pressures of Kiepe et al.\ in the MEA objective were fitted beforehand and contribute a constant to it, so \res{mea-responsive-observations-methods-data} of its observations respond to the fitted parameters.

% P:methods-selection
The fitted sets were chosen amine by amine, not by one rule.
The MEA objective contains \COtwo partial pressures only at nominal 30 wt\% and 313--\SI{333}{\kelvin}; the pressures at 15 and 45 wt\% and at higher temperatures are compared but not fitted.
The DEA objective has \res{dea-fitted-loaded-pressure-source-count-methods} loaded-pressure sources, \res{dea-lawson-fitted-pressure-count-methods} pressures of Lawson and Garst, \res{dea-seo-hong-fitted-pressure-count-methods} of Seo and Hong and \res{dea-lal-fitted-pressure-count-methods} of Lal et al.; the cell of Lawson and Garst also contained methane, which the calculation omits (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-corrections}), and their \res{dea-lawson-hot-pressure-row-count-methods} pressures at \SI{394}{\kelvin} are not fitted.
For MDEA the loaded total pressures of Kuranov et al.\ at \SI{413}{\kelvin} were set aside as an extrapolation.
For AMP all loaded total pressures of Hartono et al.\ and of Tong et al., 313--\SI{393}{\kelvin}, are fitted.
The fitted loaded pressures of MDEA and AMP are total pressures, and their \COtwo partial pressures are not fitted.

% P:methods-range
A calculation is attempted for every observation, including those outside the measured temperature range of an equilibrium constant (\cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants}).
For MEA the reacting calculations span 293.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, so R4 and R5 are extrapolated above \SI{323.15}{\kelvin}; for DEA, MDEA and AMP, Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-ranges} reports the deviations inside and outside each range separately.

% P:methods-02
Each observable follows its experimental definition: a sum reported by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) is compared with the same calculated sum \cite{Bottinger2008}, and bubble calculations omit carrier gas, so calculated $y_{\COtwo}P$ is compared with a measured \COtwo partial pressure and calculated $P$ with a measured total pressure.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-corrections} gives the conventions of the individual sources and the excluded observations.

\subsection{Calculated quantities}\label{sec:heat-method}
% P:methods-heat-01
Pressures are reactive bubble pressures at the measured temperature and apparent composition, and species fractions are those of a single liquid at an assigned pressure.
The integral absorption heat per mole of added \COtwo is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta h=\frac{H_{\mathrm{final}}-H_{\mathrm{initial}}-\Delta n_{\COtwo}h_{\COtwo}^{\mathrm{feed}}}{\Delta n_{\COtwo}},
 \label{eq:addition-heat}
\end{equation}
with both equilibrium liquids and the pure-\COtwo feed at the same temperature and pressure; negative values denote exothermic absorption.
The semi-differential heats of Kim and Svendsen are calculated over each finite addition \cite{Kim2007}.

\subsection{Objective, deviations and conditional standard errors}\label{sec:parameters}
% P:methods-04
Estimation is sequential.
The water parameters, the \COtwo--water interaction, fitted to the pressures of Kiepe et al.\ \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002}, the permittivity coefficients and the two salt-fitted water--anion interactions (\cref{sec:species}) are determined first and remain fixed.
\res{mea-jointly-fitted-parameter-count-methods} MEA parameters are then estimated jointly (\cref{tab:species-parameters,tab:interaction-matrix}), and with all shared parameters fixed, \res{dea-reactive-fitted-parameter-count-methods} DEA, \res{mdea-reactive-fitted-parameter-count-methods} MDEA and \res{amp-reactive-fitted-parameter-count-methods} AMP parameters are fitted (\cref{tab:transfer-fitted}).
Parameters were also set beforehand: the two permittivity coefficients of each amine, the DEA--water interaction and the \res{amp-pure-adjusted-coordinate-count-methods} pure-AMP parameters (\cref{tab:transfer-species}, Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-transfer-parameters}); with these, \res{dea-adjusted-coordinate-count-across-stages} quantities are adjusted for DEA, \res{mdea-adjusted-coordinate-count-across-stages} for MDEA and \res{amp-adjusted-coordinate-count-across-stages} for AMP.
Each fit minimizes
\begin{equation}
 C(\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac12\sum_jw_jr_j^2,\qquad
 r_j=\frac{y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}-y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}}{s_j}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 r_j=\frac{\ln(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})}{s_j},
 \label{eq:fit-objective}
\end{equation}
with the logarithmic form for pressures.

% P:methods-weights
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-objective} gives the scale $s_j$ and weight $w_j$ of each data set.
Several are assigned without a statistical derivation and they differ between the amines, so an objective value sets fitting priorities within one fit and does not compare accuracy between amines.
Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-objective-contributions} gives the contribution of every data set to its objective.
The loaded-solution pressures contribute \res{dea-loaded-pressures-objective-share-methods}\,\% of the DEA objective and \res{amp-loaded-pressures-objective-share-methods}\,\% of the AMP objective.
In the MDEA objective each group enters as the mean of its squared standardized residuals, so that the many densities do not dominate.
There the loaded total pressures contribute \res{mdea-loaded-pressures-objective-share-methods}\,\% and the \res{mdea-unloaded-density-count-methods} densities of the \COtwo-free solution \res{mdea-unloaded-densities-objective-share-methods}\,\%.
In the MEA objective the \res{mea-cai-binary-observation-count-methods} MEA--water observations of Cai et al.\ \cite{caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996} contribute \res{mea-cai-objective-share-methods}\,\% of the objective value, \res{mea-selected-objective-cai-weight}, but respond only to the MEA--water interaction and its temperature coefficient, so they determine those two parameters and leave the weighting among the loaded-solution data unchanged.

% P:methods-starts
The minimization is a bounded nonlinear least-squares calculation with the epcsaft package, stopped on a relative function tolerance of $10^{-10}$.
For MEA, DEA and AMP, fits from \res{reported-tied-start-count-methods} starts in different parts of the parameter box reach the same objective value; the MDEA fit was made from one start.
Each fit is a local solution.

% P:methods-06
Agreement is reported as average absolute relative deviation (AARD), relative bias and root-mean-square error (RMSE),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{AARD}=\frac{100\,\%}{N}\sum_j\left|\frac{y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}}{y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}}-1\right|,
 \qquad \operatorname{bias}=\frac{100\,\%}{N}\sum_j\left(\frac{y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}}{y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}}-1\right),
 \qquad \operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_j(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}-y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})^2},
 \label{eq:aard}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of observations in the set, restricted for AARD and bias to nonzero observations.
For the parameters that are not on a bound, the local covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol\theta_I)=s_C^2(J_I^{\mathsf T}J_I)^{-1},\qquad
 s_C^2=\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathcal R}w_jr_j^2}{N_{\mathrm{resp}}-|I|},
 \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
where $J_I$ is the weighted residual Jacobian and $\mathcal R$ is the set of the $N_{\mathrm{resp}}$ observations in the objective whose calculated value depends on at least one fitted parameter; a parameter on a bound is held fixed and has no standard error.
Observations that respond to no fitted parameter are left out of both the sum and the count: the \res{mea-kiepe-constant-pressure-count-covariance} Kiepe pressures among the \res{mea-objective-observations-methods-covariance} observations of the MEA objective, a constant contribution to it, which leaves \res{mea-responsive-observations-methods} observations and \res{mea-residual-degrees-of-freedom-methods} residual degrees of freedom, and \res{mdea-pure-water-pressures-constant-methods} pure-water pressures of the MDEA objective, which leaves \res{mdea-responsive-observations-methods} observations and \res{mdea-residual-degrees-of-freedom-methods} residual degrees of freedom.
These standard errors are conditional on the model form, the equilibrium-constant correlations, the fixed shared parameters, the scales, the weights and the local linearization; they exclude model discrepancy and systematic differences between sources and do not define prediction intervals.

\subsection{Phase-stability search}\label{sec:methods-phase}
% P:methods-phase-01
Each fitted parameter set is searched for a phase split with the tangent-plane criterion \cite{Michelsen1982}, with electroneutral trial phases \cite{Perdomo2025} and reaction-equilibrium constraints \cite{Tsanas2022,Ascani2022}:
\begin{equation}
 D(\boldsymbol w)=g(T,P,\boldsymbol w)/(RT)-\boldsymbol w^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda,
 \quad \sum_iw_i=1,\quad\sum_i z_iw_i=0,\quad w_i>0.
 \label{eq:tpd}
\end{equation}
Here $g=G^*/n$ is the molar Gibbs energy of the trial phase on the true-species basis, with $G^*$ the Gibbs energy of \cref{sec:reactions}, and $\boldsymbol w$ is its composition, restricted to the species that can form from the feed and, for a vapor, to \COtwo, water and the molecular amine; the condition $w_i>0$ applies to these species only, and a liquid trial phase also satisfies the reaction equilibria of \cref{eq:activity-reaction-equilibrium}, which do not arise in a vapor of neutral species.
$B$ is the conservation matrix, whose rows are the independent conserved groups of the true species, so that $B\boldsymbol\nu_r=0$ for every reaction; and $\boldsymbol\lambda$ holds the Lagrange multipliers of the corresponding balances at the searched state, divided by $RT$, so that $B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda$ is the tangent plane of that state.
A negative distance that is confirmed by a finite, mass-conserving transfer lowering the Gibbs energy by more than its numerical error estimate is a \emph{detected instability}, classed as toward a second liquid or toward a vapor from the density of the trial phase.
A liquid--liquid split is reported when a separate equilibrium calculation at the same feed, temperature and pressure gives two distinct liquids of lower Gibbs energy.
The searched states are the measured states of each amine and grids of temperature, amine content and loading at the concentrations and loadings of its data, mostly liquids at 1.05 and 3 times the calculated bubble pressure; the MDEA grid is restricted to the concentrations and loadings of the measured loaded solutions (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-phase} gives the grids and the starting compositions).

% P:methods-bound
Stability enters one fit, through a bound.
Only the MDEA--water interaction acts on the \COtwo-free MDEA solution, and an MDEA fit without a bound converged where that solution has a calculated liquid--liquid gap at searched states between 393 and \SI{423}{\kelvin}.
The value of the interaction at \SI{423}{\kelvin}, the highest temperature tested, is therefore bounded above at $\res{mdea-bound-binary-limit-methods}$, $10^{-4}$ below the largest tested value without a gap at \SI{1.466}{\mega\pascal}; the bound was fixed before fitting (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-mdea-bound}).
The loaded MDEA states are not constrained, and the MEA, DEA and AMP fits carry no stability condition.
