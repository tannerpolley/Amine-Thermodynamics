Dear Editors of Fluid Phase Equilibria,

Please consider “Thermodynamics of CO₂-loaded aqueous monoethanolamine with ePC-SAFT: phase equilibria, speciation, absorption heat, density and phase stability” for publication as a research article.

Polley and Hedengren's “Reactive ePC-SAFT Modeling of carbon dioxide absorption in aqueous MEA” (Next Chemical Engineering, 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.nxcen.2026.100132) already represents explicit MEA ions and couples the thermodynamics to an absorber. The present work differs in three respects: joint estimation from pressure, species, caloric, density and binary data; absorption heat and density calculated from the same Helmholtz energy; and a tangent-plane comparison showing that similar property fits can predict different numbers of liquid phases. It remains an MEA thermodynamic study, with a brief MDEA demonstration.

For nominal 30 wt% MEA, pressure AARD is 11.86% for 47 fitted and 24.2% for 112 non-fitted pressures (20.55% overall). The 94 fitted species fractions give 7.10%, fitted integral-heat RMSE is 1.84 kJ/mol CO₂ at 313.15 K, and fitted loaded-density AARD is 0.18% over 47 observations through 353.15 K, without volume translation. Non-fitted data were accessed during development; the comparisons are not blind tests.

The wider-bound fit gives detected instability at 54 states; the final parameter set has no additional liquid detected at 162 searched states between 313.15 and 393.15 K. Restricting two bounds increases fitting cost by 0.45% and leaves three fitted densities outside twice their assigned scales. This numerical comparison does not physically validate phase behavior. Six parameters reach bounds, four of the other ten have conditional standard errors larger than their absolute values, and the 0.49 pK R5 shift is within its 0.55 pK conditional standard error.

Bicarbonate and carbonate, higher-temperature caloric and density properties and pressure transfer to 15 and 45 wt% MEA remain limitations. The brief MDEA comparison reverses the cation-representation ranking between pressure sources, so neither representation is generally preferred.
