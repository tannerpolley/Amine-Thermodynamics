\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% P:introduction-01
Monoethanolamine (\MEA) in water is the reference solvent for \COtwo absorption, and its vapor--liquid equilibrium has been measured extensively~\cite{Jou1995,Mamun2005,Hilliard2008,Aronu2011,Xu2011}.
Absorbed \COtwo reacts with water and amine to form protonated amine, carbamate, bicarbonate and carbonate.
The equilibrium \COtwo partial pressure at a given loading therefore depends on both the phase equilibrium and the liquid speciation.
A model can reproduce the pressure with an implausible distribution of \MEA, \MEAH, \MEACOO, \HCOthree and \COthree, so speciation measurements provide a separate test of the same thermodynamic state.

% P:introduction-02
Speciation measurements for loaded \MEA differ in what they observe.
Quantitative NMR resolves carbamate and bicarbonate, but fast proton exchange permits only the sum of \MEA and \MEAH to be observed~\cite{Bottinger2008,Jakobsen2005}.
Matin et al.\ obtained all four major species from acid and base titrations combined with a total carbon measurement~\cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
They reported agreement with NMR for free amine, protonated amine and carbamate, and bicarbonate higher than the NMR values~\cite[pp.~6616--6617]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.
When such observations enter a parameter fit, the choice of which measurements to fit can affect which model form is selected.

% P:introduction-03
Excess-Gibbs-energy models with chemical equilibria represent this system with fitted interaction parameters.
B\"ottinger et al.\ refitted the \MEA carbamate-formation constant to NMR speciation while retaining the Bates--Pinching protonation constant and published \MEA interaction parameters~\cite{Bottinger2008}.
Zhang et al.\ fitted an electrolyte NRTL liquid model, with PC-SAFT vapor fugacities, to \COtwo solubility, heat of absorption, heat capacity and NMR speciation together~\cite{Zhang2011}, and Akula et al.\ fitted an electrolyte NRTL model jointly to \COtwo pressure and heat of absorption, leaving NMR speciation and heat capacity outside the fit~\cite{Akula2023a}.
Equations of state based on statistical associating fluid theory describe association and chain formation within one Helmholtz energy~\cite{Gross2001,Gross2002}.
For loaded \MEA, SAFT-VR models represented the reactions implicitly as association between \MEA and \COtwo sites~\cite{MacDowell2010,Rodriguez2012}, Wang et al.\ used pseudo-chemical association in a Peng--Robinson cubic-plus-association equation of state and predicted the liquid speciation~\cite{wangImprovementPRCPAEquation2018}, and PC-SAFT and SAFT-HR models combined neutral-species fugacities with ideal reaction equilibria~\cite{Nasrifar2010,Baygi2015,Najafloo2018}.
Nasrifar and Tafazzol solved the reaction equilibria of aqueous \MEA, diethanolamine and methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) as an ideal liquid, lumped the ions with water and predicted \COtwo and \ce{H2S} solubility from PC-SAFT pure-component parameters and amine--water interaction parameters from binary vapor--liquid equilibria, without parameters fitted to the loaded solutions~\cite[Sects.~3 and 6, Table~3]{Nasrifar2010}.
None of these equation-of-state models carries explicit ion activities.

% P:introduction-07
Other equations of state for loaded alkanolamines carry ions explicitly.
T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros added a Debye--H\"uckel term, with protonated-amine and carbamate ions, to a cubic two-state association equation of state for \MEA, diethanolamine and MDEA~\cite{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013}, and Neumann et al.\ combined a Helmholtz-energy equation of state for \MEA--water--\COtwo with an electrolyte NRTL model whose long-range part contains Pitzer--Debye--H\"uckel and Born terms~\cite{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Najafloo et al.\ applied an electrolyte SAFT-HR equation of state with explicit carbamate ions to MDEA blended with a carbamate-forming diamine~\cite{najaflooMeasuringSolubilityCarbon2015}, and Raeispour Shirazi and Lotfollahi added mean-spherical-approximation and Born terms to PC-SAFT for MDEA, with ion interaction parameters fitted to bubble pressures~\cite{raeispourshiraziModelingH2SSolubility2019,raeispourshiraziApplicationEPC_SAFTMBEoS2020}.
None of these combines PC-SAFT with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions for a carbamate-forming amine.

% P:introduction-04
Electrolyte PC-SAFT (ePC-SAFT) adds a Debye--H\"uckel contribution for charged species~\cite{Cameretti2005}, and Held et al.\ estimated ion parameters from densities and osmotic coefficients of aqueous salt solutions, with dispersion between ions of the same sign excluded~\cite{Held2014}.
With explicit ions and activity-based reaction equilibria, ePC-SAFT described acid-gas solubility in aqueous MDEA from parameters obtained from pure components and binary subsystems~\cite{Uyan2015,Wangler2018}, and was extended to competitive \ce{H2S}--\COtwo absorption~\cite{Cleeton2020}.
B\"ulow et al.\ added the Born term for the solvation Gibbs energy of ions to MDEA blends with sulfolane or an ionic liquid, which reduced the number of binary interaction parameters~\cite{Bulow2021a}.
Related work added concentration-dependent permittivity to the Born and Debye--H\"uckel terms~\cite{Bulow2020,Rueben2024} and predicted \COtwo solubility in aqueous and organic salt solutions~\cite{Schick2023}.
Figiel et al.\ modified the Born term to account for the solvation-shell volume and dielectric saturation near an ion (SSM+DS), with parameters estimated from ion solvation and salt activity data in water and alcohols at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}~\cite{Figiel2025}; a later correction restored a missing bracket and the ion-region permittivity in its Eq.~(7)~\cite{Figiel2026Correction}.
MDEA is a tertiary amine and forms no carbamate.
The natural next step in this line is the primary amine \MEA, in which carbamate formation makes the ion population and the solvent composition change together with \COtwo loading.

% P:introduction-05
This work fits a nine-species ePC-SAFT model with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions and explicit carbamate chemistry to \COtwo partial pressure in 30 wt\% \MEA at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and to liquid speciation at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.
Because its interaction parameters are fitted to the ternary mixture, calculations at other conditions are reported as tests outside the fit rather than as predictions in the sense of the MDEA studies~\cite{Uyan2015,Wangler2018}.

The work makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To our knowledge, this is the first application of electrolyte PC-SAFT with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions to aqueous \MEA--\COtwo with ions and carbamate chemistry treated explicitly.
    \item To our knowledge, it is also the first simultaneous estimation of liquid-phase electrolyte PC-SAFT parameters against both \COtwo partial pressures and measured liquid speciation for loaded \MEA; Zhang et al.\ fitted both observables with an electrolyte NRTL liquid model~\cite{Zhang2011}.
    \item We extend published Born-formulation comparisons to reactive aqueous \MEA, where the ionic species and their amounts change with loading, and decompose the pressure change caused by removing the Born term into activity and speciation contributions.
    \item Within the declared fitting bounds, the tested refits without a Born contribution do not recover agreement with pressure and speciation together, and the SSM+DS and original Born formulations calibrate comparably on these data; which has the lower cost depends on whether the titration bicarbonate data are fitted.
\end{enumerate}
The work also reports conditional parameter uncertainties, compares the calibrated reaction temperature dependence with a refit using literature temperature laws, and turns these results into practical lessons for fitting this model.
