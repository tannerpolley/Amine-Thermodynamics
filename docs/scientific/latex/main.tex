\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model is calibrated for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo), and its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters are then held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.
The shared water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fixed beforehand from osmotic coefficients of the sodium salts, so that no amine data determine them.
The MEA model has 11 fitted parameters and literature equilibrium constants; its fitted \COtwo partial pressures at 30 wt\% deviate by 11.3\,\%, and its pressures at other concentrations and at stripper temperatures are less accurate.
With the shared parameters fixed, the fitted loaded-solution pressures deviate by 36.6\,\% for DEA, 10.1\,\% for MDEA and 10.9\,\% for AMP.
The salt-fixed interactions are not better for every amine: compared with an interaction fitted to the MEA data, they halve the deviation of the fitted MDEA total pressures, more than double that of unfitted MDEA \COtwo partial pressures, and worsen the DEA fit.
Phase-stability searches of the fitted models detect no instability for MEA, DEA and AMP and one calculated liquid--liquid split for MDEA, far outside the measured conditions.
A first AMP fit, whose loaded-solution pressures ended at \SI{353}{\kelvin}, gave calculated splits at 363--\SI{393}{\kelvin}; fitting the measured pressures at those temperatures removed them.
A phase-stability search is thus a useful check on where a fit has been extrapolated.
The models are calibrations against data examined during development, not a basis for absorber design.

\end{abstract}
