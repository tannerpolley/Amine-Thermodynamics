\section{Model equations, inputs and parameter values}\label{sec:s-parameters}
\subsection{Hard-chain, dispersion, association and Debye--H\"uckel contributions}\label{sec:s-contributions}
% P:supplement-contributions-01
These four contributions to the residual Helmholtz energy of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:epcsaft-residual-split}) are used in their published forms.
Below, $x_i$ is the mole fraction and $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}$ the segment count of species $i$, $\rho_N=N_{\mathrm A}\rho$ is the number density, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions and $z_i$ is a charge number.
The hard-sphere diameter of a neutral species or chain cation depends on temperature \cite{Gross2001},
\begin{equation}
    d_i=\sigma_i\left[1-0.12\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u_i}{k_BT}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-diameter}
\end{equation}
with the water diameter $\sigma_w(T)$ of the main text in place of a constant $\sigma_i$; a single-sphere ion has the temperature-independent diameter $d_i=0.88\sigma_i$.

% P:supplement-contributions-02
The hard-chain term is \cite{Gross2001}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}=\bar m\,a^{\mathrm{hs}}-\sum_i x_i\left(m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}-1\right)\ln g_{ii}^{\mathrm{hs}},
    \qquad
    \bar m=\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}},
    \label{eq:hard-chain}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_0}\left[
    \frac{3\zeta_1\zeta_2}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3^2}-\zeta_0\right)\ln\left(1-\zeta_3\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\frac{3\zeta_2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\right)^2\frac{2\zeta_2^2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^3},
    \qquad
    \zeta_n=\frac{\pi}{6}\rho_N\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}}d_i^n,
    \quad n=0,\dots,3,
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-contact}
\end{equation}
where $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}$ is the hard-sphere pair distribution function at contact and $\eta=\zeta_3$ is the packing fraction.

% P:supplement-contributions-03
The dispersion term is the second-order perturbation expansion of Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    =-2\pi\rho_N I_1\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\sigma_{ij}^3
    -\pi\rho_N\bar m\,C_1I_2\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\left(\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\right)^2\sigma_{ij}^3 ,
    \label{eq:dispersion}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    C_1=\left[1+\bar m\frac{8\eta-2\eta^2}{\left(1-\eta\right)^4}
    +\left(1-\bar m\right)\frac{20\eta-27\eta^2+12\eta^3-2\eta^4}{\left[\left(1-\eta\right)\left(2-\eta\right)\right]^2}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:dispersion-compressibility}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I_1=\sum_{k=0}^{6}a_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    I_2=\sum_{k=0}^{6}b_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    a_k(\bar m)=a_{0k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}a_{1k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}\frac{\bar m-2}{\bar m}a_{2k},
    \label{eq:dispersion-integrals}
\end{equation}
where $b_k(\bar m)$ has the form of $a_k(\bar m)$, and $a_{0k}$--$a_{2k}$ and $b_{0k}$--$b_{2k}$ are their universal constants.
The pair diameter $\sigma_{ij}$ and the pair energy $u_{ij}$ are those of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:dispersion-combining-rule}).

% P:supplement-contributions-04
Hydrogen bonding follows Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite{Kleiner2007}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{assoc}}=\sum_i x_i\sum_{A\in i}\left(\ln X_A-\frac{X_A}{2}+\frac12\right),
    \qquad
    X_A=\left[1+\rho_N\sum_j x_j\sum_{B\in j}X_B\Delta^{AB}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:association}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta^{AB}=g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}\,\sigma_{ij}^3\,\kappa^{AB}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\epsilon^{AB}}{k_BT}\right)-1\right],
    \label{eq:association-strength}
\end{equation}
where $X_A$ is the fraction of molecules $i$ not bonded at site $A$ and $\Delta^{AB}$ is the strength of a bond between site $A$ on molecule $i$ and site $B$ on molecule $j$.
Cross-association between an amine species and water uses the Wolbach--Sandler rule \cite{Kleiner2007},
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon^{AB}_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(\epsilon^{AB}_i+\epsilon^{AB}_j\right),
    \qquad
    \kappa^{AB}_{ij}=\sqrt{\kappa^{AB}_i\kappa^{AB}_j}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}}{\sigma_{ij}}\right)^3 .
    \label{eq:wolbach-sandler}
\end{equation}

% P:supplement-contributions-05
The Debye--H\"uckel term of Cameretti et al.\ describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ \cite{Cameretti2005}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{DH}}=-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm D}e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2\chi_i,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm D}^2=\frac{e^2\rho_N}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2,
    \label{eq:debye-huckel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \chi_i=\frac{3}{\left(\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^3}
    \left[\frac32+\ln\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    -2\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    +\frac12\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^2\right],
    \label{eq:debye-huckel-chi}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the elementary charge, $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity, $\kappa_{\mathrm D}$ is the inverse Debye screening length and $\chi_i$ accounts for the finite ion size.

\subsection{MEA and the shared species}
% P:supplement-18
Neutral MEA parameters are the 2B values of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, except the association volume described below \cite{Baygi2015}.
\COtwo segment count, diameter and dispersion energy are from Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}.
The segment diameters and dispersion energies of bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, and the water interactions of hydronium and hydroxide, are those of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}, which Uyan et al.\ also use \cite{Uyan2015}; the water interactions of bicarbonate and carbonate are fitted to salt data (Section~\ref{sec:s-salt}).
Held et al.\ determined these water interactions with their own water parameters; they are used here unchanged with the water parameters of this work.
Ion segment count one and the diameter ties are modeling assumptions.
The Born diameter of hydronium is that of the hydrogen ion in Figiel et al.\ \cite{Figiel2025}.
The Born diameter of hydroxide, \SI{3.0811}{\angstrom}, was calculated here from the Born equation, $d^{\mathrm{Born}}=z^2e^2N_{\mathrm A}(1-1/\varepsilon_r)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0|\Delta G^{\mathrm{hyd}}|)$, where $N_{\mathrm A}$ is the Avogadro constant, with the hydration free energy $\Delta G^{\mathrm{hyd}}$ of $-106.4$ kcal/mol of Palascak and Shields \cite{palascakAccurateExperimentalValues2004} and the relative permittivity of water at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, $\varepsilon_r=78.37$.
The Born diameters of bicarbonate and carbonate are set here at \SI{3.0}{\angstrom}; in the fit with the MEA-fitted shared set and with all other parameters fixed, setting the bicarbonate diameter to 2.5, 3.5 or \SI{4.0}{\angstrom} or the carbonate diameter to 2.5 or \SI{4.0}{\angstrom} changes the objective value of the MEA fit by at most 0.16\,\% and individual fitted \COtwo partial pressures by up to about 6\,\% for bicarbonate and by less than 0.02\,\% for carbonate.
None of these inputs is fitted in the calibrations of this work.
All other interactions are zero by assumption except the fixed water interactions listed below.
\Cref{tab:supplement-species-parameters,tab:supplement-interaction-matrix} list the exact values.
Water's solvation factor and ion-region permittivity follow Figiel et al.; unit solvation factors are set here for the other components \cite{Figiel2025}.
The ion packing and Debye--H\"uckel diameters follow the ties stated in the main text.

% P:supplement-association-01
The \COtwo--water cross-association energy is \SI{878.325}{\kelvin}, half the fitted water self-association energy, with the same association volume as water.
The topology follows Schick et al.\ and Pabsch et al.\ \cite{Schick2023,Pabsch2020}.

% P:supplement-19
The main text reports the fits of the five water parameters to IAPWS-95 \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}, the \COtwo--water interaction to Kiepe pressures \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002} and the \MEA permittivity coefficients to measured permittivities.
In the water fit, the two exponential terms in $\sigma_w(T)=\sigma_0+a_1\exp(b_1T)+a_2\exp(b_2T)$, with $a_1=10.11$, $b_1=-0.01775$, $a_2=-1.417$ and $b_2=-0.01146$, were fixed at the values of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2008} while $\sigma_0=2.7804326$~\si{\angstrom} was fitted; diameters are in angstrom and temperature in kelvin.
The water fit uses IAPWS-95 vapor pressures and saturated-liquid densities at 20 temperatures, 298.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin} in \SI{5}{\kelvin} steps, and 23 isobaric heat capacities.
At those 20 temperatures the fitted parameters deviate on average by 0.075\,\% in vapor pressure, 0.036\,\% in saturated-liquid density, 0.037\,\% in liquid density at \SI{0.3}{\mega\pascal} and 0.44\,\% in heat capacity at \SI{0.3}{\mega\pascal}, with maxima of 0.17, 0.098, 0.095 and 1.49\,\%; at 19 temperatures midway between them the averages are 0.070, 0.034, 0.035 and 0.42\,\%, and at three temperatures from 298.15 to \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} at \SI{0.101325}{\mega\pascal} the density and heat capacity deviate on average by 0.038 and 0.78\,\%.

% P:supplement-20
In the permittivity fit, water coefficients, 0.3777 L/mol and 1403 K, follow Yang et al.\ \cite{Yang2024}; the \COtwo coefficients, $10^{-8}$ L/mol and 1403 K, are set in this work.
Two coefficient pairs, (0.73099 L/mol, \SI{1019.08}{\kelvin}) and (0.24645 L/mol, \SI{2436.44}{\kelvin}), give the same permittivity at the measurement temperature, \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, at any density.
The larger-volume pair is used here; the permittivity data alone do not distinguish the two, so that selection is a model assumption.
At other temperatures the pairs differ: in unloaded 30 wt\% MEA at fixed density the smaller-volume pair raises $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}-1$ by 0.19, 0.71 and 1.28\,\% at 313.15, 353.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
The 14 unloaded measurements at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} do not determine temperature or loading dependence; direct static-permittivity data over the reacting domain are missing from this calibration, so extrapolation remains a model assumption.

The shared parameters are those marked in main-text Tables~\ref{main-tab:species-parameters} and \ref{main-tab:interaction-matrix}; Tables~\ref{tab:supplement-reaction-coefficients}--\ref{tab:supplement-shared-remaining} give their unrounded values.

\subsection{Water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions from sodium-salt data}\label{sec:s-salt}
% P:supplement-salt-data
The water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fitted to osmotic coefficients of aqueous sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate, with no amine in the calculation.
The data are the values that Peiper and Pitzer tabulate from their ion-interaction model at 278.15, 288.15, 298.15, 308.15 and \SI{318.15}{\kelvin} and nominal molalities of 0.01--\SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram}, 45 for each salt \cite{peiperThermodynamicsAqueousCarbonate1982}; they are model-derived reference values, not direct measurements.
Each row is calculated at the equilibrium ionic composition printed with it, the dissolved \COtwo is removed as an ideal solute, and the residual scale is 0.01.
The sodium and chloride ion parameters and the interactions between sodium and the anions are those of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}; like the amine ions, these ions have Born and Debye--H\"uckel diameters equal to their segment diameter and a unit solvation factor.

% P:supplement-salt-fit
Each fitted interaction is linear in temperature, $k(T)=a_0+a_1(T-\SI{298.15}{\kelvin})$, with bounds of $\pm1$ on $a_0$ and $\pm\SI{0.02}{\per\kelvin}$ on $a_1$, and the three fits are made in sequence.
The water--sodium intercept is fitted first, to ten osmotic coefficients of sodium chloride at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} and 0.1--\SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram} \cite{HamerWu1972}, with the slope of Held et al.\ retained; it ends on its lower bound, $-1$, and the sodium chloride coefficients deviate by 1.51\,\%.
The water--bicarbonate interaction is fitted next and is not on a bound: $a_0=0.1117$ and $a_1=\SI{3.931e-3}{\per\kelvin}$, which is 0.1707 at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, with an AARD of 0.16\,\% on the 45 sodium bicarbonate coefficients.
The water--carbonate interaction is fitted last: its intercept ends on the lower bound, $a_0=-1$, with $a_1=\SI{1.963e-3}{\per\kelvin}$, which is $-0.9706$ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, and the 45 sodium carbonate coefficients deviate by 5.11\,\%, the calculated values being low.
For 39 coefficients of mixed bicarbonate--carbonate solutions that were not fitted, the deviation is 2.56\,\%; six further mixed rows have no calculated value.

% P:supplement-salt-target
The printed water activities of Peiper and Pitzer satisfy $-\ln a_w/M_w=\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}\sum_im_i=\phi_{\mathrm{st}}\sum_s\nu_sm_s$ within printed precision, where the first sum runs over the equilibrium solute species and the second over the stoichiometric salt.
The fitted target is the equilibrium coefficient of the ions: the molality of dissolved \COtwo, treated as ideal, is subtracted from the equilibrium numerator, and the remainder is divided by the charge-closed sum of the ionic molalities.
The signed residuals, calculated minus target, are $-0.30$ to $+0.47$\,\% for sodium bicarbonate, without a trend in temperature or molality.
For sodium carbonate every residual is negative: about $-1.1$\,\% at \SI{0.01}{\mole\per\kilogram}, $-7.4$ to $-8.3$\,\% at 0.2--\SI{0.4}{\mole\per\kilogram} and $-2.3$ to $-4.8$\,\% at \SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram}, where the value at \SI{278.15}{\kelvin} is the least negative; below \SI{0.4}{\mole\per\kilogram} the temperature changes the residual by less than 0.6 points.
For the mixed solutions, which are not fitted, the residuals run from $-3.8$\,\% at 0.1--\SI{0.2}{\mole\per\kilogram} to $+6.3$\,\% at \SI{1.0}{\mole\per\kilogram} and \SI{278.15}{\kelvin}, changing sign between 0.4 and \SI{0.8}{\mole\per\kilogram}.
The six mixed rows without a value, at \SI{278.15}{\kelvin} and 0.01, 0.02 and \SI{0.6}{\mole\per\kilogram} and at 288.15, 298.15 and \SI{308.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{0.6}{\mole\per\kilogram}, fail a charge-closure check: the minor anion obtained by closing the charge balance on the printed composition is negative.
In one row the printed values give $-0.0001$; in the other five they give zero, and floating-point subtraction leaves $-3\times10^{-18}$ to $-2\times10^{-16}$, which the check also refuses.

% P:supplement-salt-limits
Every result of the four amine models is conditional on the two intercepts that lie on their bounds.
The sodium carbonate coefficients are already low at \SI{0.01}{\mole\per\kilogram}, where the water--carbonate interaction has almost no effect, which suggests that this interaction does not carry most of the remaining error; no decomposition by term was made.
The salt data end at \SI{318.15}{\kelvin}; extrapolated, the water--bicarbonate law reaches 0.328 at \SI{353.15}{\kelvin} and 0.485 at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
Wangler et al.\ fitted a temperature-dependent water--bicarbonate interaction to the same source with their own water and ion parameters \cite{Wangler2018}, and Held et al.\ use zero \cite{Held2014}; these values are not interchangeable between parameter sets.
The \emph{MEA-fitted shared set} of the main text instead takes a constant water--bicarbonate interaction from the MEA fit, 0.7650 with a conditional standard error of 3.18, and the constant water--carbonate interaction of Held et al., $-0.25$.

\subsection{DEA, MDEA and AMP}
% P:supplement-transfer-parameters
Table~\ref{tab:supplement-transfer-parameters} lists the value and unit of each amine-specific parameter of the DEA, MDEA and AMP models, including the permittivity coefficients and the coefficients of the carbamate hydrolysis (R4) and amine dissociation (R5) correlations.
A molality-basis constant follows from the mole-fraction constant of its source by
\begin{equation}
    \ln K_{m,r}=\ln K_{x,r}-\Big(\sum_{i\ne\HtwoO}\nu_{ri}\Big)\ln\!\left(M_{\HtwoO}m^{\circ}\right),
    \label{eq:molality-conversion}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_{ri}$ is the stoichiometric number of species $i$ in reaction $r$, $M_{\HtwoO}$ is the molar mass of water and $m^{\circ}=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram}$.
These correlations are written in the form $\ln K(T)=a+b/T+c\ln(T/T_{\mathrm{ref}})+dT$ on the common-molality reference basis, with $T$ in K and $T_{\mathrm{ref}}=\SI{313.15}{\kelvin}$, which is equivalent to main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:reaction-equilibrium-constant-temperature}).
For DEA and AMP, the fitted-parameter table of the main text gives the enthalpy increment $\delta h$ and the change of $\ln K_4$ at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} relative to the starting R4 correlation, that of Aroua et al.\ for DEA \cite{arouaTemperatureDependencyEquilibrium1997} and that of Silkenb\"aumer et al.\ for AMP \cite{silkenbaumerSolubilityCarbonDioxide1998}; the final coefficients listed here already include both.
At 298.15, 313.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} the fitted DEA correlation differs from its starting correlation in $\ln K_4$ by $+0.415$, $+0.137$ and $-0.986$, and the fitted AMP correlation by $-1.073$, $-1.170$ and $-1.561$.
At \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} the conditional standard errors are 0.389 for DEA and 0.236 for AMP.
The measurements behind the DEA correlation span 303--\SI{331}{\kelvin} and the model estimates behind the AMP correlation 313.15--\SI{373.15}{\kelvin}, so the first and last of these temperatures are extrapolations.
The dispersion energy of \ce{DEACOO-}, \SI{204.0748}{\kelvin}, is that of \ce{MEACOO-} in the MEA model, and its segment diameter is the \ce{MEACOO-} diameter of that model, \SI{5.2012}{\angstrom}, multiplied by 1.1593, the cube root of the ratio of $m^{\mathrm{seg}}\sigma^3$ of DEA to that of MEA.
The dispersion energy of \ce{AMPH+} is that of AMP.
The dispersion energy of \ce{AMPCOO-}, \SI{204.0748}{\kelvin}, and its interaction with water, $-0.165523$, are those of \ce{MEACOO-} in the MEA model.
The \ce{AMPCOO-} diameter is fitted, with the \ce{MEACOO-} diameter of the MEA model, \SI{5.201154}{\angstrom}, as its lower bound, on which it ends.
An earlier AMP fit took these three inputs from the MEA fit with the MEA-fitted shared set, \SI{179.8579}{\kelvin}, $-0.0661$ and \SI{5.3480}{\angstrom}; it appears only as a comparison in Table~\ref{main-tab:amine-summary} and Section~\ref{sec:s-amp-slope}.
Fixed ionic parameters are model assumptions, not independent measurements of ionic size.
The packing diameter is the hard-sphere diameter used in the repulsive contribution.

\section{Source conventions, objective scales and weights}\label{sec:s-corrections}\label{sec:s-objective}
\subsection{MEA data}
% P:supplement-mea-species
B\"ottinger et al.\ report \ce{MEA + MEAH+} as an NMR sum and tabulate bicarbonate while noting practically absent carbonate \cite{Bottinger2008}.
Their bicarbonate-labeled observations are compared with calculated \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}}.
Matin et al.\ neglect carbonate when inferring bicarbonate from titration and carbon balance \cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}; the same comparison with the calculated sum is therefore an approximation, and these bicarbonate values are not fitted.
The Matin series labeled \SI{294.15}{\kelvin} is calculated at \SI{293.15}{\kelvin}.
Jakobsen species-to-total-amine ratios enter the objective, whereas their carbonate shares of dissolved carbon do not.
For the Jakobsen NMR calculations, dilution changes the solvent composition, and the reported NMR loading is used instead of the maximum loading \cite{Jakobsen2005}.

% P:supplement-mea-pressure
Pressures and species fractions are calculated at each source's solvent composition, including the reported molality and temperature for Hilliard and Xu \cite{Hilliard2008,Xu2011}.
Three Xu pressure observations above \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} and a B\"ottinger state with quantifiable oxazolidone are excluded; oxazolidone is absent from the model.
The pressure sources are Aronu, Hilliard, Idris, Jou, Ma'mun and Xu, with Wagner assessed separately \cite{Aronu2011,Hilliard2008,idrisSpeciationMEACO2Adducts2014,Jou1995,Mamun2005,Xu2011,wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}.
Omitting the carrier gas from the bubble calculation can matter at low \COtwo pressure; Neumann et al.\ instead include nitrogen \cite{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.

% P:supplement-mea-heat
Vinjarapu integral heats are fitted at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} from initially \COtwo-free 30 wt\% solvent \cite{HemaBhavya2025}.
Arcis integral heats use \SI{322.5}{\kelvin} and the reported pressures \cite{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
The calculation for the semi-differential calorimetry of Kim and Svendsen uses the liquid enthalpy at each interval endpoint, rather than the cumulative heat from zero loading \cite{Kim2007}.
A \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} isobar approximates these intervals because their experimental pressure histories are unavailable.
The fitted Hilliard heat capacities use 7 mol MEA/kg water.
Calculations assign \SI{101325}{\pascal}; Hilliard used sealed sample pans and did not report their internal pressure \cite{Hilliard2008}.
The other Hilliard heat-capacity comparisons use the same assigned pressure.
The objective uses the heat capacity per mole of apparent components, while the comparisons with the other heat-capacity data use the source mass basis; both include the added \COtwo.

\begin{equation}
 s_{\rho,j}=\left[u_{\rho,j}^2+(g_j u_{\alpha,j})^2\right]^{1/2},

\section{Caloric functions, worked MEA heat calculation and worked AMP phase split}\label{sec:s-files}
% P:supplement-calorics
Neutral caloric functions use NIST-JANAF for \COtwo, the MEA heat-capacity function tabulated by Zhang et al., and an ideal-gas water fit to IAPWS-95 \cite{chaseNISTJANAFThermochemicalTables1998,Zhang2011,internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}.
The ionic functions follow from the equilibrium-constant correlations and their infinite-dilution reference corrections.
A charge-proportional reference freedom cancels in electroneutral phases; no independent ionic heat-capacity functions are fitted.
The reaction enthalpies enter through these standard-state functions, and no separate reaction-enthalpy term is added.
The loading $10^{-6}$ represents nominally unloaded states in the heat-capacity calculations.
Table~\ref{tab:supplement-check-values} gives reference values of the MEA model at three bubble states for checking an implementation.

% P:supplement-reference-states
Two worked calculations give every intermediate quantity for checking an implementation; both are numerical verification cases and involve no comparison with a measurement.
Table~\ref{tab:amp-reference-state} gives the tangent-plane trial and the two-liquid equilibrium of the first AMP fit (Section~\ref{sec:s-amp-slope}), not of the AMP model, at 30 wt\% AMP, \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, a loading of 0.700 mol/mol and \SI{1.212}{\mega\pascal}, the state of its largest Gibbs-energy decrease.
The error estimate of the plane value is $1.8267\times10^{-10}$ and that of the descent along the direction of the trial phase $1.7544\times10^{-10}$, against a tangent-plane distance of $-3.697\times10^{-3}$.
On the two-liquid solution the largest residual of the chemical-potential equations is $1.8\times10^{-13}$ against a tolerance of $10^{-10}$, and the largest reaction and electrochemical-potential residuals are $7.5\times10^{-13}$ and $1.1\times10^{-13}$.
Tested against the tangent plane of that solution, five trial starts give a smallest distance of $+0.0307$, at a water-rich liquid root, and the four that end on a branch connected to the dilute vapor, at 383.4 mol/m$^3$, give $+0.0382$; these minima apply to the sampled endpoints.
Table~\ref{tab:mea-reference-state} gives the integral absorption heat and the equilibrium heat capacity of the MEA model at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} and a final loading of 0.31 mol/mol: \SI{-81.795}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} of \COtwo and 84.890 J/(mol K) per mole of apparent components, the latter reproduced by centered differences of the enthalpy within $4\times10^{-7}$ J/(mol K).
With stoichiometric numbers $\nu_{ri}$ positive for products, $\Gamma_r=\sum_i\nu_{ri}\ln\phi_i^{\mathrm{ref}}$ over the pure-water and infinite-dilution reference states at $P_{\mathrm{src},r}$ and $\Delta\nu_r=\sum_i\nu_{ri}$, the constant of reaction $r$ on ideal-gas reference states at $P^\circ=\SI{0.1}{\mega\pascal}$ is
\begin{equation}
 \ln K_{r,P^\circ}^{\mathrm{ig}}=\ln K_{r,\mathrm{src}}+\Big(\sum_{i\ne w}\nu_{ri}\Big)\ln(M_wm^\circ)+\Gamma_r+\Delta\nu_r\ln(P_{\mathrm{src},r}/P^\circ),
 \label{eq:pressure-standard-conversion}
\end{equation}
where $K_{r,\mathrm{src}}$ is the source correlation on the molality basis and $P_{\mathrm{src},r}$ is the system pressure for R1--R3, here \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal}, and \SI{0.1}{\mega\pascal} for R4 and R5; the values of $\ln K_{r,\mathrm{src}}$ and $\Gamma_r$ in Table~\ref{tab:mea-reference-state} are on the common molality basis, and their sum is the same on the mole-fraction basis of the R1 and R3 sources.
Its temperature derivative gives the reaction enthalpies of the last rows of Table~\ref{tab:mea-reference-state}, $\Delta_rh_{P^\circ}^{\mathrm{ig}}=RT^2\,\mathrm{d}\ln K_{r,\mathrm{src}}/\mathrm{d}T+RT^2(\partial\Gamma_r/\partial T)_{P_{\mathrm{src},r}}$, as the sum of a source term and a fugacity term.
At a fixed temperature and pressure either choice of $P_{\mathrm{src},r}$ gives one set of reference chemical potentials that does not depend on composition, so the phases compared in a stability search or a two-liquid calculation share one Gibbs energy; the choice changes how that set varies with pressure.
Evaluating the R1--R3 reference states at \SI{0.1}{\mega\pascal} instead of the system pressure changes little near ambient pressure: at two MEA bubble states with calculated \COtwo partial pressures of 1.562 and \SI{87678}{\pascal} it raises them by 0.00198 and \SI{4.84}{\pascal}, and it changes this integral heat from $-81.795$ to \SI{-81.850}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} of \COtwo.
Three calculated liquid--liquid splits of fits made with the MEA-fitted shared set were recalculated with the references at \SI{0.1}{\mega\pascal}.
The worked split of the first AMP fit at \SI{1.212}{\mega\pascal} persists, with a Gibbs-energy decrease of 0.01882 against 0.01881 mol, and so does the MDEA split at \SI{288.71}{\kelvin} and \SI{1.121}{\mega\pascal} of Section~\ref{sec:s-shared}, with $1.89\times10^{-3}$ against $1.94\times10^{-3}$ mol.
The MDEA split at \SI{203.1}{\mega\pascal} is not confirmed with the references at \SI{0.1}{\mega\pascal}: the two-liquid calculation did not return distinct liquids of lower Gibbs energy, so at that pressure the reference convention is not shown to be immaterial.
For the split of the first MDEA fit at \SI{383.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{121.309}{\mega\pascal}, the convention used here, with the R1--R3 references at the system pressure, confirms the split with a Gibbs-energy decrease of $4.233\times10^{-5}$ mol and a numerical error of $9.2\times10^{-10}$ mol.
With the references fixed at \SI{100}{\kilo\pascal} the candidate split lowers $G/(RT)$ by 0.0475 mol, but its liquid roots remain unresolved, so this case is undecided.

\section{Phase-stability procedure}\label{sec:s-phase}
\subsection{Search conditions}
% P:supplement-phase-method
Trial phases obey reaction equilibrium and electroneutrality and exclude species that cannot form from the feed at the searched state.
Charge-neutral initial liquid compositions include a uniform composition and near-pure neutral-species compositions; initial vapor compositions contain only the three neutral species.
Only a negative tangent-plane distance confirmed by a finite, mass-conserving transfer that lowers the Gibbs energy by more than its numerical error estimate is counted as a detected instability.
Trial compositions at which a Gibbs-energy decrease was found with other parameter sets during this work are also tested; a negative distance at one of these compositions can show that a state is unstable, but not toward which kind of phase, and it is reported separately.
Completion of the search, convergence of the residuals and positive pressure--density derivatives are numerical checks rather than physical measurements.
The deposited results identify every search that failed or did not finish.

% P:supplement-phase-grid
For MEA, the liquid grid contains 1800 states at 15, 25, 35 and 45 wt\% MEA, 313.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin} in \SI{10}{\kelvin} steps, and loading $10^{-5}$ plus 0.025--0.6 mol/mol in 0.025 steps, at 1.05 and 3.0 times the bubble pressure of the parameter set being searched.
Another 108 states use the composition of the liquid--vapor search at those two ratios.
A second liquid grid, offset from the first, contains 576 states at 20, 30 and 40 wt\%, 318.15--\SI{388.15}{\kelvin} and loadings 0.0125--0.5875, at 1.5 times the bubble pressure.
These are the 2484 liquid states of the main text.

% P:supplement-phase-census
The MEA liquid--vapor search contains six temperatures, 313.15, 318.15, 333.15, 353.15, 373.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, and nine loadings, $10^{-5}$, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.256, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 mol/mol.
Each temperature--loading pair uses three pressures, 0.50, 0.80 and 0.95 times a fixed reference bubble pressure, which is not the bubble pressure of the MEA model.
The MEA model is searched at these 162 states.
The feed is 9.7292821 mol MEA, 76.9751925 mol water and $9.7292821\alpha$ mol \COtwo.
The 6121 additional trial compositions give no negative tangent-plane distance for the MEA model at any liquid--vapor state.

% P:supplement-phase-transfer-grid
For DEA, MDEA and AMP, the search covers the measured states and a grid.
The grid spans 313.15--393.15 K in 10 K steps and loadings of $10^{-5}$ and then 0.1 upward in steps of 0.1 to the largest loading in the data of the amine, which is added, at 1.05 and 3.0 times the calculated bubble pressure; its amine concentrations are those of the fitted data, with concentrations within 1 wt\% of each other searched once.

\subsection{Stability penalty and the MDEA bound}\label{sec:s-mdea-bound}
% P:supplement-penalty
The stability penalty of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:stability-penalty}), used for the 16-parameter calibration, adds half the squared, scaled most negative tangent-plane distance of each penalized state to the objective.
The penalty is applied only when the search's own trials detect an instability toward a second liquid; an instability toward a vapor, or a negative distance at a trial composition from an earlier search, is reported without the penalty.
Later fits also test the compositions of instabilities already found.

% P:supplement-penalty-mea-dea
None of the four reported models required a penalty.
For DEA, the two initial parameter sets converge to objective values of 15.537712195487 and 15.537712195479, within the tie tolerance of $10^{-10}$, and the solution with the lower value is reported.

% P:supplement-penalty-mdea
For MDEA, the MDEA--water interaction $k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}(T)=k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}(423\ \mathrm{K})+(\mathrm{d}k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}/\mathrm{d}T)(T-423\ \mathrm{K})$ is the only fitted parameter that acts on the \COtwo-free solution.
With all other parameters fixed, the largest value without a gap in the lower convex envelope of the liquid Gibbs energy over 5490 compositions at 1.466 MPa rises from $-0.1323$ at 290 K to $-0.0670$ at 423 K, and $k_{\mathrm{MDEA,w}}(423\ \mathrm{K})$ is bounded above at $-0.0671$, $10^{-4}$ below it.
The three initial parameter sets converge to 17.401, 17.600 and 148.906; at the reported solution only this bound is active.
The need for the bound was established with the MEA-fitted shared set, with which the bounded fit has an objective value of 564.5.
Without the bound, that fit converges from one of the initial sets at 479.4 and has an instability at 14 of the 78 \COtwo-free MDEA--water liquids searched at 1.466 MPa, which are 13 MDEA mole fractions from 0.01 to 0.24 at each of 393, 403, 413, 413.15, 422.04 and 423 K. A repeated search of that unbounded setup finds 14 descents and one incomplete search, and places the onset of the calculated gap between 413.052 and 413.150 K.
The unbounded fit was not repeated with the salt-fitted interactions, which do not enter the calculation of the \COtwo-free solution.
For the MDEA model the fitted interaction equals the bound at 423 K and lies below the onset value at each of the 33 tested temperatures from 290 to 423 K (main-text \cref{main-fig:mdea-gap-onset}).
The bound was fixed before fitting and independently of the deviations: the no-gap side of the 423 K onset bracket, $-0.0670135$, minus a predeclared margin of $10^{-4}$.
At 423 K the bound lies below the no-gap edge of the onset bracket at 1.121, 1.466 and 1.811 MPa alike.
With the fitted temperature law extrapolated above 423 K, the gap first appears between 423.14375 and 423.15000 K at 1.121 MPa, between 423.15625 and 423.16250 K at 1.466 MPa and between 423.16875 and 423.17500 K at 1.811 MPa, so the bound suppresses the calculated gap through the tested temperatures and no further.
No bound acts on the loaded MDEA states.

\subsection{First AMP fit and the temperature slope of the cation--water interaction}\label{sec:s-amp-slope}
% P:supplement-amp-slope
For AMP neither the penalty nor a bound of this kind is used.
The first AMP fit used the 47 loaded total pressures of Hartono et al.\ at 313--\SI{353}{\kelvin}, the binary data and the carbamate fractions, 156 observations, with the MEA-fitted shared set.
With all nine parameters fitted, both initial parameter sets converge to an objective value of 61.2853, agreeing to $7\times10^{-12}$, and the temperature slope of the \ce{AMPH+}--water interaction, $b_{\mathrm{AMPH^+,w}}$, is \SI{-219.25}{\kelvin} with a conditional standard error of \SI{80.99}{\kelvin}.
Its search of the 666 states of Section~\ref{sec:s-phase} detects an instability at 93, and at each the parent and the trial phase are both liquid at the pressure of the parent (main-text \cref{main-fig:amp-splits}).
The two-liquid calculation of the main text confirms a split at 73 of them and not at the other 20.
The confirmed splits lie at 26.8--30.0 wt\% AMP, 363.15--393.20 K and loadings of 0.400--0.966 mol/mol.
Six are at measured states, none of them fitted in the first fit: rows 9, 10 and 11 of the Hartono et al.\ pressures at 373 K, and rows 10 and 13 of the Tong et al.\ pressures at 373.2 K and row 12 at 393.2 K; eight more measured states, rows 7 and 8 of Hartono et al.\ at 373 K, rows 6 and 7 at 393 K and rows 8, 9, 11 and 12 of Tong et al.\ at 373.2 K, have an instability without a confirmed split.
The largest decrease, 0.01881 mol in $G/(RT)$ against a numerical error of $2.8\times10^{-9}$, occurs at 393.15 K, 30.0 wt\% AMP and a loading of 0.700 mol/mol, at 1.212 MPa, where the two liquid densities are \num{44899} and \num{31612} mol/m$^3$.
Repeating the 666 searches with the search's own trials alone recovers all 93 detected instabilities and finds none at the other 573 states.

% P:supplement-amp-cause
The splits follow from the fitted slope.
Above the fitted pressures it raises $k_{\mathrm{AMPH^+,w}}$ from $-0.178$ at \SI{353}{\kelvin} to $-0.115$ at \SI{393}{\kelvin}, and the weaker cation--water attraction gives a second liquid rich in \ce{AMPH+}, bicarbonate and AMP, with carbamate mole fractions of $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$.
The splits are marginal: the smallest tangent-plane distance is about $-0.006$.
Holding $k_{\mathrm{AMPH^+,w}}$ at its value at \SI{353.22}{\kelvin} above that temperature removes every instability, and so does holding the AMP dissociation constant at its value at \SI{353.22}{\kelvin} or continuing it at constant enthalpy, which lowers $\ln K$ by 0.04 at \SI{393}{\kelvin}.
Holding the R4 constant at its value at \SI{353.22}{\kelvin}, or setting the carbamate diameter at its lower bound, leaves 93 and 90 of the 93 instabilities.

% P:supplement-amp-slope-choice
A second fit of the same data fixes $b_{\mathrm{AMPH^+,w}}$ at \SI{-150}{\kelvin}, a value chosen from a scan of fixed slopes because no instability was detected there; it is not taken from a source.
The fixed slope lies 0.86 standard errors from the fitted value, and fixing it raises the objective value by 0.78, from 61.2853 to 62.0651, while the deviation of the 47 pressures changes from 10.3 to 10.5\,\%.
Every search of this fit finishes without a detected instability.
At 373--\SI{393}{\kelvin}, the 18 total pressures of Hartono et al.\ and the 25 of Tong et al., two of them \COtwo-free, are outside both fits; calculated for one liquid, they deviate by 28.10 and 25.30\,\% for the first fit and by 28.35 and 25.47\,\% for the second, with signed deviations equal to minus these values, so each pressure is underpredicted.
Fixing the slope therefore removes the splits without improving the pressures at those temperatures.

% P:supplement-amp-hot-pressures
A search of the first fit at the measured pressure resolves a descent at seven states of each source.
At the Hartono et al.\ state at \SI{373}{\kelvin} and 0.58 mol/mol, measured at \SI{0.4065}{\mega\pascal}, its single liquid has a smallest tangent-plane distance of $-1.84\times10^{-3}$, and of the sets of phases tested the two-liquid one has the lowest Gibbs energy.
At the Tong et al.\ state at \SI{373.2}{\kelvin} and 0.455 mol/mol, measured at \SI{0.2915}{\mega\pascal}, the same holds, and the two liquids, of 11.410 and 1.138 mol, lower $G/(RT)$ by $5.57\times10^{-4}$ mol against a numerical error of $3.1\times10^{-9}$ mol.
The bubble pressure of the two-liquid solution is lower than the measured pressure where it could be calculated: 0.2525, 0.3384 and \SI{0.4451}{\mega\pascal} at the three Hartono et al.\ states at \SI{373}{\kelvin} measured at 0.4065, 0.5100 and \SI{0.6405}{\mega\pascal}, and \SI{0.3291}{\mega\pascal} at the Tong et al.\ state at \SI{393.2}{\kelvin} measured at \SI{0.6326}{\mega\pascal}.
These comparisons do not establish global stability.

% P:supplement-amp-extended
The fits to all loaded pressures add the 18 pressures of Hartono et al.\ at 373 and \SI{393}{\kelvin} and the 40 loaded pressures of Tong et al.\ to the first data, 214 observations in all, with the 105 loaded pressures sharing the weight that the 47 had.
Table~\ref{tab:supplement-amp-expanded} gives four fits to them, all with the salt-fitted interactions, with the slope fitted or fixed at \SI{-150}{\kelvin} and with the carbamate inputs of the MEA model or, as in the original fits, of the MEA fit with the MEA-fitted shared set.
The AMP model is the fit with the fitted slope and the carbamate inputs of the MEA model: the 18 pressures of Hartono et al.\ deviate by 6.90\,\% and the 40 of Tong et al.\ by 11.20\,\%, and the five \COtwo-free pressures of Tong et al., which are not fitted, by 3.85\,\%.
With the same inputs, fixing the slope at \SI{-150}{\kelvin}, 1.51 conditional standard errors below the fitted \SI{-54.976}{\kelvin}, raises the objective value from 75.1114 to 76.3527 and the deviation of the 105 loaded pressures from 10.84 to 11.02\,\%.
None of the four fits has a detected instability at the 666 states.
At the feed of the worked split of Table~\ref{tab:amp-reference-state}, the calculated bubble pressure is 1.155 and \SI{1.238}{\mega\pascal} for the two fits of the first data, against 1.907 and \SI{1.940}{\mega\pascal} for the fits to all loaded pressures with the MEA-fitted shared set, 1.886 and \SI{1.794}{\mega\pascal} for the original salt-fitted fits and 1.892512 and \SI{1.801649}{\mega\pascal} for the AMP model and its fixed-slope fit.
At \SI{2.037}{\mega\pascal}, above all of these, the first fit still splits, lowering $G/(RT)$ by 0.0185 mol, and none of the other five fits does (Table~\ref{tab:supplement-phase-replays}).
For the AMP model and its fixed-slope fit the finite screens at that pressure find no resolved descent, and the two-liquid calculations seeded from the split of the first fit coalesce, so the phase identity there is not resolved for them; the fixed-slope retry also lost a phase.

% P:supplement-amp-curvature
A local test at the fitted states does not show the difference between the fits.
At the 94 loaded states of the first data, the lowest eigenvalue of the composition Hessian of the Gibbs energy is positive for both fits of those data, with minima of 0.122 for the first fit and 0.137 with the slope fixed; at the 152 loaded states of all loaded pressures it is positive for the two fits with the MEA-fitted shared set, with minima of 0.408 and 0.439.
The eigenvalues were not calculated for the AMP model.

% P:supplement-amp-profile
The data do not determine the slope closely.
For the MEA-fitted shared set, refits with the slope fixed at a series of values give the interval within which the objective rises by less than the reference change of Section~\ref{sec:s-shared}: $-365$ to \SI{-134}{\kelvin} for the first data, with a fitted value of \SI{-219}{\kelvin} and a reference change of 1.58, and $-283$ to \SI{-77}{\kelvin} for all loaded pressures, with \SI{-181}{\kelvin} and 1.36.
The slope of the first fit lies inside the second interval, and the fits to all loaded pressures differ from the first fit in their other parameters as well, as the bubble pressures above show.
The interval was not recalculated for the AMP model, whose fitted slope is \SI{-54.976}{\kelvin} with a conditional standard error of \SI{63.029}{\kelvin}; a conditional profile of the original salt-fitted fit crosses the reference change near $-155.7$ and \SI{+47.2}{\kelvin}.

% P:supplement-amp-split-terms
Recalculated directly from the equation of state, the worked split of the first fit at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} reproduces its Gibbs-energy decrease within $1.2\times10^{-13}$ mol, the chemical potentials of \COtwo, AMP and water agree between the liquids within $3.9\times10^{-14}\,RT$, and the single liquid is locally unstable, with a lowest eigenvalue of $-0.083$ of the composition Hessian of its Gibbs energy.
Along the composition line between the two liquids, dispersion, association and the Debye--H\"uckel term make the molar $G/(RT)$ concave, with curvatures of $-0.369$, $-0.228$ and $-0.218$, against $+0.343$, $+0.211$ and $+0.236$ from ideal mixing, the hard chain and the Born term, for a total of $-0.024$; the fit with the slope fixed at \SI{-150}{\kelvin} gives $+0.030$ there.
Changing only the temperature coefficient of the \ce{AMPH+}--water interaction of the first fit from $-219.25$ to \SI{-150}{\kelvin} makes that total positive, $+3.4\times10^{-4}$, and places the Gibbs energy of the two liquids \SI{0.0190}{\mole} above that of the single liquid, so this coefficient controls the split.
With the same fitted coefficients, the exact average of Figiel et al.\ over ions and solvents (their Eq.~9) \cite{Figiel2025} in place of the average used here keeps the split and changes its Gibbs-energy decrease from 0.018814 to 0.018693 mol, by 0.64\,\%.
With the Born term removed and every other coefficient fixed, the two liquid compositions remain distinct liquid roots whose combination lies 0.364 mol below the single liquid, but the two-liquid calculations did not converge, so the amounts of the two liquids without the Born term are unresolved.
No measurement used here tests the split, which was a prediction of the first fit above the temperatures of its fitted loaded-solution pressures; the term analysis was not repeated for the AMP model, whose finite searches find no split.

Lal et al.\ compare the Kent--Eisenberg treatment graphically with observations not used to fit those earlier constants and give no numerical deviation for the observations used here \cite{lalSolubilityH2SCO21985}.
The infinite-dilution comparison of Carson et al.\ concerns a different quantity from the finite-loading integral heats fitted here \cite{carsonEnthalpySolutionCarbon2000}.
Horstmann et al.\ fit binary vapor--liquid equilibrium and excess enthalpy simultaneously and give no numerical deviation for that curve, so the two models are compared only in sign; the same heat observations are comparison only here \cite{horstmannPhaseEquilibriumExcess2002}.
The source's excess-enthalpy uncertainty expression has no absolute-value bars on its enthalpy-dependent term despite negative measured heats; it is not used to compute a negative uncertainty.

The statistic and the 56 observations therefore do not support a direct AARD comparison with the 51 observations used here.
Hartono et al.\ (2021) compare an existing electrolyte NRTL model with their AMP pressures graphically, without a scalar deviation on these observations \cite{hartonoNewSolubilityHeat2021}.

% P:supplement-published-kuranov

\section{Published parameters of Hong et al.\ on identical MEA observations}\label{sec:s-hong}
% P:supplement-hong-model
This section gives the present implementation of the model of Hong et al.\ at fixed pathway weight and its evaluation on measured MEA observations; its tabulated pressures are equal-fugacity calculations with the vapor amount fixed at $10^{-9}$ of the feed, and its liquid fails the local stability test at 131 of the 132 states inside the fitted range; Hong et al.\ themselves report a 1.53\,\% AARD against the simulator pressures to which the model was fitted (their Section~3.2 and Table~3) \cite{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.
Hong et al.\ describe loaded 30 wt\% MEA with a PC-SAFT model of the three components MEA, water and \COtwo, without ions or reaction equilibria \cite{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.
Two association schemes between MEA and \COtwo, one for the carbamate pathway and one for the bicarbonate pathway, are blended by a pathway weight, which their Table~S1 gives at 90 nodes: nine temperatures from 313.15 to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} and ten loadings from 0.1 to 1.0 mol/mol.
They fitted the model of the loaded solution to pressures calculated with a process simulator at 30 wt\% MEA, not to measurements.

% P:supplement-hong-evaluation
Their published parameters are evaluated here with the epcsaft package, and nothing is refitted.
The calculation uses the parameters of their Tables~1--3, the correction of their Eq.~10 for the symmetric two-site scheme, and the weight interpolated bilinearly in temperature and loading between the nodes \cite{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.
The weight is held fixed while each state is calculated, as in their work, so the states do not share one Helmholtz energy.
Each observation is calculated at its measured temperature and at the composition that the other two models use for it, and the deviations of the MEA model and of the model of Polley and Hedengren \cite{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026} are recalculated for the same observations.

% P:supplement-hong-observations
The compared observations are those at nominal 30 wt\% MEA inside the range of the weight table, which is the fitted range of Hong et al.
Of the 159 \COtwo partial pressures from six sources, 132 lie inside it and 14 have a loading below 0.1 mol/mol.
The other 13 are Hilliard pressures measured at 313.00--313.147 K, at most \SI{0.15}{\kelvin} below the lowest node; they are calculated at their measured temperatures with the weights of the 313.15 K nodes, and Table~\ref{tab:supplement-hong-comparison} gives the pressure deviations with and without them.
All 23 Wagner pressures lie inside the range.
Of the 86 intervals of Kim and Svendsen, 17 begin below a loading of 0.1 mol/mol, and 13 end above the loading of 0.60 mol/mol up to which the other two models are compared (Section~\ref{sec:s-files}); the remaining 56 are compared.
Of the 210 density observations, 88 are loaded solutions at 30 wt\% MEA inside the range; 75 are at other MEA contents, 22 have a loading below 0.1 mol/mol and 25 lie below 313.15 K.
Species fractions and ratios are not compared, because the model has no species and its pathway fractions are not mole fractions.
Integral heats and heat capacities are not compared, because they require an enthalpy and the states do not share one Helmholtz energy.
The pressures at 15 and 45 wt\% MEA and the binary data lie outside the weight table.

% P:supplement-hong-quantities
The pressure is the \COtwo partial pressure $y_{\COtwo}P$ at the bubble point, and the density is that of the liquid at the temperature, composition and pressure of the observation, without a phase-equilibrium calculation.
Hong et al.\ define the absorption heat by the pressure slope at fixed loading, $\Delta H=R\,\partial\ln p_{\COtwo}/\partial(1/T)$, with the weight evaluated again at each temperature \cite{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.
This pressure-slope heat is a different quantity from the calorimetric heat of Kim and Svendsen and from the enthalpy difference of main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:addition-heat}) that the other two models use; it is compared with the interval measurements as Hong et al.\ did.
The value for an interval is the mean of the released heat, $-\Delta H$, over the loadings of that interval.
At a node the interpolated weight has a kink in temperature, so the slope is taken toward higher temperature at 313.15 K, toward lower temperature at 393.15 K and on both sides at 353.15 K, where the two values differ by up to 3.51 kJ/mol of \COtwo and both are reported.

\input{tables/supplement-hong-comparison}

% P:supplement-hong-results
On the 132 pressures inside the fitted range, the Hong parameters deviate by 24.77\,\%, the MEA model by 24.97\,\% and the model of Polley and Hedengren by 50.93\,\% (Table~\ref{tab:supplement-hong-comparison}); the MEA model fitted 32 of these observations and Hong et al.\ none.
With the 13 Hilliard pressures at 313.00--313.147 K, all of which the MEA model fitted, the deviations are 26.27, 23.40 and 53.83\,\%.
On the Wagner pressures, which none of the three models fitted, the Hong parameters deviate least.
Their heat deviations exceed those of the other two models at 313.15 K and are similar at 353.15 and 393.15 K, and their density deviations lie between those of the other two models.
The 13 intervals of Kim and Svendsen that end above a loading of 0.60 mol/mol are calculated with the Hong parameters only: the RMSE is 4.58 kJ/mol for the ten at 313.15 K and 6.47 or 6.67 kJ/mol, depending on the side of the slope, for the three at 353.15 K.

% P:supplement-hong-stability
The epcsaft package accepts a bubble point only if the liquid is locally stable, that is, if the lowest eigenvalue of the Hessian of its Helmholtz energy with respect to the component amounts, at fixed temperature and volume, is not negative.
This local test is not the tangent-plane search of Section~\ref{sec:s-phase}.
In the present implementation of the Hong et al.\ model at fixed pathway weight, its liquid fails the test at 167 of the 168 measured pressure states, namely 131 of the 132 inside the fitted range, all 13 Hilliard states at 313.00--313.147 K and all 23 Wagner states.
It also fails at 62 of the 90 nodes of the weight table, where the lowest eigenvalue of the Hessian scaled by the mole fractions lies between $-1.092$ and $-0.018$.
At a state that fails the test, the tabulated pressure is an equal-fugacity calculation for that liquid, solved with the vapor amount fixed at $10^{-9}$ of the feed; it is not an equilibrium pressure of the model.
Every compared heat uses at least one such state.
The one measured state that passes, a Jou pressure at 393.15 K and a loading of 0.639 mol/mol, passes a local test, which does not establish global stability.

% P:supplement-hong-checks
The interpolation returns all 90 tabulated weights, and at weights of zero and one the residual Helmholtz energy equals that of the corresponding single scheme.
At 313.15 K and a loading of 0.50 mol/mol the calculated \COtwo partial pressure, 2.502 kPa, lies inside the interval of 2.339--2.896 kPa read from the model curve of their Fig.~4(b).
The 180 pathway fractions printed in their Fig.~5 are reproduced with largest absolute differences of 0.0012 for the carbamate pathway and 0.0064 for the bicarbonate pathway, against a printing precision of 0.00005, so the two implementations differ measurably.
At three states that fail the local test, the pressure differs from an independent equal-fugacity solution by at most $4.3\times10^{-8}$ in relative terms, and at five such states a finite-difference Hessian gives the same negative eigenvalue to four significant digits.
Of the states at which pressures were calculated with the Hong parameters, 31 pass the test; at these, the calculation with the fixed vapor amount differs from the bubble point by at most $5.5\times10^{-8}$.
On all 159 six-source pressures, the recalculated deviations of the other two models, 22.92 and 52.34\,\%, equal those of main-text Table~\ref{main-tab:mea-data}.
Repeating the whole calculation reproduces every tabulated value.
With the pressure-slope heat taken at the final loading of each interval, the convention of Hong et al., the AARD on the 54 intervals at 313.15 and 353.15 K is 11.66 or 11.09\,\% for the two slopes at 353.15 K; they report 9.48\,\% without listing the observations used \cite{hongModelingCO2Absorption2027}.
Their reported pressure AARD of 1.53\,\% refers to simulator output that is not available, so it is not reproduced here.

% P:supplement-hong-limits
The values for the Hong model are those of its published parameters, evaluated with an implementation other than that of Hong et al., on observations that they did not fit.
The MEA model fitted 45 of the 145 pressures and 32 of the 88 densities, and every observation was examined before its parameters were selected.
The deviations therefore show agreement on the same observations and do not rank the models as predictors.
The models also differ in their parameters, association schemes and fitted data, so the comparison does not show whether explicit ions are needed.
It is not a physical validation of any of the three models.
