\subsection{Caloric and density calculations}\label{sec:heat-method}
% P:methods-heat-01
Eight Vinjarapu integral heats are fitted at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal}, from initially carbon-free 30 wt\% solvent \cite[Sects.~1.1.1 and 2.2, Table~4]{HemaBhavya2025}.
The signed heat per mole of added \COtwo is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta h=\frac{H_{\mathrm{final}}-H_{\mathrm{initial}}-\Delta n_{\COtwo}h_{\COtwo}^{\mathrm{feed}}}{\Delta n_{\COtwo}},
 \label{eq:addition-heat}
\end{equation}
where both equilibrium liquid enthalpies and the pure-\COtwo feed enthalpy are evaluated at the stated temperature and pressure; negative values denote exothermic absorption.
Fourteen Arcis heats at \SI{322.5}{\kelvin} and \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} or \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal} are compared \cite[Sect.~2.4, Table~5]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.

% P:methods-heat-conditions
The four fitted Hilliard heat capacities use 7 mol \MEA/kg water, calculation pressure \SI{101325}{\pascal} and a $10^{-6}$ loading approximation for the nominally unloaded states \cite[Sects.~4.5--4.6, App.~G.2]{Hilliard2008}.

% P:methods-heat-03
Densities are calculated directly from the equation of state, without volume translation, so the density data constrain the same parameters that set the phase and reaction equilibria.
The fitted loaded observations comprise 15 Amundsen and 32 Hartono densities; five carbon-free densities use exactly zero analytical carbon, and all 67 Han densities are comparisons \cite{Amundsen2009,Hartono2014,hanDensityWater12012}.
Five Amundsen densities at loading 0.4 are excluded after comparison with Hartono values interpolated to the same loading, temperature and solvent composition; all five remain in the complete comparison.
The calculations use \SI{100000}{\pascal} for Amundsen where source pressure is unreported; other pressures follow the individual observations.
The loaded-density scale is
\begin{equation}
 s_{\rho,j}=\left[u_{\rho,j}^2+(g_j u_{\alpha,j})^2\right]^{1/2},
 \label{eq:density-scale}
\end{equation}
where $g_j$ is the density--loading secant between neighboring observations at the same source temperature and solvent composition, using a one-sided secant at the ends.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-density} gives the source-specific scales and qualifications.
The scales combine heterogeneous uncertainty information; $2s$ is not uniformly a 95\,\% interval, and Amundsen's precise high-temperature/loading uncertainty boundary remains unresolved \cite[pp.~3099--3100]{Amundsen2009}.


\subsection{Parameters and fitting}\label{sec:parameters}
% P:methods-04
Parameters were estimated in four fits, holding the earlier values fixed: five pure-water parameters to IAPWS-95, the \COtwo--water interaction and slope to Kiepe pressures, the MEA dielectric coefficients to unloaded-solution permittivities, then sixteen parameters jointly to \cref{tab:current-data-inventory}.
The joint bounded least-squares objective is
\begin{equation}
 C(\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac12\sum_jw_jr_j^2,
 \label{eq:fit-objective}
\end{equation}
where $r_j$ is a property-scaled difference or log ratio and $w_j$ its weight; Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-objective} defines every residual, scale and weight.
Loaded densities have weight $1/47$, carbon-free densities weight 10, binary MEA--water quantities unit weight and \COtwo--water pressures weight $1/39$.
At the final parameter set, binary MEA--water quantities contribute 61.85\,\% of the objective and loaded densities 0.3023\,\%; these shares describe fitting priorities rather than relative physical accuracy.
The solver uses bounded Levenberg--Marquardt least squares with exact equation-of-state and implicit equilibrium derivatives.


% P:methods-06
Agreement is reported as average absolute relative deviation (AARD),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{AARD}=\frac{100\,\%}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}-1\right|,
 \label{eq:aard}
\end{equation}
and root-mean-square error (RMSE) in the observable's units.
The solution checks for the fitted and compared pressure and species states are reported in Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-files}; they establish numerical convergence rather than physical validation.

\subsection{Phase-stability search}\label{sec:methods-phase}
% P:methods-phase-01
The selected fit enforces a finite tangent-plane stability requirement during fitting and is then assessed at offset and census states.
The final parameter set uses the original wide bounds (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-probes}); its fresh census is compared with the retained historical unstable fit at the same physical states.
The tangent-plane criterion \cite[Sect.~2]{Michelsen1982} is applied to reacting, charge-neutral trials, with reduced distance
\begin{equation}
 D(\boldsymbol w)=g(T,P,\boldsymbol w)/(RT)-\boldsymbol w^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda,
 \qquad \sum_iw_i=1,\quad\sum_i z_iw_i=0,\quad w_i>0,
 \label{eq:tpd}
\end{equation}
where $B$ represents conserved component combinations and $\boldsymbol\lambda$ their chemical-potential multipliers in the existing equilibrium.
Instability is detected only when a negative distance is accompanied by a material-conserving finite transfer that lowers Gibbs energy beyond its numerical-error estimate; Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-phase} gives the method details and the searched states.
