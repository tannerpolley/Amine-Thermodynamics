# ChemRxiv submission draft

## Title

Thermodynamics of CO₂-loaded aqueous monoethanolamine with ePC-SAFT: phase equilibria, speciation, absorption heat, density and phase stability

## Author and affiliation

Tanner W. Polley; ORCID 0009-0008-5957-4152; tpolley3@byu.edu. Department of Chemical Engineering, Brigham Young University, Provo, UT 84602, United States.

## Abstract

A thermodynamic description of reactive absorption must combine equilibrium, caloric and density properties of the loaded solvent. This work jointly estimates sixteen ePC-SAFT parameters for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) from pressure, species, integral absorption heat, density, heat capacity and binary equilibrium data, following separate water, CO₂–water and permittivity fits. For nominal 30 wt% MEA, pressure average absolute relative deviations are 11.86% for 47 fitted and 24.2% for 112 non-fitted pressures (20.55% overall), over 313.00–393.15 K; fitted pressures end at 333.15 K. The measured MEA + MEAH⁺ sum and carbamate fractions are among 94 of the 101 fitted species fractions, which deviate by 7.10%. Integral-heat root-mean-square error is 1.84 kJ/mol CO₂ at 313.15 K; loaded-density deviation is 0.18% for 47 fitted values through 353.15 K, without volume translation. A fit with wider bounds on the protonated-amine diameter and its water interaction gives similar property agreement, but instability is detected for it at 54 of the 162 searched states from 313.15 to 393.15 K; the final parameter set has no additional liquid detected at those states. This numerical search does not physically validate phase behavior. Six parameters are on bounds, and four of the remaining ten have conditional standard errors larger than their absolute values; the 0.49 pK shift in the protonated-MEA dissociation constant lies within its conditional standard error of 0.55 pK. Bicarbonate, carbonate, higher-temperature caloric and density properties, and pressure transfer to 15 and 45 wt% MEA remain limitations. In a brief MDEA demonstration, two representations of the protonated amine rank in opposite order against two pressure data sources, so neither is identified as generally preferable.
