\section{Model and transfer design}\label{sec:theory}
% P:theory-01
Each liquid contains molecular \COtwo, water, the amine A and its cation \ce{AH+}, together with bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide; the MEA, DEA and AMP liquids also contain the carbamate \ce{ACOO-}, which MDEA does not form.
Only \COtwo, water and the molecular amine enter the vapor, and one ePC-SAFT equation of state describes both phases.

\subsection{ePC-SAFT contributions}\label{sec:contributions}
% P:theory-02
The reduced residual Helmholtz energy, $a^{\mathrm{res}}=A^{\mathrm{res}}/(nRT)$, is the sum of hard-chain, dispersion, association, Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions \cite{Gross2001,Gross2002,Cameretti2005,Held2014,Figiel2025}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{res}}
    =
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}
    +a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    +a^{\mathrm{assoc}}
    +a^{\mathrm{DH}}
    +a^{\mathrm{Born}} .
    \label{eq:epcsaft-residual-split}
\end{equation}
A neutral species is a chain of $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}$ spherical segments of diameter $\sigma_i$ and dispersion energy $u_i$; an ion is a single charged sphere, except the DEA and MDEA cations, which keep the chain of their amine.
Pressure, chemical potentials and caloric properties follow from the derivatives of $a^{\mathrm{res}}$ \cite{Gross2001}, including the variation of the relative permittivity (\cref{eq:solvent-permittivity}).
Below, $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions, and $\varepsilon$ denotes a relative permittivity, as distinct from the association energy $\epsilon^{AB}$.

% P:theory-02a
The hard-chain and dispersion terms are those of Gross and Sadowski \cite{Gross2001}, the association term follows Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite{Kleiner2007}, and the Debye--H\"uckel term is that of Cameretti et al.\ \cite{Cameretti2005}; Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-contributions} of the Supplementary Material gives the four terms.
The hard-sphere diameter $d_i$ of a neutral species or chain cation depends on temperature \cite{Gross2001}, with the water diameter $\sigma_w(T)=\sigma_0+a_1\exp(b_1T)+a_2\exp(b_2T)$ in place of a constant $\sigma_i$ (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-parameters}).
A single-sphere ion has the temperature-independent packing diameter $d_i=0.88\sigma_i$ \cite{Held2014}.

% P:theory-contributions-03
The unlike-pair diameter is $\sigma_{ij}=(\sigma_i+\sigma_j)/2$, and the pair energy is
\begin{equation}
    u_{ij}=\sqrt{u_iu_j}\,\left(1-k_{ij}\right).
    \label{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}
\end{equation}
Dispersion between ions of the same charge sign, including a single-sphere ion with itself, is excluded ($u_{ij}=0$), while ion--solvent and cation--anion pairs keep \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule} \cite{Figiel2025}; the two chain cations keep the dispersion between their own segments, a choice of this model.
A temperature-dependent interaction is linear in reciprocal temperature,
\begin{equation}
 k_{ij}(T)=k_{ij}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})+b_{ij}(T^{-1}-T_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}),
 \qquad T_{\mathrm{ref}}=\SI{313.15}{\kelvin},
 \label{eq:kij-temperature}
\end{equation}
with three exceptions that are linear in temperature, $k_{ij}(T)=k_{ij}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})+(\mathrm{d}k_{ij}/\mathrm{d}T)(T-T_{\mathrm{ref}})$: the water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions, about \SI{313.15}{\kelvin}, and the MDEA--water interaction, about \SI{423}{\kelvin}.

% P:theory-contributions-04
Only donor--acceptor pairs bond, and cross-association between an amine species and water uses the Wolbach--Sandler rule \cite{Kleiner2007}.
The Debye--H\"uckel term describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$.

% P:theory-04
The Born term has the solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation form of Figiel et al.\ \cite{Figiel2025}, with its Eq.~7 as corrected in 2026 \cite{Figiel2026Correction}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{Born}}
    =
    -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0k_BT}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}x_iz_i^2
    \left[
        \frac{1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{-1}}{D_i}
        +c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}\left(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}^{-1}\right)
        \left(\frac{1}{d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}}-\frac{1}{D_i}\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:born-ssm-ds}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    D_i=d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\left[1+c_{\mathrm{shell}}\frac{f_{\mathrm{mix}}-1}{|z_i|}\right],
    \qquad
    f_{\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_kx_kf_k}{\sum_kx_k}.
    \label{eq:born-shell-diameter}
\end{equation}
Here \(d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\) is the Born diameter, \(D_i\) is the outer diameter of the first solvation shell, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=8\) is the permittivity of the region between them, and \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}=c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}=1\).
The solvation factor is \(f_k=1.5\) for water \cite{Figiel2025} and 1 for every other component.
Figiel et al.\ restrict the average in \cref{eq:born-shell-diameter} to ions and solvents; here it runs over all components and the amines and \COtwo have unit factors, so \(f_{\mathrm{mix}}=1+0.5\,x_{\HtwoO}\) and the shell diameter changes with loading.

% P:theory-05
The bulk permittivity has the Schreckenberg form reported by Yang et al.\ \cite{Yang2024}:
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}=1+\frac{n_s}{V}d_{V,\mathrm{mix}}
 \left(\frac{d_{T,\mathrm{mix}}}{T}-1\right),\qquad
 d_{q,\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}n_i d_{q,i}}{n_s},
 \quad q\in\{V,T\},
 \label{eq:solvent-permittivity}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the phase volume, the neutral set $\mathcal S$ comprises water, the molecular amine and molecular \COtwo, and $n_s=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}n_i$; the coefficients $d_{V,i}$ are given in L/mol, so with $V$ in \si{\cubic\metre} they are multiplied by \SI{e-3}{\cubic\metre\per\litre} to make $(n_s/V)\,d_{V,\mathrm{mix}}$ dimensionless.
Ions affect the permittivity only through the phase volume and the neutral amounts.

\subsection{Species and shared parameters}\label{sec:species}
% P:theory-shared
Six species are common to the four amines: \COtwo, water and the four inorganic ions.
Their parameters, their interactions with water and the equilibrium-constant correlations of their reactions (R1--R3, \cref{sec:reactions}) are set before any amine is fitted and are the same in all four models; \cref{tab:species-parameters,tab:interaction-matrix} mark them.
This is the transfer design: every difference between the amine models lies in the amine species, their interactions and their own reaction correlations.
Water has one donor and one acceptor association site, with parameters fitted to the IAPWS-95 reference equation \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}, and \COtwo has one induced-association site of each type that bonds only with water \cite{Kleiner2007,Schick2023,Pabsch2020}.

% P:theory-salt
The water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fixed from data that contain no amine.
They are fitted to the osmotic coefficients of aqueous sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate tabulated by Peiper and Pitzer from their ion-interaction model, 45 values for each salt at 278--\SI{318}{\kelvin} and up to \SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram} \cite{peiperThermodynamicsAqueousCarbonate1982}; Wangler et al.\ fixed the water--bicarbonate interaction from the same source \cite{Wangler2018}.
The fit is made at the ionic compositions printed with each tabulated value, with the dissolved \COtwo subtracted as an ideal solute, so it reproduces the tabulated coefficients of the ions conditional on those compositions and on the parameter bounds: the AARD is 0.16\,\% for sodium bicarbonate and 5.1\,\% for sodium carbonate, and six mixed-salt rows that fail a charge-closure check have no calculated value (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-salt}).
Wangler et al.\ fitted a water--bicarbonate interaction to the same source with their own water and ion parameters \cite{Wangler2018}; the two values are not interchangeable.
The fit is conditional on two values that lie on their lower bound of $-1$: the water--sodium interaction, fitted beforehand to sodium chloride, and the water--carbonate interaction at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}.
The comparison throughout is the \emph{MEA-fitted shared set}, in which the water--bicarbonate interaction is instead a constant fitted with the MEA parameters and the water--carbonate interaction is the value of Held et al.\ \cite{Held2014}.
In that fit the water--bicarbonate interaction is 0.765 with a conditional standard error of 3.18, so the MEA data do not determine it.

% P:theory-amine-species
MEA and AMP are two-site chains and DEA and MDEA four-site chains; the MEA, DEA and MDEA parameters are taken from the literature, and the five pure-component parameters of AMP were fitted here to vapor pressures and liquid densities (\cref{tab:species-parameters,tab:transfer-species}, Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-transfer-data}).
No amine species bonds with \COtwo.
The MEA and AMP cations and the three carbamates are single spheres; the DEA and MDEA cations keep the segment count and association sites of their amine, with zero self-association energy.
For every amine ion the Debye--H\"uckel and Born diameters equal the segment diameter, so no amine ion has a separately fitted Born diameter.
For the two chain cations the tied Born diameter is therefore that of one segment, \SI{2.948}{\angstrom} for \ce{DEAH+} and \SI{3.596}{\angstrom} for \ce{MDEAH+}, not the volume-equivalent diameter $\sigma(m^{\mathrm{seg}})^{1/3}$ of 5.108 and \SI{5.550}{\angstrom}.
These diameters define the parameterization and are not measured ion sizes.

% P:theory-permittivity-inputs
The permittivity coefficients of each amine were fitted beforehand to measured permittivities without \COtwo: for MEA those of the aqueous solution at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} \cite{agieienkoWhatGasStream2024a}, and for DEA, MDEA and AMP those of Hsieh et al.\ \cite{hsiehDielectricConstantsAqueous2007}.
The water coefficients are those of Yang et al.\ \cite{Yang2024} and the \COtwo coefficients are set in this work.
Use of \cref{eq:solvent-permittivity} at other temperatures and in loaded solutions is a model assumption.

\subsection{Reactions and phase equilibrium}\label{sec:reactions}
% P:theory-06
A calculation starts from an apparent feed of \SI{1}{\mole} of amine, the water amount given by the source composition and \(\alpha\)~\si{\mole} of \COtwo, where \(\alpha\) is the loading.
The liquid conserves the elements of this feed, which implies electroneutrality and leaves the independent reactions of \cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants}: R1--R3 for the shared species, carbamate hydrolysis (R4, absent for MDEA) and amine dissociation (R5).
Bubble states equate the chemical potentials of \COtwo, water and the molecular amine between the phases, and every reacting liquid satisfies, for each reaction \(r\),
\begin{equation}
    \sum_i\nu_{ri}\ln a_i=\ln K_r(T).
    \label{eq:activity-reaction-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
With the fugacity coefficient $\ln\phi_i=\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}/(RT)-\ln Z$ and compressibility factor $Z=P/(\rho RT)$, the activities are
\begin{equation}
 a_w=x_w\frac{\phi_w(T,P,\boldsymbol x)P}{\phi_w^{\mathrm{pure}}(T,P_{\mathrm{src}})P_{\mathrm{src}}},\qquad
 a_i=\frac{x_i}{M_wm^\circ}\frac{\phi_i(T,P,\boldsymbol x)P}{\phi_i^{\infty,w}(T,P_{\mathrm{src}})P_{\mathrm{src}}}\quad(i\ne w),
 \label{eq:activities}
\end{equation}
where $M_w$ is the molar mass of water, $m^\circ=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram}$, and $P_{\mathrm{src}}=\SI{100}{\kilo\pascal}$ is the pressure at which the reference states of every reaction are evaluated, the same value as the ideal-gas standard pressure $P^\circ$ of the caloric functions (\cref{sec:caloric-model}).
The correlations of \cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants} depend on temperature only, and a fixed reference pressure adds no pressure dependence to them.
At a given temperature and pressure the reference states do not depend on composition, so all phases compared at that state share one Gibbs energy.
That Gibbs energy, $G^*=G_{\mathrm{EOS}}+RT\sum_in_ib_i(T,P_{\mathrm{src}})$, where $RTb_i$ is the reference contribution of species $i$, is the function minimized in the phase searches, and at fixed amounts its pressure derivative is the volume of the equation of state,
\begin{equation}
 \left(\frac{\partial G^*}{\partial P}\right)_{T,\boldsymbol n}=V_{\mathrm{EOS}}+RT\sum_in_i\left(\frac{\partial b_i}{\partial P_{\mathrm{src}}}\right)_T\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\mathrm{src}}}{\mathrm{d}P}=V_{\mathrm{EOS}},
 \label{eq:pressure-tracking}
\end{equation}
because $\mathrm{d}P_{\mathrm{src}}/\mathrm{d}P=0$.
Equilibrium compositions minimize $G^*$, densities are calculated from $V_{\mathrm{EOS}}$ and enthalpies from \cref{eq:total-enthalpy}, so all three follow from $G^*$.
Finite differences of $G^*$, reconstructed from the equation of state and the reference contributions at the same conserved feed, verify \cref{eq:pressure-tracking} numerically at one fitted bubble state of each amine: MEA at \SI{313.02}{\kelvin} and \SI{6.675}{\kilo\pascal}, DEA at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{7.130}{\kilo\pascal}, AMP at \SI{353.00}{\kelvin} and \SI{88.79}{\kilo\pascal} and MDEA at \SI{333.16}{\kelvin} and \SI{0.745}{\mega\pascal}.
There the relative difference from $V_{\mathrm{EOS}}$ is at most $1.4\times10^{-5}$, within numerical error estimates of 0.12\,\% for MDEA, 0.46\,\% for AMP, 8.9\,\% for DEA and 10.5\,\% for MEA; the check is a numerical verification at these states, not a proof, and at the MEA and DEA states it excludes only differences larger than those estimates (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-files}, Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-fixed-reference-consistency}).
Evaluating the R1--R3 references at the system pressure instead makes the right side of \cref{eq:pressure-tracking} nonzero; refitted that way, the four models differ in their fitted pressure deviations by at most 0.23 percentage points, but the calculated MDEA phase behavior above \SI{200}{\mega\pascal} changes (\cref{sec:results-phase}, Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-files}).
With the molality $m_i=x_i/(x_wM_w)$, \cref{eq:activities} is the usual molality activity $a_i=\gamma_i^mm_i/m^\circ$ with the activity coefficient normalized at infinite dilution in water.
The infinite-dilution reference is evaluated for the charge-neutral combinations of species that appear in the reactions, and every constant is applied on one aqueous-molality basis (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-parameters}).

% P:theory-07
The correlations of R1--R3 and of MEA have the form \cite{Austgen1991}
\begin{equation}
 \ln K_r=A_r+B_r/T+C_r\ln T+D_rT+\Delta_r,
 \label{eq:reaction-equilibrium-constant-temperature}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_r$ converts the source to the common basis; Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-transfer-parameters} gives those of the other amines in an equivalent form.
R1--R3 and the R4 and R5 correlations of MEA are fixed at their literature forms, and so is R5 of DEA, MDEA and AMP.
The MEA R4 correlation of Tong et al.\ converts a constant that Aroua et al.\ measured by titration of dilute MEA solutions, not one fitted to phase-equilibrium data \cite{Tong2012,arouaEquilibriumConstantCarbamate1999}.
The R4 correlations of DEA and AMP start from a literature correlation and are then fitted: the fit adjusts the constant term and an enthalpy increment $\delta h$ that changes the temperature dependence through
\begin{equation}
 \delta\ln K(T)=-\frac{\delta h}{R}\left(\frac1T-\frac1{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right).
 \label{eq:reaction-shift}
\end{equation}
\Cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants} gives the source and the source temperature range of each constant.

\subsection{Enthalpy, heat capacity, density and excess enthalpy}\label{sec:caloric-model}
% P:theory-caloric-01
Total enthalpy follows the same Helmholtz energy and reaction standard states as the equilibrium calculation:
\begin{equation}
 H=PV-RT^2\left(\frac{\partial(A/RT)}{\partial T}\right)_{V,\boldsymbol n},\qquad
 A=A^{\mathrm{ig}}+A^{\mathrm{res}}.
 \label{eq:total-enthalpy}
\end{equation}
Neutral ideal-gas functions supply the caloric reference, and the ionic functions follow from the equilibrium-constant correlations converted from their aqueous reference states to ideal gases at $P^\circ$, $\Delta_rh_{P^\circ}^{\mathrm{ig}}=RT^2\,\mathrm{d}\ln K_{r,P^\circ}^{\mathrm{ig}}/\mathrm{d}T$; no separate reaction-enthalpy term is added (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-files} gives the conversion and a worked example).
This enthalpy agrees with $-T^2[\partial(G^*/T)/\partial T]_{P,\boldsymbol n}$: the temperature perturbations are re-solved at the same feed, and at reaction equilibrium the change of composition does not contribute to first order.
At the four fitted states named in \cref{sec:reactions} the two differ by 0.9 to 2.5~mJ per mole of amine fed, within numerical error estimates of 0.06 to 0.52~J/mol, and the same holds for the single liquid and for the two liquid inventories of the MDEA feed at \SI{373.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{204.054}{\mega\pascal} (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-files}).
Equilibrium heat capacity is $C_P=(\mathrm{d}H_{\mathrm{eq}}/\mathrm{d}T)_{P,\boldsymbol n_{\mathrm{feed}}}$, including the change in speciation.
Density is the mass of the apparent components divided by the equilibrium liquid volume, without volume translation, and a binary excess enthalpy subtracts the mole-fraction-weighted pure-liquid enthalpies from the mixture molar enthalpy.
