\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\subsection{MEA absorption and the required properties}
% P:introduction-01
Models of \COtwo absorption in aqueous monoethanolamine (\MEA) need consistent descriptions of equilibrium pressure, liquid species, heat of absorption, density and heat capacity.
Equilibrium pressure determines the gas--liquid driving force, while speciation supplies concentrations for reaction-rate expressions \cite[Sect.~1, p.~67]{Zhang2011}.
Freguia and Rochelle separate reboiler duty into desorption heat, water vaporization and sensible heating, which depends on the solvent heat capacity \cite[Eq.~13, Fig.~3, p.~1680]{Freguia2003}; absorption heating also affects the absorber temperature profile \cite[Sect.~5.3, Figs.~10--11]{Kvamsdal2008}.
Density enters column sizing and mass-transfer correlations \cite[Introduction, p.~3096]{Amundsen2009}.

% P:introduction-02
MEA forms carbamate through an overall reaction consuming two amine molecules per \COtwo molecule; carbamate reversion to bicarbonate becomes important at higher \COtwo content \cite[Eqs.~1--4, pp.~1883--1884]{MacDowell2010}.
This differs from tertiary N-methyldiethanolamine (MDEA), which promotes bicarbonate formation without forming carbamate \cite[Sect.~1, p.~1467]{Vaidya2007}.
Pressure, speciation and absorption heat therefore depend on the same reaction equilibria, which a model must combine with nonideal activities of the molecular and ionic species.

\subsection{Activity-coefficient models}
% P:introduction-03
Liu et al.\ trace the progression from Kent--Eisenberg's ideal activity and fugacity coefficients with fitted reaction constants to Deshmukh--Mather's extended Debye--H\"uckel treatment, the interaction parameters tabulated by Weiland and co-workers, Li--Mather's Pitzer treatment, and Austgen's electrolyte non-random two-liquid (e-NRTL) model \cite[Introduction, pp.~2080--2081]{Liu1999}.
In their MEA refit, Liu et al.\ warn that simultaneous estimation of the carbamate constant and interaction parameters can overfit scattered solubility data \cite[p.~2083]{Liu1999}.
B\"ottinger et al.\ instead refitted a Pitzer model's carbamate constant to nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) observations after finding systematically low calculated carbamate \cite[Sect.~5]{Bottinger2008}.
Hessen et al.'s refined e-NRTL fit included binary vapor--liquid equilibrium (VLE), freezing-point depression and excess enthalpy, and predicted speciation \cite[Sect.~5, p.~3646]{Hessen2010}.
Aronu et al.\ extended the concentration range to 15--60 wt\% MEA using an extended universal quasi-chemical (UNIQUAC) activity model fitted to solubility data, with NMR speciation predicted \cite[Sects.~1, 4.3]{Aronu2011}.

% P:introduction-04
Joint fitting to several properties is established practice for activity models.
Hilliard fitted e-NRTL parameters to pressure, absorption heat, heat capacity and NMR data, but 18 of 35 parameter estimates were smaller than their standard errors \cite[Tables~13.4-2--13.4-3, pp.~440--441]{Hilliard2008}.
Zhang et al.\ also fitted these properties jointly; their average absolute relative deviations (AARDs) in \COtwo pressure, 13.5--35.5\,\% by source, are for data used in the fit \cite[Sect.~3.3, Table~9, p.~72]{Zhang2011}.
Akula et al.\ fitted pressure and differential heat using an analytical excess-enthalpy expression, leaving speciation and heat capacity as comparisons outside the fit \cite[Sect.~1, p.~2; pp.~10--11]{Akula2023a}.
These studies establish joint fitting of pressure, speciation and caloric data, and they show that such fits can leave parameters strongly correlated or poorly determined \cite[Table~7]{Akula2023a}.
Their activity-coefficient models also need separate descriptions of the vapor phase and the liquid density.

\subsection{SAFT and other equations of state}
% P:introduction-05
Statistical associating fluid theory (SAFT) describes both phases with one equation of state \cite[Introduction, p.~1884]{MacDowell2010}, which also gives the liquid density.
SAFT models of aqueous MEA differ in how they represent the reaction products.
Button and Gubbins's early aqueous-MEA application used association without reactions or ions \cite[Sect.~1, p.~176]{Button1999}.
Nasrifar and Tafazzol combined perturbed-chain SAFT (PC-SAFT) fugacities for molecular phase equilibrium with literature reaction constants and unit liquid-phase activity coefficients for speciation, with ions confined to the liquid \cite[Sects.~3--4, Table~1; Sect.~6, pp.~7626--7627]{Nasrifar2010}.
Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh's PC-SAFT and Najafloo and Zarei's Huang--Radosz SAFT likewise used ideal activities in the chemical equilibria \cite[Sect.~3.3]{Baygi2015}\cite[Sect.~3.3]{Najafloo2018}.

% P:introduction-06
Reactive-association models represent chemistry through molecular bonding, treating carbamate and protonated amine as neutral bound pairs in SAFT for potentials of variable range (SAFT-VR) \cite[pp.~1885, 1887]{MacDowell2010}.
Rodriguez et al.\ obtained speciation from bonded fractions \cite[Fig.~9b, p.~1341]{Rodriguez2012}, as did Perdomo et al.\ with group-contribution SAFT-\(\gamma\) Mie \cite[Sect.~2.2, Eqs.~10--11; Fig.~14]{perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}.
Soft-SAFT describes absorption as aggregation and estimates absorption heat from the pressure--temperature slope, which underestimates the measured heat at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} \cite[Sect.~4.7, Eq.~26, Fig.~10b]{lloretConsistentTransferableThermodynamic2017}.
Wang et al.\ report the same difficulty: their Peng--Robinson cubic-plus-association model underestimates MEA absorption enthalpy by 49\,\% when carbamate forms, and they suggest adding ionic terms \cite[Sect.~4.2.4, p.~83]{wangImprovementPRCPAEquation2018}.

% P:introduction-07
Explicit-ion models instead carry the ions as separate species with their own nonideal activities.
MEA ions, including carbamate, appear this way in T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros's electrolyte cubic two-state association model \cite[Sect.~3, p.~48]{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013} and Neumann et al.'s combined Helmholtz-energy/e-NRTL model \cite[Sect.~3, Table~3]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Najafloo et al.\ included carbamate ions of 2-((2-aminoethyl)amino)ethanol (AEEA) in electrolyte Huang--Radosz SAFT with mean spherical approximation and Born terms \cite[p.~148, App.~A]{najaflooMeasuringSolubilityCarbon2015}.
None of these explicit-ion models is based on PC-SAFT.

\subsection{Electrolyte SAFT and ePC-SAFT}
% P:introduction-08
Cameretti et al.\ introduced electrolyte PC-SAFT (ePC-SAFT) by adding a Debye--H\"uckel contribution and fitting ion parameters to aqueous-salt vapor pressures and densities \cite[Sects.~2--3, Table~2]{Cameretti2005}.
Held et al.\ added mean ionic activity coefficients to parameter estimation because they were more sensitive to ion parameters than VLE \cite[Sect.~3]{Held2008}; revised ePC-SAFT subsequently included cation--anion dispersion \cite[Sect.~3.1.2]{Held2014}.
For amines, Uyan et al.\ predicted MDEA ternary pressures and speciation after binary estimation \cite[Sects.~4.1--4.3]{Uyan2015}, and Wangler et al.\ fitted additional interactions to osmotic coefficients \cite[Sects.~6.1--6.2]{Wangler2018}.
Cleeton et al.\ treated competitive acid-gas absorption in MDEA \cite[Sect.~2]{Cleeton2020}, while B\"ulow et al.\ extended the treatment to mixed solvents \cite[Theory, ePC-SAFT parameters]{Bulow2021a}.
All four ePC-SAFT amine studies treat MDEA, which forms no carbamate.

% P:introduction-09
Ionic activities, density and residual enthalpy can share one Helmholtz energy, but reaction constants and their heats remain external correlations.
Cleeton et al.\ add van 't Hoff reaction terms to an equation-of-state derivative \cite[Sect.~2.4, Eq.~15]{Cleeton2020}; Wangler et al.\ combine reaction enthalpies with a Henry-coefficient dissolution term \cite[Sect.~5, Eqs.~22--28]{Wangler2018}.
Electrostatic choices also matter: the ePC-SAFT advanced revision of B\"ulow et al.\ combines a Born term with concentration-dependent permittivity, whose temperature derivative enters enthalpy \cite[Sect.~2.1, Eq.~6]{Bulow2020}.
Rueben et al.'s permittivity model with two ion-specific parameters gives average deviations below 5\,\% for mean ionic activity coefficients of the studied lithium, sodium and potassium salts and hydrogen halides at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} \cite[Sect.~3.1, Fig.~1]{Rueben2024}.
Because Born diameter and permittivity choices can compensate each other \cite[Sect.~3.2, Figs.~3--4]{Novak2023}, such inorganic-salt results do not fix the Born and permittivity parameters of reacting amine ions or their temperature dependence.

\subsection{Measurements that constrain the model}
% P:introduction-10
Different measurements constrain different parts of the description.
Wagner et al.\ report literature \COtwo pressures over 30 wt\% MEA differing by up to an order of magnitude \cite[Introduction, p.~883]{wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}.
Smith et al.\ show that different constants and activity parameters can reproduce similar pressures while implying different speciation, making species measurements more discriminating \cite[Sect.~6]{smithGibbsEnergyMinimization}.
Those measurements also require care: B\"ottinger's NMR reports molecular and protonated MEA as a sum \cite[Sect.~3, Table~3]{Bottinger2008}, whereas Wong's Raman carbamate calibration uses concentrations inferred from the amine balance \cite[Sect.~3.2, Eq.~13, Table~4]{Wong2015}.
Jakobsen et al.\ estimate NMR errors of about 5--10\,\% and warn that shared peaks make minor species difficult to quantify \cite[Results]{Jakobsen2005}.

% P:introduction-11
Kim and Svendsen distinguish directly measured absorption heats from values inferred through solubility slopes and caution against converting integral heats to differential values \cite[Results and Discussion, Eq.~1, Figs.~3, 6]{Kim2007}.
Loaded-liquid densities constrain volumetric behavior: Hartono et al.\ correlate them through a loading-dependent volume-expansion term \cite[Sect.~3.1, Eqs.~3--5]{Hartono2014}.
Neumann et al.\ report predicted loaded-liquid densities with 0.92\,\% AARD against Hartono's data and 3.5\,\% against Han's complete dataset, with individual deviations reaching 12\,\% at 60 wt\% MEA; they recommend multi-property fitting to improve the density description \cite[Sect.~4.1, p.~7; Sect.~4.2, p.~8, Fig.~6]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.
Fitting equilibrium, caloric and loaded-density data together therefore combines constraints that no single property supplies, but agreement with these properties does not test phase stability.

\subsection{Phase stability}
% P:introduction-12
Michelsen applies Gibbs's tangent-plane criterion before a flash calculation because the number of equilibrium phases is not known beforehand \cite[Introduction, pp.~1--2; The tangent plane criterion, pp.~2--4]{Michelsen1982}.
Tsanas et al.\ applied reactive stability analysis to a carbamate-forming, demixing amine mixture using e-NRTL \cite[Sects.~2.5, 3.1]{Tsanas2022}.
Perdomo et al.\ obtained liquid splits in reactive-association SAFT-\(\gamma\) Mie amine mixtures \cite[Sect.~4.4.2, Figs.~17--18]{perdomoPredictiveGroupcontributionFramework2023}, while Ascani et al.\ searched stability in ePC-SAFT advanced for fully dissociated salts without reactions \cite[Sect.~3.3, Fig.~1]{Ascani2022}.
Perdomo et al.'s later formal tangent-plane criterion incorporates phase electroneutrality, with its derivation restricted to strong electrolytes \cite[Sects.~1, 6]{Perdomo2025}.
The explicit-ion ePC-SAFT amine studies reviewed here do not report a tangent-plane assessment of their parameter sets \cite[Sect.~4]{Uyan2015}\cite[Sect.~6]{Wangler2018}\cite[Sects.~2--4]{Cleeton2020}\cite[Results and Discussion]{Bulow2021a}.
A fitting objective built from single-phase properties contains no stability condition, so a parameter set can reproduce those properties and still predict an additional liquid phase at some states.

\subsection{Aim of this work}
% P:introduction-13
No explicit-ion ePC-SAFT treatment of MEA or its carbamate was found in the literature reviewed here.
We ask whether one such formulation with carbamate chemistry can represent the equilibrium, caloric and density properties of loaded aqueous MEA.
Relative to the MDEA ePC-SAFT studies, this work adds carbamate ions and their formation reaction.
Relative to the explicit-ion MEA models of T\'ellez-Arredondo and Medeiros and of Neumann et al., whose reacting-system parameters were adjusted to VLE data \cite[Sect.~4, p.~48]{tellez-arredondoModelingCO2H2S2013}\cite[Sect.~4.1, p.~7]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}, it adds caloric and loaded-liquid density observations to the joint estimation.
The Born contribution follows Figiel et al.'s solvation-shell term, which accounts for dielectric saturation near the ions and was parameterized for inorganic ions at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} \cite[ePC-SAFT section, Table~1]{Figiel2025}; here the Born diameters of protonated MEA and carbamate are fitted, and the term is used at other temperatures.
Water, \COtwo--water and dielectric parameters are fitted separately; 16 parameters, three of them in the \COtwo-hydrolysis and protonated-MEA dissociation constants, are then fitted jointly to pressure, species, caloric, loaded-density and binary VLE observations.
Calculated properties are compared with measurements at nominal 30 wt\% MEA, and pressures also at 15 and 45 wt\%.
The reported parameters and a fit with wider bounds on the protonated-amine diameter and its water interaction are then searched for additional phases at specified states; this finite search does not establish global stability.
