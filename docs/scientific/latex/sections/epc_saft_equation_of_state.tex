\section{Thermodynamic model}\label{sec:theory}
% P:theory-01
The liquid contains nine true species: \COtwo, \MEA, \HtwoO, \MEAH, \MEACOO, \HCOthree, \COthree, \HthreeO and \Hydroxide.
Pressure calculations use a reactive bubble state of one liquid and one incipient vapor at the measured temperature and apparent composition.
Species comparisons use single-liquid states at \SI{101325}{\pascal}.
Only the neutral species \COtwo, \MEA and \HtwoO enter the vapor, and the calculated \COtwo partial pressure is \(p_{\COtwo}=y_{\COtwo}P\).
The same ePC-SAFT equation of state describes both phases.

\subsection{Residual Helmholtz energy}
% P:theory-02
The reduced residual Helmholtz energy, $a^{\mathrm{res}}=A^{\mathrm{res}}/(nRT)$, is the sum of hard-chain, dispersion, association, Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions \cite{Gross2001,Gross2002,Cameretti2005,Held2014,Figiel2025}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{res}}
    =
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}
    +a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    +a^{\mathrm{assoc}}
    +a^{\mathrm{DH}}
    +a^{\mathrm{Born}} .
    \label{eq:epcsaft-residual-split}
\end{equation}
Each neutral species is modeled as a chain of $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}$ spherical segments of diameter $\sigma_i$ and dispersion energy $u_i$, and each ion as a single charged sphere with $m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}=1$.
The first three terms are those of PC-SAFT: hard-chain repulsion, dispersion attraction between segments, and hydrogen bonding between association sites.
The Debye--H\"uckel term adds the screened Coulomb interactions among ions, and the Born term adds the change in free energy when each ion is charged in the solvent rather than in vacuum.
The pressure and chemical potentials follow from density and composition derivatives of $a^{\mathrm{res}}$ \cite[Eqs.~A.22, A.32 and A.33]{Gross2001}, and caloric properties from its temperature derivatives (\cref{sec:heat-method}).
Because the permittivity in the two electrostatic terms depends on density, temperature and composition (\cref{eq:solvent-permittivity}), these derivatives include its variation.
In the equations below, $\rho_N=N_{\mathrm A}\rho$ is the number density and $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions.

\subsection{Hard-chain and dispersion contributions}
% P:theory-02a
The hard-chain term, from Wertheim's first-order perturbation theory with the hard-sphere mixture expressions of Boubl\'ik and Mansoori et al., is \cite[Eqs.~A.4--A.8]{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}=\bar m\,a^{\mathrm{hs}}-\sum_i x_i\left(m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}-1\right)\ln g_{ii}^{\mathrm{hs}},
    \qquad
    \bar m=\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}},
    \label{eq:hard-chain}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_0}\left[
    \frac{3\zeta_1\zeta_2}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3^2}-\zeta_0\right)\ln\left(1-\zeta_3\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\frac{3\zeta_2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\right)^2\frac{2\zeta_2^2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^3},
    \qquad
    \zeta_n=\frac{\pi}{6}\rho_N\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}}d_i^n,
    \quad n=0,\dots,3.
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-contact}
\end{equation}
Here $d_i$ is the hard-sphere diameter, $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}$ is the hard-sphere pair distribution function at contact, and $\eta=\zeta_3$ is the packing fraction.
Ions have one segment, so they enter the hard-sphere term but not the chain sum.
For the neutral species the hard-sphere diameter depends on temperature \cite[Eq.~A.9]{Gross2001},
\begin{equation}
    d_i=\sigma_i\left[1-0.12\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u_i}{k_BT}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-diameter}
\end{equation}
with the water diameter law $\sigma_w(T)$ of Section~S1 in place of a constant $\sigma_i$.
The ion packing diameter is the temperature-independent value $d_i=0.88\sigma_i$; the Debye--H\"uckel diameter equals $\sigma_i$ for all six ions, and the Born diameter is separate.
These diameter choices define the present parameterization; they are not independently measured ion sizes.

% P:theory-02b
The dispersion term is Gross and Sadowski's second-order perturbation expansion \cite[Eqs.~A.10--A.19, Table~1]{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    =-2\pi\rho_N I_1\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\sigma_{ij}^3
    -\pi\rho_N\bar m\,C_1I_2\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\left(\frac{u_{ij}}{k_BT}\right)^2\sigma_{ij}^3 ,
    \label{eq:dispersion}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    C_1=\left[1+\bar m\frac{8\eta-2\eta^2}{\left(1-\eta\right)^4}
    +\left(1-\bar m\right)\frac{20\eta-27\eta^2+12\eta^3-2\eta^4}{\left[\left(1-\eta\right)\left(2-\eta\right)\right]^2}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:dispersion-compressibility}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I_1=\sum_{k=0}^{6}a_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    I_2=\sum_{k=0}^{6}b_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    a_k(\bar m)=a_{0k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}a_{1k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}\frac{\bar m-2}{\bar m}a_{2k},
    \label{eq:dispersion-integrals}
\end{equation}
where $C_1$ comes from the hard-chain compressibility, and $b_k(\bar m)$ has the same form as $a_k(\bar m)$.
The coefficients $a_{0k}$--$a_{2k}$ and $b_{0k}$--$b_{2k}$ are Gross and Sadowski's universal constants.
The unlike-pair diameter is $\sigma_{ij}=(\sigma_i+\sigma_j)/2$, and the dispersion energy of a pair is
\begin{equation}
    u_{ij}=\sqrt{u_iu_j}\,\left(1-k_{ij}\right).
    \label{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}
\end{equation}
Dispersion between ions of the same charge sign, including an ion with itself, is excluded ($u_{ij}=0$ for $z_iz_j>0$), while ion--solvent and cation--anion pairs keep \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule} \cite{Figiel2025}.
The \COtwo--water, \MEA--water and \MEAH--water interactions have the reciprocal-temperature form
\begin{equation}
 k_{ij}(T)=k_{ij}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})+b_{ij}(T^{-1}-T_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}),
 \qquad T_{\mathrm{ref}}=\SI{313.15}{\kelvin},
 \label{eq:kij-temperature}
\end{equation}
with $b_{ij}$ in kelvin.
The \COtwo--water coefficients are fixed inputs, whereas the \MEA--water and \MEAH--water intercepts and slopes enter the joint fit.

\subsection{Association contribution}
% P:theory-03
Hydrogen bonding is described by Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite[Eqs.~4--6]{Kleiner2007}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{assoc}}=\sum_i x_i\sum_{A\in i}\left(\ln X_A-\frac{X_A}{2}+\frac12\right),
    \qquad
    X_A=\left[1+\rho_N\sum_j x_j\sum_{B\in j}X_B\Delta^{AB}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:association}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta^{AB}=g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}\,\sigma_{ij}^3\,\kappa^{AB}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\epsilon^{AB}}{k_BT}\right)-1\right],
    \label{eq:association-strength}
\end{equation}
where $X_A$ is the fraction of molecules $i$ not bonded at site $A$, $B$ runs over the sites on molecule $j$, and $\Delta^{AB}$ is the strength of an $A$--$B$ bond between molecules $i$ and $j$.
The site fractions are solved at every state.
Water and \MEA each carry one donor and one acceptor site (2B), and only donor--acceptor pairs bond.
\COtwo carries one donor and one acceptor induced-association site, each bonding only with the water site of the opposite role: it does not self-associate, and the \COtwo--water bonds use $\epsilon^{AB}=\epsilon_w^{AB}/2$ and $\kappa^{AB}=\kappa_w^{AB}$ (\cref{tab:species-parameters}).
This induced-association topology \cite{Kleiner2007} follows Schick et al.\ and Pabsch et al.\ \cite[Table~1, Sect.~2.4.2]{Schick2023}\cite{Pabsch2020}, with water inputs fitted to IAPWS-95 (\cref{sec:results-water}).
\MEA--water cross-association uses the Wolbach--Sandler rule \cite[Eqs.~6 and 7]{Baygi2015}\cite[Eqs.~7 and 8]{Kleiner2007},
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon^{AB}_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(\epsilon^{AB}_i+\epsilon^{AB}_j\right),
    \qquad
    \kappa^{AB}_{ij}=\sqrt{\kappa^{AB}_i\kappa^{AB}_j}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}}{\sigma_{ij}}\right)^3 ,
    \label{eq:wolbach-sandler}
\end{equation}
and \MEA and \COtwo do not bond with each other.
The \MEA parameters use Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh's 2B model, with its association volume fitted to their model for the present $\sigma_{ij}^3$ convention of \cref{eq:association-strength} (Section~S1).

\subsection{Debye--H\"uckel contribution}
% P:theory-03a
The Debye--H\"uckel term of Cameretti et al.\ describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ \cite[Eqs.~5, 13 and 14]{Cameretti2005}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{DH}}=-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm D}e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2\chi_i,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm D}^2=\frac{e^2\rho_N}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2,
    \label{eq:debye-huckel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \chi_i=\frac{3}{\left(\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^3}
    \left[\frac32+\ln\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    -2\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    +\frac12\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^2\right].
    \label{eq:debye-huckel-chi}
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa_{\mathrm D}$ is the inverse Debye screening length, with $\rho_N$ in SI units, and $\chi_i$ accounts for the finite ion size; $\chi_i\to1$ as $\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\to0$, which recovers the Debye--H\"uckel limiting law.

\subsection{Born contribution with solvation shell and dielectric saturation}
% P:theory-04
The Born term follows the solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation (SSM+DS) form of Figiel et al.\ \cite[Eqs.~5--10]{Figiel2025}, with its Eq.~7 as corrected in 2026 \cite{Figiel2026Correction}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{Born}}
    =
    -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0k_{\mathrm{B}}T}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}x_iz_i^2
    \left[
        \frac{1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{-1}}{D_i}
        +c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}\left(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}^{-1}\right)
        \left(\frac{1}{d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}}-\frac{1}{D_i}\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:born-ssm-ds}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    D_i=d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\left[1+c_{\mathrm{shell}}\frac{f_{\mathrm{mix}}-1}{|z_i|}\right],
    \qquad
    f_{\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_kx_kf_k}{\sum_kx_k}.
    \label{eq:born-shell-diameter}
\end{equation}
Here \(d_i^{\mathrm{Born}}\) is the Born diameter, \(D_i\) is the outer diameter of the first solvation shell and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion}}=8\) is the fixed permittivity assigned to the region between the ion surface and that shell.
This work uses \(c_{\mathrm{shell}}=c_{\mathrm{dielectric}}=1\), which gives the corrected Eq.~7.
The water solvation factor is \(f_{\HtwoO}=1.5\) \cite[Table~2]{Figiel2025}, and every other component, including \MEA, \COtwo and all ions, has \(f_k=1\).
Figiel et al.\ restrict the average in \cref{eq:born-shell-diameter} to ions and solvents; here it runs over all nine components, so \(f_{\mathrm{mix}}=1+0.5\,x_{\HtwoO}\).
The shell diameter therefore changes with loading through the water mole fraction, and the Born term contributes to the water activity.

\subsection{Relative permittivity}
% P:theory-05
The bulk permittivity in the Debye--H\"uckel and Born terms depends on solvent density, temperature and neutral composition through the Schreckenberg form reported by Yang et al.\ \cite[Sect.~2.1, Eqs.~1a, 2a and 2b]{Yang2024}:
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}=1+\frac{n_s}{V}d_{V,\mathrm{mix}}
 \left(\frac{d_{T,\mathrm{mix}}}{T}-1\right),\qquad
 d_{q,\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}n_i d_{q,i}}{n_s},
 \quad q\in\{V,T\}.
 \label{eq:solvent-permittivity}
\end{equation}
Here $n_s=\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}n_i$, $V$ is the phase volume, and the neutral set $\mathcal S$ comprises water, \MEA and molecular \COtwo.
The dielectric-volume and dielectric-temperature coefficients have units of volume per mole and kelvin, respectively, using consistent units for $n_s/V$.
The ion amounts do not enter the neutral mixing averages; their presence affects the phase density and neutral amounts.
This law approaches unity as the phase density approaches zero at fixed composition.
Its \MEA coefficients were fitted to unloaded \MEA--water permittivities using equation-of-state densities (\cref{sec:results-permittivity}); the water and assumed \COtwo coefficients remain fixed.
Use of the dielectric law at other temperatures and in loaded solutions is a model assumption.

\subsection{Reactions and phase equilibrium}
% P:theory-06
Property calculations use an apparent feed of \SI{1}{\mole} of total amine, introduced as \MEA, the water amount given by the source composition, and \(\alpha\)~\si{\mole} of \COtwo, where \(\alpha\) is the loading in mol \COtwo per mol total amine.
The water amount follows from the reported \MEA molality where the source gives one and otherwise from the reported \COtwo-free \MEA mass fraction.
The liquid conserves the carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and oxygen amounts of this feed.
For these nine species the four element balances imply electroneutrality and leave the five independent reactions of \cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants}.
Bubble states satisfy equal chemical potentials of \COtwo, \MEA and \HtwoO between phases.
Every reacting liquid satisfies, for each reaction \(r\),
\begin{equation}
    \sum_i\nu_{ri}\ln a_i=\ln K_r(T),
    \label{eq:activity-reaction-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is dimensionless.
Writing the equation-of-state fugacity coefficient as $\ln\phi_i=\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}/(RT)-\ln Z$, with compressibility factor $Z=P/(\rho RT)$, gives
\begin{equation}
 a_w=x_w\frac{\phi_w(T,P,\boldsymbol x)P}{\phi_w^{\mathrm{pure}}(T,P^\circ)P^\circ},\qquad
 a_i=\frac{x_i}{M_wm^\circ}\frac{\phi_i(T,P,\boldsymbol x)P}{\phi_i^{\infty,w}(T,P^\circ)P^\circ}\quad(i\ne w).
 \label{eq:activities}
\end{equation}
Here $M_w$ is water's molar mass in kg/mol, $\rho$ is molar density and $m^\circ=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilogram}$.
The infinite-dilution reference is evaluated along a charge-neutral dilution path in pure water; only charge-neutral combinations of species, as they appear in the reactions, require this reference.
Thus $a_i=(m_i/m^\circ)\gamma_i^{(m)}$, with $m_i=x_i/(M_wx_w)$ and $\gamma_i^{(m)}=x_w\phi_iP/(\phi_i^{\infty,w}P^\circ)$.
A molality-basis constant relates to the corresponding mole-fraction constant by
\begin{equation}
    \ln K_{m,r}=\ln K_{x,r}-\Big(\sum_{i\ne\HtwoO}\nu_{ri}\Big)\ln\!\left(M_{\HtwoO}m^{\circ}\right),
    \label{eq:molality-conversion}
\end{equation}
with \(m^{\circ}=\SI{1}{\mole\per\kilo\gram}\), so \(-\ln(M_{\HtwoO}m^{\circ})=4.0165\).
The R1--R3 source standard states are at system pressure, and the R4 and R5 constants carry their source standard pressure of \SI{100}{\kilo\pascal}.

% P:theory-07
R1--R3 use the Austgen correlation form \cite[Table~V]{Austgen1991},
\begin{equation}
 \ln K_r=A_r+B_r/T+C_r\ln T+D_rT+\Delta_r,
 \label{eq:reaction-equilibrium-constant-temperature}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_r$ converts the source to the common aqueous-molality basis.
R4 follows Tong et al.\ \cite[Table~5]{Tong2012}, via Aroua et al.\ \cite{arouaEquilibriumConstantCarbamate1999}; the R5 starting law is Bates and Pinching \cite[Eq.~10, Table~2, p.~351]{batesAcidicDissociationConstant1951}, also tabulated by B\"ottinger et al.\ \cite[Table~5]{Bottinger2008}.
The tabulated R2 coefficients already include their molality conversion and fitted enthalpy shift; R5 already includes its two fitted quantities: the reciprocal-temperature coefficient $A_5$ of its p$K$ form (2832~K before the enthalpy shift; Bates--Pinching 2677.91~K) and the total enthalpy shift.
Neither shift is added again when evaluating the constants in \cref{tab:reaction-equilibrium-constants}.
For either reaction the constant-enthalpy adjustment has a form that leaves $K_r(T_{\mathrm{ref}})$ unchanged
\begin{equation}
 \delta\ln K_r(T)=-\frac{\delta h_r}{R}
 \left(\frac1T-\frac1{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right).
 \label{eq:reaction-shift}
\end{equation}
R1 and R3 remain fixed source laws; R2 and R5 are calibrated chemistry parameters rather than independent measurements of reaction enthalpy.
The reacting calculations use a domain of 293.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin}; R4 and R5 are extrapolated above their source upper limit of \SI{323.15}{\kelvin}.
