\section{ePC-SAFT contribution equations}\label{sec:s-epcsaft}
% P:supplement-epcsaft-01
This section gives the hard-chain, dispersion, association and Debye--H\"uckel contributions to main-text Eq.~\ref{main-eq:epcsaft-residual-split}.
Here $\rho_N=N_{\mathrm A}\rho$ is the number density, $\mathcal I$ is the set of ions, and the hard-sphere, ion and Debye--H\"uckel diameters are those of main-text Section~2.2.

\subsection{Hard-chain contribution}
% P:supplement-epcsaft-02
The hard-chain term, from Wertheim's first-order perturbation theory with the hard-sphere mixture expressions of Boubl\'ik and Mansoori et al., is \cite[Eqs.~A.4--A.8]{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hc}}=\bar m\,a^{\mathrm{hs}}-\sum_i x_i\left(m_i^{\mathrm{seg}}-1\right)\ln g_{ii}^{\mathrm{hs}},
    \qquad
    \bar m=\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}},
    \label{eq:hard-chain}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_0}\left[
    \frac{3\zeta_1\zeta_2}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{\zeta_2^3}{\zeta_3^2}-\zeta_0\right)\ln\left(1-\zeta_3\right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:hard-sphere}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}=\frac{1}{1-\zeta_3}
    +\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\frac{3\zeta_2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^2}
    +\left(\frac{d_id_j}{d_i+d_j}\right)^2\frac{2\zeta_2^2}{\left(1-\zeta_3\right)^3},
    \qquad
    \zeta_n=\frac{\pi}{6}\rho_N\sum_i x_im_i^{\mathrm{seg}}d_i^n,
    \quad n=0,\dots,3.
    \label{eq:hard-sphere-contact}
\end{equation}
Here $d_i$ is the hard-sphere diameter, $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}$ is the hard-sphere pair distribution function at contact, and $\eta=\zeta_3$ is the packing fraction.

\subsection{Dispersion contribution}
% P:supplement-epcsaft-03
The dispersion term is Gross and Sadowski's second-order perturbation expansion \cite[Eqs.~A.10--A.19, Table~1]{Gross2001}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{disp}}
    =-2\pi\rho_N I_1\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\frac{\epsilon_{ij}}{k_BT}\sigma_{ij}^3
    -\pi\rho_N\bar m\,C_1I_2\sum_i\sum_j x_ix_jm_i^{\mathrm{seg}}m_j^{\mathrm{seg}}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{ij}}{k_BT}\right)^2\sigma_{ij}^3 ,
    \label{eq:dispersion}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    C_1=\left[1+\bar m\frac{8\eta-2\eta^2}{\left(1-\eta\right)^4}
    +\left(1-\bar m\right)\frac{20\eta-27\eta^2+12\eta^3-2\eta^4}{\left[\left(1-\eta\right)\left(2-\eta\right)\right]^2}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:dispersion-compressibility}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I_1=\sum_{k=0}^{6}a_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    I_2=\sum_{k=0}^{6}b_k(\bar m)\,\eta^k,\qquad
    a_k(\bar m)=a_{0k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}a_{1k}+\frac{\bar m-1}{\bar m}\frac{\bar m-2}{\bar m}a_{2k},
    \label{eq:dispersion-integrals}
\end{equation}
where $C_1$ comes from the hard-chain compressibility, and $b_k(\bar m)$ has the same form as $a_k(\bar m)$.
The coefficients $a_{0k}$--$a_{2k}$ and $b_{0k}$--$b_{2k}$ are Gross and Sadowski's universal constants.
The unlike-pair diameter is $\sigma_{ij}=(\sigma_i+\sigma_j)/2$, and $\epsilon_{ij}$ is the pair dispersion energy of main-text Eq.~\ref{main-eq:dispersion-combining-rule}.

\subsection{Association contribution}
% P:supplement-epcsaft-04
Hydrogen bonding is described by Wertheim's first-order theory in the form used with PC-SAFT \cite[Eqs.~4--6]{Kleiner2007}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{assoc}}=\sum_i x_i\sum_{A\in i}\left(\ln X_A-\frac{X_A}{2}+\frac12\right),
    \qquad
    X_A=\left[1+\rho_N\sum_j x_j\sum_{B\in j}X_B\Delta^{AB}\right]^{-1},
    \label{eq:association}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta^{AB}=g_{ij}^{\mathrm{hs}}\,\sigma_{ij}^3\,\kappa^{AB}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\epsilon^{AB}}{k_BT}\right)-1\right],
    \label{eq:association-strength}
\end{equation}
where $X_A$ is the fraction of molecules $i$ not bonded at site $A$, $B$ runs over the sites on molecule $j$, and $\Delta^{AB}$ is the strength of an $A$--$B$ bond between molecules $i$ and $j$.
The site fractions are solved at every state.
The site scheme and the \MEA--water combining rule are given in main-text Section~2.3.

\subsection{Debye--H\"uckel contribution}
% P:supplement-epcsaft-05
The Debye--H\"uckel term of Cameretti et al.\ describes ions of diameter $d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}$ in a continuum of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ \cite[Eqs.~5, 13 and 14]{Cameretti2005}:
\begin{equation}
    a^{\mathrm{DH}}=-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm D}e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2\chi_i,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm D}^2=\frac{e^2\rho_N}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bulk}}k_BT}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}x_iz_i^2,
    \label{eq:debye-huckel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \chi_i=\frac{3}{\left(\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^3}
    \left[\frac32+\ln\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    -2\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)
    +\frac12\left(1+\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\right)^2\right].
    \label{eq:debye-huckel-chi}
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa_{\mathrm D}$ is the inverse Debye screening length, with $\rho_N$ in SI units, and $\chi_i$ accounts for the finite ion size; $\chi_i\to1$ as $\kappa_{\mathrm D}d_i^{\mathrm{DH}}\to0$, which recovers the Debye--H\"uckel limiting law.

\section{Model inputs and fixed-parameter provenance}\label{sec:s-parameters}
% P:supplement-18
Neutral MEA parameters are the 2B values of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, except the association volume described below \cite[Table~2]{Baygi2015}.
\COtwo segment count, diameter and dispersion energy are from Gross and Sadowski \cite[Table~2]{Gross2001}.
Bicarbonate, carbonate and hydroxide sizes and dispersion energies follow Uyan et al.; their hydrogen-ion parameters are transferred here to hydronium \cite[Table~4]{Uyan2015}.
Ion segment count one and the diameter ties are modeling assumptions.
Fixed Born diameters for bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, and the fixed water interactions with carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, are model inputs without independent MEA-specific parameter measurements. All other interactions are zero by assumption except the fixed water interactions listed below.
\Cref{tab:supplement-species-parameters,tab:supplement-interaction-matrix} list their exact values; they are not attributed to the cited size/dispersion source.

The ion packing diameters, $0.88\sigma$, are 4.708, 4.2821756, 2.578048, 2.149136, 3.049552 and \SI{1.775576}{\angstrom} for \MEAH, \MEACOO, bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, respectively; their Debye--H\"uckel diameters equal the segment diameters in main-text Table~\ref{main-tab:species-parameters}.

\section{Objective and data roles}\label{sec:s-objective}
% P:supplement-objective-00
Eight disjoint groups contain 307 objective entries, including 39 checks on the separately fitted and subsequently fixed \COtwo--water inputs.
The pressure--species--heat group contains 47 pressures, 94 species fractions and eight integral heats; seven additional species observations and six Jakobsen ratios enter separately.
The remaining groups are 47 loaded densities, five carbon-free densities, 50 Cai binary quantities, 39 Kiepe pressures and four heat capacities.

% P:supplement-objective-01
The bounded least-squares objective is main-text Eq.~\ref{main-eq:fit-objective}, with residuals
\begin{equation}
 r_j=\begin{cases}(y_{c,j}-y_{o,j})/s_j,&\text{difference},\\[4pt]
 \ln(y_{c,j}/y_{o,j})/s_j,&\text{log ratio}.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:fit-residuals}
\end{equation}
Here $y_{c,j}$ and $y_{o,j}$ denote calculated and observed values.
Loaded densities have weight $1/47$, so their group contributes a mean scaled squared error rather than a sum growing with its observation count.
They contribute 0.449 of total cost 148.5105, or 0.302\,\%; individual departures from $2s$ are reported separately.
Carbon-free densities have scale \SI{0.5}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} and weight 10; Cai temperature and vapor-fraction scales are \SI{1}{\kelvin} and 0.01 with unit weight, and Kiepe \COtwo--water pressures have weight $1/39$ \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002}.
These weights set fitting priorities; the heat scales are not measured standard uncertainties.
\Cref{tab:objective-groups} specifies every group.

\section{Caloric inputs and numerical checks}\label{sec:s-files}
% P:supplement-16
The neutral ideal-gas caloric functions use the \COtwo Shomate correlation from NIST-JANAF and the MEA heat-capacity polynomial tabulated by Zhang et al.\ \cite{chaseNISTJANAFThermochemicalTables1998,Zhang2011}.
Water uses a fit to the ideal-gas part of IAPWS-95 over 273.15--\SI{400}{\kelvin} \cite[Eq.~5, Table~1]{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}.
On 10001 verification points, the maximum relative water ideal-gas heat-capacity error is $6.50\times10^{-7}$, a numerical fit error rather than physical uncertainty.
At \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}, water is anchored to $h^\circ=-241826$ J/mol and $s^\circ=188.834$ J/(mol K), at standard pressure 100 kPa \cite{chaseNISTJANAFThermochemicalTables1998}.
MEA enthalpy and entropy are zero at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} as a conserved-component reference convention.
The ionic reference functions are completed from the five reaction constraints and temperature-dependent infinite-dilution corrections.
A minimum-norm convention fixes the charge-proportional reference freedom, which cancels in electroneutral phases; no additional ionic heat-capacity functions are fitted.

The heat-capacity consistency checks include equilibrium enthalpy derivatives, the $10^{-6}$ unloaded loading approximation and conversion by the complete analytical-feed mass.
These checks verify numerical solutions rather than physical phase behavior.
Numerical values, software identities, checksums and calculation commands accompany the electronic data.

\section{Conditional parameter errors and correlations}\label{sec:s-uncertainty}
% P:supplement-covariance-01
For the ten parameters free of active bounds, the weighted residual Jacobian $J_I$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol\theta_I)=s_C^2(J_I^{\mathsf T}J_I)^{-1},\qquad s_C^2=\frac{2C}{307-10}.
 \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian has rank ten, 297 degrees of freedom and variance factor 1.000071.
The six parameters at bounds are held fixed for this covariance.
The unknown common residual variance, weights, source inputs and local linearization condition these errors; dependence within an experimental campaign is not propagated.
\Cref{tab:supplement-correlations} gives the parameter order and correlations.


% P:supplement-covariance-02
Covariance propagation includes parameter correlations and gives relative pressure standard errors of 5.41--16.00\,\% over the 47 retained fitted pressure states.
These quantify local parameter uncertainty, excluding model discrepancy and future measurement noise.
For sensitivity ranking, each free coordinate moves by one conditional standard error with other coordinates fixed; the reported fractional changes are derivative estimates.
Each bound coordinate is fixed at 1\,\%, 5\,\% and 10\,\% of its original interval inward, with the other coordinates refitted.
The ranking uses the first inward step; these perturbations differ in size from the one-standard-error changes and do not establish statistical significance.
The two weakest scaled-Jacobian directions have singular values 0.001578 and 0.004922: the former is dominated by the bicarbonate--water interaction (coefficient $-0.949$), the latter by opposing carbamate and \MEAH Born diameters (0.766 and $-0.602$).
They include bound coordinates, so two-sided motion need not be feasible.
The short profiles locate neither confidence limits nor a global optimum and do not requalify phase stability.

\section{Phase-search definition and confirmed splits}\label{sec:s-phase}
% P:supplement-05
The complete 162-state table for both fits is supplied as CSV in the data deposit.
The grid has six temperatures, 313.15, 318.15, 333.15, 353.15, 373.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, and nine loadings, $10^{-5}$, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.256, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6.
At each temperature and loading, the reference pressure $P^*$ is the bubble pressure of the outgoing restricted fit, and the three test states are at $P/P^*=0.50$, 0.80 and 0.95.
The 54 reference pressures range from 6.4123 to \SI{1768.9}{\kilo\pascal} and are listed in the electronic data.
Both fits are searched at the same 162 states.
Phase calculations use 9.7292821 mol analytical MEA, 76.9751925 mol water and $9.7292821\alpha$ mol \COtwo.
All extensive Gibbs differences below refer to that feed, rather than the one-mole total-amine property basis.

% P:supplement-phase-summary-01
Nine trial phases per state give 1458 trials for each fit; every trial optimization completes, and none is unresolved.
No instability is detected for the final parameter set, and all 162 of their existing equilibria were searched with the native liquid-parent requirement; the reference pressures retain their outgoing-record basis.
Instability is detected for the historical unstable fit at 54 states, by 143 trials that lower the Gibbs energy: ten states each at 313.15 and \SI{318.15}{\kelvin}, nine each at 333.15, 353.15 and \SI{373.15}{\kelvin} and seven at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
The 81 states at 353.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin} lie above the temperatures of the fitted \COtwo pressures.

% P:supplement-phase-confirmation-01
At each state with detected instability, the trials that lower the Gibbs energy are grouped by phase kind, supported species and composition, with maximum composition difference $10^{-6}$ within a group, and one representative of each group seeds one split calculation of at most 400 iterations.
A split is confirmed when it converges to the requested tolerance with negative $\Delta G/(RT)$, raw residuals at most $10^{-10}$, positive reduced-Hessian minimum, positive phase amounts and positive pressure--density derivatives.
The 91 attempts confirm 51 distinct two-liquid paths at 32 of the 54 states with detected instability, at every temperature (\Cref{tab:phase-splits}).
The water mole fraction is 0.40--0.74 in the water-poor liquid and 0.68--0.97 in the water-rich liquid.
The 40 unsuccessful attempts end with vanished or coincident phases or a nonpositive reduced Hessian; failure to confirm does not remove a detected instability.
The homogeneous-liquid density comparisons discussed by Neumann et al.\ do not test phase splitting at the states searched here \cite[Sect.~4.2]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.

% P:supplement-06
Four liquid starts and five vapor starts are used at each converged equilibrium state.
The liquid starts are a uniform positive composition and three compositions with 0.99 assigned to \COtwo, MEA or water, distributing 0.01 among the other species in proportion to an existing full-support phase.
Each liquid start is charge-balanced.
Vapor starts are uniform, nearly pure \COtwo, nearly pure MEA, nearly pure water, and the ideal-gas estimate $w_i\propto\exp(\pi_i-s_i)$, where $\pi_i=(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda)_i$ and $s_i$ is the dimensionless standard chemical potential in the density representation.
Only the three neutral species enter a vapor start.

% P:supplement-07
A Newton procedure and an Ipopt minimization use exact first and second derivatives, with MA57 linear algebra and a tolerance of $10^{-10}$ on the recomputed residuals used to decide convergence, after the solver's row scaling.
The final state must satisfy material and charge balances, positive compressibility and nonnegative reduced-Hessian curvature.

% P:supplement-08
For each optimized trial, the tangent-plane distance $D$ is main-text Eq.~\ref{main-eq:tpd}; the multipliers refer to the existing reacting equilibrium.
A donor phase $d$ with positive amounts of every supported trial species has transfer capacity $\beta_{\max}=\min_i(N_dx_{di}/w_i)$.
The donor with greatest capacity is used.
The transfer slope is
\begin{equation}
 S=g_w/(RT)-\sum_iw_i\mu_{di}/(RT)+r_{P,d},\qquad
 r_{P,d}=1-v_d F_{v,d}-Pv_d/(RT),\qquad F=A^{\mathrm{res}}/(RT),
 \label{eq:donor-slope}
\end{equation}
where $v_d$ is molar volume and $F$ is evaluated on a one-mole phase basis.
The term $r_{P,d}$ corrects for the small pressure residual of the existing phase.
A negative $D$ and $S<-E_S$ trigger finite transfers at $\beta=\beta_{\max}/2,\ldots,\beta_{\max}/512$.
Each transfer conserves every species amount: $x'_{di}=(N_dx_{di}-\beta w_i)/(N_d-\beta)$, leaving the other phases unchanged.
Both phase densities are closed at the specified pressure.
A trial detects instability only if $\Delta G/(RT)<-E_{\Delta G}$ for an admitted transfer; a negative value within that error estimate remains unresolved as a decrease.

% P:supplement-09
The numerical-error estimate combines association-solver derivative error, the Euler identity defect, floating-point summation and the correction applied to restore the material balance.
For a one-mole phase define
\begin{align}
 E(w)&=(1+|v|)f+\left|F-vF_v-\sum_iw_iF_{n_i}\right|+\gamma_{2N+4}M,\\
 E_S&=E(w)+(1+|v_d|)f_d+E_{\mathrm{feas}}+\gamma_{N+3}M_S,\\
 E_{\Delta G}&=(N_d-\beta)E(x'_d)+\beta E(w)+N_dE(x_d)+E_{\mathrm{feas}}+\gamma_{N+3}M_G.
 \label{eq:phase-error}
\end{align}
Here $f$ is the reported residual-Helmholtz gradient error estimate in the SI volume and amount columns, $N=9$, $\gamma_m=m\epsilon/(1-m\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon$ is machine precision.
The sum magnitude is $M=|F|+\sum_i|w_i[\ln(w_i/v)-1+s_i]|+|Pv/(RT)|$.
$M_S=|g_w/(RT)|+\sum_i|w_i\mu_{di}/(RT)|+|r_{P,d}|$ and $M_G$ sums absolute values of the three extensive Gibbs terms in the transfer difference.
The feasibility estimate is $E_{\mathrm{feas}}=\sum_{p,i}(|\delta n_{pi}|+|\delta n^{\mathrm{intended}}_{pi}-\delta n_{pi}|)|\mu_{pi}/(RT)|$, where the correction solves the balance and charge defects with amount-weighted linear algebra.
No uncertainty in the supplied standard-state functions is included in this numerical estimate.
Finite-transfer compositions must normalize and be charge-neutral to $10^{-12}$; species and conserved-quantity amount errors must be at most $10^{-12}\max(1,n_{\mathrm{feed}})$ mol.
These are numerical resolution rules, not experimental uncertainty bounds or a global stability proof.
