\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
Can a common electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) formulation describe loaded aqueous monoethanolamine while keeping literature reaction constants and avoiding a predicted additional liquid?
A joint calibration with eleven adjustable parameters and tied amine-ion Born diameters preserves pressure, speciation, caloric and density agreement without a phase-stability penalty.
For nominal 30 wt\% monoethanolamine, fitted carbon-dioxide pressure average absolute relative deviation is 11.64\,\%, integral absorption-heat root-mean-square error is \SI{1.98}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} carbon dioxide, and fitted loaded-density deviation is 0.152\,\% without volume translation.
The parameter restrictions have a measurable cost: pressure deviation on the same Ma'mun observations rises from 19.43 to 33.47\,\% relative to the less restricted calibration.
Finite searches detect no additional-liquid instability for the adopted parameters; an unpenalized comparison fit instead predicts an ion-rich, water-poor liquid through a coupled energy balance with a prominent dispersion change.
Concentration transfer, carbonate shares and high-temperature absorption heat remain inaccurate.
All comparisons use data seen during development, and parameter uncertainty is conditional on the fit.
The results support a common thermodynamic calculation and a bounded calibration, rather than independent validation or a global stability proof.
Part I supplies the equations and shared inputs for the companion study of other amines.

\end{abstract}
