The pure-water fit reproduces IAPWS-95 saturation density, vapor pressure and heat capacity over the fitted interval, with interleaved temperatures providing a numerical check of the correlation \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}.
The water inputs remain fixed during MEA estimation; the comparison is with a reference equation rather than independent measurements.

% P:results-binary-01
The \COtwo--water interaction remains fixed after estimation against Kiepe pressures, while the MEA--water interaction is jointly estimated with the amine-ion parameters \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002,caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996}.
\Cref{fig:mea-water} compares the resulting MEA--water bubble temperatures with the fitted observations of Cai et al.
The permittivity coefficients of MEA were estimated from unloaded solutions at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}; these observations do not determine the temperature or loading dependence used in the reacting mixture \cite[Supporting Information, Tables~S1--S2]{agieienkoWhatGasStream2024a}.

% P:results-parameter-interpretation
Only the \MEAH--\MEACOO interaction is at a bound for the present model; its lower-bound value doubles the unlike-pair dispersion energy under \cref{eq:dispersion-combining-rule}.
The other parameters have conditional standard errors listed beside their values in Table~\ref{supp-tab:supplement-fitted-parameters}.
The water--bicarbonate interaction and the opposing amine-ion size changes are weakly determined; their large conditional standard errors preclude identifying the fitted values as unique molecular properties.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-uncertainty} gives the correlations, the propagated pressure intervals and refits with the bound interaction fixed slightly inside its bound, which test whether the fit depends on the bound; none includes model discrepancy, uncertainty of the equilibrium-constant correlations, correlated errors within one experimental source or uncertainty of a phase boundary.

\subsection{Phase stability of the present model}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:results-phase-opening
Stability is checked because ePC-SAFT ion-parameter choices can predict inconsistent liquid--liquid demixing in aqueous salt solutions \cite[Sect.~2.4]{Held2014}.
For loaded aqueous MEA, B\"ottinger et al.\ observe a homogeneous liquid in their measured subset \cite[Sect.~3]{Bottinger2008}, although that observation does not establish homogeneity over the entire searched domain.
With the searches of \cref{sec:methods-phase}, the present model shows no detected instability at any of the 2484 liquid states or 162 liquid--vapor states, and every search finished (Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-phase} gives the states by pressure ratio).
Phase stability was not imposed in the fit of the present model.

The species curves show molecular MEA being replaced by protonated MEA and carbamate as loading increases, followed by increasing bicarbonate (\cref{fig:speciation}, with the aggregates defined in \cref{sec:data-methods}).
Agreement with the NMR amine sum does not independently establish the separate molecular and protonated fractions.
The carbonate share remains far below Jakobsen's observations, even though the total-amine ratios are fitted; the two observables constrain different combinations of species.
The fitted pressures do not determine that minor-species partition uniquely.

Carbonate formation depends on its equilibrium constant and relative ionic activities, so a small carbonate share cannot be attributed to a single parameter from these comparisons.
Titration-derived bicarbonate totals also differ from the NMR observations, as Matin et al.\ discuss \cite[pp.~6616--6617]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}.

\subsubsection{Absorption heat and heat capacity}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The fitted integral heats and the Arcis comparison probe different temperature and pressure conditions (\cref{fig:heat}).
The calculated Arcis absorption is less exothermic than measured, with a larger discrepancy in the lower-pressure series.
The measurements themselves differ between those series at nearby loadings \cite[Table~5]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}; neither a pressure-only explanation nor a unique model contribution is isolated by this comparison.

At \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, the Kim--Svendsen finite-interval heats deviate more than with the model of Polley and Hedengren (\cref{tab:same-data}; Table~\ref{supp-tab:kim-alternatives} gives alternative source exclusions).
Extrapolated equilibrium constants, approximated pressure histories and missing chemistry may contribute, but the comparison does not isolate a cause, and high-temperature caloric accuracy is not demonstrated.

% P:results-cp
Heat capacities outside the fitted Hilliard states, from Weiland et al.\ at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} and 30 wt\% MEA up to loading 0.5 mol/mol and from Concepci\'on et al.\ for unloaded MEA at mass fraction 0.2997 and 313.15--\SI{353.15}{\kelvin}, are described with smaller deviations than with the model of Polley and Hedengren; neither model fitted them (\cref{tab:same-data}).

\subsection{Effect of the parameter restrictions}\label{sec:results-joint}
% P:results-joint-01
\Cref{tab:choice-cost} gives the effect of each cumulative restriction defined in \cref{sec:parameters}.
From the 16-parameter calibration to the present model, five fewer adjustable parameters leave the objective value and the fitted pressure and density deviations nearly unchanged and reduce the number of parameters at bounds, while the deviation of the pressures that are not fitted rises slightly.
The present model is the most restricted set whose fitted pressure and density deviations do not exceed those of the 16-parameter calibration; every further restriction in \cref{tab:choice-cost} has larger fitted and not-fitted pressure deviations than the present model.
The cumulative changes quantify the combined effect of the selected assumptions, not the isolated physical effect of each restriction.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-literature-01
\Cref{tab:same-data} compares the present model with the model of Polley and Hedengren \cite{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}, whose equation-of-state calorics are calculated here from their published specification.
The present model agrees better on most pressure, species, density and heat-capacity sets but worse on the Aronu 45 wt\% pressures and the Kim heats at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
Because the two models were fitted to different data, these deviations do not rank the models.

% P:results-literature-matched
On the 19 identical Ma'mun observations (\cref{tab:matched-pressure}) \cite{Mamun2005}, the present model has a larger pressure deviation than the 16-parameter calibration and than the PC-SAFT calculation of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, and a smaller one than the SAFT-HR calculation of Najafloo and Zarei.
Zhang et al.\ fitted their eNRTL model to these data, whereas the calculations of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh and of Najafloo and Zarei lie outside their fitting objectives \cite[Table~9]{Zhang2011}\cite[Table~5]{Baygi2015}\cite[Table~5]{Najafloo2018}.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:limits}
% P:results-limits
These results support the present model as a calibration against data examined during development, not as an independently validated model.
Its weakest results are the pressures at 15 and 45 wt\% MEA and the Ma'mun pressures, the carbonate shares and the absorption heats at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.
The parameter uncertainty excludes model discrepancy and the uncertainty of the equilibrium-constant correlations; the R1--R3 range is the range stated for the correlation rather than verified measurement coverage; R4, R5 and the permittivity correlation are extrapolated beyond their source data; and the absence of an additional liquid phase rests on finite searches.
Missing pressure histories, source inconsistencies, approximations in the comparisons, omitted carrier gas and omitted oxazolidone further limit interpretation.
The property comparisons alone do not justify using the model to predict absorber or stripper operation.
