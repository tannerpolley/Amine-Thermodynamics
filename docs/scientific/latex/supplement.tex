\section{Model inputs and sources of the fixed parameters}\label{sec:s-parameters}
% P:supplement-18
Neutral MEA parameters are the 2B values of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, except the association volume described below \cite[Table~2]{Baygi2015}.
\COtwo segment count, diameter and dispersion energy are from Gross and Sadowski \cite[Table~2]{Gross2001}.
Bicarbonate, carbonate and hydroxide sizes and dispersion energies follow Uyan et al.; their hydrogen-ion parameters are transferred here to hydronium \cite[Table~4]{Uyan2015}.
Ion segment count one and the diameter ties are modeling assumptions.
Fixed Born diameters for bicarbonate, carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, and the fixed water interactions with carbonate, hydronium and hydroxide, are model inputs without independent MEA-specific parameter measurements.
All other interactions are zero by assumption except the fixed water interactions listed below.
\Cref{tab:supplement-species-parameters,tab:supplement-interaction-matrix} list their exact values; they are not attributed to the cited size/dispersion source.
Water's solvation factor and ion-region permittivity follow Figiel et al.; unit solvation factors for the other components are assumptions \cite[Table~2, Eqs.~5--10]{Figiel2025}.
The ion packing and Debye--H\"uckel diameters follow the ties stated in the main text.

% P:supplement-association-01
The \COtwo--water cross-association energy is \SI{878.325}{\kelvin}, half the fitted water self-association energy, with the same association volume as water.
The topology follows Schick et al.\ and Pabsch et al.\ \cite[Table~1, Sect.~2.4.2]{Schick2023}\cite{Pabsch2020}.
The association strength here carries $\sigma_{ij}^3$, whereas Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh report their MEA association volume, 0.037470, for a $d_{ij}^3$ strength \cite[Table~2]{Baygi2015}.
The value used here, 0.036305439, is the constant fitted by least squares with the epcsaft package to the vapor pressures and saturated-liquid densities of their own model at 21 temperatures from 293.15 to \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}.

% P:supplement-19
The main text reports the fits of the five water parameters to IAPWS-95 \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}, the \COtwo--water interaction to Kiepe pressures \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002} and the \MEA permittivity coefficients to measured permittivities.
In the water fit, the two exponential terms in $\sigma_w(T)=\sigma_0+a_1\exp(b_1T)+a_2\exp(b_2T)$, with $a_1=10.11$, $b_1=-0.01775$, $a_2=-1.417$ and $b_2=-0.01146$, were fixed while $\sigma_0=2.7804326$~\si{\angstrom} was fitted; diameters are in angstrom and temperature in kelvin.

% P:supplement-20
In the permittivity fit, water coefficients, 0.3777 L/mol and 1403 K, follow Yang et al.; the \COtwo coefficients, $10^{-8}$ L/mol and 1403 K, are assumptions \cite[Table~6]{Yang2024}.
At the measurement temperature, two coefficient pairs give identical permittivities at any density.
The larger-volume solution is used here; the permittivity data alone do not distinguish these solutions, so that selection is a model assumption.
The 14 unloaded measurements at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin} do not determine temperature or loading dependence; direct static-permittivity data over the reacting domain are missing from this calibration, so extrapolation remains a model assumption.

Vinjarapu heat scales use the reported uncertainty converted to J/mol \COtwo \cite[Table~4]{HemaBhavya2025}.
The relative and constant parts of the species scales, the log-pressure scale, the \COtwo-free-density scale, the Cai scales and the weights are assigned choices without a statistical derivation.

\section{Source conventions and density scales}\label{sec:s-corrections}\label{sec:s-density}
% P:supplement-density-01
Amundsen's density and loading inputs are \SI{2}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} and 2\,\% of loading for the fitted loaded series; whether the density estimate applies at high temperature and loading is unresolved \cite[pp.~3099--3100]{Amundsen2009}.
Hartono's inputs are \SI{0.02}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre} and 0.01 mol/mol loading; the density input is the reported expanded uncertainty with coverage factor two and is used without division by that factor \cite[Table~3, p.~1810]{Hartono2014}.
The calculations adopt the loading $\alpha$ tabulated in Hartono's Table~3.
The accompanying tabulated \COtwo mole fractions imply different loadings under the stated solvent composition; that source inconsistency remains unresolved.
The density comparisons and conditional standard errors therefore depend on the adopted loading.
The secant construction in main-text Eq.~(\ref{main-eq:density-scale}) combines these with loading uncertainty.
These scales therefore do not uniformly represent standard uncertainties.

% P:supplement-sources
All density observations, including the excluded Amundsen loading-0.4 series, remain in the electronic data with source pressure, composition and calculated value.
The source comparison does not identify one experiment as correct.
For Jakobsen NMR calculations, dilution changes the solvent composition, and the reported NMR loading is used instead of the maximum loading \cite[Sample preparation, Tables~A1--A2]{Jakobsen2005}.
The carbonate-share comparison differs from the fitted species-to-total-amine ratios.

\section{Caloric functions and reference values}\label{sec:s-files}
% P:supplement-calorics
Neutral caloric functions use NIST-JANAF for \COtwo, the MEA heat-capacity function tabulated by Zhang et al., and an ideal-gas water fit to IAPWS-95 \cite{chaseNISTJANAFThermochemicalTables1998,Zhang2011,internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}.
The ionic functions follow from the equilibrium-constant correlations and their infinite-dilution reference corrections.
A charge-proportional reference freedom cancels in electroneutral phases; no independent ionic heat-capacity functions are fitted.
The reaction enthalpies enter through these standard-state functions, and no separate reaction-enthalpy term is added.
The loading $10^{-6}$ represents nominally unloaded states in the heat-capacity calculations.
Table~\ref{tab:supplement-check-values} gives reference values of the present model at three bubble states for checking an implementation.

\section{Phase-search conditions}\label{sec:s-phase}
% P:supplement-phase-grid
The liquid grid contains 1800 states at 15, 25, 35 and 45 wt\% MEA, 313.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin} in \SI{10}{\kelvin} steps, and loading $10^{-5}$ plus 0.025--0.6 mol/mol in 0.025 steps, at 1.05 and 3.0 times the bubble pressure of the parameter set being searched.
Another 108 states use the composition of the liquid--vapor search at those two ratios.
A second liquid grid, offset from the first, contains 576 states at 20, 30 and 40 wt\%, 318.15--\SI{388.15}{\kelvin} and loadings 0.0125--0.5875, at 1.5 times the bubble pressure.

% P:supplement-phase-census
The liquid--vapor search contains six temperatures, 313.15, 318.15, 333.15, 353.15, 373.15 and \SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, and nine loadings, $10^{-5}$, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.256, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 mol/mol.
Each temperature--loading pair uses three pressures, 0.50, 0.80 and 0.95 times a reference bubble pressure calculated with an earlier parameter set of this work.
The present model is searched at these 162 states, whose pressures are not recalculated from its own bubble pressures.
The feed is 9.7292821 mol MEA, 76.9751925 mol water and $9.7292821\alpha$ mol \COtwo.

% P:supplement-phase-method
Charge-neutral initial liquid compositions include a uniform composition and near-pure neutral-species compositions; initial vapor compositions contain only the three neutral species.
Only a negative tangent-plane distance confirmed by a finite, mass-conserving transfer that lowers the Gibbs energy by more than its numerical error estimate is counted as a detected instability.
At each liquid--vapor state the tangent-plane distance is also evaluated at trial compositions at which a Gibbs-energy decrease was found with other MEA parameter sets during this work.
A negative distance at one of these compositions is reported separately and is not counted as a phase split.
The 6121 additional trial compositions also give no negative tangent-plane distance for the present model at any liquid--vapor state.
Completion of the search, convergence of the residuals and positive pressure--density derivatives are numerical checks rather than physical measurements.
The deposited results identify every search that failed or did not finish.

% P:supplement-phase-interpolation
If a penalized fit still shows a detected instability or a search that did not finish, parameter sets on the straight line between a reference set with neither and the penalized solution are tested in steps of 0.01 and then by 10 bisections, and the set nearest the penalized solution that passes is taken.
The fit of the present model required neither the stability penalty nor this interpolation.

\section{Shared parameters for Part II}\label{sec:s-shared}
% P:supplement-shared
Main-text Table~\ref{main-tab:shared-parameters} lists the shared parameters rounded for display; Tables~\ref{tab:supplement-reaction-coefficients}--\ref{tab:supplement-interaction-matrix} and \ref{tab:supplement-shared-remaining} give the unrounded values, including those that the main-text table states as rules.
Part II also keeps the shared component charges, segment counts, association scheme, electrostatic model choices, standard states and R1--R3 reaction definitions fixed.
The water--bicarbonate interaction is the MEA-fitted value, whereas the carbonate--water interaction is an assumed fixed value.
The weakly determined bicarbonate interaction and the extrapolations of the permittivity correlation and of the equilibrium-constant correlations limit the strength of a transfer claim before amine-specific comparisons.
