\subsection{Calibrated model}\label{sec:results-calibration}
% P:results-02
The selected SSM+DS fit, F1, reproduces the 47 fitted \COtwo partial pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} with an AARD of 13.81\,\% and the 94 fitted species at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} with an AARD of 7.89\,\% (\cref{tab:residual-summary}).
The mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of the fitted pressures is 0.007, so the calibration carries no material pooled pressure bias.
\Cref{fig:pressure,fig:speciation} compare the calculated pressures and species with all measurements, including those outside the fit.

% P:results-03
At F1 the \HCOthree--water coefficient lies on its upper bound of \(+0.5\), where the cost gradient \(\partial C/\partial k\) is \(-10.81\), so the cost would decrease further if the bound were relaxed; its value is set by the bound rather than estimated from the data.
The conditional standard errors of the three interior interaction coefficients are 0.012--0.027 (\cref{tab:binary-interaction-parameters}), and the largest absolute correlation, 0.482, links \(k_{\MEACOO,\HtwoO}\) with \(k_{\MEAH,\HtwoO}\).
The reciprocal-temperature slope is \(b=\SI{107.96}{\kelvin}\) with conditional standard error \SI{22.93}{\kelvin}.
Its precision is conditional on the active bounds, fixed inputs, declared residual scales and residuals treated as independent; it does not identify a unique molecular mechanism.
The \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficient is only 1.19 conditional standard errors above its lower bound, which further limits a symmetric local uncertainty interpretation.

\subsection{Heat of absorption and matched pressure comparison}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
Adding the eight Vinjarapu heats reduces their root-mean-square error (RMSE) from 4.57 to \SI{2.13}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} relative to the matched no-heat fit (\cref{fig:heat}).
The fitted heats range from \(-81.75\) to \(-81.51\)~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo, against measured values of \(-85\) to \(-79\), with heat AARD 2.30\,\%.
The 141-target pressure/species subtotal rises from 30.090 to 30.331 (0.80\,\%); pressure cost rises 7.58\,\%, B\"ottinger species cost from 2.974 to 3.213 and Matin cost falls from 19.326 to 18.737.
Fitted-pressure AARD changes from 13.26 to 13.81\,\% and species AARD from 7.94 to 7.89\,\%.

% P:results-heat-02
Arcis RMSE falls from 11.63 to \SI{8.36}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} overall: from 14.14 to 10.58 at \SI{0.51}{\mega\pascal} and from 7.00 to 3.74 at \SI{1.03}{\mega\pascal}.
The positive mean calculated-minus-observed difference, \SI{7.50}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}, means the model remains less exothermic than Arcis.
The calorimeters differ in temperature and pressure, and this transfer discrepancy does not establish that either measurement set is wrong.

% P:results-heat-03
The calorimetric gain comes with a higher pressure AARD at 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} (\cref{tab:heat-pressure-tradeoff}).
The Hilliard/Jou packet and canonical cohorts have different row sets and are kept separate; their higher-temperature rows were outside this fit but had been accessed during earlier model development.
The canonical 80\,\si{\degreeCelsius} pressure AARD changes only from 19.41 to 19.61\,\%, while the B\"ottinger 80\,\si{\degreeCelsius} species AARD rises from 12.65 to 13.14\,\%.
Two Hilliard/Jou packet rows overestimate pressure by 103.6\,\% at \SI{100}{\degreeCelsius}, loading 0.5889, and by 132.8\,\% at \SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, loading 0.00323; both are converged predictions.
This is a trade-off of the tested parameterization with fixed chemistry, five coordinates, bounds and residual scales; it does not uniquely locate the limitation in reaction chemistry.

\subsection{Born term}\label{sec:results-born-off}
% P:results-04
Within the tested model, bounds, objective and two starts, refitting all five parameters without the Born term (F3) raises the fitted-pressure AARD from 13.81 to 80.25\,\% (Supplementary Table~S1).
The pressure targets carry most of the cost increase, and both the \HCOthree--water and the \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficients reach their bounds.
This comparison does not establish physical necessity of the Born term or identify the mechanism of the pressure change.

\subsection{Born form and fitted observation set}\label{sec:results-born}
% P:results-07
The SSM+DS (F1) and original Born (F2) forms were fitted with the same parameters, bounds, weights, reaction constants, ion inputs and eight heat targets.
Without fitting Matin's pool, SSM+DS has the lower cost, 32.057 against 60.008, and lower fitted-pressure AARD, 13.81 against 36.07\,\%; its fitted-species AARD is also lower, 7.89 against 8.44\,\% (\cref{tab:residual-summary}).
Their pressure/species subtotals are 30.331 and 57.349, respectively.
The two forms therefore do not calibrate pressure comparably under this objective.
In F2 the \MEAH--\MEACOO coefficient also lies on its lower bound of \(-1\).

% P:results-08
Matin et al.\ obtained bicarbonate from a titration that assigns carbonate to bicarbonate, and they report bicarbonate above the NMR values of Jakobsen et al., whereas their free amine, protonated amine and carbamate agree more closely (Fig.~2, p.~6616; Fig.~3 and discussion, p.~6617)~\cite{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012,Jakobsen2005}.
On that basis the 18 Matin pool targets are not fitted in F1 and F2.
F1 lies below them, with a mean \(\ln(x_{\mathrm{calc}}/x_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-1.094\) (\cref{tab:residual-summary}; Supplementary Table~S5).

% P:results-09
Adding the 18 pool targets preserves the Born-form cost ordering (\cref{fig:pool-effect}): SSM+DS F4 has cost 47.614, below 66.602 for original Born F5.
Fitting the pool lowers its AARD for both forms and raises the cost of the 149 base targets for both (\cref{fig:pool-effect}b; Supplementary Table~S3).
F5 represents the titration pool more closely, with AARD 29.51 against 42.13\,\% for F4, while F4 retains a lower fitted-pressure AARD, 13.34 against 34.34\,\%.
These comparisons describe the tested observation sets, bounds and objective; the residual signs do not establish which bicarbonate measurement is correct.

\subsection{Temperature and concentration outside the fit}\label{sec:results-80c}
% P:results-10
Except for the Wagner et al.\ rows, the comparisons at 80--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} are not independent of fixed values of F1 and F2 (\cref{sec:parameters}), so they test conditions outside the five-parameter fit but are not independent temperature predictions.
On the 21 measured pressures at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, F1 gives an AARD of 19.6\,\% and underestimates the pressures on average (\cref{tab:residual-summary}; Supplementary Table~S5).
The Wagner et al.\ pressures, which entered no fitting or selection step, give 23.11\,\% near \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} (\SI{353}{\kelvin}) and 16.23\,\% near \SI{119}{\degreeCelsius} (\SI{392}{\kelvin}), and F1 again lies below them on average.
At 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}, beyond the fitted temperatures, the 54 evaluated pressures give 30.84\,\%.
The 11 B\"ottinger species at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, measured in a 31 wt\% solution, are represented more closely by F2 than by F1, while the pressure comparisons vary by group: F2 is closer near \SI{353}{\kelvin}, F1 near \SI{392}{\kelvin} (\cref{tab:residual-summary}).

% P:results-11
On the 105 measured pressures at 40--\SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, F1 gives an AARD of 20.09\,\%.
This group combines fitted targets with rows outside the fit, so it measures pooled agreement, not accuracy outside the fit.
The Aronu et al.\ rows deviate most, with a mean \(\ln(p_{\mathrm{calc}}/p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) of \(-0.355\), so the pooled value is dominated by underestimation of these measurements (Supplementary Table~S5)~\cite{Aronu2011}.

% P:results-12
Applied unchanged at other amine concentrations, F1 gives AARDs of 105.54\,\% at 15 wt\% \MEA and 56.32\,\% at 45 wt\%; it overestimates the pressures at 15 wt\% and underestimates them at 45 wt\%, so its parameters are calibrated for 30 wt\% \MEA.
Over nine Jakobsen NMR points at 20 and \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius}, the calculated carbonate share of dissolved carbon is 0.25--1.15 times the observed share for F1 and 0.84--2.17 times for F2~\cite{Jakobsen2005}.
One of the ten points, at \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} and loading 0.21, is excluded from these ranges because its observed share, 0.119, is out of line with the 0.010--0.019 of the other four \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} points; the source dilution and loading definitions are unresolved, and all ten comparisons are listed in Supplementary Table~S6.

\subsection{Source reaction laws}\label{sec:results-source-laws}
% P:results-13
F6 uses the same 149 targets with a different temperature description: R2 and R5 follow their source correlations, the \COtwo dispersion energy is the published \SI{169.21}{\kelvin}, and the five interaction parameters are refitted.
Its pressure/species subtotal, 30.555, is close to F1's 30.331, but its heat cost is 10.347 against 1.726, making the total cost 40.902 against 32.057.
On the 54 canonical pressures at 100--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} it gives 54.98\,\% against 30.84\,\% for F1, and its calculated pressures are too low.
At 15 wt\% \MEA, F6 gives 46.58\,\% against 105.54\,\% for F1, but at 45 wt\% it is farther from the measurements than F1 (\cref{tab:residual-summary}), so neither fit carries over consistently between amine concentrations.

% P:results-14
F6 changes the reaction laws, the \COtwo dispersion energy and the refitted interactions together, so the difference from F1 does not isolate the effect of the reaction laws.
Both fits use their reaction correlations over the same declared domain, 293.15--\SI{393.15}{\kelvin}, which extends beyond the source ranges; the R4 source correlation, for example, ends at \SI{323.15}{\kelvin}.
The comparison therefore assesses model behavior beyond the fitted temperatures.
The pressure/species subtotal at or below \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} alone gives little distinction between the two temperature descriptions, and the better higher-temperature agreement of F1 comes with fixed values estimated from data at 80--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} (\cref{sec:parameters}).
For modelers, the temperature response of this model must come from data that carry it directly, such as heat of absorption or equilibrium measurements above \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-15
Like the electrolyte NRTL model of Zhang et al., which was fitted to \COtwo solubility, heat of absorption, heat capacity and NMR speciation~\cite{Zhang2011}, and unlike that of Akula et al., which left NMR speciation outside its fit~\cite{Akula2023a}, the present model fits speciation together with pressure, so the residuals of its 94 fitted species targets are calibration results (\cref{tab:literature-model-comparison}).
Unlike the ePC-SAFT models of MDEA~\cite{Uyan2015,Wangler2018,Bulow2021a}, it fits ion interactions to the loaded ternary mixture, including the \MEAH--\MEACOO interaction, which exists only in loaded solution.
Published pressure deviations for 30 wt\% \MEA cover different row sets and temperature ranges, so no accuracy ranking against those models is claimed.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:results-limits}
% P:results-16
F1 describes \COtwo partial pressure in 30 wt\% \MEA, fitted at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, and liquid speciation, fitted at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, and integral heat, fitted at \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius}; its comparisons at 80--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} rest partly on fixed values estimated with data at those temperatures.
Its ion parameters are effective values: the \MEAH and \MEACOO parameters were fitted to speciation data, the \HCOthree--water coefficient lies on its bound, and the \MEAH--water slope has only conditional precision.
The conditional standard errors treat residuals as independent and exclude the uncertainty of the fixed inputs.
The Matin numerical values were checked against Table~S.1, while the calculations treat the reported \SI{21}{\degreeCelsius} as equivalent to \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius}.

% P:results-17
The physical equation-of-state fit contains no density targets; a separate density-only volume translation uses four Amundsen et al.\ calibration states at 50 and \SI{70}{\degreeCelsius} and loadings 0.3 and 0.4~mol \COtwo/mol \MEA, on a \COtwo-free 30 wt\% solvent basis~\cite[Table~3, p.~3097]{Amundsen2009}.
For those four calibration states, at an assumed pressure of \SI{100000}{\pascal}, the translated density deviations range from \(-0.15\) to \(+0.25\)\,\%.
The translation is \(v_{\mathrm{corr}}=v_{\mathrm{EOS}}+c(x_{\MEAH}+x_{\MEACOO})\), with \(c=\SI{39.5143}{\cubic\centi\metre\per\mole}\); it changes only the reported density, not equilibrium or calorics.
The source does not report the measurement pressure, and its general uncertainty estimate of \(\pm\SI{0.002}{\gram\per\cubic\centi\metre}\) for loaded solutions excludes the highest temperatures and loadings~\cite[pp.~3099--3100]{Amundsen2009}.
Three solution heat-capacity spot checks were also evaluated; the loaded \SI{45}{\degreeCelsius} deviation changes from \(+0.44\) to \(+1.48\)\,\% and the loaded \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius} deviation from \(-4.53\) to \(-3.73\)\,\% relative to the no-heat fit, without establishing caloric qualification.
These four calibrated density deviations do not establish density transfer, and the model remains unqualified for absorber and stripper calculations.
