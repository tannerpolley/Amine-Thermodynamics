\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
The effect of the Born term on reactive vapor--liquid equilibrium is examined for aqueous monoethanolamine (\MEA), whose carbamate chemistry changes the ion population with \COtwo loading.
A nine-species electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model combines explicit ionic speciation with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions.
Five binary interaction parameters are fitted to 47 \COtwo pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, 94 liquid species measurements at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and eight integral heats at \SI{40}{\degreeCelsius} in 30 wt\% \MEA.
Fitted-pressure and species average absolute relative deviations (AARD) are 13.81 and 7.89\,\%.
Wagner pressures near 80 and \SI{119}{\degreeCelsius}, which entered no fitting or selection step, give 23.11 and 16.23\,\%; other high-temperature data informed fixed inputs.
Within the tested model, bounds, objective and two starts, removing the Born term and refitting raises fitted-pressure AARD to 80.25\,\%, without establishing physical necessity or a mechanism.
The solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation form retains the lower cost both with and without the titration bicarbonate pool.
Against a matched no-heat fit, heat root-mean-square error (RMSE) falls from 4.57 to \SI{2.13}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} for fitted Vinjarapu data and from 11.63 to 8.36 for predicted Arcis data, while Hilliard/Jou packet pressure AARD rises from 18.12 to 29.66\,\% at \SI{100}{\degreeCelsius} and from 24.11 to 41.55\,\% at \SI{120}{\degreeCelsius}.
This trade-off and the conditional parameter uncertainty limit temperature extrapolation and concentration transfer.
\end{abstract}
