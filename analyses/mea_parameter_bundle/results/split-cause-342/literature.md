# Experimental phase evidence

This reading answers step 6 of [#342](https://github.com/tannerpolley/Amine-Thermodynamics/issues/342). It does not supply measured liquid–liquid coexistence boundaries. The local reading shelf is ignored; the paper text is not distributed. `source-hashes.json` records the Markdown copies checked byte-for-byte against their current Zotero companions. Aronu was read from Zotero PDF `W2NNU9H6`, including its appended corrigendum; its text extraction reports a malformed annotation bounding box, without preventing reading of the text.

- **Böttinger, Maiwald and Hasse (2008), DOI 10.1016/j.fluid.2007.09.017, Section 3, Fig. 1 and Table 3; Sections 4–7.** The experimental section explicitly describes transporting a homogeneous mixture from a stirred view cell to the nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer. The window check establishes absence of gas after filling. MEA concentrations are 20 and 30 mass% in the unloaded solvent, temperatures 293–353 K, loadings up to 1.01 mol CO₂/mol MEA, and pressures 5–25 bar; the maximum loading varies with temperature. This is explicit homogeneous-liquid evidence in its measured subset, not a survey of every state at 15–45 mass% and 313–393 K. The extended Pitzer model fits carbamate and oxazolidone equilibrium constants and physical interaction parameters to species data, retaining the MEA protonation law from literature. Its comparison with gas solubility is secondary to the chemical measurements; Section 7 states that no new gas-solubility measurements were performed. The data do not separately resolve MEA and MEAH⁺.

- **Amundsen, Øi and Eimer (2009), DOI 10.1021/je900188m, Experimental Section, Tables 2–4, Discussion and Conclusions.** The loaded-solution measurements cover 20, 30 and 40 mass% MEA, 298–353 K and loading 0–0.5. The paper reports no second liquid in these measurements. It does report gas-bubble formation that prevents density measurements at the highest loadings and temperatures, and difficulty obtaining homogeneous samples and avoiding evaporation. Those limitations prevent treating unmeasured states as homogeneous-liquid observations. Binary density/viscosity correlations are fitted; loaded properties are compared with the Weiland correlations. This is property-measurement evidence, not a measured liquid–liquid equilibrium study.

- **Aronu et al. (2011), DOI 10.1016/j.ces.2011.08.042, Sections 2.1–2.2 (pp. 6394–6395), Tables 3–5 (pp. 6397–6398), Sections 3.3–3.5 and 4.2–5 (pp. 6396–6404); corrigendum (2012), pp. 246–247.** Measurements cover the requested 15, 30 and 45 mass% MEA concentrations, 313–393 K, with liquid sampling and loadings extending beyond 0.6 in parts of the dataset. The apparatus descriptions and results report vapor–liquid equilibrium and no liquid–liquid split; they do not explicitly assert a homogeneous liquid at every requested state. The extended UNIQUAC model fits selected ionic size and interaction parameters to the new partial and total pressures and literature physical-solubility data. Species, excess enthalpy and freezing-point comparisons are not fitted in that work. No physical stability proof follows from successful correlation of these pressures. The corrigendum corrects the total-pressure layout and missing loadings; it reports no additional liquid phase.

- **Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.cherd.2014.07.017, Sections 2, 3.1–3.3, Table 5 and Section 4.** PC-SAFT with ideal Smith–Missen chemical equilibrium uses pure-fluid fitted parameters and a binary MEA–water interaction fitted to binary vapor–liquid data. Ternary partial pressures are not fitted, and mixing enthalpies are not fitted. The ternary description has one liquid containing molecular and ionic species and one molecular vapor; comparisons span 298–443 K, including 15.3 and 30 mass% MEA subsets. Section 3.2, Eq. (11), p. 795, gives the unlike-energy combining rule: decreasing the interaction constant increases the unlike well depth at fixed pure-component energies. Its sign and square-root placement were checked visually against Zotero PDF `JWH69DKG`, p. 7, as well as the Markdown companion. This supports the sign interpretation of the numerical parameter probe; it does not identify a unique microscopic cause. The one-liquid description is a published modeling convention, not an experimental homogeneous-liquid observation and not evidence that this repository's electrolyte model must have identical stability.

**Conclusion:** explicit homogeneous-liquid wording exists for Böttinger's measured subset. For the broader concentration/temperature range, the evidence read here is absence of a reported second liquid in the cited measurements, with sampling and gas-formation limitations. A universal assertion of observed single-liquid behavior throughout the entire rectangular domain would exceed these sources. No new literature equation or parameter is adopted in this diagnosis.
