\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory model is calibrated for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo), and its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters, with water--anion interactions from sodium-salt osmotic coefficients, are held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.
With every reaction reference at \SI{100}{\kilo\pascal}, equilibrium, density and enthalpy follow from one Gibbs energy, whose pressure derivative differs from the volume by at most a relative $1.4\times10^{-5}$, at numerical resolutions of 0.12--10.5\,\%.
Fitted \COtwo partial pressures deviate by 11.34\,\% for the 11-parameter MEA model at 30 wt\%, more at other concentrations and stripper temperatures, and by 42.75\,\% for DEA; fitted total pressures deviate by 10.82\,\% for AMP and 4.39\,\% for MDEA.
The MDEA fit, provisional because only one of 64 drawn starts was evaluable, deviates by 29.21\,\% from 101 unfitted Ermatchkov \COtwo partial pressures.
Phase-stability searches find no instability for MEA or at 666 bubble-pressure-scaled AMP states, and no confirmed MDEA split.
For DEA they confirm splits at four states whose measured densities the single-liquid calculation matches; these are failures of the DEA model.
A first AMP fit, with pressures to \SI{353}{\kelvin}, split at 363--\SI{393}{\kelvin}, also at six measured-pressure controls; a refit adding 58 pressures to \SI{393}{\kelvin} confirms none, and at those controls four descents point toward a vapor and two have none.
The searches show where a fit extrapolates or fails; the models are calibrations, not design tools.

\end{abstract}
