\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
Do two calibrations of an equation of state that reproduce the pressures of aqueous amines loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo) about equally well predict the same liquid phases?
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model of monoethanolamine (MEA) is calibrated first, and its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters are held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.
For AMP, two fits with objective values of 61.3 and 62.1 deviate by 10.3 and 10.5\,\% from the same 47 fitted total pressures, yet the first gives 73 calculated liquid--liquid splits at 363--\SI{393}{\kelvin}, above its fitted temperatures, and the second none in 666 finite phase-stability searches.
For MDEA, changing one shared interaction within the tolerance of the MEA fit lowers the deviation of the 66 fitted total pressures from 20.1 to 11.2\,\%, raises that of the pressures outside the fit from 25.8 to 33.4\,\% and introduces two splits.
The MEA model deviates by 11.6\,\% from 47 fitted \COtwo partial pressures and the DEA model by 28.5\,\% from 30; neither has a detected instability, but the DEA model gives the wrong sign of the water--DEA excess enthalpy.
With the reported MDEA model no second liquid is confirmed at the measured states, and one two-liquid solution is found at \SI{262.9}{\mega\pascal}, far above process pressures.
These are calculated phase behaviors of calibrated models: the measurements do not establish coexistence, and the models, calibrated against data examined during development, provide neither validation nor a basis for absorber design.

\end{abstract}
