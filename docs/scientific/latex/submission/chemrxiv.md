# ChemRxiv submission draft

## Title

Thermodynamics of CO₂-loaded aqueous amines with ePC-SAFT: monoethanolamine calibration, transfer to DEA, MDEA and AMP, and phase stability

## Author and affiliation

Tanner W. Polley; ORCID 0009-0008-5957-4152; tpolley3@byu.edu. Department of Chemical Engineering, Brigham Young University, Provo, UT 84602, United States.

## Abstract

An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory model is calibrated for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (CO₂), and its water, CO₂ and inorganic-ion parameters are held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted. The shared water–bicarbonate and water–carbonate interactions are fixed from sodium-salt osmotic coefficients. Every reaction reference state is at 100 kPa, so equilibrium, density and enthalpy follow from one Gibbs energy, as verified numerically at fitted states. The MEA model has 11 fitted parameters and literature equilibrium constants; its fitted CO₂ partial pressures at 30 wt% deviate by 11.34%, and it is less accurate at other concentrations and stripper temperatures. The fitted loaded-solution pressures of the other amines deviate by 42.75% for DEA, 4.39% for MDEA and 10.82% for AMP; the MDEA fit is provisional, because only one of 64 drawn starts could be evaluated. Phase-stability searches find no instability for MEA or at 666 AMP states scaled to the bubble pressure, and no confirmed split for MDEA. For DEA they confirm splits at four states of measured density: the single-liquid calculation matches those densities while the model's own phase calculation favors two liquids, contrary to treating the samples as one liquid. A first AMP fit, with loaded-solution pressures to 353 K, split at 363–393 K; a refit adding 18 pressures at 373–393 K and 40 at 313–393 K confirms none, with four of six measured-pressure controls unresolved. Phase-stability searches show where a fit is extrapolated or fails. The models are calibrations, not a basis for absorber design.

## Keywords

ePC-SAFT; monoethanolamine; diethanolamine; methyldiethanolamine; 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol; parameter transfer; phase stability.

## Data availability

The electronic data comprise processed observations, parameter values, numerical results and the analysis and figure code under the MIT license; third-party measurements remain under the terms of their sources. They will be deposited in Zenodo, and the DOI will be added at submission. A software index in the deposit lists the versions, checksums and commands needed to repeat the calculations. The four reported models were calculated with version 1.3.1 of the epcsaft package, and the MEA fits with the MEA-fitted shared set with version 1.3.0; the current release, 1.3.2, was not used for them. Referees will receive access to the compiled epcsaft package through the corresponding author.
