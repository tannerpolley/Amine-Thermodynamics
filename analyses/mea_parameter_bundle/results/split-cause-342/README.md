## Energy convention

The main figure and interpretation use `wide-isobaric` and `F2-isobaric`. Each of the 51 confirmed configurations from #255 retains the phase amounts and mole fractions, including vapor where present. Native liquid/vapor pressure roots determine density separately for each parameter record at the same imposed temperature and pressure. `compositions.csv` retains every parent/split phase composition, amount, density, calculated pressure, pressure error and pressure-density derivative. Across 908 isobaric phase evaluations, the largest absolute pressure error is 3.56×10⁻⁵ Pa and every pressure-density derivative is positive (minimum 2599.50 Pa m³/mol). These are native density-root diagnostics, not a chemical-equilibrium or global-stability proof for the fixed compositions.

The no-suffix `wide` and `F2` rows are supplementary fixed-density controls. Packing coordinates are recomputed from the native packing-fraction coefficient to preserve their recorded density when segment diameters change. Those controls are evaluations of the Helmholtz-plus-imposed-pressure-volume functional, rather than mechanically equilibrated F2 phases. At the illustrated water-poor composition, F2's pressure at the old density is about 39.95 MPa instead of the imposed 21.18 kPa; this is why the main comparison relaxes density.

The calculation reuses `phase_diagnosis_255.py` and the native compiled equilibrium objective. Contribution values are extensive $G/(RT)$ in mol for the feed of 9.7292821 mol MEA, 76.9751925 mol water and loading times 9.7292821 mol CO₂ (30 mass% unloaded MEA). They are not per mole of mixture. Multiplication by $RT$ gives joules. Ideal mixing and completed standard-state terms are retained together as the native total minus the explicit residual-plus-PV terms, following the existing producer. Sum closure checks bookkeeping, not an independent evaluation of that remainder. The residual Helmholtz sum is also checked against the native state value. The original native totals are replayed to $10^{-12}$ relative to their magnitude; contribution differences close to $10^{-12}$ relative to the difference, the issue's floating-point limit. The largest observed relative closure error is 3.67×10⁻¹³ across 408 decompositions.

`contributions.csv` retains eight parameter/rule/density comparisons for every confirmed split, and `contribution-changes.csv` their F2-minus-wide differences within each rule/density comparison. `compositions.csv` retains all species in every parent/split phase. The representative figure uses the most negative retained wide-record energy change, without selecting on F2's result. Zero cation-mixing and dipolar terms remain in the table and are omitted from the figure. `energy-checks.json` summarizes the retained numerical checks.

## Scope

This is numerical attribution within the fixed electrolyte model, not physical validation, global stability proof, liquid–liquid equilibrium measurements, parameter uncertainty propagation, or qualification for an absorber. F2's fitted and not-fitted property comparisons remain with #338/#340 and are not rescored here. The experimental evidence distinguishes explicit homogeneous-liquid wording for a measured subset from absence of a reported liquid split in a broader dataset. Manuscript use requires the owner's later promotion of the independently reviewed notebook commit.

The Born term has the largest absolute change in two of the 51 isobaric configurations (dispersion in 49), so the conditional step 5 is evaluated as well. The `wide-solvent-only-isobaric` and `F2-solvent-only-isobaric` rows, alongside their fixed-density controls, reuse #285's alternative: select the solvent-only model family, replace dielectric volume/temperature coefficients with the earlier solvent relative-permittivity coefficients and correlations, and leave every other parameter fixed. This changes the model rule as a diagnostic; it does not refit or adopt it. `F2-parent-comparison.csv` additionally compares each fixed-composition split with F2's own equilibrated census parent, separately for isobaric and fixed-density evaluations. A negative difference against the old parent alone is not a stability finding for F2, because that old parent is no longer a stationary F2 state.
