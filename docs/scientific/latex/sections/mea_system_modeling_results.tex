The pure-water fit reproduces IAPWS-95 saturation density, vapor pressure and heat capacity over the fitted interval, with interleaved temperatures providing a numerical check of the correlation \cite{internationalassociationforthepropertiesofwaterandsteamRevisedReleaseIAPWS2018}.
The water inputs remain fixed during MEA estimation; the comparison is with a reference equation rather than independent measurements.

% P:results-binary-01
The \COtwo--water interaction remains fixed after estimation against Kiepe pressures, while the MEA--water interaction is jointly estimated with the amine-ion parameters \cite{kiepeExperimentalDeterminationPrediction2002,caiBinaryIsobaricVaporLiquid1996}.
Their errors are reported by observable in \cref{tab:residual-summary}.
The MEA--water temperature--composition curves show the binary constraint on that interaction before the reacting ternary mixture is considered (\cref{fig:mea-water}).
The dielectric coefficients were estimated from unloaded solutions at \SI{298.15}{\kelvin}; these observations do not determine the temperature or loading dependence used in the reacting mixture \cite[ESI Tables~S1--S2]{agieienkoWhatGasStream2024a}.

\subsection{Phase stability of the fitted models}\label{sec:phase-method}
% P:results-phase-opening
Loaded aqueous MEA is observed as a homogeneous liquid in B\"ottinger et al.'s measured subset \cite[Sect.~3]{Bottinger2008}.
That observation motivates checking the model for a spurious additional liquid, although it does not establish experimental homogeneity over the entire numerical search domain.
The adopted F2 parameters give no detected instability at 1908 liquid-grid states, 576 intermediate liquid states and 162 liquid--vapor states, with no unavailable or incomplete search in these sets.
Trial compositions taken from Gibbs decreases resolved for other MEA parameter sets also give no negative distance for F2; 6121 such compositions are evaluated at every liquid--vapor state.
No stability penalty entered the F2 fit.

% P:results-phase-split
A 16-parameter comparison fit obtained without the stability penalty shows what the search detects: it gives a resolved Gibbs decrease at 54 of the same 162 liquid--vapor states.
It is distinct from F0, the stability-penalized calibration of the restriction sequence.
Its strongest confirmed split, at \SI{333.15}{\kelvin}, loading 0.5 mol/mol and \SI{21.175719}{\kilo\pascal}, produces a water-poor, ion-rich liquid alongside a water-rich liquid and vapor.
The calculated water mole fractions are 0.590900 and 0.973235 in the two liquids; these are model compositions, not measured second-liquid compositions.
Figure~\ref{fig:split-energy} compares the energy balance for this split under the unpenalized parameters and F2.
Phase amounts and compositions remain frozen for this comparison, while densities are recalculated at the imposed pressure.

\begin{figure}[pos=!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{split-energy.pdf}
\caption{The false liquid split involves opposing energy contributions. Extensive split-minus-parent Gibbs contributions at \SI{333.15}{\kelvin}, loading 0.5 mol/mol and \SI{21.175719}{\kilo\pascal}, for the unpenalized comparison fit and F2 evaluated at the same frozen phase amounts and compositions. \label{fig:split-energy}
\end{figure}

% P:results-phase-cause
The Born contribution is strongly favorable to the illustrated split and becomes more favorable with F2, while dispersion supplies the largest upward change.
Across the confirmed configurations, dispersion has the largest absolute change in 49 of 51 cases; Born has the largest change in the other two.
The split therefore reflects a coupled energy balance with a prominent dispersion change, rather than one uniquely identified microscopic cause.
A negative energy change of F2 relative to the frozen parent liquid is not itself an F2 instability test, because that parent is not an equilibrated F2 state.

% P:results-phase-probes
Two distinct isolated changes remove all detected descents in the finite liquid--vapor search: replacing only the \MEAH segment diameter by its F2 value, or replacing only the \MEAH--water interaction constant.
In the segment-diameter probe, the Debye--H\"uckel, packing and Born diameters remain at the unpenalized fit's values.
These probes lie outside the tied parameterization of F2, and neither is a fitted parameter set with assessed property agreement.
They show that the sufficient parameter change is not unique; they do not identify a molecular mechanism or uncertainty in a stability boundary.

\subsubsection{Absorption heat and heat capacity}\label{sec:results-heat}
% P:results-heat-01
The fitted integral heats and the Arcis comparison probe different temperature and pressure conditions (\cref{fig:heat}).
The calculated Arcis absorption is less exothermic than measured, with a larger discrepancy in the lower-pressure series.
The measurements themselves differ between those series at nearby loadings \cite[Table~5]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}; neither a pressure-only explanation nor a unique model contribution is isolated by this comparison.

% P:results-heat-kim
The Kim--Svendsen comparison deteriorates at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} (\cref{tab:same-data}).
At that temperature its finite-interval heat RMSE exceeds that of the Polley--Hedengren parameters on the common intervals (\cref{tab:same-data}).
Both calculations include the inconsistent printed intervals in this comparison; Table~\ref{supp-tab:kim-alternatives} reports alternative source exclusions.
Reaction-law extrapolation, approximated pressure histories and missing chemistry can affect the calculated heat, but this result does not identify a unique cause.
The discrepancy prevents interpreting the common enthalpy formulation as demonstrated high-temperature caloric accuracy.

\subsection{Parameter choices and conditional uncertainty}\label{sec:results-joint}
% P:results-joint-01
Table~\ref{tab:choice-cost} gives the measured cost of each cumulative restriction defined in \cref{sec:parameters}.
From F0 to F2, five fewer adjustable parameters leave the objective value and the fitted pressure and density deviations nearly unchanged and reduce the number of parameters at bounds, while the deviation of the pressures that are not fitted rises slightly.
F2 is the most restricted set whose fitted pressure and density deviations do not exceed those of F0; every set beyond it, F3 to F5, has larger fitted and not-fitted pressure deviations than F2.
The cumulative changes quantify the cost of the selected assumptions, not the isolated physical effect of each restriction.

\subsection{Comparison with published models}\label{sec:results-literature}
% P:results-literature-01
Table~\ref{tab:same-data} compares the adopted formulation with the 2026 Polley--Hedengren parameters on identical observations \cite{polleyReactiveEPCSAFTModeling2026}.
The Polley--Hedengren equation-of-state calorics are calculated here from their published thermodynamic specification; their empirical column enthalpy correlations are not substituted.
The comparison shows gains in pressure and density agreement alongside remaining and worsened quantities, including high-temperature heat and the more concentrated pressure series.
Because the fitting roles differ, these values do not define an across-role model ranking.
Their published fitting account does not establish whether every pressure and species observation was fitted; the mixed-role labels in \cref{tab:same-data} preserve that uncertainty.

On the 19 identical Ma'mun observations (\cref{tab:matched-pressure}) \cite{Mamun2005}, F2 has a larger pressure deviation than F0 and than the PC-SAFT calculation of Baygi and Pahlavanzadeh, and a smaller one than the SAFT-HR calculation of Najafloo and Zarei.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:limits}
% P:results-limits
These results support F2 as a calibration, with calculations at fixed parameters, against data seen during development; no comparison establishes independent validation.
The adopted calibration also has measured weaknesses: on the 19 Ma'mun observations, the restrictions raise the pressure AARD from 19.43\,\% for F0 to 33.47\,\% for F2, and at \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} the Kim--Svendsen heat RMSE of F2, \SI{31.77}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo, exceeds the \SI{29.49}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} \COtwo of the Polley--Hedengren parameters.
The parameter uncertainty is conditional on this fit and therefore excludes model discrepancy and reaction-law uncertainty.
The absence of an additional liquid rests on finite searches rather than a global proof, and the false split of the comparison fit has no unique microscopic cause, so the stability of F2 cannot be attributed to one restricted parameter.
The R1--R3 temperature range is Austgen's correlation range, not demonstrated measurement coverage, and R4, R5 and the unloaded dielectric law are extrapolated beyond their direct source constraints.
Pressures at other MEA concentrations, carbonate shares and the highest-temperature heats remain the weakest parts of the calibration, and missing pressure histories, source inconsistencies, observable approximations, omitted carrier gas and omitted oxazolidone further limit interpretation.
No absorber or stripper operating prediction is qualified by the property comparisons alone.
