\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
The effect of the Born term on reactive vapor--liquid equilibrium is examined for aqueous monoethanolamine (\MEA), whose carbamate chemistry changes the ion population with \COtwo loading.
Electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) combines explicit ionic speciation with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions.
A nine-species ePC-SAFT model with Debye--H\"uckel and Born contributions was fitted through five binary interaction parameters to 47 \COtwo partial pressures at 40 and \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} and 94 liquid species measurements at 20--\SI{60}{\degreeCelsius} in 30 wt\% \MEA, with average absolute relative deviations (AARD) of 14.0\,\% and 8.3\,\%.
On pressures near 80 and \SI{119}{\degreeCelsius} (353 and \SI{392}{\kelvin}) that entered no fitting or selection step it gives 22.5\,\% and 22.3\,\%; the other data at 80--\SI{120}{\degreeCelsius} informed fixed reaction-enthalpy shifts and are not independent temperature tests.
Refitted without the Born term, the model gives a fitted-pressure AARD of 53.6\,\%.
A pressure decomposition separates the changes in activity and speciation caused by removing the Born term.
The solvation-shell and dielectric-saturation (SSM+DS) and original Born terms calibrate comparably; which has the lower cost depends on whether titration bicarbonate data are fitted.
The calibrated pressure and speciation describe a limited concentration domain; they do not establish transfer to other solvent compositions.
Electrolyte-term comparisons should state which speciation data are fitted, and independent equilibrium measurements above the fitted temperatures are needed to constrain the model's temperature response.
\end{abstract}
