\section{Data and parameter estimation}\label{sec:data-methods}
\subsection{Experimental data and their use in fitting}
% P:methods-01
Loading is $\alpha=n_{\COtwo,\mathrm{added}}/n_{\mathrm{amine,total}}$, and unloaded-solvent mass fraction is $w_{\MEA}=m_{\MEA}/(m_{\MEA}+m_{\HtwoO})$.
Three data-role terms are used throughout: \emph{fitted} means an observation enters the objective of the reported fit; \emph{not fitted} means it is outside that objective; \emph{comparison only} means a different quantity or different conditions from those the model was built for, never used to choose a fit.
Prior inspection or use in choosing parameters is disclosed separately in \cref{tab:current-data-inventory}.
The objective contains 307 quantities; the binary \COtwo--water pressures remain in it as checks on a previously fitted, fixed interaction.
All subsequent property calculations use the adopted parameters without adjustment.

% P:methods-02
Each species observable follows its experimental definition.
B\"ottinger et al.\ report \ce{MEA + MEAH+} as an NMR sum and tabulate bicarbonate while noting practically absent carbonate \cite[p.~134, Table~3]{Bottinger2008}.
Their bicarbonate-labeled observations are compared with calculated \ce{HCO3- + CO3^{2-}}.
Matin et al.\ neglect carbonate when inferring bicarbonate from titration and carbon balance \cite[pp.~6615--6616, Eqs.~18--19b]{matinFacileMethodDetermination2012}; the same aggregate comparison is an observation approximation, and these bicarbonate values are not fitted.
The Matin series labeled \SI{294.15}{\kelvin} is calculated at \SI{293.15}{\kelvin}.
Jakobsen species-to-total-amine ratios enter the objective, whereas their carbonate shares of dissolved carbon do not; the calculation accounts for dilution during NMR preparation \cite[Tables~A1--A2, Sample preparation]{Jakobsen2005}.

% P:methods-03
Pressure and species point calculations use each source's solvent composition, including reported molality and temperature for Hilliard and Xu \cite[App.~D.4]{Hilliard2008}\cite[Table~1]{Xu2011}.
Three Xu pressure observations above \SI{393.15}{\kelvin} and a B\"ottinger state with quantifiable oxazolidone are excluded; oxazolidone is absent from the model.
The pressure sources are Aronu, Hilliard, Idris, Jou, Ma'mun and Xu, with Wagner assessed separately \cite{Aronu2011,Hilliard2008,idrisSpeciationMEACO2Adducts2014,Jou1995,Mamun2005,Xu2011,wagnerSolubilityCarbonDioxide2013}.
Carrier gas is omitted from the bubble calculation: calculated $y_{\COtwo}P$ is compared with measured \COtwo partial pressure rather than imposing the experimental total pressure.
This approximation can matter at low \COtwo pressure; Neumann et al.\ instead include nitrogen \cite[Sect.~4.2, Fig.~5]{neumannNewModelCombining2022}.

\subsection{Absorption heat, heat capacity and density}\label{sec:heat-method}
% P:methods-heat-01
Vinjarapu integral heats are fitted at \SI{313.15}{\kelvin} and \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} from initially carbon-free 30 wt\% solvent \cite[Sects.~1.1.1 and 2.2, Table~4]{HemaBhavya2025}.
Signed heat per mole of added \COtwo is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta h=\frac{H_{\mathrm{final}}-H_{\mathrm{initial}}-\Delta n_{\COtwo}h_{\COtwo}^{\mathrm{feed}}}{\Delta n_{\COtwo}},
 \label{eq:addition-heat}
\end{equation}
where both equilibrium liquids and the pure-\COtwo feed are evaluated at the same temperature and pressure; negative values denote exothermic absorption.
Arcis integral heats use \SI{322.5}{\kelvin} and the reported pressures \cite[Sect.~2.4, Table~5]{arcisEnthalpySolutionCarbon2011}.
Kim--Svendsen semi-differential calorimetry measures finite additions; the corresponding calculation uses the liquid enthalpy at each interval endpoint, rather than the cumulative heat from zero loading \cite[Tables~1--3]{Kim2007}.
A \SI{1.734}{\mega\pascal} isobar approximates these intervals because their experimental pressure histories are unavailable.

% P:methods-heat-conditions
The fitted Hilliard heat capacities use 7 mol MEA/kg water at \SI{101325}{\pascal}, with loading $10^{-6}$ for nominally unloaded states \cite[Sects.~4.5--4.6, App.~G.2]{Hilliard2008}.
The objective uses molar-feed heat capacity, while comparisons with the broader heat-capacity data use the source mass basis.
The feed basis includes added \COtwo in both conversions.

% P:methods-density
Densities come directly from the equation of state, without volume translation.
The fitted loaded observations comprise Amundsen and Hartono data, while Han densities are not fitted \cite{Amundsen2009,Hartono2014,hanDensityWater12012}.
Five Amundsen observations at loading 0.4 are excluded from fitting after comparison with interpolated Hartono values; they remain in the displayed density comparisons.
The loaded-density residual scale is
\begin{equation}
 s_{\rho,j}=\left[u_{\rho,j}^2+(g_j u_{\alpha,j})^2\right]^{1/2},
 \label{eq:density-scale}
\end{equation}
where $g_j$ is the secant between neighboring loadings at the same source temperature and composition, with a one-sided secant at either end.
These heterogeneous scales are not uniformly standard measurement uncertainties, so twice a scale does not uniformly define a confidence interval.
Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-density} gives the source inputs and their qualifications.

\subsection{Parameter estimation}\label{sec:parameters}
% P:methods-04
Estimation proceeds from pure water against IAPWS-95, through the \COtwo--water interaction against Kiepe pressures and the MEA dielectric coefficients against unloaded-solution permittivities, to the joint MEA fit.
Earlier subsystem coefficients remain fixed in the joint fit.
The adopted set, labeled F2 for the parameter-choice comparison, fixes R2 and R5 to their literature laws and ties the \MEAH and \MEACOO Born diameters to their segment diameters.
Eleven remaining parameters minimize
\begin{equation}
 C(\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac12\sum_jw_jr_j^2,\qquad
 r_j=\frac{y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}-y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}}{s_j}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 r_j=\frac{\ln(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})}{s_j},
 \label{eq:fit-objective}
\end{equation}
with the residual form, scale and weight specified by quantity in Table~\ref{supp-tab:objective-groups}.
Bounded Levenberg--Marquardt least squares uses exact equation-of-state and implicit equilibrium derivatives.
Two starts are examined for each restriction; the lower-cost converged start is reported, with convergence determined by the optimizer tolerance rather than an iteration limit.
These are local solutions, not a global parameter search.

% P:methods-weights
Pressure uses a log-ratio residual with an assigned relative allowance; species fractions and ratios use relative-plus-floor scales.
Vinjarapu heat scales come from the reported heat uncertainty, density scales combine density and loading information, and the heat-capacity scale uses Hilliard's stated accuracy on the molar-feed basis \cite[Table~4]{HemaBhavya2025}\cite[Sect.~4.6]{Hilliard2008}.
The binary-temperature, vapor-fraction and carbon-free-density allowances, together with the group weights, are assigned fitting choices.
The reasons for their particular magnitudes were not recorded; they are not reconstructed as statistical sampling variances.
Thus the objective specifies relative fitting priorities, and its value is not an absolute measure of physical accuracy.

% P:methods-06
Agreement is reported as average absolute relative deviation (AARD) and root-mean-square error (RMSE),
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{AARD}=\frac{100\,\%}{n}\sum_j\operatorname{abs}\!\left(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}/y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}-1\right),
 \qquad \operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_j(y_j^{\mathrm{calc}}-y_j^{\mathrm{obs}})^2}.
 \label{eq:aard}
\end{equation}
AARD describes error relative to observations; it is not an uncertainty interval.
For the interior parameters, local covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol\theta_I)=s_C^2(J_I^{\mathsf T}J_I)^{-1},\qquad
 s_C^2=\frac{2C}{N-|I|},
 \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
where $J_I$ is the weighted residual Jacobian and active-bound parameters remain fixed.
These errors condition on the fitted model, source laws, scales, weights and local linearization.

\subsection{Phase-stability analysis and stability penalty}\label{sec:methods-phase}
% P:methods-phase-01
The reacting tangent-plane criterion tests charge-neutral trial phases \cite[Sect.~2]{Michelsen1982}:
\begin{equation}
 D(\boldsymbol w)=g(T,P,\boldsymbol w)/(RT)-\boldsymbol w^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda,
 \quad \sum_iw_i=1,\quad\sum_i z_iw_i=0,\quad w_i>0.
 \label{eq:tpd}
\end{equation}
Here $g$ is molar Gibbs energy, $B$ describes conserved component combinations, and $\boldsymbol\lambda$ contains their chemical-potential multipliers in the parent equilibrium.
A negative distance must be accompanied by a material-conserving finite transfer that lowers Gibbs energy beyond its numerical error estimate before instability is counted.
An unavailable or incomplete search supplies no evidence of stability.

% P:methods-phase-02
Each fitted set is searched on the liquid-state grid and on liquid--vapor states defined in Section~\ref{supp-sec:s-phase}.
The liquid grids use 1.05 and 3.0 times the candidate's bubble pressure; separate checks use 1.5 times that pressure and intermediate compositions and temperatures.
At each liquid--vapor state, trial compositions that previously produced a resolved Gibbs decrease are evaluated in addition to optimized trials.
These supplied-composition distances are separate checks, not newly resolved phase splits.

% P:methods-penalty
If a split is detected, the whole searched liquid-state set enters the penalized objective:
\begin{equation}
 r_k^{\mathrm{stab}}=\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{stab}}}\frac{\tau_k}{s_{\mathrm{stab}}},\qquad
 C_{\mathrm{pen}}=C+\frac12\sum_k(r_k^{\mathrm{stab}})^2,
 \quad w_{\mathrm{stab}}=1,
 \quad s_{\mathrm{stab}}\in\{10^{-2},3\times10^{-3}\}.
 \label{eq:stability-penalty}
\end{equation}
The two scales are successive numerical penalty strengths; $\tau_k$ is the most negative distance on a resolved descending branch, or zero when a completed search finds none.
No zero is assigned to an unavailable search.
If needed, a parameter segment from a separately admissible anchor to the penalized solution is shortened in 0.01 steps, then refined by ten bisections to recover an admissible endpoint.
The adopted F2 fit required neither a penalty stage nor this retreat.
