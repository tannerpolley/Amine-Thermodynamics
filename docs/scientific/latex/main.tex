\begin{abstract}
% P:abstract-01
An electrolyte perturbed-chain statistical associating fluid theory (ePC-SAFT) model is calibrated for aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) loaded with carbon dioxide (\COtwo), and its water, \COtwo and inorganic-ion parameters are then held fixed while diethanolamine (DEA), methyldiethanolamine (MDEA) and 2-amino-2-methyl-1-propanol (AMP) are each fitted.
The shared water--bicarbonate and water--carbonate interactions are fixed beforehand from sodium-salt osmotic coefficients, so no amine data determine them.
The MEA model is the benchmark and supplies the fixed carbamate energy of DEA and AMP. It has 11 fitted parameters and literature equilibrium constants; its fitted \COtwo partial pressures at 30 wt\% deviate by 11.3\,\%, and its pressures at other concentrations and at stripper temperatures are less accurate.
With the shared parameters fixed, the fitted loaded-solution pressures, \COtwo partial pressures for DEA and total pressures for MDEA and AMP, deviate by 42.71\,\% for DEA, 4.15\,\% for MDEA and 10.84\,\% for AMP.
Phase-stability searches detect no instability for MEA or at the 666 searched AMP states, and confirm two MDEA liquid--liquid splits, at 204 and \SI{263}{\mega\pascal}, far above the measured pressures.
For DEA they confirm splits at four states whose densities were measured as one liquid, so the DEA model fails there.
A first AMP fit, with loaded-solution pressures to \SI{353}{\kelvin}, split at 363--\SI{393}{\kelvin}; a refit adding 58 measured pressures there confirms no split, though four measured states remain unresolved.
Phase-stability searches thus show where a fit has been extrapolated or fails.
The models are calibrations against data examined during development, not a basis for absorber design.

\end{abstract}
